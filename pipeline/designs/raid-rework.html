The Raid, reworked from the WoW research

A proposal, not built yet. Every row was checked against how the three bosses are built today (the code, 12 November), and against the raid's own rules (each screen decides hits on its own hero; shared state goes through the server; the autopilot must be able to play it). Fights for one and two players are not touched: they are measured, and WoW has no evidence under ten players.

A correction first. I told you the Ring of Hands needs a fixed 32 spots and leaves a party of 31 to 35 no margin. That was wrong: as built it puts a gold spot for every hero standing and holds when four in five are held. The research document is corrected.

What already works: keep it

  • The rooms that transform (the hall breaks open, the floor cracks into islands, the rings turn, the crust falls). This is the most-praised family of WoW fights: Blackhand's floors, Krosus' bridge, Ragnaros.
  • Hold Still burns only the one who moves: the kind of dance players love.
  • Take the Blade hands a player a powerful toy: the "personal job" players love most (Kael'thas' weapons).
  • Lantern Chain: only the pair pays. Pairs work at every party size.
  • The Hour Strikes costs only whoever is in a burning arm.
  • Counts per five heroes standing: the same rule WoW settled on (one more target per five players).

The big choice: who pays for a mistake

You asked for a raid that is hard and challenging. The research says players love hard fights but hate one player's mistake costing everyone. Six of our mechanics charge the whole party today (capped, never a wipe from full health):

Tier B: one moment per boss that players will remember

Sun Warden: Twelve Strikes

At 10 % he stops fighting and strikes the hour twelve times. Each strike sets one arm on fire, in clock order, shown on the dial on his back. The party runs round the hall just ahead of the fire. Survive the twelfth strike and he falls to his knees for the last blow.

A race against a clock everyone can read. Lessons used: the room as a clock; a miss burns only the one who missed.

Ash and Ember: the Twin Kneel

Bring both twins to two thirds, and later to one third, within 5 s of each other: both kneel together for 8 s, and the room changes gently (the bridges stand longer, the flood rises slower). Miss it and the room changes hard. At the end they must fall within 10 s of each other, or the one left raises the other at a quarter of its health.

The two halves of the party have to talk across the wall of fire. Lessons used: Halion's balance, the Twin Emperors.

Heart of Noon: The Last Beat

At 15 % the rule turns over. Moving is free now, but damage only counts on the beat: on the beat it hits harder, off it barely scratches. The beat is heard and seen on the floor, and quickens to the end, like a song's last chorus.

The finale flips the rule the players have learned all fight. Lessons used: change the rule each phase; make the last boss feel huge.
