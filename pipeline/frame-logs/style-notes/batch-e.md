# Style notes from batch-e reference videos

1. **Wind is shape, not colour.** Every wind effect (seo, Yordin) reads through tapered crescent strokes with a bright leading edge and a ragged or hooked tail. Rule: build wind from tapered ribbon strips with an alpha-erosion mask; never from round soft particles alone.
2. **Two wind dialects, pick one per effect.** Soft: translucent white sheets at 0.3–0.5 opacity that build brightness where they overlap. Hard/toon: opaque flat colour bands (light inner edge, saturated middle, dark outer edge) with thorn or sawtooth cuts on the outer edge and no glow. Do not mix the two in the same layer.
3. **Spread arcs over many tilted planes.** Crescents on random tilted planes around the centre make a flat-sprite effect read as a 3D sphere or tornado from any camera. A single horizontal ring reads as a decal.
4. **Hand-drawn shapes, procedural motion.** The best-looking parts (geodraws spikes and ribbons, Yordin blades, seo crescents) look hand-drawn: claw spikes, tapered brush strokes, white edge highlights. They are animated procedurally (UV scroll, erosion threshold, rotation, scale with overshoot). Rule: author the shape as a custom mesh or mask with a drawn silhouette, and animate it with a shader wipe instead of a flipbook.
5. **Dark behind bright, always.** geodraws' dark base under the pillar, Vladyslav's charcoal smoke behind blue lightning, seo's dark rocks against white arcs, yougelly's indigo nebula behind gold. The AI explosion fails because it has no dark layer and blows the frame to white. Every effect needs one normal-blended dark layer.
6. **Telegraph thin, pay off wide.** A star glint plus a needle-thin line (geodraws), a falling spark (Vladyslav), a ground swirl (seo): a tiny, precise mark before the big shape makes the payoff feel huge. Keep the telegraph on the same axis and in the same colour as the payoff.
7. **One impact frame beats a long flash.** geodraws uses a single inverted frame (white screen, black silhouettes); the good explosion uses a 3-frame puff. Rule: 1–3 frames of extreme contrast at the hit (FinishPass flash, inverted if the style is anime), then back to normal values at once.
8. **Hold, then reveal.** seo hides the caster in dust for a beat before the cyclone appears; Vladyslav shows a pale cloud dome before the lightning. A short hold in a low-energy state makes the release land harder.
9. **Fade by changing state, not by lowering alpha.** Arcs darken into teal ghosts (seo), blades break into thorn shards (Yordin), the pillar collapses to a line and a star (geodraws), the beam breaks into a sparkle trail on the floor (yougelly), embers land and twinkle out (Vladyslav). Uniform fades look like the AI version.
10. **One hue family plus one accent.** geodraws is all crimson from near-white pink to near-black; yougelly is gold with indigo; seo is white and mint with grey dust; the aura is a warm sun against a teal pool. Rule: build value with one hue ramp, use the complementary colour only as a backdrop or accent.
11. **Auras are compositions, not particles.** The onlyxyz aura is two poles (a sun over the head, a pool at the feet) linked by a faint ray, with one thematic custom sprite (dandelion seeds). Rule: an aura needs a ground anchor, a vertical element that leaves the silhouette readable (transparent at the top), and one signature shape.
12. **Concentrate detail at one point.** yougelly puts a detailed rune circle at the beam source and keeps the beam simple; small theme props (music notes, clef) are few and tiny. Rule: one detailed focal element per beat, everything else simple.
