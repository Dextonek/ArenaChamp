# Twenty-Two Years of Raid Bosses Teach One Rule

*A reference on World of Warcraft raid boss mechanics, from Molten Core (2004) to The Venomous Abyss (2026), for a designer building three party raid bosses. It synthesises five research notes compiled on 12 November 2026.*

*Evidence labels:*

- *MEASURED: a count with a stated method.*
- *OPINION: one author, one thread or one player.*
- *DESIGNER: a Blizzard encounter designer speaking.*
- *SNIPPET-ONLY: seen only in a search summary.*
- *"(Unverified)": a detail from a researcher's memory that no fetched page confirmed.*

## 1. Summary

**What the mechanics are.** WoW's roughly 450 raid encounters run on about twenty-five mechanic types: stack, spread, swap the tank, kill the adds, interrupt, dispel, kite, hide, dance, carry and the rest. That count is about 170 from Classic to Wrath, 144 from Cataclysm to Legion, 70 in Battle for Azeroth and Shadowlands, and 69 since. **The fights players remember add one rule found nowhere else, or make two familiar rules collide.** Examples of a unique rule are Thaddius' polarity, Netherspite's beams, Yogg-Saron's sanity and Averzian's tic-tac-toe board.

**The newest era.** The 12 raids and 69 encounters from Dragonflight to Midnight run on a settled skeleton:

- an energy bar that peaks at 100;
- a soft enrage, made of stacking damage or a filling floor;
- one or two signature rules per boss;
- on Mythic, a rule about players touching each other, or a split into realms.

Player-aimed mechanics and puzzles are far more common than in Classic. Vehicles and special buttons now come from the encounter, not from classes.

**Raid size.** Size went from a fixed 40 to a flexible 10-30 with Mythic fixed at 20. Midnight then added a flexible Mythic for 15-25 on its one-boss raids. The reusable scaling rule is roughly **one extra target per five players**. The War Within's hotfixes repeatedly cut damage, health and target counts for 10-player groups.

**What players love and hate.** No large poll exists. The one sizeable poll gave **the Lich King 42%** as best final boss, from an unpublished number of voters.

- *Loved:* a personal job, a dance where a miss kills only the dancer, a room that changes each phase, difficulty players choose, and a huge villain.
- *Hated:* fights that cannot be sped up (the Spine of Deathwing is the most-named), long fights with the hard part late (Sylvanas), one player's error wiping everyone, the character taken away, and add-on puzzles.

**Difficulty.** Race to World First pull counts measure difficulty, not fun. They run from 100 pulls (Gallywix) to 725 or 731 (Uu'nat, sources differ). The walls are the fights that chain the most individual duties.

**For the designer's raid.** The Sun Warden, Ash and Ember, and the Heart of Noon already follow most of the evidence: one signature rule each, rooms that transform, and counts that scale per five players. The open question is the plan's rule that **"a failure costs the whole party"**. The evidence argues instead for failures that fall mostly on whoever failed, and that unfold over seconds rather than wiping at once.
## 2. The catalogue by mechanic type: about twenty-five verbs carry every fight since 2004

Across about 450 encounters from Molten Core to Ula'tek, the same small set of verbs carries almost every fight:

- stand apart, or stand together;
- move out, or move as one;
- swap the tank;
- kill the adds;
- interrupt the cast, or remove the debuff;
- click the thing, or carry the thing.

What makes a boss memorable is rarely a new verb. It is **which two or three verbs collide at the same moment**. On the Lich King, Defile wants the raid spread while the Val'kyr want it stacked. On Festergut, Vile Gas wants ranged spread but the spores want them gathered ([Warcraft Wiki: Lich King](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lich_King_(tactics)); [Festergut](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Festergut)). The other way to be memorable is **one rule that exists nowhere else**: polarity, beams blocked by bodies, a ghost with its own action bar, a sanity meter, a boss you heal.

The type tags below are the researchers' own classification. No source counts WoW mechanics by type. Each entry gives how the mechanic works, what it asks of players, and the clearest examples across the eras.

### Tank swap

**How it works.** A stacking debuff on the active tank grows until the other tank must taunt. It is near-universal from Firelands on. Examples of the vulnerability attached to it:

- Mortal Wound at −10% healing per stack (Fankriss, Gluth).
- Wrath of Djaruun at **+3000% damage taken** on Heroic Rashok ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Rashok,_the_Elder)).
- The Arcanocannon at **+1000%** on Plexus Sentinel ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Plexus_Sentinel)).

**What it asks.** Two tanks with a shared rhythm. The forced swap is Blizzard's stated answer to "making sure there's something for the second tank to do" ([PCGamesN, Hazzikostas](https://www.pcgamesn.com/wow/world-warcraft-52-raids-art-and-science-raid-design)).

**Variants worth stealing:**

- **Vaelastrasz** kills his tanks on a schedule; the guide's advice is "bring five warriors".
- **Fetid Devourer**: the second tank stands beside the first and soaks every fourth swing.
- **Orgozoa**: the debuff helps the tank until 10 stacks kill.
- **Fractillus**: the tank's slam places the walls.
- **Loom'ithar**: the tank's strand cuts the exit.

Sources: [Warcraft Tavern BWL](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/bwl/), [Fetid Devourer](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Fetid_Devourer), [Orgozoa](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Orgozoa), [Fractillus](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Fractillus).

### Stack and soak (shared damage)

**How it works.** A hit is split among everyone it touches, or it needs a minimum number of players.

**What it asks.** That players gather on cue, and often that soak groups rotate because of a lingering vulnerability. By era 2 this was the single most common mechanic.

**Examples:**

- Patchwerk's Hateful Strike, taken by high-health off-tanks.
- Huhuran's **15 nearest players** in nature-resistance gear.
- Bloodboil's three rotating groups of five.
- Ta'yak's Unseen Strike: "3,000,000 Physical damage split amongst all players" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Blade_Lord_Ta%27yak)).
- Guarm's three element groups.
- Volcoross: two globs, **at least 4** each.
- Smolderon: **at least 5**, alternating groups.
- Mythic Sszorak: **14 players** in one circle ([Method](https://www.method.gg/guides/the-venomous-abyss/sszorak)).
- The Jailer's Desolation: **17 or more** soakers on Mythic ([Method](https://www.method.gg/guides/sepulcher-of-the-first-ones/the-jailer)).

### Spread

**How it works.** A marked player harms those near them, so they leave the group. The forms are a bomb (Baron Geddon's Living Bomb, 8 s to leave), a chain (C'Thun's Eye Beam, which doubles with each jump), or a growing pool (the Lich King's Defile, "the main cause of wipes").

**What it asks.** Personal awareness. The cost of a miss usually lands on the neighbours, which is why this family dominates "friendship breaker" lists ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/5-boss-mechanics-in-world-of-warcraft-that-break-friendships,950560)).

**Clearest examples:**

- C'Thun's fixed 40-player grid.
- Gruul's Shatter.
- Raszageth's Static Charge, which hurts less the farther its carrier stands.
- L'ura's Mythic circle on every player.

### Adds

**How it works.** Extra enemies to kill, hold, kite or prevent. Adds are the most common tag in every era.

**Defining variants:**

- **Adds that heal or empower the boss on arrival**: Gluth's zombies, Vashj's elementals, the Fallen-King's orbs, which wipe the raid on contact.
- **Adds that must die together**: Dimensius' Living Mass respawns unless all of it dies at once.
- **A whole fight of adds**: Kel'Thuzad's phase 1, Horridon, which Blizzard modelled on "Lady Deathwhisper or M'uru" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Horridon)).
- **An add phase that ends in a wipe if any add is left**: Chimaerus' Ravenous Dive.

### Interrupts

**How it works.** A cast that must be stopped. In Classic it was a class tool on a rotation (Kel'Thuzad's Frostbolt). It has become a counted job:

- Midnight Falls' Termination Prism needs **4 kicks each**.
- On Mythic Fallen-King Salhadaar, some images cannot be kicked until others are, which forces a fixed order.
- On Mythic Ula'tek, each Weakened Doomscale needs **3 kickers**.

Sources: [Method, Midnight Falls](https://method.gg/guides/march-on-queldanas/midnight-falls), [Method, Salhadaar](https://www.method.gg/guides/the-voidspire/fallen-king-salhadaar).

**Encounter-given interrupts.** Two fights hand players the interrupt itself: Tortos' kicked turtle shells and Amber-Shaper's player constructs ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Tortos)).

### Dispel (and do-not-dispel)

**How it works.** Remove a debuff, but often only at the right place or time.

**Examples:**

- Noth's curse becomes "an instant wipe" if it is left up.
- Rotface: the infected player walks to the edge before the dispel, so the ooze merges.
- The Lich King's Necrotic Plague is dispelled onto adds.
- Sinestra's Wrack bounces to two players and keeps its timer, so dispels are scheduled.
- Huhuran's Wyvern Sting and Jin'do's curse must **not** be dispelled.

Sources: [Warcraft Tavern Naxxramas](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/naxxramas/), [Rotface](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Rotface), [Sinestra](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sinestra_(tactics)).

### Kiting

**How it works.** One player leads a threat away.

**Examples:**

- Tanks: Anub'Rekhan around the room, Ossirian from crystal to crystal.
- The target of a fixate: Supremus, Thok, Big Bad Wolf.
- An assigned kiter: Vashj's Striders, Rotface's Big Ooze, Gluth's zombies.
- Modern versions use tethers that must be stretched to snap: Rasha'nan at 10 yards, Loom'ithar at 40.
- Vorasius' fixating adds are dragged onto walls to break them.

**What it asks.** That one player handles a separate mini-game while the raid fights.

### Line of sight and cover

**How it works.** Hide behind something:

- a wall (Firemaw, Chromaggus' breaths);
- a frozen player (Sapphiron, Sindragosa's Ice Tombs);
- pillars (Shriekwing, the Jailer);
- spikes (Goroth);
- asteroids (Sarkareth);
- bramble barriers players grow by healing (Larodar).

**Sindragosa is the landmark.** Players become the cover the others hide behind, and must then be freed: not too early, or the cover is gone ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sindragosa_(tactics))).

### Positional dance

**How it works.** The whole raid moves in a pattern:

- Heigan's four erupting floor zones, the "Safety Dance";
- Onyxia's Deep Breath lanes;
- Sartharion's lava walls;
- Durumu's maze;
- the Council of Blood's Danse Macabre, where bosses call steps ("Shimmy right!") and each player moves to a floor shape;
- Mug'Zee, where the whole raid crosses sides together.

**What it asks.** Shared timing. It is beloved when a miss kills only the dancer ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2021/02/26/whats-favorite-raid-mechanic); [Council of Blood](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Council_of_Blood)).

### The room that changes

**How it works.** The arena itself is the mechanic. It comes in six forms.

- **Floors that fill with permanent hazards.** Vashj's spore pools, Terros, Magmorax, Wrathion, Vorasius' Dark Goo. This is the era's cheapest room change: positioning becomes a resource spent over the fight, and the enrage arrives when the floor runs out.
- **Floors that break.** Blackhand's three floors at 70% and 30%, Fallen Avatar dropping the raid to the chamber below, Krosus smashing the bridge as the enrage, Ula'tek's four platforms.
- **Platforms the raid moves across.** Madness of Deathwing, Sennarth's climb, Tindral's flight, Ansurek's ascent, Crown of the Cosmos' gateways.
- **Moving machinery.** Thogar's trains, Blackfuse's conveyor, Hans'gar's presses, Lockenstock's belts.
- **Walls players raise and break.** Echo of Neltharion, Fractillus, Vorasius.
- **Arenas that shrink as the enrage.** Raszageth's storm, Nexus-King Salhadaar's spikes, Sylvanas' Raze.

Sources are on each boss line in sections 4 and 5. Room changes do three jobs at once: they mark the phase visibly, reset positioning habits, and work as a soft enrage players can see.

### Vehicle and gimmick

**How it works.** The encounter hands players a new body or a new button.

**Examples:**

- Razorgore's mind-control orb.
- The Karazhan Chess Event.
- Teron Gorefiend's ghost with its own action bar.
- Kil'jaeden's dragon orbs.
- Malygos' drakes.
- Flame Leviathan's siege vehicles.
- Putricide's abomination.
- The Gunship.
- Alysrazor's wings.
- Socrethar, where a tank pilots a construct. Blizzard: "What if you let the player control a raid boss?" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Socrethar_the_Eternal)).

**The newest era's versions:**

- Vexie's bikes.
- Stix's garbage balls, up to 4 players.
- Council of Dreams' duck.
- Plexus Sentinel's Phase Blink.
- Dimensius' flying mounts.

**The verdict splits.** Players love a gimmick that adds a verb, and hate one that "takes away your character" (Malygos) ([Icy Veins](https://wp-prod.icy-veins.com/?p=76965)).

### Council and multi-boss

**How it works.** Several bosses at once. They must die together or they revive, and the kill order changes the fight.

**Lineage:**

- **Classic:** Thekal's three must die within about 10 s. The Bug Trio's kill order decides the loot. The original Four Horsemen needed **eight tanks**.
- **TBC:** Maulgar's five, several needing non-standard tanks.
- **Wrath:** Iron Council, where "Steelbreaker last" is the hard mode.
- **Later:** Primal Council's Convocations stack per member killed. Council of Dreams heals a dying member by 30/50/100% (Normal, Heroic, Mythic). Vaelgor & Ezzorak must stay **within 10% health** and **more than 15 yards apart**.

Sources: [Four Horsemen (Classic)](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Four_Horsemen_(Classic)), [Council of Dreams](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Council_of_Dreams), [Method](https://www.method.gg/guides/the-voidspire/vaelgor-ezzorak).

### Intermission

**How it works.** A break in the boss fight that is a different fight:

- Ragnaros' Sons of Flame march to the hammer, and each player is assigned a Son by position;
- Raszageth's "vault integrity" drains while she is shielded;
- Ansurek reels the raid in with Predation Threads;
- the Entombed Sentinels' orb-count mini-game.

**What it asks.** The raid re-forms around a new objective. Common shape: the boss is immune, the raid must break a shield or kill adds before a timer.

### Enrage (hard and soft)

**How it works.** Three shapes recur across all eras.

- **A hard timer.** Patchwerk at 7 minutes, Brutallus at about 6, Blood-Queen at 5:30, Kurog's Mythic at **10 minutes**.
- **A growing buff or shrinking interval.** Gruul's Growth at +15% per 30 s, Loatheb's Inevitable Doom, Smolderon's four stacks of Heating Up.
- **A room that fills or shrinks.** Terros, Krosus, Wrathion.

**The modern habit.** The third cast of the big ability never ends: Ky'veza's Eternal Night, Rik Reverb's Hype Fever. A soft enrage is the gentler tool, because a weaker group sees more of the fight before it fails, where a hard timer only says no.

### Personal resource bars

**How it works.** Every player has a meter that mechanics fill, with escalating thresholds. This is era 2's signature tool:

- Atramedes' Sound, where ten gongs are a limited budget.
- Cho'gall's Corruption, with thresholds at 25/50/75/100.
- Yogg-Saron's Sanity, where zero means permanent mind control.
- Sha of Pride's Pride.
- Norushen's role trials.
- Jaina's Chilling Touch, which freezes at 20 stacks.
- N'Zoth's Sanity.
- Twin Fangs' Eternal Venom, which kills at 10.

**Why it travels well.** The bar belongs to each player, so it **does not depend on raid size**. That is the property a 2-to-40-player game most needs ([Warcraft Wiki: Cho'gall](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Cho%27gall_(tactics)); [Atramedes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Atramedes)).

### Splitting into groups or realms

**How it works.** Part of the raid goes elsewhere. The lineage grows step by step:

- C'Thun's stomach;
- Gothik's living and dead sides;
- Kalecgos' spectral realm, with a 60 s re-entry lockout;
- Yogg-Saron's brain portals: 4 portals on 10-player, 10 on 25-player;
- Halion's two realms with balanced damage;
- Garrosh's Realm of Y'Shaarj;
- N'Zoth's mind;
- Kel'Thuzad's phylactery;
- the Bloodbound Horror's Unseeming;
- Chimaerus' spirit realm.

**The extreme.** Midnight Falls splits the raid into three realms that each see only part of a rune sequence, then into two dimensions where **touching a player of the other dimension is instant death** ([Method](https://method.gg/guides/march-on-queldanas/midnight-falls)).

### Assigned single-player jobs

**How it works.** Jobs fall into four kinds.

- **(a) Pre-assigned specialists.** Magtheridon's cube clickers; Maulgar's mage tank; Vashj's Strider kiter.
- **(b) Rotations forced by a lockout.** Netherspite's beam shifts; Larodar's 4-minute Dream Fatigue.
- **(c) Jobs where "the mechanic picks you".** Teron's ghost, Living Bomb, Frost Beacon.
- **(d) Tool operators.** Kil'jaeden's dragons, Putricide's abomination, Ula'tek's **8 egg carriers**.

**How the jobs scale.** Kind (c) scales to any party size, because it needs no roster planning. Kinds (a) and (b) need spare bodies, and they broke down at 10 players. Razuvious' priests were replaced by two crystals anyone can click ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Instructor_Razuvious)).

**The players' view.** The most-praised mechanic of all time is a job of this family: Nefarian's class calls, named by three independent sources ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/the-5-best-raid-mechanics-in-wow-of-all-time,1239457/)).

### Puzzle and memory

**How it works.** A rule to work out, usually by talking.

**Examples:**

- Yor'sahj's colour priority.
- Durumu's maze.
- Dark Animus' anima chain.
- Mekkatorque's codes: on Heroic and Mythic, only other players can see a bot's code.
- Fatescribe's ring rotation, where an odd count turns clockwise.
- Lords of Dread's impostor vote, an "Among Us" phase.
- Averzian's tic-tac-toe board.
- The Entombed Sentinels' orb counts, shown and then hidden.
- L'ura's rune memory game.

Sources: [Mekkatorque](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/High_Tinker_Mekkatorque_(tactics)), [Lords of Dread](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lords_of_Dread).

**Puzzles depend on not being solved by add-ons.** Blizzard balanced earlier puzzles around "WeakAura solvers", then removed add-on access to auras in patch 12.1 so that puzzles "challenging to solve again" could return (SNIPPET-ONLY, Dylan Barker; [Icy Veins news, page returned 403](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/boss-fights-in-midnight-wont-need-weakauras-anymore/)). The 12.1 aura change itself is confirmed in an interview with the encounter designers ([Icy Veins interview](https://wp-prod.icy-veins.com/blizzard-interview-add-ons-raid-design-and-mythic-in-patch-12-1/)).

### Player-aimed or player-placed mechanics

**How it works.** The boss hands players an object to place: a puddle, a beam, a wall, a knockback, a bomb. This type grew most in the newest era.

**Examples:**

- Diurna's staff beam through rows of eggs.
- Sikran's Decimate lines through Simulacra.
- Echo of Neltharion: knockback targets break chosen walls.
- Vashnik: Froth waves aimed through totems are the **only** way to remove them.
- Ula'tek's Caustic Waves along an arrow, which wipe the raid if aimed at it.
- Sludgefist: the tank aims each charge into one of 4 pillars, and the fifth charge wipes.

**Older ancestors:** Ragnaros' Magma Traps, triggered on purpose at the edge, and Zon'ozz's bouncing orb.

### Other types the notes support

- **Mind control of or by players.** Razuvious' understudies tanked by priests; Yogg-Saron's insanity; Blood-Queen's missed bite.
- **Threat as the mechanic.** Classic's threat wipes: Onyxia, Shazzrah, Void Reaver's untauntable threat ladder.
- **Chosen difficulty inside the fight.** Hakkar's priests, Sartharion's 0-3 drakes, Ulduar's hard modes, Yogg-Saron's Keepers from 4 down to 0. Blizzard later judged these "inconsistent and sometimes unclear" and moved difficulty to a menu ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-04-28-blizzard-on-raid-design-evolution.html)).
- **Choice that changes the fight.** Igira's weapons; Lei Shen's conduits; Megaera's heads; Madness' platform order.
- **Healing or roles inverted.** Valithria is won by healing her. On Anub'arak, healers keep the raid low on purpose. Immerseus' puddles are healed to full. Tsulong is an ally by day.
- **Damage race or gear check.** Patchwerk, Brutallus, Archavon, the Butcher. Blizzard names one "Patchwerk" per raid ([Warcraft Wiki: Malkorok](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Malkorok_(tactics))).
- **No boss at all.** Spoils of Pandaria, Hellfire Assault, Eonar: waves, escorts or lanes instead of a boss.
- **PvP-style enemies.** Faction Champions, "much like an arena match", without threat ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Faction_Champions)).
- **Hidden information.** Private auras (patch 10.1, 2023) hid debuffs from add-ons:
  - Echo of Neltharion's Volcanic Heart.
  - Smolderon's Seeking Inferno.
  - Fyrakk's orb debuffs, untagged on 1 February 2024.

  Midnight then made hidden information the encounter's own rule: ghosts visible only to their target on the Coiled Altar, and runes split by realm on L'ura ([Raider.IO](https://raider.io/news/613-private-auras-were-they-successful)).
- **Touching rules.** Mythic's favourite addition is a rule about players touching each other. Thaddius' polarity is the ancestor. Terros, the Amalgamation Chamber, Lockenstock, the Entombed Sentinels and L'ura's Severance carry it on.
- **Secret phases.** Ra-den is "the 'Algalon' of the zone", with mechanics "deliberately obscured". Mythic Argus has "secret phases". The Jailer has a secret Mythic phase ([Warcraft Wiki: Ra-den](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ra-den_(tactics))).
## 3. Scaling: from fixed 40 to "one more target per five players"

### Five size models in twenty-two years

WoW has used five ways of setting raid size:

| Era | Sizes and difficulties | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Classic (2004-06) | 40, with two 20-player raids. "There are no difficulty levels or variable raid sizes in Vanilla WoW" | [Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/wow-classic/guides/raids/) |
| TBC (2007-08) | 25, plus two 10-player raids (Karazhan, Zul'Aman). "No new difficulty modes were added" | [Warcraft Tavern](https://www.warcrafttavern.com/tbc/guides/raids/) |
| Wrath (2008-10) | Every raid in parallel 10 and 25. Difficulty first in-fight (Sartharion's drakes, Ulduar), then a Normal/Heroic menu (Trial of the Crusader, Icecrown, switchable per boss) | [Engadget, Hazzikostas retrospective](https://www.engadget.com/2014-04-28-blizzard-on-raid-design-evolution.html) |
| Cataclysm and Mists (2010-14) | 10 and 25 on one shared lockout with the same loot; Raid Finder at a fixed 25 from patch 4.3; Flexible 10-25 in Siege of Orgrimmar (5.4) | [Warcraft Wiki: Raid Finder](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Raid_Finder); [Flexible Raid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid) |
| Warlords to Midnight (2014-26) | Normal and Heroic flexible 10-30; Mythic fixed at 20; then flexible Mythic 15-25 on Midnight's one-boss raids (Sporefall, the Lair) | [Warcraft Wiki: Flexible Raid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid); [Instance difficulty](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Instance_difficulty) |

**Each change was a reaction to a cost**:

- **40 to 25.** Bigger raids "didn't necessarily make players feel more heroic" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-04-28-blizzard-on-raid-design-evolution.html)).
- **Equal 10s and 25s (Cataclysm).** Making them equal meant "increasing the difficulty of 10-player mode", and "spreading out requires more coordination with more players in the same space". It also created "a persistent downward pressure on raid size": "Growing from 10 to 25 was nearly impossible logistically" ([Wowhead, Hazzikostas](https://www.wowhead.com/news/warlords-pvp-gearing-blog-patch-5-4-7-hotfixes-for-april-28-raid-philosophy-part-236385)).
- **Flexible.** Blizzard asked "why force someone to have exactly 9 or 24 friends?" Flexible was "roughly 20% to 25% easier (numerically speaking) than Normal mode... while preserving all of the fight mechanics" (same source).
- **Why Mythic is 20.** The answer is explicit and matters for any designer. Community manager Lore: "We chose to put Mythic at 20 largely for the function of raid design... It's impossible for every group to have every class... At 20, it becomes a lot more acceptable for us to say 'you should probably bring a Mage to Spellsteal this.'" Fifteen was judged too small to guarantee one of every class ([Engadget, 2013](https://www.engadget.com/2013-11-13-mythic-raiding-why-20-man.html)).

| Ragnaros' Molten Seeds | 10 players | 20 players | [Ragnaros](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ragnaros_(Firelands_tactics)) |
| Alysrazor fliers | 1 | 3 | [Alysrazor](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alysrazor) |
| Cho'gall's Darkened Creations | 4 | 10 | [Cho'gall](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Cho%27gall_(tactics)) |

- **Thresholds grew faster than head count.** The Twin Val'kyr shield absorbs **175,000 on 10-player and 700,000 on 25-player**: four times the amount for 2.5 times the players ([Twin Val'kyr](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Twin_Val'kyr)).
- **So did health.** Archavon needed **7,670 raid DPS on 10 against 33,236 on 25** ([Archavon](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Archavon_the_Stone_Watcher)).
- **Some jobs were recast for the smaller raid.** Razuvious' priest mind-controllers became two crystals anyone can click on 10-player ([Razuvious](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Instructor_Razuvious)).
- **Some mechanics existed only in the larger raid**: Deathwhisper's mind control, and Kologarn's forced tank rotation.

**2. The flexible rule (Siege of Orgrimmar onward).** "There are generally no set breakpoints for boss abilities. With a few exceptions, **each person in the raid adds a 20% chance for the boss ability to target an extra person or have an extra effect**" ([Warcraft Wiki: Flexible Raid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid)). That is about one extra target per five players.

- **It replaced hard breakpoints.** In a search summary, a developer said Shadow Word: Bane hit 2 targets at 10-14 players and 3 at 15. The weighted rule gives a 13-player raid 3 targets 60% of the time.
- **Fixed counts were kept where groups must pre-plan**, such as Imprison on the Sha of Pride (SNIPPET-ONLY, [Wowhead](https://classic.wowhead.com/news=225217)).

**3. Explicit target ranges in Blizzard's tuning notes (Shadowlands).** From the February 2021 Castle Nathria tuning post:

- "Seismic Upheaval now targets **4 to 10 players, scaling from 10 to 30**-player raid sizes, on Normal and Heroic"; Raid Finder 3-5.
- "Heart Rend now targets **1 to 3 players**".
- Inerva "throws fewer bottles for smaller raid sizes, scaling from 1 to 3 (was 2 to 3)" on Normal, and 2 to 4 on Heroic.
- Stone Legion Generals' damage was cut **25-33% for 10-player raids** and raised for large ones.

Source: [Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/dungeon-and-raid-tuning-february-9/863057).

**4. Hotfixes at the small end (The War Within).** The same pattern repeats raid after raid: damage, health and target counts were cut for small Normal and Heroic groups.

- **Ovi'nax:** smaller groups got fewer Sticky Web targets and longer Poison Burst casts ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Broodtwister_Ovi'nax)).
- **Liberation of Undermine:** most bosses had damage cut for smaller groups, **largest at 10 players** ([Warcraft Wiki, Rik Reverb page notes](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Rik_Reverb)).
- **Rik Reverb:** the duration of Lingering Voltage scales with group size.
- **Nexus-King Salhadaar:** Banishment hits fewer players in smaller groups, with "no change at 30 or on Mythic" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Nexus-King_Salhadaar_(tactics))).

**5. Rules that scale themselves.** These rules adjust to the number of players present without any per-size tuning:

- **"Damage split among those hit."** Used by the most common soak family.
- **"Force divided among those chained."** The Eye of the Jailer's Dragging Chains, whose pull is "reduced by the number of players being chained" ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Eye_of_the_Jailer_(tactics))).
- **Per-player resource bars.** These do not depend on raid size at all.

The rule that does not scale is **"at least N players"**, which is fixed in the ability text. Two cases apply on the flexible difficulties as well:

| Boss | Players needed |
|---|---|
| Volcoross | 4 per glob |
| Smolderon | 5 |

At 10 players these take a far larger share of the group than at 30. *Inference, not documented:* this may be one reason the 10-player hotfixes recur. The hotfix notes themselves name only damage, health and target counts.

Mythic-only thresholds never meet a 10-player group, so they show only how far fixed counts go at 20: Mythic Ulgrax 6, Twin Fangs 4 per hit, Sszorak 14, the Jailer's Desolation 17.

### How difficulty levels differ, with numbers

**Difficulty changes more than numbers**:

- **Heroic usually adds one mechanic per boss.** Examples: Putricide's Unbound Plague, the Lich King's Shadow Traps, Ky'veza's Eclipse.
- **Mythic often adds a rule or a whole phase.** Examples: Garrosh's Mythic-only stage 4, Archimonde's Nether Ascension, Mar'gok's two runestones at once, Velhari's lingering auras, the Jailer's secret phase. Mythic also turns "puddles" into wipes: on Mythic Fallen-King Salhadaar, missed kicks and orbs are fatal instead of leaving puddles ([Method](https://www.method.gg/guides/the-voidspire/fallen-king-salhadaar)).

**Lower difficulties remove the single point of failure:**

- Raid Finder (from Dragon Soul) promised "removal or modification of specific mechanics that normally make the raid dependent on the performance of a single player", plus **+5% per wipe up to 10 stacks** ([Warcraft Wiki: Raid Finder](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Raid_Finder)).
- Mekkatorque's codes are visible to everyone in Raid Finder.
- Council of Dreams gets shared damage in Raid Finder.
- Restless Cabal ignores already-used relics in Raid Finder.
- Story Mode (from Queen Ansurek in 2024) is a further step below Raid Finder.

**What the designers say.** Lower difficulties use "longer pass/fail rules", and Mythic is harsher. The designers **prefer a fight to "fall apart over a few seconds" rather than wipe instantly, "so players can understand what went wrong"** ([Seramate on a Wowhead interview, Aug 2026](https://seramate.com/news/2026/08/22/ellesmere-encounter-design-interview-1f19e39f), a secondary source).

**What this means below 10 and at 40.** WoW offers **no evidence below 10 players**, and its only 40-player evidence is Classic, which was tuned when "bringing more players generally made things easier" ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2014-04-28-blizzard-on-raid-design-evolution.html)). The 10-player versions suggest which mechanics survive shrinking: movement patterns, interrupts, kiting, and a soak shared between two tanks. Two kinds do not survive: multi-group rotations, and councils with special tanks.
## 4. The newest expansions boss by boss: 12 raids, 69 encounters, one energy-bar skeleton

As of 12 November 2026, Dragonflight, The War Within and Midnight hold **12 raid instances and 69 encounters**: three Dragonflight raids, three War Within raids and six in Midnight (The Voidspire, The Dreamrift, March on Quel'Danas, The Venomous Abyss, plus the one-boss Sporefall and the one-boss Lair, The Tidebound Grotto). Almost every fight in this era runs on the boss's **energy bar**. A fixed loop of abilities ends in a big ability at 100 energy, and a stacking soft enrage ends the fight: a damage buff per cycle, or a floor that fills up. On top of that loop each boss has one or two rules of its own, such as a tic-tac-toe board, a duck, a garbage ball the players steer, or a memory game split across three realms. Mythic usually adds one more rule that ties players to each other.

How to read this section:

- **Sources.** Dragonflight and War Within boss lines rest on Warcraft Wiki boss pages, which reproduce the in-game Encounter Journal. Midnight boss lines rest mostly on **Method's Mythic strategy guides** (updated May to November 2026). Those guides describe the Mythic version and rarely the Normal one. Where Method did not label a mechanic Mythic-only, it is described here as "per Method's Mythic guide".
- **Tags.** Mechanic types are given in square brackets, matching the catalogue in section 2.
- **Interpretation.** Short connecting clauses ("so the raid soaks it fully") are the researcher's reading of the ability's purpose, not text from the source.

### Midnight (2026): three raids at launch, a bonus boss, a Lair, then The Venomous Abyss

Midnight was released on 2 March 2026 ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/World_of_Warcraft:_Midnight)).

**Season 1 had three raids with nine bosses**: The Voidspire (6), The Dreamrift (1) and March on Quel'Danas (2). Story Mode is open for The Voidspire and March on Quel'Danas ([Blizzard](https://worldofwarcraft.blizzard.com/en-us/news/24223664)). The Voidspire and The Dreamrift opened on 17 March 2026, with Mythic on 24 March. March on Quel'Danas opened on 31 March ([Questionably Epic](https://questionablyepic.com/season-one-raids); [CogConnected](https://cogconnected.com/2026/04/world-of-warcraft-march-on-queldanas-raid-overview/)).

Midnight then added two one-boss raids with a **flexible Mythic for 15 to 25 players**, a format new to WoW:

- **Sporefall** (patch 12.0.7, June 2026).
- **The Tidebound Grotto** (the "Lair", patch 12.1).

It also added a full 8-boss raid, **The Venomous Abyss** (patch 12.1, opened 18-19 August 2026). Its wiki infobox says "Player limit: 25", and what that means is unexplained ([Warcraft Wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Venomous_Abyss)).

**What the era tells a designer.** Across the 69 encounters:

- **Mythic most often adds a rule about players touching each other.** Examples: Terros, the Amalgamation Chamber, Lockenstock, Entombed Sentinels, Raszageth's Inversion, and L'ura's Severance.
- **Realm splits recur in every expansion.** Examples: Sarkareth, Bloodbound Horror, Echo, Chimaerus, Midnight Falls.
- **Vehicles and special actions are handed out by the encounter, not by classes.** Examples: bikes, garbage balls, ducks, dragonriding, flying mounts, Phase Blink, feeding the boss, and mushrooms.

Splits and encounter-given actions are the era's choice of how to make 20 people play differently without depending on who brought which class.

## 6. What players love and hate: one poll, small samples, and the pull counts

### Mechanic families loved and hated

The verdict below is OPINION unless a designer is cited. **Memorable and hated are not opposites:** a strong gimmick is remembered either way, and execution decides which side it lands on.

| Mechanic family | Verdict | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| A personal job for a class or role | **Loved** (3 independent sources for class calls) | Nefarian's class calls, Valithria's healer orbs |
| A dance where a miss kills only the dancer | **Loved** | Heigan; Council of Blood ("amazing seeing all 30 players moving in sync"; "more amusing than destructive") |
| A room or rule that changes each phase | **Loved** | Lei Shen's quadrants, Deathwing's platforms, Blackhand's floors, Yogg-Saron's brain room |
| Difficulty players choose inside the fight | **Loved** | Yogg-Saron's Keepers, Mimiron's Firefighter ("crazy but fun") |
| Scale, voice and music; the story villain last | **Loved** | Lich King (42%), Ragnaros ("made the players feel small"), Dimensius, Denathrius |
| Hidden information solved by talking | **Loved** (new, thin) | Midnight's coffin room: "kudos to that room where you have to find the thing" ([Icy Veins](https://wp-prod.icy-veins.com/blizzard-interview-add-ons-raid-design-and-mythic-in-patch-12-1/)) |
| Length fixed by script; waves or escort with no boss | **Hated**: "can't be sped up" recurs in 3 independent sources | Spine, Galakras, Spoils, Hellfire Assault, Ultraxion |
| A long fight with the hard part late | **Hated** | Sylvanas; Heroic Sha of Fear ("20 min monotonous slugfest"); a Mythic guild column: "around a 15 minute encounter, and we're getting to around eight minutes in on our best pulls" (SNIPPET-ONLY) |
| One player's error wipes everyone | **Hated** but memorable | Rygelon, Dark Animus, Living Bomb, Defile, Razorgore's orb |
| The character taken away; vehicles | **Hated** | Malygos' drakes, Razorgore |
| Needing an add-on to solve | **Hated** | Tindral, Echo of Neltharion; Blizzard's answer was to remove add-on access to auras from patch 12.1 |
| Reaction-time tests that gear cannot help | **Disliked by the designers** | L'ura's glaives (nerfed) |
| Time sinks between pulls | **Hated** | Monologues, runbacks, forced roleplay |

**The inference that matters most.** The same mechanic family lands on either side depending on **who pays for a miss**. A dance is loved when a miss kills only the dancer. A bomb is hated when it kills the neighbours.
## 7. Design lessons, and what they mean for the Sun Warden, Ash and Ember, and the Heart of Noon

### Ten lessons, with the weight of evidence behind each

The evidence is mostly opinion, plus designer statements and a few measured funnels. Each lesson below says which kind it rests on.

1. **Make the final boss the story villain, and make it look huge.** The only sizeable poll gave the Lich King 42%, ahead of Illidan. Ragnaros is praised because the room "made the players feel small". Dimensius and Denathrius are praised for scale, voice and music. *Basis: measured poll (sample size unknown) plus opinion.* ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/you-have-chosen-these-are-the-5-best-final-bosses-of-world-of-warcraft,465862))
2. **Change the rules or the room every phase.** Hazzikostas wants "distinct phases ... instead of you just stabbing its ankle for five minutes straight". The most-praised fights rebuild the arena: Lei Shen, Blackhand, Deathwing's platforms, Star Augur. *Basis: designer plus opinion.* ([PCGamesN](https://www.pcgamesn.com/wow/world-warcraft-52-raids-art-and-science-raid-design))
3. **Give every player, and every role, a named job.** Class calls are the most-cited loved mechanic. Blizzard designs for "something for the second tank to do", and gives healers triage rather than "flat-rate damage". *Basis: designer plus opinion.*
4. **Let a miss punish the one who missed.** The Council of Blood dance is "more amusing than destructive" because only the misstepper dies. Rygelon, Dark Animus and Living Bomb are hated because one error kills all. Where a raid-wide failure is right, designers let the fight "fall apart over a few seconds" so players see what went wrong. *Basis: opinion plus designer.* ([Mein-MMO](https://mein-mmo.de/en/5-boss-mechanics-in-world-of-warcraft-that-break-friendships,950560); [Seramate](https://seramate.com/news/2026/08/22/ellesmere-encounter-design-interview-1f19e39f))
5. **Never make a fight whose length players cannot shorten.** "Can't be sped up" recurs in three independent worst-of sources: Spine, Galakras, Spoils, Hellfire Assault, Ultraxion. *Basis: opinion, repeated.*
6. **Keep the hard part early, or reachable for practice.** Sylvanas is the most-hated boss in one ranking. Blizzard nerfed her because players were "getting out of the first phase much later than originally anticipated". Hazzikostas keeps "the epic ten to fifteen minute encounter" for late bosses. *Basis: designer plus opinion.*
7. **Telegraph everything in one visual language.** The designers' words: "very clear telegraphing or very clear signaling", targeting marks a few seconds before damage, cones that show at once how a creature will behave. *Basis: designer.* ([Icy Veins interview](https://wp-prod.icy-veins.com/blizzard-interview-add-ons-raid-design-and-mythic-in-patch-12-1/))
8. **Do not take the hero away, and do not test raw reaction speed.** Malygos' drakes are hated ("takes away your character"). L'ura's glaives were cut because they tested reaction time rather than gear. *Basis: opinion plus designer.*
9. **Ramp smoothly; avoid a single wall boss; offer optional hard modes on top.** The measured Mythic funnel drops 72.8% at one boss, against 11% on Heroic (third-party data). Watcher's warning: a too-hard boss is "a brick wall that entirely blocks progress". Players love choosing difficulty themselves (Yogg-Saron's Keepers, Mimiron's Firefighter). *Basis: measured plus designer plus opinion.* ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2013-01-17-dev-watercooler-watcher-on-encounter-tuning.html))
10. **Puzzles must be solved by players talking, not by a helper tool.** Tindral and Echo were hated as "weak aura bosses". Blizzard removed add-on access to auras in 12.1 so that puzzles could return, and the interviewer praised Midnight's room "where you have to find the thing". *Basis: designer plus opinion.*

### Scaling lessons for a raid of 2 to 40

- **Scale by share, not by fixed count.** WoW's own data shows the cleanest rule. Wrath kept the touched share roughly constant (beacons 2 of 10 against 5 of 25). Flexible raids add about **one target per five players**. Blizzard's 2021 notes write explicit ranges, such as "4 to 10 players, scaling from 10 to 30".
- **Fixed thresholds weigh far more on small groups.** A rule like "at least 4" or "at least 5" takes 40-50% of a 10-player group but under 20% of a 30-player one. The War Within's repeated cuts for 10-player groups are documented as cuts to damage, health and target counts. Linking them to the thresholds is an inference.
- **Use mechanics that need no scaling.** Personal resource bars, "damage split among those hit", and pairs (the smallest coordination unit) all work at any size.
- **Bind special jobs to objects, not to classes.** WoW kept Mythic at 20 precisely so it could demand a class. Below 20 it replaced class jobs with clickable objects (Razuvious' crystals).
- **WoW's modern evidence covers 10 to 30.** Flexible Normal and Heroic run up to 30. Nothing in WoW covers fewer than 10 players, and only Classic covers 40. So the 2- and 5-player bands and the 40 band are the designer's own territory.

### The three bosses against the evidence

The raid's plan in `pipeline/designs/raid-party.md`:

- **Bands.** The fight takes its size from who is present: 1-2 → 2, 3-5 → 5, 6-10 → 10, 11-15 → 15, 16-20 → 20, 21-30 → 30, 31-40 → 40.
- **Mechanics.** Each boss has one mechanic for any party, plus mechanics that appear only at 10+, 20+ and 40.
- **Rooms.** The room transforms at the second phase and again at the last.
- **Four rules for all:**
  - "a failure costs the whole party";
  - "the numbers come from the players standing";
  - "in the last phase two of them may overlap";
  - each is "announced by a name on the cast bar and a first-time hint".

### The transforming rooms

**They match the most-praised family of all.**

- **The Sun Warden's hall** first breaks open, then the sun comes down in a growing ring. This has three precedents:
  - Blackhand's floors;
  - Krosus' bridge, whose shrinking is the enrage;
  - Raszageth's closing storm.
- **Ash and Ember's pit** cracks into islands, then floods until one island is left. Precedents: Nefarian's lava and pillars, and Ula'tek's breaking platforms.
- **The Heart's room** has turning rings, then crust that falls sector by sector. The rings recall Nymue's moving lanes and Blackfuse's conveyor; the falling crust recalls Terros and Fallen Avatar.

**The lesson from those fights:** tie the room to a clock players can read. The falling crust is the best example, a floor too small to stand on if the fight runs long. One caution applies: the moving fights (Sylvanas, Halondrus) were "the era's most expensive and most criticised", because a late mistake means repeating the walk.

### Two tensions to decide on

**1. Who pays for a miss.** The doc's rule that "a failure costs the whole party" is in tension with the strongest pattern in the opinion evidence. Mechanics land on the hated side when one player's error kills the others, and on the loved side when a miss kills only the one who missed. The designers' answer is the middle path: a raid-wide failure that "falls apart over a few seconds" instead of wiping at once. This rests on opinion and designer statements, not on measurement.

**2. A wall before the finale.** In the measured funnels the wall is often the boss just before the end:

- the Silken Court took 304 pulls, against Ansurek's 404;
- the Coiled Altar caused a 72.8% drop in Mythic guild kills, against 11% on Heroic.

The Undermine tier peaked before its finale, which pleased one critic. For ordinary groups, a wall before the last boss means they never see the finale. Whichever of the three bosses comes second should not be the hardest. Twin Flames, the largest of the three mechanics to build, is the likeliest candidate to become that wall.
## Conclusion

WoW has spent twenty years inventing new rules, but its lasting lessons are about **who pays for a miss**, and **whether players can see what is coming**. Loved and hated bosses are not separated by mechanic type. Dances, carries and splits appear on both sides. They are separated by three things:

- whether the failure lands on the one who failed;
- whether better play shortens the fight;
- whether the rule can be read off the screen without an add-on.

Midnight shows the same arc. Blizzard removed add-on access to auras so that hidden-information puzzles could return. It cut L'ura's glaives because they tested reflexes, not gear. Meanwhile, its hardest fight became a memory game split across three realms.

For a raid of 2 to 40, the most reusable tools WoW found are:

- counts that scale with the share of players present, about one per five;
- personal bars and pairs, which need no scaling at all;
- rooms that transform as a visible clock.

The Sun Warden, Ash and Ember, and the Heart of Noon already use all three. Their main open risk is not their ideas. It is the plan's rule that every failure costs the whole party, the one pattern players have most consistently resented.

No source covers parties below 10, or modern design at 40. At those bands, the game's own simulator and playtests will be the only evidence.
