# WoW raid boss mechanics, era 2: Cataclysm, Mists of Pandaria, Warlords of Draenor, Legion

How to read these notes
- Every boss line names its mechanics, what the raid must do, and what failure costs. Mechanic types are tagged inline in square brackets, using the era 1 vocabulary plus three additions: [tank swap], [spread], [stack/soak], [adds], [interrupt], [dispel], [kite], [LoS] (line of sight), [mind control], [dance] (positional pattern), [hazard] (ground or room hazard), [gimmick] (vehicle or special-action fight), [council] (several bosses at once), [phase], [intermission], [enrage], [resource] (debuff or resource management), [split] (raid divided into groups or realms), [job] (a named player is assigned a task), [dps race], [room change] (the arena itself changes during the fight), [puzzle] (memory, pattern or logic task), [choice] (the raid chooses an order or a buff that changes the fight).
- Source rule: each boss line rests on the Warcraft Wiki (warcraft.wiki.gg) page linked at its start. Those pages hold the in-game Adventure Guide (Encounter Journal) ability text plus a community "Strategy" section; they were read through the wiki's own API (page source), which gives the full page text. Named abilities, numbers and quoted phrases come from those pages. Numbers are the page's patch state (often Cataclysm Classic or later-patch values, sometimes rescaled), so use them as proportions, not absolutes. Anything marked "(unverified)" was not on a fetched page and comes from the researcher's memory: treat it as a lead, not a fact.
- Raid lists: the Cataclysm list (six raids, 31 encounters) is confirmed by the wiki's Adventure Guide copy — [Warcraft Wiki, Adventure Guide Cataclysm raids](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Adventure_Guide_Cataclysm_raids). The Mists, Warlords and Legion lists are confirmed the same way, from the wiki's Adventure Guide copies: Mists has MSV 6, HoF 6, ToES 4, ToT 13 including Ra-den, and SoO 14 ([Warcraft Wiki, Adventure Guide Mists of Pandaria raids](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Adventure_Guide_Mists_of_Pandaria_raids)). Warlords has Highmaul 7, BRF 10 and HFC 13 ([Adventure Guide Warlords of Draenor raids](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Adventure_Guide_Warlords_of_Draenor_raids)). Legion has EN 7, ToV 3, NH 10, ToS 9 and Antorus 11 ([Adventure Guide Legion raids](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Adventure_Guide_Legion_raids)). No raid is missing from the assignment's list (Cataclysm 6, Mists 5, Warlords 3, Legion 5). World bosses (Mists' Sha of Anger, Galleon, Nalak, Oondasta, the Celestials, Ordos; Warlords' Drov, Tarlna, Rukhmar, Kazzak; Legion's Broken Isles and Argus world bosses) are outdoor encounters, not raid instances, and are not covered.

## 1. For each boss: what are its defining mechanics?

### Takeaway
The four expansions hold 19 raid instances and 144 encounters (Cataclysm 31, Mists 43 counting Throne of Thunder's hidden Ra-den, Warlords 30, Legion 40 counting Trial of Valor's 3), all catalogued below. The era's signature is the "one rule per fight" design made into a full-raid system: a personal resource bar (Atramedes' Sound, Cho'gall's Corruption, Norushen's Corruption, Gul'dan's and Kil'jaeden's bars), a fight that rebuilds the arena (Ragnaros' platform, Deathwing's back, Blackhand's three floors, Thogar's tracks, Fallen Avatar's collapsing floor), and from Warlords on, Mythic-only extra phases and mechanics that turn a known fight into a new one.

### Cited Findings

#### CATACLYSM (2010-2012) — 10 and 25 players, Normal and Heroic (Raid Finder added for Dragon Soul)

##### Baradin Hold (10/25; a PvP-zone raid of one boss per patch) — sources: boss pages linked below
- **Argaloth** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Argaloth)) — Meteor Slash is split among everyone in front of him and leaves a debuff on the first tank, so the raid stacks in two halves on two tanks who taunt back and forth [stack/soak][tank swap]. Consuming Darkness must be dispelled at once [dispel]. At 66% and 33%, Fel Firestorm: the raid spreads and keeps moving while fire drops under players, then restacks [spread][hazard][phase].
- **Occu'thar** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Occu%27thar)) — Searing Shadows: a cone that doubles Shadow damage taken for 30 s, so tanks swap on every cast [tank swap]. Focused Fire puddles a random player's location (12 yd) [hazard]. Eyes of Occu'thar: one eye on every player; any eye left alive 10 s explodes for raid-wide damage, so the raid gathers at one spot and AoEs them [adds][stack/soak].
- **Alizabal, Mistress of Hate** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alizabal)) — Skewer stuns the tank for 8 s and makes them take +150% damage: the other tank must taunt at once or the raid wipes [tank swap]. Seething Hate: fixed damage (200k/600k in 10/25) split among everyone within 6 yd of the target, so the whole raid stays stacked [stack/soak]. Blade Dance: everyone runs out of 13 yd [dance]. Hard enrage at 5 minutes [enrage].

##### Blackwing Descent (10/25; 6 encounters; Nefarian opens after the other five) — sources: boss pages linked below; [WoW Insider guide index](https://www.engadget.com/2011-01-04-wow-insiders-guide-to-blackwing-descent.html)
- **Omnotron Defense System** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Omnotron_Defense_System)) — Council of four golems (Arcanotron, Electron, Magmatron, Toxitron) [council]. Arcanotron drops a Power Generator under an ally (+50% damage and mana for anyone within 5 yd, friend or foe), so both the raid and the golems want to stand in it; Arcane Annihilator must be interrupted [interrupt][hazard]. Heroic: Nefarian overcharges a generator so it grows and explodes. Two golems are active at a time and rotate, the inactive ones gaining a shield whose effect punishes attacking them; Electron's Lightning Conductor needs spreading, Magmatron's Incineration hits the raid after a targeted flame, Toxitron's chemical bomb slimes and poison adds need kiting (rotation and the other three golems' details (unverified): the fetched text was cut after Arcanotron).
- **Magmaw** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Magmaw)) — Pillar of Flame sunders the ground under a player, then explodes and spawns Lava Parasites that infect whoever they reach; the ranged group shifts between two set spots and kites/AoEs the worms [kite][adds][dance]. Mangle: the tank is picked up in his mouth (heavy damage, armor -50% afterwards, threat wiped), the off-tank taunts at once [tank swap]. While the tank is held, assigned melee "ride" his exposed pincers and chain his head to a spike, which impales him and he takes double damage for a burn window [gimmick][job][phase]. If no one is in melee he stacks Molten Tantrum (+100% fire damage per stack) [enrage]. Heroic adds meteors and Blazing Bone Constructs.
- **Atramedes** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Atramedes)) — Landmark resource fight. He is blind and hunts by Sound: every player has a 0-100 Sound bar that rises when they are hit by his abilities (Sonar Pulse discs, Modulation, Sonic Breath, flame patches), and at 100 he channels Devastation on them, quickly killing them [resource]. Ten Ancient Dwarven Shields (gongs) around the room: striking one resets everyone's Sound, interrupts his cast and stuns him, but he then destroys that shield, so the gongs are a limited budget that works as a soft enrage [job][enrage]. Ground phase: Searing Flame is interrupted by a designated gong-striker; air phase: Roaring Flame Breath follows a player faster the higher their Sound, and a kiter strikes a gong to make himself the new target [kite][job][phase]. Heroic: Obnoxious Fiends ride players' backs and add Sound; berserk at 10 min.
- **Chimaeron** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Chimaeron)) — Pip's Bile-O-Tron 800 sprays the raid with a buff that stops any hit from taking a player above 10,000 health below 1, so healers keep everyone just above 10k rather than topped [resource]. Massacre periodically drops everyone to 1 HP and can knock the Bile-O-Tron offline; then the raid must stack and be healed to full, since one Caustic Slime kills [stack/soak][spread]. Double Attack after Break stacks: tanks taunt before it [tank swap]. At 20%, Mortality cuts all healing by 99%: a pure burn [dps race][phase]. Heroic: Nefarian adds Mocking Shadows (constant raid damage) at the same time.
- **Maloriak** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Maloriak)) — The vial he throws into his cauldron sets his kit for 40 s [phase]: Red (Consuming Flames, a frontal Scorching Blast split among everyone in the cone, so the raid soaks it together) [stack/soak]; Blue (Biting Chill, Flash Freeze ice blocks to break) [spread]; Green (Debilitating Slime: everyone, players and adds, takes +100% damage, so this is when the Aberrations he releases are AoE'd) [adds]. Release Aberrations frees three adds from growth chambers each time, each add buffing its neighbours (Growth Catalyst, +20% damage, -20% damage taken within 10 yd) until the Green vial suppresses it; his casts are interrupted to limit them (which casts, and the chamber count (unverified): the cast list was cut) [interrupt]. At 25%: Release All plus two Prime Subjects that must be tanked, Magma Jets, Absolute Zero orbs, Acid Nova [phase][adds]. Heroic: Dark Magic vial (taunt-immune, Engulfing Darkness cone, Vile Swill adds).
- **Nefarian's End** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Nefarian%27s_End)) — P1: Nefarian in the air, undead Onyxia on the ground; they attack 100% faster within 50 yd of each other, so they are tanked far apart [tank swap][council]. Onyxia's Electrical Energy bar fills over time and faster with each Electrocute (raid-wide hit every 10% of Nefarian's health); full = Electrical Overload, a wipe, so Onyxia is a DPS race [resource][dps race]. Animated Bone Warriors are kept powerless by keeping them out of breaths (breath recharges them) [adds]. P2: the floor floods with lava except three pillars; the raid splits onto them and interrupts each Chromatic Prototype's Blast Nova [room change][split][interrupt]. P3: lava recedes, Shadowblaze Spark fire spreads and re-animates skeletons [hazard]. Heroic: Dominion mind-controls players toward portals, Explosive Cinders must be carried away. Berserk at 10:30.

##### The Bastion of Twilight (10/25; 4 bosses Normal + Sinestra Heroic-only) — sources: boss pages linked below
- **Halfus Wyrmbreaker** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Halfus_Wyrmbreaker)) — A weekly-random kit: five captive dragons (Nether Scion, Slate Dragon, Storm Rider, Time Warden, Emerald Whelps) each grant Halfus or his Proto-Behemoth an ability; on Normal three are awake each week, and freeing one weakens Halfus but then the dragon is bound and must be tanked or killed; dragon deaths give Dragon's Vengeance (+100% damage taken, stacking) [choice][adds]. Malevolent Strikes stack a healing-reduction on the tank: swap at about 10 stacks [tank swap]. Storm Rider's Shadow Nova becomes interruptible once its dragon is freed [interrupt]. Heroic: all five abilities active, the raid picks which dragons to free and in what order [choice].
- **Valiona and Theralion** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Valiona_and_Theralion)) — Twin dragons with one health pool that swap between air and ground [council][phase]. Valiona's Blackout absorbs 20,000 healing and when it ends deals 250,000 split among nearby players, so the raid stacks on it before dispelling [stack/soak][dispel]; Devouring Flames is a breath whose damage falls with distance from its origin, so the raid runs to the far side [dance]. Theralion's Engulfing Magic mirrors a player's damage and healing onto allies within 10 yd: run out [spread]; Twilight Meteorite is split among nearby players [stack/soak]. Deep Breath and Dazzling Destruction pull players hit into the Twilight Realm, a second plane with exploding orbs and exit portals [split][hazard]. Heroic: Twilight Sentries fire from the Twilight Realm into the real one. Hard enrage at 10 min.
- **Ascendant Council** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ascendant_Council)) — Four elemental councillors in three stages [council][phase]. Stage 1: Feludius (water) and Ignacious (fire), damage split so both reach 25% together; Feludius' Water Bomb soaks players (Waterlogged) and his Glaciate (damage falling off with distance) then freezes every Waterlogged player, while Hydro Lance is interrupted [interrupt][spread]. (Ignacious' kit, the fire that thaws frozen players, (unverified): cut from the fetched text.) Stage 2: Arion and Terrastra; the answer is element-cancelling: touching Arion's cyclone gives Swirling Winds (levitating, safe from Terrastra's Quake), standing in Terrastra's Gravity Well grounds a player (safe from Arion's Thundershock), so before each alternating raid-wide hit every player moves to the right one [dance][resource]. Lightning Rod marks 1 player (3 in 25-man) to step away [spread]. Stage 3: the four fuse into the Elementium Monstrosity with their combined remaining health; Electric Instability grows over time, so leftover health from stages 1-2 is the soft enrage [enrage][dps race]; Gravity Crush lifts 1 player (3 in 25) [job].
- **Cho'gall** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Cho%27gall_(tactics))) — Landmark resource fight: every player has a Corruption bar (0-100) fed by nearly every mechanic (adds' Depravity and Corrupting Crash, Sprayed Corruption, standing in Spilled Blood, Debilitating Beam...) [resource]. Thresholds punish: 25 Accelerated (gains 2/s, dispel it), 50 Sickness (vomits a cone onto allies), 75 a Malformation on the back that shadow-bolts allies, 100 Absolute: becomes a Faceless One that cannot be healed. Conversion makes players worship him (interrupt or CC them) [interrupt][mind control]. Fury of Cho'gall forces tank swaps [tank swap]. Corrupting Adherents spawn from portals; Fester Blood animates their spilled blood into oozes [adds]. Stage 2 (25%): raid-wide Corruption of the Old God scales with each player's bar, so the whole fight is a budget [phase][enrage]. Hard enrage 10 min. Heroic: two adherents in 25-man, Flame/Shadow adds, Spiked Tentacles.
- **Sinestra (Heroic only)** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sinestra_(tactics))) — Starts at 60% health. Stage 1: Wrack, a stacking DoT that bounces to two nearby players when dispelled, keeping its remaining duration, so dispels are scheduled across the raid [dispel][job]; Twilight Slicer: two orbs fixate on players with a cutting beam between them [dance]; whelps whose death puddles resurrect each other [adds]. Stage 2 (30%): a mana duel between the red dragon Calen and Sinestra; healers must heal Calen (Pyrrhic Focus) while the raid splits to break two Pulsing Twilight Eggs whose shields drop only when Sinestra is attacked, and stuns Twilight Spitecallers [split][gimmick]. Stage 3: Essence of the Red (+100% haste, 5% mana per second) for a 3-minute burn [dps race].

##### Throne of the Four Winds (10/25; 2 encounters on floating platforms) — sources: boss pages linked below
- **Conclave of Wind** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Conclave_of_Wind)) — Three djinn (Anshal, Nezir, Rohash) on three platforms joined by wind bridges [council][split]. Any djinn left with no player on its platform casts a wipe (Withering Winds -100% healing, Chilling Winds, Deafening Winds silence), so each platform keeps a team [split][job]. All three share an energy clock: at 90 energy they cast their ultimates at once (Zephyr, Sleet Storm, Hurricane) and the bridges vanish [phase]. A djinn that dies resurrects after 1 minute (Gather Strength), so all three must die within a minute [dps race]. Enrage at 8 min.
- **Al'Akir** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Al%27Akir_(tactics))) — Fought on a disc in the sky [hazard]. Stage 1: Squall Line, a wall of tornadoes sweeping round the platform with one gap the raid must move through [dance]; Wind Burst knocks everyone back (off the platform unless in melee) [hazard]; Lightning Strike chains between players in a cone, so the raid stands in small piles of 2 or 3 [spread][stack/soak]. Stage 2 (80%): stacking Acid Rain, Stormlings to kill (their death gives Al'Akir Feedback, +damage taken) [adds]. Stage 3: he shatters the platform; the whole raid flies (Eye of the Storm buff) in three dimensions, dodging Lightning Clouds that cover a whole altitude [room change][dance]. Berserk at 9 min.

##### Firelands (10/25; 7 bosses, the gate to Ragnaros opens after the first five; Majordomo after Baleroc) — sources: boss pages linked below
- **Beth'tilac** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Beth%27tilac)) — Two-level fight [split][room change]: a tank, a healer and some damage dealers climb threads (made by taunting Cinderweb Spinners down) to the web above to fight her, avoiding meteor holes that drop them to the floor; the ground team kills spiderlings, spinners and drones, keeping spiderlings from being eaten (Consume heals her 10%) [adds][job]. After three Smoldering Devastations (players on the web must drop through the centre hole before each) she descends; stage 2 is a soft enrage of stacking Frenzy and The Widow's Kiss on tanks [tank swap][enrage].
- **Lord Rhyolith** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lord_Rhyolith)) — Steering fight ("Drive his feet"): in stage 1 he ignores players and takes 80% less damage until he has run over volcanoes, which strip his Obsidian armor; the raid steers him by damaging his left or right foot (the more-damaged side turns him that way (unverified detail)) [gimmick][dance]. Adds spawn often [adds]. At 25% his armor shatters, he becomes tankable, and the raid groups behind him and burns [phase][dps race]. Heroic: Liquid Obsidian adds (focus them) and Unleashed Flame beams that chase players.
- **Alysrazor** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alysrazor)) — Flight gimmick [gimmick][job]: Molting drops Molten Feathers; three feathers give Wings of Flame, and 1 player (3 in 25-man) flies after her through fire rings (stacking haste, up to Alysra's Razor) while the ground handles Blazing Talon Initiates (interrupt Fieroblast) [interrupt], Voracious Hatchlings that imprint on the nearest player and throw a Tantrum unless the tank drags them onto Plump Lava Worms [adds][tank swap]. Stage 2: Fiery Tornadoes in concentric lanes moving in alternate directions, with lethal winds beyond 60 yd and a vortex in the middle: a "frogger" lane dance [dance][hazard]. Stage 3: she crashes (Burnout, +50% damage taken); Clawshapers re-ignite her and must be interrupted [phase][interrupt]. 15-min enrage.
- **Shannox** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Shannox)) — Boss with two dogs [adds]: Riplimb (tanked, untauntable, fetches the spear) and Rageface (untankable, leaps on random players; Face Rage is broken by a big hit). Shannox throws Immolation and Crystal Prison traps: the raid walks the dogs into traps (a trapped dog is stunned/encased) and never steps on one [hazard][kite]. Both pets go berserk (+400% damage) when Shannox reaches 30%, so the dogs are killed first or the order planned [dps race]. Jagged Tear stacks on tanks [tank swap]. Heroic: Riplimb revives 30 s after death while Shannox lives.
- **Baleroc, the Gatekeeper** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Baleroc)) — Shards of Torment (1 in 10-man, 2 in 25-man) channel a stacking beam on the nearest player; if no one stands near, Wave of Torment hits the raid, so damage dealers take turns soaking a shard and stepping out at set stacks; afterwards they are Tormented (shadow vulnerability, -50% healing) [stack/soak][job][resource]. Healer economy: direct heals on Torment victims build Vital Spark, which becomes Vital Flame (bonus healing) when spent on the tank with Blaze of Glory, so healers swap duties on a schedule [resource][job]. Blaze of Glory stacks on his tank (+20% physical damage taken, +20% max health) and each one grows his fire damage; his alternating Inferno Blade and Decimation Blade make the tank's damage spike, and a Tormented tank dies to them, so tanks never soak shards (blade numbers (unverified): cut from the fetched text) [tank swap]. Countdown: two players are linked and must touch [job]. 6-min enrage.
- **Majordomo Staghelm** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Majordomo_Staghelm)) — The raid's formation picks his form: 7 or more players clustered = Scorpion (Flame Scythe, a frontal cleave split among those hit); spread out = Cat (Leaping Flames to a ranged player leaving a 20-yd fire pool and a Spirit of Flame add) [stack/soak][spread][choice]. His abilities strengthen with each transform and his energy regen climbs the longer he stays in one form, so the raid chooses when to switch (stack vs spread rhythm) [enrage][dance]. Heroic: Concentration bar (+25% output per 25, lost when hit). 10-min enrage.
- **Ragnaros** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ragnaros_(Firelands_tactics))) — Fought on a platform above lava; he stays in place. Sulfuras Smash on a random player's direction leaves Lava Waves rolling outward that must be dodged [dance]. Wrath of Ragnaros blasts a random player's location (6 yd) and knocks players back; Hand of Ragnaros knocks back everyone within 55 yd, so melee stand where the knockback is safe; Magma Trap lands at a player's location and, when stepped on, erupts for raid-wide damage and launches the triggering player into the air, so traps are placed at the edge and one player triggers each on purpose [hazard][job]. Intermissions at 70% and 40%: Splitting Blow buries Sulfuras and 8 Sons of Flame march to it; each is slowed below 50% health; one that arrives deals Supernova to the whole raid, so every player has an assigned Son by position [intermission][adds][job]. Stage 2: Engulfing Flame torches a third of the platform; Molten Seeds land under 10 players (20 in 25) and become fixating Molten Elementals [spread][adds][hazard]. Stage 3: Living Meteors fixate on players; hitting a meteor knocks it back [kite][adds]. Heroic stage 4 at 10%: he walks free, Superheated stacks, Dreadflame spreads across the floor; Cenarius, Hamuul and Malfurion help (frost zones freeze meteors, roots stun Ragnaros, Cloudburst lets players extinguish fire) [phase][hazard][gimmick]. Normal ends at 10%.

##### Dragon Soul (10/25 Normal/Heroic, plus the first Raid Finder; 8 encounters) — sources: boss pages linked below
- **Morchok** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Morchok)) — Stomp is split among nearby players but the two closest take double, so tanks stand closest and the raid stacks near the boss [stack/soak]. Resonating Crystal: three players (more in 25) run to it and stay linked to soak its blast [stack/soak][job]. Crush Armor stacks: tank swap [tank swap]. Black Blood of the Earth: everyone hides behind earth spikes (line of sight) [LoS]. Heroic: he splits into Morchok and Kohcrom, so the raid splits in two teams [split].
- **Warlord Zon'ozz** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Warlord_Zon%27ozz)) — Void of the Unmaking: an orb that rolls forward until it hits a player, dealing damage split among those nearby, then ricochets the other way and hits harder each bounce; the raid bounces it between a melee group and a ranged group, then steers it into the boss [stack/soak][job][dance]. That strips his stacking Focused Anger and opens a black phase with tentacles (Heroic: killable tentacles that reduce a stacking Black Blood debuff) [phase][adds]. Disrupting Shadows: dispel knocks back (Heroic: everyone within 10 yd) [dispel]. Enrage 6 min.
- **Yor'sahj the Unsleeping** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Yor%27sahj_the_Unsleeping)) — Three coloured globules (four on Heroic) crawl to him; each one that arrives gives him a colour power (purple Deep Corruption, green Digestive Acid, blue Mana Void, black adds, red, yellow) [adds][choice]. The raid kills one; the others become invulnerable, so for each colour combination the raid chooses which one to deny by a fixed priority [puzzle][choice]. Void Bolt stacks: tank swap [tank swap].
- **Hagara the Stormbinder** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Hagara_the_Stormbinder)) — Ice Tombs to break, Ice Lance spread [adds][spread]; Focused Assault avoided by stepping away. Lightning phase: the raid chains lightning from an overloaded Crystal Conductor to the next three by standing in a strung-out line between them [gimmick][job]. Ice phase: the centre floods with water, four rotating ice walls sweep the platform, the raid splits into four to break four Binding Crystals [room change][split][dance]. Each special phase ends with her taking +100% damage for 15 s [phase].
- **Ultraxion** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ultraxion)) — A 6-minute hard-enrage race in the Twilight Realm [dps race][enrage]. Hour of Twilight about every minute: everyone presses the Heroic Will button (leave the realm for 5 s, unable to act), except 1 player (3 in 25-man) who must stay and absorb it or the raid wipes [stack/soak][job]. Fading Light on the tank and random players: press Heroic Will at the last seconds; the tanks swap on it [tank swap][resource]. Unstable Monstrosity raid damage speeds up every minute [enrage]. Aspects give single-healer buffs to assign [job]. Heroic: Looming Darkness means a soaker cannot soak twice in a row.
- **Warmaster Blackhorn** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Warmaster_Blackhorn)) — Gunship fight on the Skyfire [gimmick]. Stage 1: three waves of elite drakonids to tank and swap [adds][tank swap]; drakes' Twilight Barrage hits the ship unless players stand under it, so each quarter of the deck has assigned soakers [stack/soak][job]; Twilight Onslaught (800k split among players and ship) [stack/soak]; Sappers run for the bridge and blow 20% off the ship [adds]; harpooned drakes are killed by ranged. Stage 2: Blackhorn on deck with Vengeance (+1% damage per 1% missing health) and Shockwave [enrage][dance]. Heroic: Goriona lands and must be killed first (Siphon Vitality), Deck Fire.
- **Spine of Deathwing** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Spine_of_Deathwing)) — Landmark: the arena is Deathwing's back in flight [room change][gimmick]. Barrel Roll: if players are all on one side, he rolls and throws off everyone not rooted; the raid triggers rolls on purpose to shed adds, standing in Grasping Tendrils to hold on [dance]. Loop: kill a Corruption tentacle, let Hideous Amalgamations absorb nine Corrupted Blood residues (kill the bloods where the amalgamation stands), then kill the amalgamation beside an armour plate so its Nuclear Blast pries the plate up, then burn the Burning Tendon before the plate reseals; three plates [adds][puzzle][dps race]. Searing Plasma healing absorbs [resource]. Heroic: two tendons per plate and stacking Degradation, widely judged harder than Heroic Madness.
- **Madness of Deathwing** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Madness_of_Deathwing)) — Four platforms around his body, each held by an Aspect who gives a passive buff and a special power (Alexstrasza kills Blistering Tentacles, Nozdormu slows the Elementium Bolt, Ysera halves damage briefly, Kalecgos makes damage splash); the raid destroys one limb per platform and loses that Aspect's power until stage 2, so the platform order is a strategic choice [choice][split][room change]. Each platform: Assault Aspect, Mutated Corruption (Impale tank swaps), Elementium Bolt (slow and kill, or take Elementium Blast), Regenerative Bloods, Blistering Tentacles; if the limb is not dead when Cataclysm finishes, the raid dies [dps race][adds][tank swap]. Stage 2 at 20%: fragments (Shrapnel) and Terrors, Corrupted Blood ramping [phase][adds]. Hard enrage 15 min. Heroic: Corrupting Parasite that explodes and becomes an add, Congealing Blood that heals him.

#### MISTS OF PANDARIA (2012-2014) — 10 and 25 players, Normal and Heroic, Raid Finder for all; Flexible added with Siege of Orgrimmar

##### Mogu'shan Vaults (6 encounters) — sources: boss pages linked below
- **The Stone Guard** ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Stone_Guard)) — Council of four stone dogs (Amethyst, Cobalt, Jade, Jasper; three of them, rotating weekly, in 10-man and Raid Finder) [council]. ### Inferences
- Across the four expansions, the dominant new tool is the per-player resource bar that every mechanic feeds (Atramedes' Sound, Cho'gall's Corruption, Sha of Pride's Pride, Norushen's Corruption, Xavius' Corruption, Demonic Inquisition's Torment, Kilrogg's Fel Corruption). It turns many small hits into one visible budget per player, and a threshold ladder (25/50/75/100) lets one mechanic escalate without new rules. This suits a 2-40 player game well, because the bar is personal and does not depend on raid size.
- The second big tool is the role-specific job. Healers healing enemies (Immerseus' Contaminated Puddles, Tsulong by day, Brackenspore's mushrooms), tanks taking control of a boss (Socrethar), one player carrying an item (Iskar's Eye of Anzu), and role trials (Norushen's three Tests). These give each role a mechanic of its own instead of "everyone dodge".
- Phase changes increasingly change the room rather than the boss's numbers. See section 3.
- Mythic from Warlords on often adds a new phase or a new rule set rather than higher numbers (Mar'gok's two runestones, Velhari's lingering auras, Dragons of Nightmare all four at once, Desolate Host's realm-switch limit, secret Mythic phases on Argus).

## 2. Which mechanic type does each belong to?

### Takeaway
Every boss line in section 1 carries its type tags. The clearest examples per type are tabled at the end of section 5. Across these eras, soak/split damage (damage divided among everyone in an area or line) becomes the single most common mechanic. Tank swaps on a stacking debuff are close to universal, appearing on nearly every boss after Firelands. Pure interrupts and dispels become secondary jobs.

### Cited Findings
- Split-damage soaks appear in every raid tier. Examples include Alizabal's Seething Hate "split amongst all of the nearby players" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alizabal)), Ta'yak's Unseen Strike "3,000,000 Physical damage split amongst all players" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Blade_Lord_Ta%27yak)), Ka'graz's Molten Torrent "650000 Fire damage split evenly among all players within 8 yards" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flamebender_Ka%27graz)), Harjatan's Unchecked Rage "16000000 Physical damage, split between all enemies in a cone" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Harjatan)), Sassz'ine's Hydra Shot "split among those struck" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Mistress_Sassz%27ine)) and Guarm's breaths "3000000 ... split evenly among enemies within a cone" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Guarm)).
- The Adventure Guide in Warlords and Legion gives every boss a per-role brief ("Damage Dealers / Healers / Tanks" bullet lists). So each boss is explicitly designed with a separate job for each role. See for example [Tectus](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Tectus), [Gorefiend](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Gorefiend) and [Gul'dan](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Gul%27dan_(tactics)).
- Blizzard named its own "Patchwerk" (a damage-race boss with few mechanics, after the Naxxramas boss) in several raids: Malkorok, "our 'Patchwerk-style' boss of the zone" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Malkorok_(tactics))); Fel Lord Zakuun, "the 'Patchwerk' of the zone" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Fel_Lord_Zakuun)); and the Butcher, "frequently compared with Patchwerk" (community strategy text, [wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Butcher)). Each raid deliberately has one plain damage-check fight.
- Blizzard also named the older fights it was echoing. Horridon recalls "Lady Deathwhisper or M'uru" (add waves) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Horridon)). Immerseus recalls Marrowgar and Viscidus ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Immerseus)). Gorefiend recalls "C'Thun and Yogg-Saron, where players are devoured" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Gorefiend)). Socrethar recalls Razorgore: "What if you let the player control a raid boss" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Socrethar_the_Eternal)). The High Council brings back Gurtogg Bloodboil ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Hellfire_High_Council)). Ra-den is "the 'Algalon' of the zone" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ra-den_(tactics))).

### Inferences
- Blizzard works from a recurring set of archetypes and re-skins them (Patchwerk, add-wave, devoured-into-the-stomach, control-the-boss, bonus secret boss). A designer can plan a raid as a mix of archetypes: one damage race, one add-wave fight, one realm split, one room-change fight and one council.

### Gaps
- No source gives counts per mechanic type across the era. The tags are the researcher's classification of the fetched descriptions.

## 3. Which encounters were landmark designs, and which changed the room during the fight?

### Takeaway
The landmark designs of these eras share one move: the fight's main rule is structural, not numeric. The arena moves or breaks (Spine, Blackhand, Fallen Avatar, Krosus), the raid is sent somewhere else (Garrosh's realms, Archimonde's Nether, Star Augur's worlds, Kil'jaeden's darkness), time is manipulated (Elisande, Chronomatic Anomaly), or the boss is secret (Ra-den, Mythic Argus). Several are stated design goals in Blizzard's own encounter notes.

### Cited Findings
- **Spine of Deathwing** — the raid fights on the dragon's back while he rolls to throw players off; the goal is to pry off three armour plates, not to kill a boss ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Spine_of_Deathwing)). Heroic is described as "one of the hardest fights on Dragon Soul (it is even considered by many to be more difficult than heroic Madness)" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Spine_of_Deathwing)).
- **Madness of Deathwing** — four Aspect platforms; each limb destroyed costs the raid that Aspect's power until stage 2, so the platform order is a strategic choice ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Madness_of_Deathwing)).
- **Lei Shen** — Blizzard's design note: "the Thunder King isn't just a battery that needs to recharge; he's actually a power source himself... the citadel draws power from him". The four conduits he charges by standing near them decide his abilities ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lei_Shen_(tactics))).
- **Ra-den** — "This is the 'Algalon' of the zone; a bonus boss for the high-end raiders... The mechanics of the fight are deliberately obscured in the Dungeon Journal... hopefully you'll enjoy a sense of discovery" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ra-den_(tactics))).
- **Durumu** — the Disintegration Beam maze ("Just follow the maze... and don't panic!") and the colour cones that reveal hidden adds ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Durumu_the_Forgotten)).
- **Dark Animus** — "a fixed quantity of... Anima... a certain puzzle-solving element... players will need to carefully manage how they shift around this finite quantity of Anima" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Dark_Animus)).
- **Ji-Kun** — "designed to highlight the strong vertical elements of the terrain, without adding actual aerial combat" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ji-Kun)).
- **Norushen** — each player clicks a light and enters a test built for their own role (damage, healing or tanking) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Norushen_(tactics))).
- **Garrosh** — "players must battle through some of the most memorable locations where the sha manifested in Pandaria", as 60-second trips into the Realm of Y'Shaarj ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Garrosh_Hellscream_(tactics))).
- **Paragons of the Klaxxi** — nine bosses, three at a time; each dead paragon leaves a power for one role to use ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Paragons_of_the_Klaxxi)).
- **Spoils of Pandaria** — "It's been a long time since we've made a Raid encounter that didn't have a single conventional 'boss'" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Spoils_of_Pandaria)). **Hellfire Assault** follows it: "Like Spoils of Pandaria... a raid 'boss' without a single named boss" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Hellfire_Assault)). **Eonar** is a lane-defence fight ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Eonar_the_Life-Binder)).
- **Siegecrafter Blackfuse** — "the U-shaped conveyor belt that encircles the chamber, allowing players to jump up into the workings of the assembly line" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Siegecrafter_Blackfuse)).
- **Blackhand** — "Upon reaching 70% and 30% health, Blackhand causes the floor beneath the players to give out, falling to a lower level of the tower and changing the environmental hazards" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Blackhand_(tactics))).
- **Operator Thogar** — "Trains periodically pass through the encounter area, dropping off troops or providing support" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Operator_Thogar)). That trains also kill anyone standing on the tracks is (unverified) on the fetched text.
- **Iron Maidens** — a team rides loading chains to the Dreadnaught to sabotage its main cannon before it finishes Warming Up, while the rest dodge its bombardment ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Iron_Maidens)).
- **Archimonde** — at 40% Nether Banish sends the tank and anyone within 8 yd to the Twisting Nether; killing Shadowed Netherwalkers there opens the way back ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Archimonde_(Hellfire_Citadel_tactics))). The Mythic-only final phase begins at 40% ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2015/07/16/method-gets-world-first-archimonde-kill/), via search summary).
- **Star Augur Etraeus** — "At 90%, 60%, and 30%... he will activate his observation deck to project the raid through the nether overlooking different worlds"; Grand Conjunction star-sign pairing ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Star_Augur_Etraeus)).
- **Elisande** — "When her health is low... she rewinds time to send players back to the point at which they first engaged her... new abilities mixed in with echoes of her past actions. She can perform this feat twice" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Grand_Magistrix_Elisande_(tactics))).
- **Gul'dan** — the Eye of Aman'Thul gives each role its own power button, which he disrupts at 40% ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Gul%27dan_(tactics))).
- **Mistress Sassz'ine** — Hydra Shot soak lines; Sarukel's Devouring Maw, stopped by carrying Befouling Ink to him ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Mistress_Sassz%27ine)).
- **Fallen Avatar** — "the Fallen Avatar shatters the floor, dropping players into the chamber below... Rupture Realities now shatters the ground below him until the platform is completely destroyed" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Fallen_Avatar)).
- **Kil'jaeden** — at 40% the darkness of Deceiver's Veil limits sight to 8 yd until players find Illidan; then Darkness of a Thousand Souls at Argus, survived inside rifts ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Kil%27jaeden_(Tomb_of_Sargeras_tactics))).
- **Argus** — stage 4 kills the whole raid; the players continue as ghosts, collect motes and are revived ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Argus_the_Unmaker)). Mythic had "secret phases that aren't detailed in the in-game Dungeon Journal or spoiled on the test server" ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/world-of-warcraft-legions-final-boss-is-so-tough-even-its-best-guilds-cant-beat-him), via search summary).

**Room-changing fights (arena altered during the fight):**
- Floors that break or collapse: Blackhand (two floor collapses, three arenas) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Blackhand_(tactics))); Fallen Avatar (floor shatters, then the platform crumbles under him) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Fallen_Avatar)); Krosus (bridge smashed section by section, a shrinking arena as the enrage) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Krosus)); Al'Akir (stage 3 shatters the platform, and the raid flies) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Al%27Akir_(tactics))); Lei Shen stage 3 (the chamber walls open and Violent Gale Winds push players) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Lei_Shen_(tactics))).
- Floors that flood or fill: Nefarian (lava except three pillars) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Nefarian%27s_End)); Hagara's ice phase (water in the centre, rotating ice walls) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Hagara_the_Stormbinder)); Jin'rokh (statues broken by the tank flood water pools) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Jin%27rokh_the_Breaker_(tactics))); Brackenspore (spreading moss) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Brackenspore)); Butcher Mythic (permanent Pale Vitriol pools) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/The_Butcher)); High Council (Reap leaves permanent burning ground) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Hellfire_High_Council)).
- Moving machinery and vehicles: Thogar's trains; Hans'gar and Franzok's conveyor belts and stamping presses ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Hans%27gar_and_Franzok)); Blackfuse's conveyor belt ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Siegecrafter_Blackfuse)); Blackhorn's gunship deck ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Warmaster_Blackhorn)); Spine of Deathwing (the arena is a rolling dragon).
- Changing platforms: Zor'lok (a new platform every 20%) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Imperial_Vizier_Zor%27lok)); Elerethe (moves platform as a roc) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Elerethe_Renferal_(tactics))); Imonar (retreats across trapped walkways) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Imonar_the_Soulhunter)); Ta'yak (the raid is blown down the hall) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Blade_Lord_Ta%27yak)); Beth'tilac (web level and ground level) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Beth%27tilac)); Ji-Kun (nests above and below) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ji-Kun)); Madness of Deathwing and Conclave of Wind (several platforms) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Madness_of_Deathwing), [wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Conclave_of_Wind)); Hasabel (portal platforms) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Portal_Keeper_Hasabel)).
- The raid transported to another realm or arena: Garrosh (Realm of Y'Shaarj); Sha of Fear Heroic (Dread Expanse) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sha_of_Fear)); Archimonde (Twisting Nether); Star Augur (projected over other worlds); Tichondrius (Illusionary Night) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Tichondrius_(tactics))); Kil'jaeden (darkness, then the view to Argus); Il'gynoth (into the hollow of the tree, then sealed out) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Il%27gynoth)); Elisande (time rewound to the pull).
- A floor that cycles with time: Sisters of the Moon (a projected moon whose light and dark halves move through the moon's phases) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sisters_of_the_Moon)); Tsulong (night and day) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Tsulong)); Elegon (the vortex floor switches off in stage 3) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Elegon)).

### Inferences
- A room change does three jobs at once. It marks a phase visibly, resets positioning habits, and acts as a soft enrage (Krosus, Fallen Avatar). For a game with small parties, a shrinking or breaking arena is cheap to understand and scales with no per-player tuning.
- "Go somewhere else" phases (realms, nether, dream, stomach) split the raid without needing more bosses. The same tool works at 2 players (one goes in, one holds) and at 40 (a team goes in).

### Gaps
- Mythic-only phases are confirmed by name and ability list, but not by strategy text: Archimonde's Nether Ascension phase, Gul'dan's "The Demon Within", Argus' Sargeras abilities, and Garrosh's "Stage Four: Mythic Only". How each one plays (positions, assignments) is not in the fetched text. Kil'jaeden's Mythic differences were not separated on his page.

## 4. How did raid size work, which mechanics scaled with it, and why did Blizzard move to flexible size and a 20-player Mythic?

### Takeaway
Cataclysm put 10- and 25-player raids on one shared lockout with the same loot, and added Raid Finder (fixed 25) with Dragon Soul in patch 4.3. Mists kept that model and added Flexible raiding (Siege of Orgrimmar, 10-25). Warlords made Normal and Heroic flexible (10-30) and fixed Mythic at 20. Blizzard said it chose 20 "largely for the function of raid design": a fixed size lets designers count on having certain classes and lets mechanics be tuned to an exact head count. In flexible modes, mechanic counts scale smoothly: "each person in the raid adds a 20% chance for the boss ability to target an extra person or have an extra effect".

### Cited Findings
- **Cataclysm:** "10- and 25-player modes of a single raid will share the same lockout. You can defeat each raid boss once per week per character", and the loot tables were combined ([Engadget, Apr 2010, via search summary](https://www.engadget.com/2010/04/26/cataclysm-raid-progression-refinements/)). Hazzikostas looked back on this choice in 2014. "we decided to consolidate 10-player and 25-player modes into a single difficulty, single reward tier, and single raid lockout". "Unifying the difficulty of the two modes mainly involved increasing the difficulty of 10-player mode". "no longer could we err on the side of just letting 10-player be easier". "spreading out requires more coordination with more players in the same space". "equalizing rewards encouraged a persistent downward pressure on raid size"; "Growing from 10 to 25 was nearly impossible logistically". It also left many players "who had enjoyed success in 10-player Normal" "unable to get any sort of foothold in Cataclysm raiding" ([Wowhead, Hazzikostas on Raid Design Part Two](https://www.wowhead.com/news/warlords-pvp-gearing-blog-patch-5-4-7-hotfixes-for-april-28-raid-philosophy-part-236385)).
- **Raid Finder:** first released in patch 4.3.0 for Dragon Soul. It is "always in 25-player mode", with simplified fights: "removal or modification of specific mechanics that normally make the raid dependent on the performance of a single player". A stacking Determination buff (+5% per wipe, up to 10 stacks) is applied after wipes ([wiki, Raid Finder](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Raid_Finder)). In Warlords, Raid Finder "scales down" when players leave before a boss ([Engadget, Nov 2013](https://www.engadget.com/2013-11-08-major-raid-changes-coming-in-warlords-of-draenor.html)). Hazzikostas: Raid Finder "generally lacked sufficient challenge for even the most casual of organized raid groups"; friends-and-family groups "often got stuck at Elegon... or Garalon... without any clear way forward" ([Wowhead, Part Two](https://www.wowhead.com/news/warlords-pvp-gearing-blog-patch-5-4-7-hotfixes-for-april-28-raid-philosophy-part-236385)).
- **Flexible (patch 5.4, Siege of Orgrimmar only):** "a separate difficulty that sat between Raid Finder and Normal... available for premade groups of 10 to 25 players"; minimum 8 players to queue; a loot-based lockout (bosses can be killed again without loot) ([wiki, Flexible Raid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid)). Hazzikostas: "tuned to be roughly 20% to 25% easier (numerically speaking) than Normal mode... while preserving all of the fight mechanics"; "a new scaling system to allow the content to adapt to any raid size between 10 and 25 players"; "why force someone to have exactly 9 or 24 friends?"; the aim was to "remove as many obstacles as possible that might get in the way of friends who just want to be able to raid together" ([Wowhead, Part Two](https://www.wowhead.com/news/warlords-pvp-gearing-blog-patch-5-4-7-hotfixes-for-april-28-raid-philosophy-part-236385)).
- **Warlords (patch 6.0.2):** Flexible renamed Normal, old Normal renamed Heroic, both scaling 10-30; Heroic renamed Mythic, "statically tuned around 20 players" ([wiki, Flexible Raid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid)). Raiding Azeroth Part 3 (Apr 30, 2014): "we're consolidating 10-player and 25-player Heroic into a new fixed-size 20-player Mythic difficulty"; "Consolidating to a single raid size for Mythic will allow us to focus on delivering the best possible experience for our competitive hardcore raiders"; "each raid difficulty will have its own weekly lockout"; "Heroic is a flexible-size loot-based lockout; Mythic is a fixed-size boss-based lockout"; "precise 10 vs. 25 Heroic tuning has continued to be a major challenge" ([Engadget reprint of Part 3](https://www.engadget.com/2014-04-30-raid-design-evolution-and-warlords-of-draenor.html)).
- **Why 20, in Blizzard's words (Community Manager Lore):** "We chose to put Mythic at 20 largely for the function of raid design." "It's impossible for every group to have every class, and often that means they're lacking in certain tools"; "We want to be able to use those sorts of mechanics again. We can't do that when we're designing with a 10-player raid size in mind." "At 20, it becomes a lot more acceptable for us to say 'you should probably bring a Mage to Spellsteal this.'" Greg Street: "We still think it's the best design." ([Engadget, Nov 13 2013](https://www.engadget.com/2013-11-13-mythic-raiding-why-20-man.html)). The same article reports, as its own summary, that 15 was judged too small to guarantee one of every class.
- **Later note:** the wiki says Mythic "is always scaled for 20 players, however this may change for future raids, as the Sporefall raid in patch 12.0.7 will be the first raid with Mythic difficulty being flexible" ([wiki, Instance difficulty](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Instance_difficulty)).
- **How mechanics scale in flexible modes:** "There are generally no set breakpoints for boss abilities. With a few exceptions, each person in the raid adds a 20% chance for the boss ability to target an extra person or have an extra effect." ([wiki, Flexible Raid](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Flexible_Raid)).

**What scaled with raid size — concrete examples from the boss pages (10-player / 25-player):**
- Soak or split counts: Ultraxion's Hour of Twilight must be absorbed by 1 player in 10-man, 3 in 25-man ("One person (or three in 25-man) must remain in the twilight realm or the raid will instantly wipe"). Fading Light hits the tank plus 1 (10) or 3 (25) others ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ultraxion)). Baleroc's Shards of Torment: 1 crystal in 10, 2 in 25 ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Baleroc)). Alizabal's Seething Hate: 200k in 10 vs 600k in 25 total to split ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alizabal)).
- Targeted marks: Arion's Lightning Rod, 1 player in 10, 3 in 25; the Elementium Monstrosity's Gravity Crush also takes more targets in 25 ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ascendant_Council)). Ragnaros' Molten Seed lands under 10 players (20 in 25) and Lava Bolt hits 4 players (10 in 25) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ragnaros_(Firelands_tactics))). Ragnaros Heroic's Cloudburst lets "up to three players" use it in 25 ([same page](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ragnaros_(Firelands_tactics))). Thok's fixate picks one of the three farthest non-tanks in 10-man, a random one of six in 25-man ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Thok_the_Bloodthirsty)).
- Add counts: Cho'gall's Darkened Creations, 4 in 10 and 10 in 25; on 25-player Heroic, two Corrupting Adherents spawn at a time and "double the normal amount of blood" ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Cho%27gall_(tactics))). The Stone Guard has only three of four guardians (rotating weekly) in 10-player and Raid Finder ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Stone_Guard)). Ji-Kun Heroic's guarded nests differ by size (nests 2, 4, 8, 10, 12 in 10-man; 2, 6, 12, 17, 22, 29 in 25-man) ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Ji-Kun)).
- Job counts and timers: Alysrazor strategy sends "1 (3 in 25man)" fliers ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Alysrazor)). On the Sha of Fear, the shrine timer is 90 s in 10-man and LFR, but only 45 s if a 25-man raid brings two tanks instead of three ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Sha_of_Fear)). Al'Akir's Lightning Strike piles are 2 players in 10-man, 3 in 25 ([wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Al%27Akir_(tactics))).
- Health and damage values: most Mists and later pages list separate numbers per size and difficulty (e.g. Iron Qon's "LFR/10/H/25/H" values, [wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Iron_Qon); Kargath's LFR, Normal, Heroic and Mythic values, [wiki](https://warcraft.wiki.gg/wiki/Kargath_Bladefist_(tactics))).

### Inferences
- Two scaling models appear. Cataclysm and Mists used hand-set counts per size, for example 1 vs 3 soakers or 10 vs 20 seeds, roughly in proportion to raid size. Flexible modes added extra targets or effects at about one per five players (the 20% per player rule). For a 2-40 player game, the flexible rule ("one more target per N players, with a floor") is the more reusable model. Fixed-size jobs (exactly one gong striker, one Eye of Anzu holder) do not scale and need a floor size.
- Blizzard's stated cost of equalising sizes: the smaller group's version had to be made harder, and mechanics that need spreading behave differently with more bodies. Fixing Mythic at one size was the answer for the hardest tier. A designer serving 2-40 players should expect to hand-tune at least the top difficulty per size band.

### Gaps
- No source gave the exact per-ability scaling formulas Blizzard uses in flexible modes beyond the wiki's 20%-per-player summary. Blizzard's own Part 3 post was read only as Engadget's reprint excerpt.
- The original 5.4 Flexible announcement text was not found. Its rationale here comes from Hazzikostas' 2014 retrospective.

## 5. Which fights are remembered as the best and the worst, and how long did the hardest take?

### Takeaway
The hardest fights of these eras, by world-first attempt counts, are the final bosses. Mythic Kil'jaeden took Method 653-654 pulls, Heroic (Mythic-equivalent) Garrosh 638, Mythic Archimonde 472 and Mythic Argus 320. Heroic Firelands Ragnaros took "over 500", an estimate. The ones critics most called the worst are Dragon Soul's Deathwing pair (long, repetitive, hardest part last) and random, low-control fights like Lord Rhyolith. Throne of Thunder is the raid most often praised. No source gives a scored "best fights" consensus.

### Cited Findings
- **Pull counts (world first):**
  - Mythic Archimonde: Method, 472 attempts, July 2015. A close race with Paragon. "it has been quite a while since a world first clear has taken this long, since Al-Akir in Cataclysm's Tier 11" ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2015/07/16/method-gets-world-first-archimonde-kill/) and [mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/world-of-warcraft-world-first-archimonde-167,45138/), via search summary).
  - Mythic Kil'jaeden: Method, July 2017, after "653 wipes" (kill on the 654th attempt; sources differ by one). Exorsus was second, 7 days later ([Blizzard Watch](https://blizzardwatch.com/2017/07/17/method-claims-world-first-mythic-kiljaeden-kill/), [mein-mmo](https://mein-mmo.de/en/wow-kiljaeden-firstkill-mythic,165861), via search summary).
  - Mythic Argus: Method, 13 December 2017, 320 attempts ([Guinness World Records](https://www.guinnessworldrecords.com/world-records/506549-first-guild-to-defeat-world-of-warcraft-legion-final-boss-argus-the-unmaker), via search summary). It was hard partly because of secret Mythic phases not in the journal or on the test server ([PC Gamer](https://www.pcgamer.com/world-of-warcraft-legions-final-boss-is-so-tough-even-its-best-guilds-cant-beat-him), via search summary).
  - Heroic Garrosh (Siege of Orgrimmar): Method, 638 attempts. "The fight has several phases in different locations, so guilds often gained only seconds of progress". Heroic Ragnaros (Firelands): DREAM Paragon, "over 500" (an estimate; not publicly tracked) ([mein-mmo, 2023](https://mein-mmo.de/en/the-5-hardest-wow-bosses-that-professionals-needed-hundreds-of-attempts-to-defeat,962270)).
  - Kil'jaeden's difficulty is attributed to "knockback mechanics that required instant reactions to avoid falling off the platform. Demon Hunters could fly back, so other classes were at a disadvantage" ([mein-mmo, 2023](https://mein-mmo.de/en/the-5-hardest-wow-bosses-that-professionals-needed-hundreds-of-attempts-to-defeat,962270)).
- **Worst (Cataclysm, one critic):** Chase Hasbrouck's five worst Cataclysm encounters ([Engadget, June 2012](https://www.engadget.com/2012/06/22/the-5-worst-raid-encounters-in-cataclysm/)).
  1. Madness of Deathwing: too long and repetitive, the hardest part comes last, "a very buggy fight", and as a finale it "doesn't get there at all".
  2. Spine of Deathwing: "does not feel epic", "terribly flat", the same cycle repeated, with the soft enrage putting the hardest part at the end.
  3. Lord Rhyolith: randomness players "could not plan for", "We got RNG'd".
  4. Morchok: "just dull" on Normal.
  5. Maloriak: "the overall mechanic was confusing" and Heroic's dark phase favoured mana classes.
- **Annoying fights (community):** an Icy Veins "most hated raid bosses" list focuses on "length, runback annoyance, forced role-playing segments, or other time sinks", and names Siege of Orgrimmar as having two bosses on it. The page itself returned 403, so the bosses named are not confirmed ([Icy Veins](https://www.icy-veins.com/wow/news/the-most-hated-raid-bosses-in-wow/), via search summary). Forum users also flag Durumu and the Sha of Fear as tedious ([Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/your-top-3-and-bottom-3-raid-bosses/2107757/1), via search summary).
- **Best (community, weak sourcing):** Throne of Thunder "continues to echo as one of the best raids in WoW" ([highgroundgaming](https://www.highgroundgaming.com/best-wow-raids/), via search summary). A raider.io tier-list commenter called Blackhand "probably the best end boss ever" ([raider.io](https://raider.io/news/251-the-ultimate-raid-tier-list), via search summary). A forum commenter singled out Durumu ("great tight enrage fight") and Lei Shen. Another listed Gul'dan among their favourites ([Blizzard forums](https://us.forums.blizzard.com/en/wow/t/your-top-3-and-bottom-3-raid-bosses/2107757/1), via search summary).
- **Blizzard's own account of which fights failed which audience:** Elegon and Garalon stopped friends-and-family groups ([Wowhead, Part Two](https://www.wowhead.com/news/warlords-pvp-gearing-blog-patch-5-4-7-hotfixes-for-april-28-raid-philosophy-part-236385)).

### Inferences
- The fights criticised as worst share three faults: one cycle repeated with no new phases, the hardest part placed at the end of a long fight (so each wipe costs many minutes), and randomness the raid cannot plan around. The fights praised or remembered (Lei Shen, Durumu, Blackhand, Archimonde, Kil'jaeden) change the arena or the rules in each phase, so progress feels like learning new sections.
- End bosses with Mythic-only phases produced the longest world-first races (400-650 pulls). Hidden phases (Ra-den, Mythic Argus) are a deliberate tool for the very top players, not for a general audience.

### Gaps
- No systematic poll or scored ranking of "best fights" across these four expansions was found. The best-fight evidence is a handful of community comments read through search summaries.
- Pull counts for Mythic Mar'gok, Mythic Blackhand, Mythic Gul'dan, Mythic Xavius, Mythic Helya, Heroic Ra-den and Heroic Sinestra were not looked up.

### Room-changing fights (summary)
- **Floors that break:** Blackhand (two collapses, three arenas), Fallen Avatar (floor shatters, then crumbles), Krosus (bridge broken section by section), Al'Akir (platform shattered, the raid flies).
- **Floors that flood or fill:** Nefarian (lava except three pillars), Hagara (ice phase), Jin'rokh (water pools), Brackenspore (moss), the Butcher on Mythic (permanent pools).
- **Moving machinery:** Thogar (trains), Hans'gar and Franzok (belts and presses), Blackfuse (conveyor), Blackhorn (gunship), Spine of Deathwing (the dragon rolls).
- **Platform hopping:** Zor'lok, Elerethe, Imonar, Madness of Deathwing, Conclave of Wind, Beth'tilac, Ji-Kun, Hasabel, Ta'yak's hall.
- **Transported elsewhere:** Garrosh (Realm of Y'Shaarj), Sha of Fear on Heroic (Dread Expanse), Archimonde (Twisting Nether), Star Augur (other worlds), Tichondrius (Illusionary Night), Kil'jaeden (darkness, then Argus), Il'gynoth (the hollow), Elisande (time rewound to the pull).
- **Cycling floor:** Sisters of the Moon (moon phases), Tsulong (day and night), Elegon (vortex).

### What scaled with raid size (summary)
- **Soakers:** Ultraxion (1 vs 3), Baleroc's shards (1 vs 2).
- **Marks and targets:** Arion's Lightning Rod (1 vs 3), Ragnaros' Molten Seeds (10 vs 20) and Lava Bolts (4 vs 10), Thok's fixate pool (3 vs 6), Ragnaros' Cloudburst users (up to 3 in 25).
- **Adds:** Cho'gall's creations (4 vs 10) and two adherents on 25-player Heroic; Stone Guard (3 guardians in 10-man vs 4); Ji-Kun's guarded nests.
- **Fixed damage to split:** Alizabal (200k vs 600k).
- **Timers:** Sha of Fear's shrine timer depends on tank count in 25-man.
- **Flexible modes:** about one extra target or effect per five players (+20% chance per player), on a 10-player floor (10-25 in Mists, 10-30 in Warlords and Legion).
- **Mythic:** fixed at 20 so mechanics can assume class tools and exact counts.
