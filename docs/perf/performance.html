In short. The game was held back by drawing, not by its rules: every pixel of floor was shaded two or three times, the menu drew a 3D scene nobody could see, and every shader was compiled twice, the second time with the game frozen. Those are fixed with the picture unchanged. On top of that there is now a Graphics setting; its Auto level fits the sharpness to the device and holds 60 frames a second where the fixes alone do not. High, the look as it was, is still the default: the pictures of each level are below, for you to say whether Auto should be the start.

How this was measured

A tool (pipeline/tools/perf.py) plays six fixed scenes of the real game in a browser, with the real graphics card, and times every frame of 25 seconds of each: the game's own step, the drawing, and the wait until the card has really finished the frame. Each scene is the same fight every time (the clock and the dice are the tool's), so before and after can be laid side by side. A frame must take under 16.7 ms for 60 frames a second, under 33 ms for 30. On a real screen a frame that misses one refresh waits for the next: a game at 24 ms a frame shows as 30 a second half the time, so the step from 24 to under 16.7 is the one that is felt.

  • Before is the game as it is live, measured again today from a frozen copy of that code, on the same computer.
  • Desktop: 1920 x 1080. Phone screen: 844 x 390 points at 3 pixels a point, with touch. The slowed rows use Chrome's own processor throttle (4 and 6 times).
  • The graphics card is this computer's Intel UHD 630, an ordinary laptop's. It stands in for a phone's card too: no real phone was measured.
  • The scene tables come from a browser without a window, which is fair for frame times but compiles shaders late: for stutters read the Loading section, taken in a visible browser.
  • On the slowed rows the browser overlaps the processor's work with the card's, as a real one does; on the unslowed rows the two are added. So compare before with after along a row, not one row with another.

Drawing (the graphics card)

  • Floors are no longer shaded two or three times. The engine drew solid things in the order their materials were made: the ground first, then every floor laid over it. Each pixel of floor was shaded for each layer, and a lit, textured floor that fills the screen is the dearest thing in the picture. Now the big surfaces are drawn last, the highest first, and what lies under another is skipped. Night Temple duel 38 → 24 ms, Tower floor 33 → 18 ms, Sun Warden 38 → 24 ms on the desktop, nearly all of it from this; the picture is the same.
  • The menu no longer draws the fight's scene. The menus cover the whole canvas, but the 3D scene behind them was drawn through the whole post chain every frame. Play screen 17 → 7 ms.
  • Three full-screen passes fewer: no multisampled canvas (the last pass is one quad), no copy pass when nothing bends the picture, tone mapping folded into the finish pass. About 2 ms a frame at full HD.

Stutters

  • The shader warm-up really warms up. It compiled shaders for drawing straight to the canvas, but the game draws into the post chain's own target, which is a different shader: each was built again the first time it was drawn. And the engine quietly swapped its shadow type at the first shadow, which changed the identity of every shader once more. Both are fixed; the warm-up also builds both heroes' spell effects before the fight. Waiting for shaders behind the first loading page: 2.5 → 0.35 s. In the fight's first 20 seconds: frames over 50 ms 154 → 47, the longest 884 → 100 ms.
  • The post chain's and the reflections' shaders compile in the background from the moment the page opens, and nothing is drawn behind the loading page until they are done.
  • A built shader is kept. The engine deleted a shader when the last thing using it went (a damage number, a bolt) and built it again the next time.

The processor

  • No garbage from the cover and path checks. The helpers that ask "how far is this point from that wall" made a small object and an array on every call, thousands of times a frame in a hall with much cover. Now none, with results the same to the last bit, so no hero walks a hair differently. Raid of ten: garbage 83 → 27 MB a second; a duel 21 → 10.
  • No shader looked up for nothing. The distortion pass changed the set of lights the engine counted, which made it look every lit material's shader up again, twice a frame; a see-through two-sided glow was drawn in two goes and looked up twice as well. Raid of ten: script 11.0 → 7.4 ms a frame on the desktop, which is four to six times that on a phone.
  • The scene's matrices once a frame, not once for each of its draws; hidden spell effects out of the scene (a session kept every effect it ever built, and the engine walked all of them every frame).

Loading

  • The reflections and the Play screen's hero renderer are made when first needed, not before the menu. Menu on screen: 2.4 → 1.2 s on the desktop.
  • The heroes' clips are fetched while the menu is idle.

New: the Graphics setting

  • High (the look as it was), Medium, Low and Auto, on a button at the top of the menu, kept in the browser. Only sharpness and the shadows under the figures change; spells, lights and glow are the same at every level.

The picture is the same, and so are the fights

Each scene's fight is written down as it is played (health and places every second). With the spells built at the first cast as before, the changed game plays the very same fights as the live one, to the last number. The same frame before and after, pixel by pixel, differs only by the film grain (about 1 of 255 on average; no pixel far apart). The Raid's own rule checks pass unchanged (234, 77, 246, 40 and 143 rows), and the server's test.

What is still slow, and why

  • A big raid on a phone. Ten heroes in the Hall of Noon are about 760 draw calls a frame, and a phone's processor needs most of a frame just to issue them (mid-range phone: 106 → 53 ms a frame at High, 43 ms at Auto; that is still only about 23 frames a second). Half of them are the shadow pass drawing the hall's many small blocks a second time; Auto and Low leave those out. The rest are the heroes themselves (about ten separate pieces each) and the hall (about 120 pieces). To go further the hall's blocks would have to be drawn as a few batches instead of one by one, and a hero's pieces joined in Blender: a change to how arenas and heroes are built, not done here.
  • Lit surfaces are dear on an integrated card. A floor that fills the screen costs about 7 ms at full HD, most of it the soft reflections every lit material takes from the sky. One layer of floor is the least there can be; the Night Temple still has two (a see-through ring floor over its apron). Fewer pixels (Medium, Low, Auto) is what helps here.
  • At High on integrated graphics a full-HD fight is 34 to 54 frames a second, not 60 (18 to 29 ms a frame; it was 26 to 46). Auto gets 60 there by drawing 1275 x 717 instead of 1920 x 1080 and letting the browser stretch it.
  • The first cast of some spells still costs one long frame (60 to 100 ms): what a spell makes at the moment it is cast (a bolt, a danger zone's shape) is not known before, so its shader is built then, once a session.
  • The Play screen's turning hero has its own small renderer, and costs about 8 ms a frame on this card. Fine at 60, but it is the menu's whole cost.
  • Downloads were not made smaller. The game's script is 5 MB (1.4 MB as sent) because every spell of every hero is in it; splitting it by hero would shorten the download on a slow line but not the start, which was measured to be shaders, not script. Models are sent uncompressed (a hero 0.5 MB, the clips 1.3 MB), and every file is asked for again on each visit (the server answers "unchanged" for each). Both are settings of the build and the deploy, left for a decision.
  • Garbage. A duel still makes 10 MB a second (from 21), a raid of ten 27 (from 83): the rest is spread over many spells and the engine's own drawing.

What was not measured

  • A real phone, and Safari on an iPhone: the phone rows are this computer with a phone-sized screen and a slowed processor. A phone's graphics card may be faster or slower than this one.
  • Online play: a network duel or a raid through the server. The scenes are offline; the raid of ten has nine scripted partners on one screen.
  • A long session: the scenes are 25 seconds. Memory that only grows over an hour would not show.
  • The Auto level's following of the frame rate during a fight on real slow hardware: the choice before the fight is measured, the adjusting during it was only seen to work in a visible browser on this computer.
  • Whether it feels smooth: that is for a person playing.

Running it again

All of it is one tool, documented in docs/pipeline/performance.md:

  • python pipeline/tools/perf.py scenes --label after: every scene on every device (about 25 minutes).
  • python pipeline/tools/perf.py load --label after --headed: loading and the first fight, after npm run build.
  • python pipeline/tools/perf.py parts | passes | profile | census | flips --scene raid-party: where a scene's time goes.
  • python pipeline/tools/perf-report.py: this page.
