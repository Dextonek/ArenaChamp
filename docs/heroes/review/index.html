The heroes' model review

2026-11-16 · all 41 models of the fourth style (Pim and Pom are two) checked through every clip the game plays on them. Not pushed, not deployed.

What was checked

Every hero was posed by the game's own figure code through every clip it plays: standing, walking, running, sprinting, backing off, the basic attack, each of its four spells (with the cast style the game picks), a hit, a stun, a knock-up and the fall. In each pose every vertex was measured: a held thing (staff, blade, lantern) inside the body, the head, the robe or the cape; a forearm or hand inside the chest, hips, legs or cloth; a leg coming out through a skirt; the feet under or above the floor. Every flag over a few centimetres was then drawn with the offending points in red and looked at by eye. Each hero also got a sheet of all its clips (close and from the game's camera, below in its entry) and a CPU duel on the game page, the 41 spread over all nine arenas, filmed at each cast and attack; three on a Climb floor. Tools: pipeline/tools/hero-review.js, hero-review-sheet.js, hero-review-duel.js, hero-review-close.js; this page pipeline/tools/hero-review-page.py.

What was wrong, and is fixed

Three faults ran through the whole roster. All three are fixed in the game's code (src/motion/real.ts and two lines in HeroFigure.followReal): no model was rebuilt, no clip, timing or spell changed, and the fights are the same fights.

1. Every hero stood sunk into the floor (all 41)

3 to 12 cm, deepest on Mabel, Professor Puff and Oblivion: the soles and toes were cut off by the floor. The animation pack's own figure stands exactly on the floor in its idle; our bodies' ankles sit a little lower in a longer leg, and the boots a little under the sole. Each model is now raised as it loads, by just enough that its lowest sole stands on the floor through the idle. In a stride the big boots of the heavy heroes rolled their toe or heel up to 13 cm under the floor (the pack's own figure dips 2 to 3): the model now rises by the difference while that lasts and settles back after, so no sole goes more than about 4 cm under (measured on every hero walking, running, sprinting and backing off).

2. Hands went into coats, robes and aprons (most of the cloth-wearers)

The pack's clips hang the hands close to a slim figure's hips; on a long coat or a flared robe they went inside it (Kaia's storm coat, Marin's apron, Shiori's sleeves, Mabel's skirt; up to 15 cm). The older figures have had a fix for this since 2026-11-10 (you accepted it then): the arm swings out from the shoulder just enough to clear the body's outline, the elbow keeping its bend. The fourth style now has it too, eased so an arm does not flap in and out with each stride. It never acts during a blow, a cast or a knock-up, where the hands go where the clip sends them.

3. Staffs, scepters, lyres and lanterns lay on their side in every cast (16 of the 18 who hold one in the left hand)

The pack's push (the clip of every ranged attack and most casts) turns the palm to the front, and what the hand held turned with it: every staff lay across the chest, its head at the hero's right shoulder and pointing away from the target; Wick's lantern lay on its side. Now the hand keeps it upright through the blow or cast, as it is held standing: a short one (a scepter, a lyre, a lantern, a wand) leans toward the target, a long staff stands straight or leans back a little so its foot stays out of the robe. Anka's fan and Silvane's hand mirror are left as the clip holds them, held out flat: their face is what shows, and upright turned them edge-on. A dash, a slam or a swing carries the item as its clip does (upright, a staff went through the robe of a hero bent deep into a dash).

For you to decide (not changed)

  1. The staffs upright in casts (fix 3 above) changes how 16 heroes look while casting. The head pointed away from the target, so it is treated as a fault, but it is also a look: if you liked the crosswise thrust better, it is one line to undo (in HeroFigure.followReal, the line that calls uprightHeld).
  2. Professor Puff casts with his empty left hand while the brass nozzle hangs in his right (Q W E R and his attack): the pack has no right-hand push. A nozzle in the left hand would be a model change (his look), so it is yours to say.
  3. Sefu's headdress cuts into his right shoulder for a moment in Scarab Stride (the pack's dash clip bends the head down): a lower headdress or another clip for that spell would change his look or his motion.
  4. Big Yoko's arms now stand out over his rope about 25 degrees (they were inside it). If that reads too wide, the rope could be built a little slimmer.
  5. Fenna's staff brushes her antlers and her robe's flare while she walks: a shorter staff or narrower antlers would change her look.
  6. Small, seen only close and for a moment, left as they are: Kaia's crook through her hair as she falls; Big Yoko's forearm in his belly in Charge; Gus's broom foot in his robe while stunned; Shiori's brush foot at her hem in Flowing Script; a claw past Sirix's chest in Barbed Stinger; Grandma Ironknit's needle tip at her skirt in Knitting Needle.
