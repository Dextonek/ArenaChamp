The spell blocks: a trial on three heroes

2026-11-16. Before deciding on the other ~150 spells, twelve spells of three heroes chosen for simple shapes (Sefu, Professor Puff, Big Yoko) were tried on the blocks. Eight went onto them; four stayed code. All of them play exactly as before. The design: the framework page, docs/pipeline/hero-framework.md section 12.

Recommendation: some shapes only, and no big conversion project

Do not convert all ~150. A conversion makes a spell's rules short and in one place, and its danger zone is the hit's own shape. But it saves only about a fifth of the lines, because four fifths of a spell is its look, which moves as it is. A third of the spells chosen as the easiest did not fit. And the prints prove the fight, not the picture.

Use the blocks where they pay: every new hero's spells; a spell whose hero is being fixed or rebalanced anyway; and the clean shapes when convenient (a bolt, a circle with a fuse, a zone round or in front of the caster, a sure hit). Leave the dashes that bite or carry, the bolts with their own flight, and the spells with a second act as code until a second spell needs the same block.

What a spell's rules look like now

Before, the rules were spread through a 200–300 line class: constants at the top, a flag, the hit in the middle of the frame code, the danger zone written again in telegraphs(), the sliders in buildGui(). After, the class keeps only the drawing, and the rules sit at the end.

Sarcophagus, Hot Air blocks + hook

Sefu's seal is a sure hit that must still find them in the circle (inside: 1.6); the burst a frame after the seal ends is 12 lines of code in a hook. Puff's push round him is a zone, then a move away from the aim at the impact (move: { at: 'impact' }); which way is "away" is a 9-line hook.

What stayed code, and why

  • Scarab Stride (Sefu, E) stayed code: the bite is judged each frame on the stretch of the eased dash he just ran, nearest enemy only, then poison in two ticks; no damage slider (bite and poison instead). The lane block judges differently (anyone the front has passed), so it would change who gets bitten.
  • Air Hose (Puff, Q) stayed code: the bolt leaves 0.8 m ahead of him, aims at the clicked point instead of the aim, ends when its bubbles have cleared (not at a fixed time), draws its own bolt when no real bolt flies, and has no loop switch. Four options for one spell.
  • Pop! (Puff, R) stayed code: a sure hit, but it fizzles if he lost sight of the enemy or it is beyond 11 m when the pin lands, knocks from where he stands then (not where he cast), and its danger circle follows the enemy. Three options; it would fit the sure hit block with them.
  • Sumo Charge (Yoko, E) stayed code: a run that catches the first enemy it sweeps over and carries them ahead of him every frame. A "catch and carry" block would serve it and likely Pim's Charge and Bram's Shove; worth building only with them.

What the blocks gained in the trial

Typed and small, each because a shape repeated or a number had to stay a slider. The runner (src/heroes/framework/) grew from 393 to 551 lines; Anka's four spells play as before on it (her prints after every step).

  • A sure hit (target): Shepherd's Crook, Sarcophagus; Pop! with three options.
  • A zone judged once (zone, any of the engine's shapes): Stomp, Salt Throw, Palm Strike, Hot Air.
  • Sliders (tune): a number of the rules can name a slider, or be worked out from them (Hot Air's push × 7). Spells without a damage slider or a loop switch keep their old settings, so the tool page and the Rust export see the same keys.
  • A fuller hit: pull, status, projectile, where the knock comes from.
  • The look's moments before the rules (releasing, impacting) and its ticking while idle: the old files often drew first and hit second, and the dice (sparks, puffs) must keep their order or the fight changes.
  • Facing aim, a move at the impact, a rules hook every frame (a shove, a burst later).

Not included: the seven Rust-ported heroes' 28 spells, which wait for the Rust port to be graded again (it reads their settings by name).

  • Risk to play: low. A rule that changes shows in the two fight prints at once, and every spell cast in them is proven. Spells the prints never cast (Scarab Stride, Time Skip and three others) rest on the tool page's self test only.
  • Risk to the look: medium, and unchecked. The prints see health, places and cooldowns, not particles. Each look is copied by hand into a new class; a slip (a wrong point, a lost puff) would play the same fight and look different. A per-spell clip beside the old one would close it, at a few minutes a spell.
  • Order traps: the dice must roll in the same order (a puff before or after the hit is another fight); objects must be made in the same order; a file that ages its particles while idle must keep doing so. All three were met in this trial and are now moments of the blocks.

A new hero's spell now

The card's line and the rules, then the look. The danger zone, the hit, the tool page's sliders and the cast's timing all come from the rules; the look is told the moments (cast, frame, impact, after) and draws.

// src/heroes/<hero>/spells/tidebreak.ts (a sketch, not a real spell)
export const tidebreakModule = spell<null, 'slow'>({
  label: 'tidebreak', category: 'hero: tide',
  targeting: { mode: 'self', radius: 3.6 },
  seconds: 1.6, state: () => null,
  rules: {
    windup: 0.45, facing: true,
    shape: { kind: 'zone', area: (c) => ({ kind: 'cone', x: c.from.x, z: c.from.z, radius: 5, angle: 60, dir: c.yaw }) },
    hit: { damage: 55, knock: 3, color: TIDE_CSS.foam, status: { kind: 'slow', seconds: 'slow' } },
  },
  tune: { slow: [1, 0, 2, 0.1] },
  look: TidebreakLook,      // the wave: made in its constructor, drawn in frame / impact / after
  bloom: { enabled: true, strength: 0.4, radius: 0.4, threshold: 0.86 }, frame: 3, maxDamage: 120,
});
