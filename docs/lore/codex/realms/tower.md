# The Tower and the Ring

*The centre of the Dial: the Gnomon, the Ring at its foot, the fallen sun beneath, the Tear above.* · [Realms](README.md) ·
[The Truce of the Ring](../factions/truce.md) · [The Climb](../climb/README.md)

## The land

The Tower stands on a low plain of grey stone in the middle of the Dial, the **Crater of Noonfall**, a shallow bowl four miles
across that the sun's heart made when it came down. Before the Fall the plain was green and held a midsummer market; now nothing
grows on the crater's floor but a pale moss that glows faintly in the early morning when the light rises. Round the crater's rim
stand the camps, inns, stables and shrines of the **Ringside**, the town of the Truce, where every people on the Dial lives
side by side because the Truce requires it and the trade rewards it.

The Tower itself rises from the crater's centre: a single shaft of pale grey stone, smooth, without a seam, wider at the base than a
city square and narrowing to a point lost in cloud. It has one door at the bottom, the gate, and no windows anyone has found.
Climbers say it is larger inside than out, which the Hourkeepers have measured and cannot explain. It is: it holds the hours
of realms, and an hour has no walls. The Tower is the Aumbry, a collector of worlds made on another world, Senmere, and it took the
sun ([The Tower and the Sun](../tower-and-sun.md)).

## Its places

- **The Ring.** The arena at the Tower's foot, on the spot where the straw-rope wrestling circle stood on the day of the Fall. A
  round floor of raked sand ringed with straw rope, a pale line inside the rope, and stands of grey stone rising in tiers for
  forty thousand. Above it the Arbiter's box. The ring stamped on the floor of every duel is drawn after the one Big Yoko
  defends.
- **The Hundred Doors.** Under the stands, a gallery of a hundred doors that open onto the realms: the Sands, the Deep, the white
  north, the Wood's edge and the rest. They were there when the Kilnborn masons dug the stands' foundations in year 3, already
  hung, already locked. Nobody built them. [Wick](../heroes/wick.md) keeps them.
- **The Truce Table.** A long table of black oak in the hall behind the Ring, where every duel ends with winner and loser eating
  side by side. Its first leg is [Madame Cleaver](../heroes/madame-cleaver.md)'s old cauldron stand.
- **The Tally-house.** The counting house under the stands where [Mr. Ledger](../heroes/mr-ledger.md) keeps the Tally: every duel,
  every debt, at interest. The obol is struck in its cellar.
- **The couriers' loft.** Above the north stand, where the Truce's couriers sleep between runs. [Pip](../heroes/pip.md) lives here.
- **The Salt Line.** The edge of the Ring, salted every dawn by [Morwen](../heroes/morwen.md) so that no stray working reaches the
  stands.
- **The Hall of Noon.** Beneath the Tower, reached by a stair from the Ring's west tunnel: a round hall of black glass where the
  **Heart of Noon**, the fallen sun's heart, still burns in a pit at the centre. Every morning its light rises through the Tower and
  out of its top, and that is the day. Nobody goes into the Hall without the Truce's leave, and few come out unburned.
- **The Cinder Pit.** Beside the Hall, a deep pit of embers where the sun's two last embers burn as **Ash and Ember**: two shapes of
  fire who watch each other and anyone who comes near.
- **The gate.** The Tower's one door, on its east face, which opens only for a champion of the Ring, and opened for the first time
  in year 90. Beyond it, the Climb.
- **The Sun Court.** At the Tower's top, above the clouds: the court where the choir sang the Hymn of Noon and the **Sun Warden**
  kept his post. Its great dial still strikes the hours of the old day, wrong now, and can be heard on still nights from the Ring.
- **The Noon Seat.** Above the Sun Court, where the sun rested at noon. It is empty. Whoever reaches it decides what hangs there.
- **The Tear.** Above everything, where the sun hung: a rent in the sky, black with a burning pink edge, a hand's breadth wider every
  year. Things come through it.

## Rulers and law

The Tower and the Ring are ruled by **the Truce of the Ring** ([faction](../factions/truce.md)): its officers, its five clauses and
its Arbiter. No realm holds land in the crater; the Ringside's people answer to the Truce and to the Tally. The law is the Truce's
and is enforced by duel: a quarrel at the Ringside is settled in the Ring like any other, and a breach of the Truce goes to the Gaol
of Oaths. The Ringside has the lowest crime and the highest prices on the Dial.

## Faith and festivals

No faith rules here; all of them have a shrine on the crater's rim, and the Truce keeps them in order of arrival. The one festival
everyone keeps is **the Opening of the Ring**, on midsummer: the anniversary of the Fall and of the Truce, when the Ring opens its
year with Big Yoko's bout against [Bram](../heroes/bram.md)'s stone, the reading of the Truce, the Arbiter's whistle and a feast at
the Truce Table that lasts until the slip makes it the next day.

## Food and trade

Everything, from everywhere, at a markup. The Ringside's own food is the **truce-pot**, a stew that every kitchen keeps on the fire
and anyone may add to, so no two bowls are the same. The Ring's trade is champions: their keep, their weapons, their wagers and
their debts.

## Old wounds

The festival market that was here on the day of the Fall is gone with everyone in it; its dead are remembered by a ring of white
stones on the crater's rim. The first years of the Truce were not peaceful: the founders fought in the Ring for months over the
clauses. And year 90's blank page in the Tally, the duel that opened the gate with no champion recorded, has never been explained to
the Ringside. The Tower took the hour of that duel: its winner was Mum, whom it could not take.

## Its stake in the Tower

The Truce exists because of the Tower, and its fourth clause (the gate opens only for a champion of the Ring) is what keeps the
realms fighting in the Ring instead of on the Field. If anyone reaches the Noon Seat, the Truce's great promise is kept, and it may
no longer be needed. Its officers know it.
