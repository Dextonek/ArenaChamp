# Gloaming

*The castle-city on the north-western heights, at the eleventh hour, where the light fails first.* · [Realms](README.md) ·
[Gloaming folk](../peoples/gloaming.md) · [The Long Dead](../peoples/longdead.md) · [The Court of Dusk](../factions/dusk.md)

## The land

Gloaming is a land of high ridges and deep valleys in the north-west of the Dial, between Frostfall and the Walking Wood: vine
terraces on the southern slopes, dark pine on the northern, rivers in steep gorges, and on the highest ridge the castle-city of
Gloaming itself, built round the Night Temple and above the ossuaries. Before the Fall the Tower's shadow reached it at evening, and it
was the Dial's land of late suppers and good wine.

On the day of the Fall the light left Gloaming first. For the year after there was no full day there, only a long blue dusk, and to
this day Gloaming's mornings come late and its evenings early, and the light in between is the colour of a candle through violet
glass. Its people changed in the dusk; so did its vines, which now ripen black.

## Places

- **The castle-city.** Walls and towers of dark stone climbing a ridge in rings, each ring a district: the vintners' ring, the mirror
  quarter, the bell ring, the noble houses at the top. Lamps burn all day. The streets are busiest at midnight.
- **The Night Temple.** At the city's heart, a temple of black stone round a great dark mirror that reflects nothing, before which the
  names of the dead are said each night. On its west wall, the place where [Nyx](../heroes/nyx.md)'s shadow first fell is still pale.
  [Corbel](../heroes/corbel.md) perches on its eaves, on a ledge [Bram](../heroes/bram.md) carved. The Truce's arena of the Night
  Temple is its forecourt.
- **The ossuaries.** Beneath the city, halls of bone older than the Court, stacked skull on skull, lit by teal soulfire, sealed by the
  Ossuary Pact four and a half centuries ago. [Mortis](../heroes/mortis.md) is their lord.
- **The Mirror Hall.** The great hall of the Mirror house, walls of mirrors facing mirrors, where [Silvane](../heroes/silvane.md) keeps
  the Court's secrets.
- **The Countess's castle.** On the westernmost height, above the oldest vineyards: [Vesper](../heroes/vesper.md)'s seat, where the Court
  of Dusk meets.
- **The harlequins' hall.** The Court's hall of revels, masks and music, where [Mum](../heroes/mum.md) plays.
- **Lantern-under-Hill.** A village in the valley below the castle, where the night-road wardens live and the Countess's old steward
  still keeps his garden.
- **The night roads.** Roads east to Frostfall and south to the Wood, patrolled by the Court's wardens with lanterns of violet glass.
- **The fox woods.** Thickets in the valleys round Gloaming, home of the Briarkin's Fox court and [Kohaku](../heroes/kohaku.md).

## Rulers and law

The **Court of Dusk** ([faction](../factions/dusk.md)) rules: the noble houses of Gloaming bound together under the Countess, with the
Long Dead's lord in the ossuaries as an ally by pact. Each house governs its own lands and keeps its own debts; the Court decides what
concerns all of them. The law is old, courteous and full of exceptions for those who can afford them. The commons have their own
courts in the valleys and trust them more.

## Faith and festivals

The Vigil ([Gloaming folk](../peoples/gloaming.md)). Festivals:
- **The Long Evening**, on midsummer: Gloaming does not mourn the Fall but keeps the night after it, with every lamp lit and every
  door open from dusk to the late morning.
- **The Naming**, every night at the Night Temple: the names of the dead said before the dark mirror.
- **The Masque of Houses**, at midwinter: the noble houses' great revel, which the harlequins lead and at which debts are traded.

## Food and trade

Black grapes and black wine, game, mushrooms, dark bread, plum brandy, and the Court's famous evening suppers. Gloaming sells wine,
mirrors, glass, silver, velvet, and safe passage on the night roads; it buys grain (on credit, from the Tally, for a hundred years),
salt and iron.

## Old wounds

- **The Long Dusk.** The year without a day killed the old and the sick, and changed the living. Gloaming has never quite recovered
  its numbers.
- **The debt.** The first harvests after the Fall were bought on credit from the Truce's Tally-house, and the Court has paid the
  interest ever since without touching the principal. Mr. Ledger has written about it this year.
- **The Wood.** The Court buries its overflow dead in the Wood's soil; the Moot hates it, and Mortis raises them anyway.
- **Aubade.** A new sun would burn Gloaming. The Noonsworn mean to hang one.

## Its stake in the Tower

The Court wants the Noon Seat kept empty: no new sun to burn them, and their roads, their dead and their secrets kept. It keeps the
letter of the Truce and very little of its spirit, and its champions climb to see what is in the Seat before anyone else does.
