# Aubade

*The white city on the morning cliffs, at the first hour of the Dial.* · [Realms](README.md) · [Aubade folk](../peoples/aubade.md)
· [The Noonsworn](../factions/noonsworn.md)

## The land

Aubade stands on the eastern rim of the Dial, on white limestone cliffs that drop four hundred feet into the Outer Sea. Before the
Fall the Tower's shadow reached Aubade at the first hour of the morning, and the light reached it before anywhere else: the city
was built to catch it. Inland of the cliffs lie the **Terraces**, a long slope of vines, olives and fig orchards cut into steps by
a thousand years of gardeners, and beyond them the dry hills where the Aubade folk graze goats and the shrine wrestlers run at dawn.
To the south the cliffs fall away to the Sands; to the north, a day's ride, they meet the Frostfall road at the Waymeet, the oldest
inn on the Dial.

The weather has been worst here since the Fall, after the Coast: the morning mists the city was famous for now come at any hour,
and some days the sun rises behind them and is never seen.

## The city and its places

- **The Bell Towers.** Aubade has a hundred bell towers, one for every singer of the old choir, and the city is laid out round
  them. Every morning at first light they ring the sun up in order, from the cliff edge inland, and the hundredth is rung short.
  The bells were all recast by [Campana](../heroes/campana.md) in the eightieth summer, and answer one another.
- **The White Barracks.** The fortress of the Noonsworn's paladins, a square of white stone round a parade ground on the highest
  point of the cliffs. The wings of the order hang in its hall on frames, one for every paladin who has fallen, and the empty
  frames wait for the living.
- **The Lantern-Shrine.** Where the Ember of Aubade was kept for ninety years, in a small round temple of glass on the cliff edge.
  Since [Orren](../heroes/orren.md) took the Ember up it stands empty, and the Noonsworn keep its lamps lit anyway.
- **The Choir Loft.** The great hall where the choir of a hundred rehearsed the Hymn of Noon. It is kept as it was on the morning
  of the Fall: a hundred chairs, ninety-eight of them with a lily on the seat. [Cantor](../heroes/cantor.md) still teaches there.
- **The Dawn Stair.** Four thousand steps cut into the cliff face from the city down to the harbour of **Lowlight**, where the
  fishing boats and the Truce's ferries put in. Lowlight is poorer than the city above, and where the light comes last.
- **The low quarter.** The houses under the cliff's overhang, home of lamplighters, porters, ropemakers and bell-casters, where
  the dawn reaches at mid-morning. The Noonsworn recruit their best soldiers here and their elders almost never.
- **The shrine cliffs.** A stretch of the cliffs south of the city, terraced with straw-rope wrestling rings, where the shrine
  wrestlers train at dawn and throw salt to clean the ring. [Big Yoko](../heroes/big-yoko.md) learned there.
- **Campana's foundry.** The bell foundry in the low quarter, its furnaces fed with charcoal from the Wood and copper from the
  Deep, which pours a bell every new moon.

## Rulers and law

Aubade has no king. It is ruled by the **Council of Bells**, a hundred ringers, one for each tower, chosen by the households of
each tower's quarter for seven years. In practice the Council does what the Noonsworn's elders advise, because half the ringers
are Noonsworn and the other half have families. The law is short and old: an oath is binding, a debt is binding, a bell may not be
silenced, and no one may be turned away from the city at dawn. Justice is public: a judgement is rung from the judge's tower, and
the whole city hears it.

## Faith and festivals

The faith is the Oath of the Dawn ([Aubade folk](../peoples/aubade.md)). The festivals:
- **Midsummer**, the day of the Fall: the bells ring at noon for the only time in the year, the city stands silent for the space
  of the Hymn, and the names of the hundred are read. Then the city goes to the Ring, because midsummer is when the Ring opens.
- **First Morning**, the day after: a feast of new bread and black honey for everyone born in the last year, and for Auriel, who
  was born on it.
- **The Ringing of the Hours** every new moon: each tower rings once, in order, and children run between them to see who is fastest.
- **The Low Verse** on the shortest day, sung only in the low quarter, a song asking the sun kindly to come back.

## Food and trade

Bread, olives, white cheese, figs, goat, grilled fish from Lowlight and the black honey of the cliff flowers; a pale, dry wine and a
bitter herb liquor drunk at funerals. Aubade sells bronze, bells, paper, olive oil, wine and soldiers; it buys copper and tin from the
Deep, charcoal and wax from the Wood, salt and fish from the Coast and, quietly, glass from the Sands.

## Old wounds

- **The choir.** Every family in Aubade lost someone at the Fall, in the choir or in the panic on the cliffs when the sun went out.
  The city has never stopped mourning, and grief has made it certain: it is easier to believe the sun can be hung again than to
  believe a hundred singers died for nothing.
- **The War of the Banners.** Aubade sent its paladins to fight for the Azure League against Harrowmark, and lost a third of them on
  the Field. Its old soldiers still will not eat at the same table as anyone who served the crimson banner, Truce or no Truce.
- **The Sands.** Aubade has never accepted that the Dune Throne calls the sun the Father. The quarrel is old and courteous, and the
  Noonsworn and the Throne have nearly come to blows in the Ring more often than any two factions but the Noonsworn and the Court.

## Its stake in the Tower

Aubade wants the sun hung again over the Tower, and has a plan: a champion of the Noonsworn wins the climb, and the Ember of Aubade
is carried to the Noon Seat. The city has spent ten years' tithes on Orren and on the paladins who walk with him, and every morning
the bells ring a little louder. If the Noonsworn fail, Aubade has nothing else to believe in, which is what frightens the low quarter.
