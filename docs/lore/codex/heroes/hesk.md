# Hesk, the Golemwright

*The Underforge · [Kilnborn](../peoples/kiln.md) · from [the Deep](../realms/deep.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

In the Underforge the heavy work is done by golems: bodies of clay and iron, as tall as a doorway, that carry ore, turn wheels, haul cages and hold up
galleries while the masons work, and move because a word is written on their brows. A golem obeys whoever wrote the last word on it, and nothing else.
The craft is old in the Deep, older than the Fall, when the golems were small and clumsy and moved by words cut in stone; since the Fall, when the Caldera's
fire came up, golems fired in it have woken stronger and stayed awake longer, and the craft has become one of the Underforge's great masteries. Hesk is its master.

He was born in the forty-first summer after the Fall in the clay pits under the southern hills, the son of a potter, and he grew up with clay to the elbows: a
heavy, careful, quiet boy who liked to make things that did what they were told. He made his first golem at twelve, a figure the size of a cat, from his
father's clay, fired in the kiln behind the house, and wrote on its brow, in the masons' marks, the only word he knew that a golem would obey: "carry". It carried
a cup across the table and stopped. He was so happy he could not speak.

His first great golem was a giant of Caldera clay and iron, twice the height of a man, which he built at nineteen to hold up the new galleries above the great lift,
and on whose brow he wrote a word he had found cut in the wall of a cave by the black water, in a script older than the masons' marks, because it was beautiful. The
giant woke at once and worked as no golem had ever worked. For a month the Underforge talked of nothing else. Then, in the sixtieth summer, a fox spirit of the Fox
court, [Kohaku](kohaku.md), visiting the Deep for reasons of her own, read the word on its brow, liked it, and took it. The golem stopped, turned, and walked into the
black water of the Deep, and was never seen again. Hesk has made a hundred golems since and keeps them closer, never more than a room away, and has sworn to have the
word back from her. She says she has forgotten which one it was.

The masons' marks are a poor script for golems, too few words and all of them blunt, and the word from the cave was gone. For twenty-five years Hesk made do: his
golems worked, and slept, and woke again only when he rewrote them. Then, in the eighty-sixth summer, a letter came from Frostfall: the Hourkeepers' library had a new
great calligrapher, a girl of sixteen called [Shiori](shiori.md), the last of the great brushes, whose writing came true, and she had heard about the golems. Hesk went
north, forty-five years old and clay to the elbows, and stood in the library while a girl younger than his apprentices wrote one word on a scrap of rice paper and
handed it to him without a word. He put it on the brow of a golem in the library's courtyard, and the golem stood up, looked round, and bowed. Shiori taught him the
script for a year. The word on every golem's brow in the Underforge is in her hand now, copied by his, and they wake and stay awake as the cave's word did.

He is a heavy, careful man with clay to the elbows and a stylus behind his ear, in a leather apron with tablets hung from the belt, and he fights in the Ring with his
golems: he writes and they obey, and in a fight, whoever writes the last word wins. [Bram](bram.md), the mason, thinks a friend should be made of stone and should
choose you; Hesk thinks that is very sentimental, and that Gerald, Bram's pillar, is a block with a face. They argue about it every year at the Hall of Marks, and
neither has convinced the other, and both are secretly afraid the other is right.

## People and appearance

Kilnborn of the clay pits, born after the Fall: fifty-nine, heavy and careful, with ash-grey skin under pale dried clay to the elbows, a stylus behind his ear, a leather
apron with clay tablets on the belt. His look is not built yet; its brief is in the [revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Slow, careful and precise, the voice of a man who chooses every word because he knows what words can do. He speaks in instructions. He never says anything he has not
thought about twice.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Every word is an order. Watch which one I write."
- **Picked:** "Stand. Walk. Obey."
- **Winning:** "Written. Done."
- **Winning:** "You did exactly as you were told."
- **Losing:** "A smudged letter. I'll rewrite it."
- **Losing:** "Clay breaks. Clay is cheap."
- **Meeting Kohaku:** "Give me back the word, fox."
- **Meeting Bram:** "Your rock still talking, Bram? Mine just listens."

## Secrets

- He has been back to the cave by the black water. The old word is gone from the wall. In its place, cut fresh, in the same old script, is his own name.
- The first golem is still walking, under the Deep, with no word on its brow. He has heard it at night through the rock. Something must be telling it where to go.
- He envies Bram's friendship with Gerald more than he has ever envied anything.

## What he wants from the Tower

To read whatever is written on it. Every made thing has a word somewhere that tells it what to do, and he believes the Tower is a made thing.

## Ties

- **[Kohaku](kohaku.md)**, sworn enemy: she stole the word from his first golem's brow and it walked into the black water.
- **[Bram](bram.md)**, rival: a friend made of stone who chooses you, against a servant made of clay who obeys.
- **[Shiori](shiori.md)**, his teacher: she taught him the script; the word on every golem's brow is in her hand.
