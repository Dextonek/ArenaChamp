# Auriel, the Dawn Paladin

*The Noonsworn · [Aubade folk](../peoples/aubade.md) · from [Aubade](../realms/aubade.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Auriel was born in the bell quarter of Aubade at first light on the morning after the Fall, while the city was still waiting to
see whether the sun would rise at all. It rose late and thin, a white coin behind haze, and the bells rang it up for the first
time without the choir; and in the house of the White Bell a ringer called Aude gave birth to a daughter and named her for the
light. Her father was not there. He was one of the hundred who had sung the Hymn of Noon at the Tower's top the day before, and
he was not one of the two who came down.

The Noonsworn took her birth for a sign, which they were doing with everything that year. A child born to a choir widow, on the
first morning, at the first bell: they wrote it in the book of the dawn, and when Aude died of the grey fever in the third winter,
they took the child into the White Barracks to be raised as a paladin. Nobody yet understood what it meant to be Noon-touched.
They found out slowly. Auriel's year-mates grew up around her; she did not. At twenty she looked four, at fifty she looked ten.
She learned the sword from a paladin who grew old teaching her, and then from his pupil, and then from his pupil's pupil, and she
outlived all three of them as a child. She says the Barracks taught her patience. The paladins who served with her say it taught
her to be alone in a full room.

She was sworn at seventy-five, when she looked fifteen: the youngest-looking paladin the order ever took and, by years, one of
the oldest. They put the dawn line on her brow at the first bell and gave her the wings. The wings of the paladins are armour, not
feathers: white plates hinged on a frame across the back, made in imitation of the honour guard who once walked the sun's road
across the sky, or so the order says. In Auriel they look, everyone agrees, as if they had grown there.

Her first years under oath were the Truce's ordinary work: escorting the bell-founders' caravans down the Dawn Stair, guarding
the Ember of Aubade in its lantern-shrine, standing at the Ring's gate on midsummer as the Noonsworn's champion-in-waiting. Her
first duel in the Ring was in the eightieth summer, against a Dune Throne champion who had called the sun the Father's property.
She won it in thirty heartbeats and then helped him up, which the crowd liked and the Noonsworn elders did not. Her shields, the
Ring noticed that day, burst outward when they break: a smith's trick of the order, which in her hands became a habit and then a
creed. *Every night ends. I am how.*

The night she means is the Court of Dusk's. In the eighty-sixth summer a Noonsworn bell caravan was found on the night road below
Gloaming with its ringers alive, pale and asleep, and two of them never woke. The Court said wolves. Auriel rode to the castle-city
alone and demanded a champion, under the Truce, at the Ring. The Countess came herself. [Vesper](vesper.md) and Auriel have fought
eleven times since, five to five with one called by the Arbiter for darkness, and neither has said the other's name kindly once.
Auriel believes Vesper is everything the long dusk has made of the Dial: a thing that should have ended and found a way not to.
She also knows, and will not say, that Vesper is the only person in the Ring who fights her as an equal and not as a sign.

The Noonsworn have given her the duty she wanted most and feared most. When [Orren](orren.md) took up the Ember of Aubade and swore
never to set it down except to fight, it was Auriel who put the oath to him, and Auriel who walks in front of him wherever he
carries it. If the Noonsworn win the climb, the plan is simple: Auriel opens the way, Orren carries the Ember up the Tower, and at
the Noon Seat it is hung, and the sun rises whole. She has said it so often that it sounds like a prayer.

She has rivals among the living. [Sefu](sefu.md) of the Sands says the sun is his father's, and Auriel has told him in public
that the sun is nobody's father; he answered that she has never met hers either, and she has not forgiven it. And
[Corbel](corbel.md), the stone-winged watcher of the Night Temple, waits on high places for her to tire, because her dawn turns
him back toward stone; she has never yet tired first.

Now, in the hundredth summer, the Tower's gate is open to champions of the Ring, and Auriel has more right to climb than almost
anyone. She has gone up as far as the Sea's crown and come down again to wait for Orren. People ask her what she will do if the
sun is hung and the Noon-touched begin to age again. She says she will grow up. She smiles when she says it.

## People and appearance

Aubade folk, the people of the morning cliffs, and a sworn paladin of the Noonsworn. She looks twenty and is a hundred. Tall and
straight-backed, as Aubade children are taught; warm skin; long hair of dark gold bound with gold thread, one thread for each oath
kept, which by now is most of her hair. The dawn line runs gold down her brow. She wears white and gold plate with a halo ring of
gold above her helm, a kite shield on her left arm and a sword in her right, and the wings of plates across her back. In the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md) her figure is unchanged: it already reads as her people.

## Voice

Clear and carrying, made for battlefields and bell towers; she speaks in full sentences and in oaths, and rarely raises her voice
because she rarely needs to. She laughs seldom, but all the way. To those she trusts she is dry and a little tired. To the Court of
Dusk she is very polite.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "First light. Stand behind me."
- **Picked:** "The oath of the dawn holds. Let us see if you do."
- **Winning:** "There. Morning."
- **Winning:** "Get up. The sun does, every day."
- **Losing:** "Night is long. It is not forever."
- **Losing:** "Write it down. I will be back at first bell."
- **Meeting Vesper:** "Countess. You look tired. Shall I let some light in?"
- **Meeting Orren:** "Keep the Ember close. I will keep the rest away."

## Secrets

- The Hourkeepers have a theory, which they have told nobody but the Noonsworn elders and Auriel: if a sun hangs over the Tower
  again and the hours run true, the Noon-touched may age all at once. Auriel would be a hundred. She has decided to carry the sun
  up anyway, and has told no one she knows.
- Her father's name was Ansel, a tenor of the choir. [Cantor](cantor.md) knew him and has never told her that he was the one
  who missed the beat. He is not sure it is true. She does not know Cantor knows anything.
- She has met Vesper once outside the Ring, on a night road, without armour, and they talked until morning. Neither has told the
  Court or the Noonsworn.

## What she wants from the Tower

To climb with Orren to the Noon Seat and hang the sun there, whole, so the hours run true. And, if the Sun Warden still keeps his
post at the Tower's top, to ask him one thing: whether the Hymn of Noon was still being sung when the sun fell, and whose voice
was the last.

## Ties

- **[Vesper](vesper.md)**, sworn enemy: the dawn and the countess who would keep it from coming. Eleven duels, five to five, and
  one night on a road that neither will speak of.
- **[Orren](orren.md)**, comrade: she swore him to the Ember of Aubade, and walks in front of him wherever he carries it.
- **[Sefu](sefu.md)**, rival: who owns the sun. The sky, says the Paladin; my father, says the King. He told her she never met hers.
- **[Corbel](corbel.md)**, rival: her dawn turns him back toward stone, and he waits on the eaves for her to tire.
