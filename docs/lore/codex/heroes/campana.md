# Campana, the Bellwright

*The Noonsworn · [Aubade folk](../peoples/aubade.md) · from [Aubade](../realms/aubade.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

Campana was born in the low quarter of Aubade, twenty-two years before the Fall, in the foundry under the cliff where the city's bells are
cast. Her father was a bell-founder and her mother a ringer, and she was raised between the furnace and the tower, with bronze in her hair and
the sound of bells in her bones. By twelve she could tell a bell's pitch by the weight of its mould; by sixteen she could hear a crack in a bell
before it rang. She was a strong, short-tempered, laughing girl, and she meant to cast a bell that the Sun Warden would hear at the Tower's top.

Her first great bell was not for Aubade. The spring before the Fall, the harbour of Sallowmouth on the Coast ordered a new bell for the sea
temple's tower, to call the fishing boats home through the fog, and the old founder, her father, was ill. Campana cast it, at twenty-two, alone:
a bell of Aubade bronze with the harbour's arms on its waist and a voice that carried eleven miles out to sea. She went down to the Coast to hang
it, and heard it ring the boats home on its first evening, and came home certain she had found her life.

Three months later the sun fell, and the sea came in over Sallowmouth, and her bell went under with the tower. It is still there: on the Climb's
first floors the Bell-Warden rings it every morning, in a harbour the Tower holds as it was on the morning the water came. Campana did not know
that for ninety years. She knew only that her bell had drowned, and that in Aubade a hundred bell towers rang the thin sun up every morning and
it did not come up any stronger.

She grew into the city's best founder in the years after the Fall, Noon-touched, slower to age than her apprentices. She believed, and believes,
that the sun fell because the world went quiet. The Hymn, the bells, the songs of the cliffs: they were the Dial's voice to the sky, and the sky
had been held up by being spoken to. In the last years before the Fall, she says, Aubade sang the Hymn for pride, the Coast stopped whistling for
the wind, the Wood fell silent, and the sky had nothing left to listen to. She believes that enough bells rung together, at the right pitch, at the
Tower's top, would call the sun back.

In year 80 the Council of Bells let her prove it. She recast all hundred bells of Aubade, one a month for eight years, each tuned to answer the
others: strike one and the ring spreads to the next, which is how a whole city wakes at once. The morning the hundredth was hung, the whole city rang
together for the first time, and the sun rose, people swore, a little brighter. The Hourkeepers measured it and say it did not. Campana says they
measured the wrong thing.

She is the bell-founder of the Noonsworn, a strong woman in a heavy leather apron over temple plum, bronze to her elbows. Her bells answer each
other in the Ring as they do in Aubade, and her hammer can ring a bell from across the field. [Cantor](cantor.md) is her closest friend: her bells
keep his beat, and his Hymn tells her where to hang them. He is the only person who has told her about the hair behind the beat, and she the only
person who believes it does not matter. [Itzel](itzel.md), the Obsidian Herald of the Underforge, says Campana's bronze would crack in a real fire,
and has offered more than once to show her; Campana says black glass shatters if you so much as hum at it, and has offered to hum. They have fought
in the Ring four times, two each.

In the hundredth summer she is casting the bell she has dreamed of since she was a girl: a bell for the Tower's top, for the Sun Court, to be
carried up the Climb by the Noonsworn and rung at noon in the Noon Seat. It is the largest bell ever cast. It is not finished. She will not say how
they mean to carry it.

## People and appearance

Aubade folk of the low quarter, Noon-touched: twenty-two at the Fall, she looks forty-two. Strong and short for her people, with golden-brown skin,
dark hair bound back with gold thread, the dawn line on her brow, and forearms bronzed by the furnace. A heavy leather apron over temple plum, a hand
bell in the left hand and a bell-hammer in the right. Her look is not built yet; its brief is in the [revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Loud and warm, a foundry voice made for shouting over furnaces, with a laugh that rings. She talks about everything in terms of sound: a person is
"sharp" or "flat", a plan "rings true". She hums the pitch of whatever she is about to hit.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Listen. Now listen again."
- **Picked:** "Hear that? That's you, cracking."
- **Winning:** "Rung clear."
- **Winning:** "That's the note. Hold it."
- **Losing:** "Cracked. I'll recast."
- **Losing:** "Flat. Very flat. Again."
- **Meeting Cantor:** "Give me the beat, old friend."
- **Meeting Itzel:** "Shall I hum, Herald? Just a little?"

## Secrets

- She has been up the Climb to the first floor and heard her drowned bell ringing. She sat down on the quay and wept, and the Bell-Warden sat
  beside her and did not ask why.
- The great bell for the Noon Seat has a flaw in its waist that she cannot hear but knows is there. She has told no one.
- She once asked Mum, in writing, for the pitch of the Hymn's last note. Mum wrote back one line: a note, on a stave, with no name.

## What she wants from the Tower

To ring her great bell in the Noon Seat at noon, and call the sun back. And to bring her drowned bell home.

## Ties

- **[Cantor](cantor.md)**, friend: her bells keep his beat, and his Hymn tells her where to hang them.
- **[Itzel](itzel.md)**, rival: bronze against black glass, both born in fire; each says the other's craft would crack.
