# Pim and Pom, the Changeling Twins

*The Greenwood Moot · [Briarkin](../peoples/briar.md), the Acorn court · from [the Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

In the first winter after the Fall, the winter of the Long Dusk and the Winter of Mud, the snow came early to the Frostfall road, and one night
in the dark of the year someone left a woven satchel of birch bark on the doorstep of a stone cottage at the Walking Wood's north-eastern edge.
In it, wrapped in moss and acorn-silk, were two children, a boy and a girl, perhaps a year old, with pointed ears and green eyes, asleep, holding
hands. There was no note. There was an acorn cap on the boy's head and a blue leaf on the girl's.

The cottage was [Grandma Ironknit](grandma-ironknit.md)'s. She took them in, and that night, while she was warming milk, she turned round to find the
girl in the boy's place and the boy in the girl's, and the satchel empty, and both of them laughing. She turned back to the milk. When she turned
round again they had swapped back. She understood then what they were, and why they had been left: Briarkin children of the Acorn court, born two
for one, one life between them. Nothing like them had been born in the Underbough before the Fall. The courts were frightened, and left them on the
doorstep of the one Briarkin they knew who had chosen to live among mortals, and hoped.

Ironknit raised them anyway. She named them, since nobody else had: Pim for the boy and Pom for the girl, after the sound their feet made on her
floor, which has not changed in ninety-nine years. They grew up in the cottage on the Frostfall road, among her spindles and her cat, with the
Wood on one side and the road on the other, and every traveller on the road a new audience. They look ten and have for ninety-nine years: Briarkin
children age as they please, and Pim and Pom have never pleased to age.

They share one life between them. When one of them is hurt the other feels it; when one of them is out of breath the other breathes for both. They
swap places whenever anybody looks away, and sometimes when they do not; they tag in and out of a fight as if it were a game, because for them it is.
Pim is the one in the acorn-cap helm with the wooden sword, who runs in first and asks questions after; Pom is the one in the blue leaf hood with the
slingshot, who stays back and laughs, and is the cleverer, and lets Pim think he is. Which one is which at any moment is a question they consider
nobody else's business.

The Moot keeps them because nobody else can. They have no court to go back to: the Acorn court has never asked for them. They sit in the Moot when they
feel like it and speak for nobody in particular, and the Moot has learned to let them, because what they say, in between the jokes, is usually right.

They came to the Ring at twenty, by count, which in their eyes was ten, and were given their first card by the Arbiter within the hour for swapping places
mid-bout without leave. They hold the Ring's record now, thirty-one cards, every one for the same thing; [Referee Whistler](referee-whistler.md) has tried
to explain the rule to them for ninety-eight years, and they have listened very politely, together and then one at a time. [The Great Zambini](the-great-zambini.md)
adores them: they are his favourite audience and his worst assistants, and he has twice tried to saw one of them in half on stage only to find that he had
sawn the other, and neither of them in half.

## People and appearance

Briarkin of the Acorn court, born two for one. Small, quick and ten-looking, with long pointed ears, big green eyes of one colour, and nut-brown skin.
Pim: an acorn-cap helm with an amber leaf crest, an amber leaf-collared tunic, a wooden sword with a glowing edge. Pom: a blue leaf hood with a glowing
bead, a blue tunic, a slingshot with a glowing blue band and stone. In the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) their figures are unchanged: they already read as
their people.

## Voice

Two voices, high and quick, finishing each other's sentences, interrupting each other, and occasionally speaking in perfect unison, which is unsettling.
Pim is louder. Pom is funnier. Both laugh at everything.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Pim: Tag! Pom: You're it."
- **Picked:** "Pom: Which one's which? Pim: Guess!"
- **Winning:** "Pim: Got you! Pom: Got you twice."
- **Winning:** "Both: Again! Again!"
- **Losing:** "Pom: Your turn. Pim: No, yours."
- **Losing:** "Pim: That doesn't count. Pom: It counts a little."
- **Meeting Referee Whistler:** "Pim: Card? Pom: Card! Both: Thirty-two!"
- **Meeting The Great Zambini:** "Pom: Do the hat one. Pim: Do the sawing one!"

## Secrets

- They remember their mother. She was of the Acorn court, and she did not want to leave them; the court made her. They know her use-name. They have never
  told Ironknit, so as not to hurt her.
- If one of them dies, they do not know whether the other will. They have decided never to find out, and have a rule that one of them always runs away.
- There are forty pairs like them in the Underbough now. Pim and Pom know every one, and visit them, secretly, on the Moot's nights.

## What they want from the Tower

To play in it. It is the largest house on the Dial, and nobody has ever played hide and seek in it properly. Pom also wants to know why children like them
started being born, and thinks the answer is at the top. Pim wants to slide down the outside.
