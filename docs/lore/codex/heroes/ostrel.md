# Ostrel, the Ferryman

*A loner · [the Long Dead](../peoples/longdead.md) · from everywhere the dead are* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

The dead of the Dial go somewhere, and Ostrel takes them there. He is the ferryman of the dead: tall and gaunt, in a long fog-grey hooded coat lined in deep blue with coins sewn on
its hem, poling a long black boat along rivers of pale light that run wherever he lays them, across fields, through walls, down streets, to wherever it is the dead are going. He does
not say where that is. He says it is a good place, and that it is quiet.

He was a boatman of the Sefet in the Sands, four hundred years before the Fall, who poled the funeral barges of the river towns down to the Valley of Lids, carrying the dead to be
weighed. He was good at it; he had a gift for quiet, and the families of the dead asked for him by name. He drowned at fifty, in a flood, carrying a barge with a young girl's body on
it, and the barge went down with him. Three days later, on the riverbank, he got up. His Reason, the linen strip the Long Dead keep inside the ribs, says: "to finish the crossing".
He did not know which crossing. He went back to the river and took up his pole, and has been finishing crossings since.

He found, over the first century, that the river he poled was not only the Sefet. A boatman of the dead can lay a river wherever the dead are: a stream of pale light that runs from
where someone has died to where they are going, which only the dead and the Long Dead can see, and which he can pole a boat along. He began to carry the dead of the whole Dial: the
drowned of the Coast, the frozen of the north, the old in their beds, the soldiers on their fields. He took his coins from their families, one each, and sewed them on his coat, and
it is very heavy now.

Since the Fall the dead have been restless and the rivers crowded. The dead of the Dial get up more often than they did, without a will of their own, and wander, and Ostrel finds
them and takes them across, gently, and they lie down. He serves no faction, because every faction's dead ride with him. He brings the dead of the Sands to [Nebet](nebet.md)'s scale,
as he did when he was alive, and she weighs them true; they are comrades, the only two officers of the dead on the Dial who do not quarrel. [Marin](marin.md)'s tide carries his rivers
down to the sea, and she asks him nothing, and he is grateful. He brings the dead of Gloaming across when [Mortis](mortis.md) lets him, which is never.

Mortis is his oldest quarrel. The Ossuary Lord gathers the Restless and gives them work: walls, spires, soldiers. He thinks of it as a kindness. Ostrel, who is one of the Long Dead
himself and has carried the dead to rest for four hundred years, thinks of it as theft: the dead have somewhere to be, and Mortis keeps them from it. They have not spoken civilly in a
century. In the Ring, Ostrel lays his rivers across the floor and carries his enemies away on them, and Mortis's dead, when they meet, lie down.

The other Long Dead find him strange. Most of them got up in the ossuaries under Gloaming, where the Pact gives them halls; Ostrel got up
on a riverbank in the Sands, alone, and has never once been to Gloaming except to collect. He keeps the Long Dead's courtesies anyway, and
their honesty: he has never told a passenger a lie, not even a kind one.

The Deep's own ferryman, who brings the living back across the black water, is not him, and they do not speak. Ostrel says the living should cross on their own.

## People and appearance

One of the Long Dead, drowned four centuries ago, a Dune boatman by birth. Tall and gaunt: vellum skin drawn on bone, the jaw and knuckles showing, pale blue eyes of cold fire; a long
fog-grey hooded coat lined in deep blue, its hem heavy with coins; a long black pole. His card drew him as a gaunt man; in the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) his look brief makes him plainly
of the Long Dead.

## Voice

Low, slow and very gentle, the voice of someone who has said "it's all right" to a great many frightened people. He never hurries anyone. He calls the living "passengers" and the dead
"passengers" too.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Everyone crosses. The question is when."
- **Picked:** "Step aboard. Mind the water."
- **Winning:** "Across. Rest now."
- **Winning:** "Another crossing finished."
- **Losing:** "Not my crossing. Not yet."
- **Losing:** "The river waits. So do I."
- **Meeting Mortis:** "Let them go, old man. They have somewhere to be."
- **Meeting Nebet:** "Another one for the scale. Weigh her kindly."

## Secrets

- The girl whose body he was carrying when he drowned was never found. Her crossing is the one he has to finish. He has looked for her for four hundred years, and he thinks, now, that
  she is in the Tower.
- Since the Climb opened, the dead of the Climb's floors have not been crossing. The rivers he lays there run uphill.
- He knows where the dead go. It is quiet. It is not where anyone thinks.

## What he wants from the Tower

To know where the dead of the floors go, because they are not coming to him; and to find the girl from the river.

## Ties

- **[Marin](marin.md)**, friend: her tide carries his rivers down to the sea, and she asks him nothing.
- **[Nebet](nebet.md)**, comrade: he brings the dead of the Sands to her scale, and she weighs them true.
- **[Mortis](mortis.md)**, sworn enemy: the ferryman takes the dead across to rest; the Ossuary Lord keeps them back to work.
