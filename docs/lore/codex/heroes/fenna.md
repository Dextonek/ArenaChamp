# Fenna, the Grove Warden

*The Greenwood Moot · [Wood folk](../peoples/wood.md) · from [the Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Fenna was born in the village of Third Glade, deep in the Still Wood, fifteen years before the Fall, the daughter of a forester and a
beekeeper. She was a quiet, stubborn girl who talked to trees, as Wood children do, and unlike most Wood children she listened for an answer.
At twelve she planted an acorn in her mother's garden and sat by it every evening for a year; the village thought she was simple. At thirteen
the oak was taller than she was, which no oak should be in a year, and the village stopped thinking anything aloud.

She was fifteen and in the garden on the day of the Fall. She felt the Wood move before she saw it: a shudder through the ground, as of a great
animal getting to its feet. Then every tree in Third Glade leaned east, toward the Tower, and pulled. The roots came up out of the soil with a
sound like a sail tearing, and the forest began to walk. Third Glade's houses were trampled in an afternoon. Fenna stood in her mother's garden
with her hand on her own oak, which had not moved, and told the trees round it to stop, and they did, for a moment, because she asked.

That moment made her life. The Wood walked on, and has walked for a hundred years, east toward the Tower, at night mostly, as if it had felt the
sun go and set off to find it; and Fenna has spent those hundred years keeping it from trampling the Dial. She swore the Moot's oath at
sixteen, the youngest to swear it, and within the year she grew horns: not the antlers of bone that the Moot's hunters grow, but branches,
living wood, that put out leaves in spring and blossom in early summer and drop their leaves in the autumn like any tree. The Moot made her its
**Grove Warden**, and she has been it ever since.

She walks ahead of the Wood. Where a grove is about to walk over a village, she asks it to go round. Where a stand of pines has walked into a
river and is drowning, she leads it out. Where the Wood's walking has torn up a field, she plants flowers, because a flower grows faster than a
tree and holds the soil until the trees come back. She draws strength from standing among the things she has planted, and in the Ring she plants
flowers wherever she fights. She holds the gate of the Climb's Wood, where the forest is still in its first year of walking, and roots rise as
cover where she stands.

She argues with [Hank](hank.md), the Moot's woodcutter, about every tree he fells. He says a tree that walks into a village must be cut; she says it
must be asked to leave. He says he asks, with an axe. She calls him a butcher and he calls her a gardener; both are right, and they have shared a
fire every winter for twenty years, at a clearing on the Wood's edge where his hut and her garden happen to meet. She has never once won the
argument, or lost it.

And she has an enemy. In the seventy-first summer, on the Brass Road where it skirts the Wood's south-eastern edge, [Vitriol](vitriol.md) of the Dune
Throne tested a new fume on a garden by the road: Fenna's, a mile of wildflowers she had planted to hold the soil where the Wood had walked. Every plant
died in an hour. He called it a successful test, and wrote to the Moot to thank it for the use of the garden. She has not forgotten. They have fought in
the Ring six times, and she has planted a flower on every spot where he fell.

## People and appearance

Wood folk, sworn to the Moot at sixteen, and Noon-touched: fifteen at the Fall, she looks thirty-five. A cloak of layered leaves, a pale bark collar,
ruddy skin and auburn hair, and branches like antlers growing from her head, living wood, in leaf; a living staff that blooms in her left hand. In
the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) her figure is unchanged: she already wears her people's horns, the most unusual of them.

## Voice

Soft, unhurried and certain, the voice of someone used to waiting for things to grow. She speaks to people and plants in the same tone, and expects
the same obedience from both. She is never sarcastic, which makes her rare rebukes land hard.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Stand still. Let something grow."
- **Picked:** "Roots first. Then we talk."
- **Winning:** "There. Now something can grow here."
- **Winning:** "Rest. The flowers will keep you company."
- **Losing:** "Cut back. Not cut down."
- **Losing:** "Spring comes. Always."
- **Meeting Hank:** "Put the axe down. Just this once."
- **Meeting Vitriol:** "You owe me a garden, alchemist."

## Secrets

- Her horns are getting longer each year, and the roots of them, she can feel, go deeper. She thinks she is slowly becoming a tree, and that this is
  what the Moot's oath means. She does not mind as much as she should.
- The oak she planted at twelve walked away with the Wood on the day of the Fall. It is the Elder Root in the Climb's Wood. She has stood in front of it
  and known it, and it knew her.
- She keeps the root Hank cut from the Tower's wall in a pot in her garden. It has grown a leaf.

## What she wants from the Tower

The Tower's roots cut, and the Wood allowed to stop walking. She believes the Wood walks because the Tower is pulling it. And she wants her oak back.

## Ties

- **[Hank](hank.md)**, rival: she grows them, he fells them. They argue every day and share a fire every winter.
- **[Vitriol](vitriol.md)**, sworn enemy: his fumes killed a whole garden of hers, and he called it a successful test.
