# Hank, the Moss Hewer

*The Greenwood Moot · [Wood folk](../peoples/wood.md) · from [the Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Hank was born at the edge of the Still Wood twenty-two years before the Fall, in a woodcutters' village called Holloway that no longer exists, the
fourth son of a hewer. He was big from the start and quiet from the start, and he learned the axe at eight, the saw at ten, and the rule of the
Wood folk woodcutters at the same time: a felled tree must be paid back with a planted one. He has kept it every day of his life. By twenty he was the
best hewer on the Wood's edge, with a braided beard already halfway down his chest, and he had planted, by his own count, two thousand trees.

He was twenty-two and felling a beech on the day of the Fall. The beech, half cut through, pulled up its roots and walked away from him, with his axe
still in it. Then the whole Wood walked. Holloway was in its path. Hank stood in the middle of the village with a borrowed axe and asked the trees,
one after another, to leave: he cut the ones that would not. He saved the village that day, and lost it that winter, when the Wood came back the other
way in the night. His father died under an oak. Hank buried him and planted the oak's acorn on the grave.

Holloway's survivors built a new village on skids, to follow the Wood, and named it Hankshold, to his great embarrassment; it stands now at the Wood's
eastern edge, wherever that is this year, with the other edge villages. They have no champion of their own. Hank is their champion. When the Walking Wood
walks into a village, someone has to ask it to leave, and Hank asks with an axe.

He swore the Moot's oath in the first year after the Fall, because he thought the villages should have a voice in it, and within the year he grew horns:
broad branching antlers of bone, like a stag's. He found them inconvenient. They caught in branches and doorways and made him three feet taller than any
roof in Hankshold. The next spring, when the sworn shed their antlers and plant the tips, he did not wait for them to fall; he sawed them off, flat, at
every tip, and planted the tips. He has done it every spring since. They grow back broader every year. The Moot says it is the only thing he plants
willingly, which is unfair, and also the only joke the Moot has ever made.

He is the Moot's woodcutter: huge and quiet, in a moss-green tunic and a fur mantle, with a braided beard and a great one-bit axe whose edge glows green
from a working the Briarkin put on it in exchange for a winter's firewood. He grows trees too, fast, because a felled tree must be paid back: in the Ring
he plants pines that are cover while they stand and fall where he chops them. [Fenna](fenna.md) the Grove Warden calls him a butcher and he calls her a
gardener; they have argued about every tree he has felled for twenty years, and shared a fire every winter for the same twenty, at the clearing where his
hut and her garden meet. He hunts with [Arden](arden.md), the Huntmaster, who tracks what lives under the trees while Hank deals with the trees, and drinks
with [Bo](bo.md), the Ramherd, with whom he has shared a fire, a flock and a great many silences.

In year 47 the Underforge's timber buyers and the Moot came close to war over a stretch of the Wood's edge on the old Harrow road, and the quarrel was
settled in the Ring: Hank against an Underforge champion. Hank won, and the edge was the Wood's. The Underforge still buys the timber; it pays Hank's price
now, which includes a planted tree for every felled one, planted by the buyer.

## People and appearance

Wood folk of the Wood's edge, sworn to the Moot, and Noon-touched: twenty-two at the Fall, he looks forty-two. Huge and quiet, with a braided brown beard,
a leather brow band, a moss-green tunic under a fur mantle, and the one-bit axe held low with its edge aglow green. Today he has no horns. In the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md) he is a revamp: broad branching antlers, sawn flat at every tip, ruddy freckled skin and moss-green eyes, so that he becomes
the Horned, and stands apart from every other workman in the Ring.

## Voice

Deep, slow and few-worded. He talks to trees more than to people and says "mind your heads" more than anything else. When he is angry he goes quiet; when
he is very angry he goes back to work.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Timber. Mind your heads."
- **Picked:** "One to fell, one to plant."
- **Winning:** "Down. I'll plant one for you."
- **Winning:** "Clean cut."
- **Losing:** "Bark's thicker than you'd think."
- **Losing:** "Saw me off, did you? It grows back."
- **Meeting Fenna:** "I'll plant two. Happy?"
- **Meeting Bo:** "Fire tonight? Bring the cider."

## Secrets

- He has been up the Climb to the Wood's eleventh floor and cut a piece of a root that went up into the Tower's wall. It bled sap that glowed. He gave it to
  Fenna and did not tell her he had cut his hand on it, and that the cut has not healed.
- The acorn he planted on his father's grave grew into an oak that the Wood took when it walked. He has looked for it for a hundred years.
- He can read, and writes poems about trees, which nobody has seen.

## What he wants from the Tower

For the villages at the Wood's edge to stop being walked over. If the Tower is why the Wood walks, he would like to have a word with it, with the axe.

## Ties

- **[Fenna](fenna.md)**, rival: she grows them, he fells them. They argue every day and share a fire every winter.
- **[Arden](arden.md)**, friend: the Wood's two hunters, one of trees, one of what lives under them.
- **[Bo](bo.md)**, friend: they have shared a fire, a flock and a great many silences.
