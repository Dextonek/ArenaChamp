# Itzel, the Obsidian Herald

*The Underforge · [Kilnborn](../peoples/kiln.md) · from [the Deep](../realms/deep.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

Where the fire of the fallen sun rises through the Ember Caldera, it cools at the crater's edges into black glass: obsidian, sharp as nothing else on the
Dial, that rings when struck and holds an edge for a hundred years. The Kilnborn who work it are the Caldera's glass-cutters, a family trade apart from the
smiths, older than the Fall in the hill country's flint-knappers and transformed by it. Itzel was born to them, in the fifty-fifth summer after the Fall, in a
glass-cutter's house on the Caldera's rim, where the fire lights the windows red all night and the floor is warm in winter.

She was a lean, intense, silent child who could sit for hours on the black glass shelf above the fire, watching it move. The fire under the Caldera moves: slow
currents under a crust, cracks that open and close, a breathing that the Kilnborn say is the fallen sun's. At ten she told her mother that the fire was talking,
and her mother, who had heard such things before from Caldera children, told her to listen carefully and say nothing. At fifteen Itzel walked out onto the crust,
which nobody does, and stood on it for an hour, barefoot, while the glass-cutters shouted from the rim, and walked back with her feet unburned and a word she would
not repeat.

The Underforge made her its **Herald** at twenty, the voice of the Caldera's fire in the Hall of Marks, an office the Hall created for her because nobody knew what
else to do with a girl who could walk on the sun. She speaks for the fire. When the forge should be fed, or rested, or when a seam of the Deep is about to crack
from the heat below, it is Itzel who says so, and the Hall listens, because she has never been wrong. She cuts the black glass the miners dig from the Caldera's
cooled edges: [Mabel](mabel.md) of the mole-folk digs it out, Itzel cuts it, and the two of them run the trade in black glass that pays for half the Underforge's iron.

She is a lean warrior in black lacquered armour inlaid with turquoise, under a mantle of jade and white feathers that the Caldera's glass-cutters wear at their
festivals, with a broad blade of obsidian in her right hand that she cut from the crust she stood on at fifteen. Her strikes split the ground in straight lines:
a chasm to her enemies, a road to her, as the fire under the Dial has always been. She fights in the Ring to carry the Underforge's word, and her word is sharp.

She has one public quarrel. [Campana](campana.md), the bell-founder of the Noonsworn, casts bronze in a furnace fed with Caldera charcoal, and Itzel thinks her bronze
would crack in a real fire, and has offered more than once to show her. Campana says black glass shatters if you so much as hum at it, and has offered to hum. Bronze
against black glass, both born in fire: they have fought in the Ring four times, two each, and Itzel says the next will settle it. She is lying; she has started
looking forward to them.

The Caldera is burning lower. Itzel knows it better than anyone, because the fire tells her. It says it is tired. It says it is waiting. She has not told the Hall
of Marks what for.

The glass-cutters of the Caldera keep a festival of their own at the Kindling, older than the Underforge: they dance on the black glass
shelf in mantles of jade and white feathers, traded up the Brass Road from the Sands' oases a century ago, to thank the fire for the glass.
Itzel leads it now. She dances barefoot, as she walked on the crust, and the glass under her feet glows where she steps. The Kilnborn
children of the rim follow her across the shelf and try to step where she stepped, and the older glass-cutters watch the fire, and say that
it watches her back.

## People and appearance

Kilnborn of the Caldera's glass-cutters, born after the Fall: forty-five, lean and intense, with ash-grey skin and ember seams at the throat that glow when she is
angry, and pale amber eyes; black lacquered armour inlaid with turquoise, a mantle of jade and white feathers, a broad obsidian blade. Her look is not built yet; its
brief, which gives her the Kilnborn's skin, is in the [revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Low, flat and formal, the voice of a herald: she pronounces rather than speaks. She refers to herself and the fire together as "we". She rarely jokes; when she does,
nobody is sure.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "The ground has an opinion. Let me show you."
- **Picked:** "The fire speaks. I carry its word."
- **Winning:** "Split. As spoken."
- **Winning:** "The road is open. You are not on it."
- **Losing:** "Cooled. Not quenched."
- **Losing:** "Black glass breaks. It also cuts."
- **Meeting Campana:** "Bronze, bell-woman? In a real fire?"
- **Meeting Mabel:** "Dig deep, sister. I'll cut clean."

## Secrets

- The word the fire gave her on the crust at fifteen was a name. She believes it is the sun's own. She has never said it aloud, because she thinks it would wake
  something.
- The fire is waiting for someone to come down the Underforge's drill shaft. It has told her who. It is Orren.
- She hums when she is alone. Campana must never know.

## What she wants from the Tower

To carry the fire's word to the Heart of Noon, which she believes is where the Caldera's fire is speaking from, and to hear what the Heart answers.

## Ties

- **[Campana](campana.md)**, rival: bronze against black glass, both born in fire; each says the other's craft would crack.
- **[Mabel](mabel.md)**, comrade: Mabel digs the black glass out of the Caldera, and Itzel cuts it.
