# Mortis, the Ossuary Lord

*The Court of Dusk · [the Long Dead](../peoples/longdead.md) · from [Gloaming](../realms/gloaming.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Three hundred years before the Fall, in a tall narrow house in the scholars' ring of Gloaming, a man called Aurel Vane sat up late over a
question. He was a scholar of the dead: he had spent forty years in the ossuaries under the city with a lamp and a notebook, among the halls
of bone the Ossuary Pact had sealed a century and a half before, asking the Long Dead why they had got up and the others had not. He had filled
sixty notebooks. On the night in question he had found, he believed, the answer, and he dipped his pen to write it down, and his heart stopped.

Three days later, in the family crypt under the house, he got up. His Reason, the linen strip the Long Dead keep inside the ribs, says only:
"to finish the sentence." He has never told anyone what the sentence was. He gave up his living name, which is cut on a stone in the family crypt
that he does not visit, and took an ossuary name, Mortis, and went down into the halls of bone to work.

He found that he was very good at it. Every dead thing that falls near him leaves its bones, and every bone, he discovered, is his to raise: not
as a person, as the Long Dead rise, but as a thing, a wall, a spire, a soldier, a place to stand. Within a century the Long Dead of the ossuaries had
made him their lord, because he understood them better than they understood themselves, and because he was the only one of them who could make the
Restless, the dead who get up without a will, do anything useful. Under Gloaming lie the ossuaries, halls of bone older than the Court of Dusk, stacked
skull on skull and lit by teal soulfire, and Mortis is their lord.

Since the Fall the dead of the Dial have been restless, and Mortis offers them work. The drowned of the Coast, the soldiers of the Banners, the hungry
of the Long Dusk: the dead get up more often now, without a will of their own, and wander, and frighten the living. Mortis gathers them. He gives them
walls to stand in, spires to hold up, ranks to march in, a place, which is more than the living gave most of them. He thinks of it as a kindness.
[Ostrel](ostrel.md), the ferryman of the dead, one of the Long Dead himself, who takes the dead across to rest, thinks of it as theft, and the two have
not spoken civilly in a century. [Morwen](morwen.md), the Truce's Saltwarden, lays her salt so that his skeletons cannot reach the Ring's crowd, and he
has never forgiven her for making his servants look foolish in front of forty thousand people.

He allied himself with the Court of Dusk when Vesper founded it, by the terms of the old Ossuary Pact, as the Lord Below: an ally, not a subject. The Court
buries in the Wood's soil the dead it cannot fit under Gloaming, which the Greenwood Moot hates, and Mortis raises them anyway, which the Moot hates more.

There is one body that will not stay down. In year 40, after a duel on the Field of Banners, Mortis buried the ashes of [Anka](anka.md), the Firebird,
in a lead box under the ossuaries, because the dead should stay where they are put and an Ember that rises again offends everything he is. She rose in
the box, melted it, and walked out through the Night Temple in the middle of the Naming, leaving burning footprints on the floor. He buried her again in
year 66, in iron, under the black water of the Deep, and in year 83, in salt, which nearly worked. She has never stayed. He fights in the Ring for more
ground, and to bury her a fourth time. He has a box ready. It is very beautiful.

He is not cruel, and he would be hurt to be called so. He is patient, scholarly, dry and courteous, and he thinks the living make far too much fuss about
the end of things. "Rest," he says, "is for those who have somewhere to be."

## People and appearance

One of the Long Dead, three hundred years from his death and long past vellum: a skull face with teal soulfire in the sockets under a dark hood with teal
wisps, a long robe in grey-green with carved rib arcs over the chest, and a skull-topped staff in the left hand. In the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) his
figure is unchanged: he already reads as his people.

## Voice

Dry, slow and scholarly, with a faint echo, as if he were speaking from the bottom of a stair. He lectures. He corrects your grammar while he kills you.

## Secrets

- The sentence he died finishing was the answer to why the dead get up. He has never written it, because if he finishes it his Reason ends and he lies down.
  Some nights he is tempted.
- He envies Anka. She rises without a Reason. He has studied her ashes, all three times, and found nothing in them but ash.
- The Restless have got up more often every year since the Fall, and he has measured it: the curve matches the Hourkeepers' paling of the light exactly.

## What he wants from the Tower

More ground, and more dead to give it to. And, at the top, the Noon Seat kept empty, as the Pact requires; but if there is a body up there, he would like
to bury it.

## Ties

- **[Anka](anka.md)**, sworn enemy: he keeps the dead in the ground; she will not stay there. He has buried her three times.
- **[Morwen](morwen.md)**, sworn enemy: salt against the risen dead; her lines are why his skeletons cannot reach the Ring's crowd.
- **[Ostrel](ostrel.md)**, sworn enemy: the ferryman takes the dead across to rest; the Ossuary Lord keeps them back to work.
