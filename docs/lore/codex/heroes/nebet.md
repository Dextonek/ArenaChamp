# Nebet, the Heartweigher

*The Dune Throne · [Dune folk](../peoples/dune.md) · from [the Sands](../realms/sands.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

When a king of the Sands dies, his heart is drawn by the embalmer and carried to the Hall of Weighing in the Valley of Lids, and set in the pan of a great scale of gold,
and weighed against a single feather. If the heart is lighter, the king is buried with honour and will rise with the Father; if it is heavier, the king is buried
anyway, because he is a king, and the Weigher says nothing, and remembers. The Weigher is the Throne's judge of the dead, one of the Five Offices older than any king, and
for the last sixty years the Weigher has been Nebet.

She was born in Tamerit in the thirty-eighth summer after the Fall, the daughter of a scribe of the Throne Hall, and raised among the court's records: lists of kings and
their hearts' weights, going back six centuries, which she read as other girls read stories. She was a tall, still, serious child who liked things to balance. At twelve she
corrected the Hall's account of a royal grandmother's heart by a grain, and the old Weigher, who was ninety and blind, sent for her. She was his apprentice for twenty years,
and at thirty-two the Weigher. She is the youngest who has ever held the scale.

She is tall and still, in white linen with a lapis headcloth and a broad gold collar, with the Weigher's scale of two gold pans in her left hand and a hooked bronze blade in her
right. The feather in her pan, the Sands say, is one of Anka's, taken from the surf of the Drowned Coast on the morning after the Fall by a Dune trader who sold it to the old
Weigher for its weight in gold. It is the lightest thing on the Dial, and every royal heart since has been weighed against it. Nebet would like a fresh one. A feather that old
has dried, and she suspects it has grown lighter, and that the Sands' kings have been found heavier than they were. She has asked [Anka](anka.md), in public, for a fresh one,
and Anka has told her to reach.

[Ostrel](ostrel.md), the ferryman of the dead, brings the dead of the Sands to her scale: when the Restless of the Sands get up, as they do more often since the Fall, he poles
them across his river to the Valley of Lids, and Nebet weighs them, and they lie down. They are comrades, the only two officers of the dead on the Dial who do not quarrel; he
brings them, and she weighs them true. And she weighs the dead for [Sefu](sefu.md)'s Throne, and once, at his order, weighed the living: in the ninety-second summer, a noble of
the river towns accused of plotting against the crown was brought to the Hall of Weighing alive, and Nebet set his living heart, in his living chest, against the feather, by a
working no Weigher had used in four hundred years. It was heavier. Sefu had him executed. Nebet has never told anyone how it felt.

She keeps the Hall of Weighing as the old Weigher kept it: a long cool hall cut into the Valley's cliff, lit by a single shaft of the thin
day that falls through a hole in the rock onto the scale at noon. She sleeps in a cell beside it. Every morning she cleans the pans with
sand from the Glass Flats and checks the beam against a set of weights cast in the first king's reign, and every evening she writes the
day's weighings in the Valley's book, in the same hand as her predecessors, so that six hundred years of hearts read as one long list.

In the Ring she weighs the living, and the heavier one pays. A scale hangs over her fights, tipping toward whoever has more health left, and her blows grow with how far it tips
against her. She likes to be behind.

## People and appearance

Dune folk of the Throne Hall's scribes, born after the Fall: sixty-two, tall and still, dark-skinned, with kohl to the temples and gold on the lids; white linen, a lapis headcloth,
a broad gold collar, the gold scale in the left hand and a hooked bronze blade in the right, a white feather at the shoulder. Her look is not built yet; its brief is in the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Quiet, precise and grave, a judge's voice; she speaks of weight and balance, and never of guilt. She pauses before every sentence, as if setting it in the pan.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Let us see which of us is heavier."
- **Picked:** "The scale is waiting."
- **Winning:** "Weighed. Found wanting."
- **Winning:** "The scale has spoken. I only listen."
- **Losing:** "Lighter than I thought. Interesting."
- **Losing:** "The pans will settle. They always settle."
- **Meeting Anka:** "A fresh feather, bird. Just one."
- **Meeting Sefu:** "Majesty. Your heart is heavy today."

## Secrets

- The king whose heart was heavier than the feather and was buried anyway, in the Valley's oldest record, was Sefu's and Asenet's father. Nebet found it at twelve.
- The noble she weighed alive was innocent. The scale was right: his heart was heavy, with grief, not treason. She knew, and said only "heavier", as the office requires.
- She has weighed her own heart, once, in secret. She will not say which way it tipped.

## What she wants from the Tower

To weigh the Heart of Noon. The Father's heart, against the feather. If it is heavier, she does not know what the Sands will do.

## Ties

- **[Anka](anka.md)**, rival: Nebet weighs hearts against one of Anka's feathers, and would like a fresh one from the bird herself.
- **[Sefu](sefu.md)**, comrade: she weighs the dead for his Throne, and once weighed the living for him.
- **[Ostrel](ostrel.md)**, comrade: he brings the dead of the Sands to her scale, and she weighs them true.
