# Morwen, the Saltwarden

*The Truce of the Ring · [Coast folk](../peoples/coast.md) · from [the Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

Morwen was born in Tern's Landing, a fishing town on the Drowned Coast south of the old port, in the thirty-ninth summer after the Fall,
in a stone cottage at the end of a spit of shingle that her grandmother had built and the sea, everyone said, would one day take. Her
grandmother was a hedge witch of the old Coast kind: she sold charms against drowning, laid out the dead, cured warts, and knew that a line
of salt and a nail of cold iron keep out most of what walks at night. Morwen learned all of it at her knee, and learned early that it was
true.

The Coast after the Fall was a frightening place to be a child. The sea stood higher than it ever had, the storms came when they liked,
and the drowned of Sallowmouth did not always stay drowned: on certain nights at the lowest tides, shapes came up the shingle from the
direction of the old port, walking the way men walk who are trying to get home from work. Her grandmother poured salt across the doorstep
on those nights and hung an iron nail over the lintel, and the shapes stood at the line and did not cross it, and in the morning they were
gone. Morwen watched them through the shutters. She was not afraid of them. She was angry that nobody else did anything.

When she was twenty the sea took the cottage, as everyone had said it would, in a spring tide that came a day early. Her grandmother was
inside. Morwen was on the shingle with a pouch of salt, laying a line round the boats. She laid it round the cottage instead, too late, and
the sea went over it as if it were not there. She learned that day what salt cannot stop. She has never forgiven the sea, and she has never
stopped carrying salt.

She spent the next twenty years walking the coast as a hedge witch for hire: laying out the drowned, warding fishing boats, salting the
doorsteps of harbour widows, and, increasingly, being asked to deal with the restless dead of the old port. She was good at it. She did not
kill them; she could not. She drew lines they would not cross and herded them back to the water, gently, as her grandmother had. Word went up
the coast.

In the ninety-first summer, at the Ring's midsummer opening, a stray bolt of lightning from [Kaia](kaia.md)'s first duel in the Ring
went wide and emptied the western stands. Nobody died, but several hundred people lost their eyebrows, and the Truce decided the Ring
needed an edge that no working could cross. Somebody from the Coast remembered the hedge witch. The Truce hired her.

Every dawn since, before the Ring opens, Morwen walks its edge with a pouch of salt and pours a line that no spell may cross, so that the
crowd goes home with its eyebrows. It is called the **Salt Line**, and she is the Saltwarden, one of the Truce's officers. She does not much
like champions, who are loud and careless and think every line is a challenge. She likes boundaries, and people who keep to them, and she
steps into the Ring herself to teach that to the ones who do not.

Her two great quarrels are with things that do not keep to lines. [Mortis](mortis.md), the Ossuary Lord, raises skeletons wherever he
fights, and before she came his dead now and then climbed into the stands; Morwen's salt is why they cannot reach the crowd now,
and Mortis has never forgiven her for making his servants look foolish. And [Kohaku](kohaku.md), the fox spirit of the Fox court, for whom
a line of salt is the one fence she cannot cross, has tried every night for nine years to find a gap in the Salt Line, to prove she can.
She has not. Morwen checks the line twice on the nights after Kohaku fights, and finds paw prints along it, right up to the edge.

## People and appearance

Coast folk of Tern's Landing: lean and weathered, sea-tanned, with salt-bleached hair going grey at the temples, worn in a knot; tide
lines tattooed round both forearms to the elbow, eleven of them. A grey-green shawl over a black dress, a pouch of salt at the hip and an
iron nail on a cord round her neck; a short staff of driftwood in the left hand. Her look is not built yet; its brief is in the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Low, dry and unhurried, with the Coast's flat vowels. She speaks to champions the way she speaks to the restless dead: firmly, patiently,
as to someone who does not yet understand the rules. She never shouts. She does not need to.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Step over. I'll wait. The salt will wait longer."
- **Picked:** "Mind the line. It minds you."
- **Winning:** "Stay on your side."
- **Winning:** "There. Back where you belong."
- **Losing:** "The salt's still there. Remember that."
- **Losing:** "Everything crosses eventually. Not today, though."
- **Meeting Mortis:** "Keep your bones on that side, old man."
- **Meeting Kohaku:** "Still looking for the gap? There isn't one."

## Secrets

- The shapes that came up the shingle when she was a child were the drowned of Sallowmouth trying to get home. One of them was her
  grandfather. Her grandmother knew, and salted the door anyway.
- She is not sure the Salt Line is what stops workings at the Ring's edge. She thinks it might be the Ring itself, and that the salt only
  shows where. She has not told the Truce.
- She has been up the Climb to the Sea's chapter, and walked the drowned harbour at the tide, and recognised the cottage.

## What she wants from the Tower

To lay a line round it. Not to keep anyone out: to see which side the Tower is on.

## Ties

- **[Mortis](mortis.md)**, sworn enemy: salt against the risen dead; her lines are why his skeletons cannot reach the Ring's crowd.
- **[Kohaku](kohaku.md)**, rival: a line of salt is the one fence a fox spirit cannot cross, and Kohaku has tried every night.
