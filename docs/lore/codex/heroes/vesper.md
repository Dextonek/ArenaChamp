# Vesper, the Crimson Countess

*The Court of Dusk · [Gloaming folk](../peoples/gloaming.md) · from [Gloaming](../realms/gloaming.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Vesper was Countess of the Gloaming heights before the Fall, the head of the Countess's house, the oldest of Gloaming's noble
families and the one that held the night roads. She was thirty-five, widowed young, clever, and pale even for Gloaming; she
liked the evening because her vineyards were most beautiful in it, and the morning because she could sleep through it. She was
not a monster. Her tenants say she was a fair landlady and a terrible card player.

On the day of the Fall the light left her lands first. Her castle stands on the westernmost height of Gloaming, the eleventh hour
of the Dial, and when the sun stopped over the Tower and cracked, the shadow of the Tower lay across her vines and stayed there.
For a year there was no full day in Gloaming, only a long blue dusk; crops failed, the old and the sick died, and the Gloaming
folk changed, their eyes beginning to shine back the lamplight. Vesper changed more than anyone. She stopped needing the sun.
Then she stopped needing bread. Then, in the hungry winter of the first year, she found what she did need, and it was blood.

She has never told the whole story of that winter, and the Court does not ask. What is known is that the Countess's house came
out of it with its tenants alive, its roads open and its lady not a day older than she had been at the Fall, and that she has not
aged since. She is not Noon-touched; she simply stopped. The blood keeps her where she was, and she pays for every spell in her
own and drinks it back from her enemies. She learned early to go low, close to empty, before she feeds. It is the only way, she
says, to be sure you are still the one deciding.

When the realms fought the War of the Banners she kept Gloaming out of it, and when the Truce was sworn she was the first of the
northern nobles to sign, because she understood before anyone what the Truce was for: a way for a weak realm to survive strong
ones. Out of the long dusk she built the **Court of Dusk**, the nobility of Gloaming bound together by one belief, that the dusk is
the Dial's true age and the night has as good a claim as the day. She leads it, and she is its sharpest mind and its best fighter.
Its long nights are bought on credit: the first harvests after the Fall were bought from the Truce's Tally-house, and
[Mr. Ledger](mr-ledger.md) holds the note. The interest grows. The Court has never missed a payment, and has never once paid down
the principal.

The Noonsworn are her enemy because a new sun would burn her where she stands, and every one of her people with her. She does not
want the Dial dark for its own sake; she wants it to stay as it is, with its long evenings and its quiet roads and its dead at
rest under Gloaming, and she will do a great deal to keep the Noon Seat empty. She keeps the letter of the Truce and very little of
its spirit, and the Arbiter watches her champions twice.

Her hardest enemy is [Auriel](auriel.md), the paladin born on the first morning, who came to Gloaming in the eighty-sixth summer
demanding a champion for two sleepers on the night road. Vesper came herself, because she thought she might lose and wanted to
know what it felt like. She did lose. Eleven duels later they are even, and Vesper has said, privately, that she does not know
what she will do with her evenings if one of them wins for good. [Orren](orren.md), who carries the Ember of Aubade on his back,
is the other: the Ember's light burns her to come near, and she has sworn to put it out. And everywhere she goes,
[Nyx](nyx.md) stands in her shadow, the Court's knife, who walked off the wall of the Night Temple on the day of the Fall and
chose Vesper as the first person worth guarding.

Now, in the hundredth summer, the Tower's gate is open, and the Court must climb too: an empty Noon Seat is not empty for long.
Vesper has gone up as high as the Wood's crown, and come back with a look on her face that Silvane, who reads faces, did not like.

## People and appearance

Gloaming folk, and the clearest case of what the long dusk can make of them. She looks thirty-five: tall and narrow, skin white
with the blue-grey cast of her people, dark lips, black hair swept up under a small crown of red points. Her eyes shine crimson-violet
in the dark. She wears a crimson gown over black with a high collar of blood-red blades, a gold edge, and a scepter with a blood
drop in the left hand. Touched on 2026-11-16 by the [revamp plan](../revamp.md): the eyeshine and the blue-grey skin, so that she reads as
Gloaming folk and not only as pale.

## Voice

Low, warm and amused, with the manners of a hostess who has outlived all her guests. She never hurries a sentence and never wastes
one. When she is angry her voice gets softer. Her laugh is real, and rare, and nobody in the Court likes to hear it in a duel.

## Secrets

- The first person she fed on, in the hungry winter, was her own steward, who offered. He lived, and is still alive at
  Lantern-under-Hill, a very old man who will not hear a word against her.
- She has worked out that if the sun is hung again she will not burn at once, but age the hundred years she skipped in a single
  morning. She would rather burn.
- The night on the road with Auriel was her idea.

## What she wants from the Tower

The Noon Seat kept empty, and to see with her own eyes what is in it before anyone else does. If the Tower can be made to hold the
hour at dusk, she wants that too, though she has not said so to the Hourkeepers.
