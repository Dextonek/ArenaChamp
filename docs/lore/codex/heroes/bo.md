# Bo, the Ramherd

*The Greenwood Moot · [Wood folk](../peoples/wood.md), ram-horned · from [the Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Bo was born in a shepherds' summer town in the high pastures above the Wood, forty years before the Fall, and has herded sheep for as long as he can
remember. The high pastures are where the forest gives way to the hills, open grass and limestone and wind, with the Wood a dark line below and the
Tower a grey needle on the eastern horizon; the hill families drive their flocks up in spring and down in autumn, and keep to themselves, and are
reckoned slow by the valley folk because they think before they speak. Bo thinks longer than most.

He was forty and his beard was just going silver on the day of the Fall. He was on the high ridge with three hundred ewes when the sun stopped over the
Tower, and he watched it fall from the best seat on the Dial. He says he does not remember being afraid. He remembers the sheep all turning, at the
same moment, to face the Tower, and standing quite still, and then all turning back and going on grazing. He decided then that the sheep knew something,
and he has watched them more carefully ever since.

The Wood began to walk below him, and the hill families found that the forest's edge came up the slopes in places, and went down in others, so that a
pasture might be a wood next summer and a wood a pasture. The flocks followed the grass. Bo followed the flocks, and learned to read where the Wood would
walk next by where the sheep would not go. When the Moot gathered after the War of the Banners to decide what the Wood folk would do about the walking,
the hill families sent Bo to speak for them, because nobody else would sit still that long. He swore the Moot's oath the same year. In the hills the oath
grows ram horns, and his came in a great curl from each temple within the year; he wears a bronze headband between them that his wife made him, and has
worn it since she died.

He is a herdsman-druid of the old hill kind: he knows the weather by the fleece, the season by the lambs and the Wood's mood by the sheep, and the hill
workings, which are slow and patient and mostly to do with getting large animals to go where you want. He carries a crook hung with a bell that his sheep
will follow anywhere. Really they follow whoever is nearest, which he will tell you is a lesson about leaders. He sits on the Moot because somebody has to
speak for the hills, and he speaks slowly, and the Moot has learned that when Bo starts a sentence it should wait for the end of it.

His friends are the Wood's other quiet men. [Hank](hank.md), the woodcutter, with whom he has shared a fire, a flock (the year Hankshold's sheep were
caught in a walking grove and Bo brought them out) and a great many silences. And [Barry Cloudsley](barry-cloudsley.md), the Windward's augur on the
Spires, whose forecasts Bo trusts, which makes one of them: shepherds watch the sky, and Bo has compared Barry's forecasts to his fleece for thirty years and
says Barry is right more often than the sheep, which is high praise. They write to each other every month. Barry sends forecasts; Bo sends wool.

He fights in the Ring when a quarrel reaches his pastures, and sends the flock in first. The flock is real, mostly, and the rams are very real, and very
large, and the Briarkin worked something into the bell long ago that nobody fully understands.

## People and appearance

Wood folk of the high pastures, sworn to the Moot and ram-horned, and Noon-touched: forty at the Fall, he looks sixty. A tidy silver beard under a calm
weathered face with level grey brows and steady eyes, grey hair under a bronze headband, a great ram horn curling from each temple, a deep umber wool cloak
with a grey fleece mantle, and the crook in the left hand with its glowing warm bell. In the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) his figure is unchanged: his horns
are his people's.

## Voice

Slow, warm and deep, with the hill accent and long pauses in the middle of sentences. He addresses everyone as if they were a nervous sheep, kindly. He has
never been heard to hurry.

## Voice lines

- **Losing:** "The hill's still there. So's the flock."
- **Meeting Hank:** "Bring the cider. I've brought the silence."
- **Meeting Barry Cloudsley:** "What's the sky say, Barry? The fleece says rain."

## Secrets

- The sheep turned to face the Tower on the day of the Fall, and they do it again, all of them, every year at noon on midsummer, and stand still for the
  space of nine heartbeats. Bo has counted. The Hourkeepers would want to know, and he has not told them.
- His wife was a Briarkin of the Acorn court who chose to live in the hills and grow old. She died in year 61. Pim and Pom are, he suspects, her kin.
- The bell on his crook was hers.

## What he wants from the Tower

To know what the sheep know. He thinks they are listening for something at noon, and that the answer is at the top.

## Ties

- **[Hank](hank.md)**, friend: they have shared a fire, a flock and a great many silences.
- **[Barry Cloudsley](barry-cloudsley.md)**, friend: shepherds watch the sky, and Bo is the one man on the Dial who trusts Barry's forecasts.
