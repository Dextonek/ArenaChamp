# Grandma Ironknit, the Weaver

*The Hourkeepers · [Briarkin](../peoples/briar.md), the Spindle court · from [the Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

The Hourkeepers say every life on the Dial is a thread, and Grandma Ironknit holds the spindle. She is a tiny old fate-spinner in a dark hooded shawl knitted in bands of rose
thread, with two needles as long as swords and a grey cat called Mr. Biscuit, and she knits lives into the cloth of the Dial in a stone cottage on the Frostfall road, at the
Walking Wood's north-eastern edge. The Hourkeepers have always known what she is. The Wood folk pretend not to. The rest of the Dial thinks she is a very old woman, which she
is, in a way: she is a Briarkin of the Spindle court, and she has been old for three hundred years.

The Spindle court is the smallest of the Briarkin's courts and the strangest. Its fae sit, wherever they please, with spindles, and spin; and what they spin, the Briarkin say,
is the thread of what happens. Not fate, exactly: a Spindle fae cannot make anything happen. But they can see the thread, and follow it, and, if they are very careful, knot it,
so that a life that was about to fray holds a little longer. They have silver eyes of one colour, long clever fingers, and great age worn lightly, and they are rarely seen
outside the Underbough, because mortals who learn what they do tend to ask for things.

About three hundred years before the Fall, a Spindle fae chose to live among mortals. Nobody in the Underbough knows why, and she has never said; the Wood folk's story is that
she fell in love with a woodcutter, and the Hourkeepers' story is that she wanted to see a thread from close up. She built a stone cottage on the Frostfall road, put a spinning
wheel in the window, took the use-name Ironknit (the Spindle court's word for a spinner who has chosen to live among mortals), and began to knit. She has been there since. She
was knitting when the sun fell, and she says she felt every thread on the Dial jerk at once, as if someone had pulled the whole cloth, and that she dropped three stitches and
has never found them.

Two centuries before the Fall the Hourkeepers came to her door and asked her to keep the threads, as they keep the hours, and she agreed, and became one of
them: the Weaver of the Hourkeepers, who keeps the record of lives as the library keeps the record of years. She knits it. Somewhere in her cottage, in a cloth that never ends, is
a row for everyone alive on the Dial.

In the first winter after the Fall the Underbough left two Briarkin children of the Acorn court on her doorstep, born two for one, one life between them: [Pim and Pom](pim-and-pom.md).
The courts were frightened. They left them with the one Briarkin they knew who had chosen to live among mortals, and hoped. Ironknit raised them anyway, and named them, and has
kept them for ninety-nine years, and loves them more than any thread. She also taught [Thessa](thessa.md) to sew, a Northern girl who came to her door at eight to learn needlework,
and to sew places once cloth was too easy. And fifty years ago [Mr. Ledger](mr-ledger.md), the Tithe-Keeper, who has courted nobody in a hundred and sixty years, came to her door
and asked to write her into his book: the only time he has ever offered to record a person rather than a debt. She knitted him a scarf instead. He wears it under the collar every
day. She knows exactly how much it means, because she knitted it.

In the Ring she ties her lines across the floor, rose threads that bind whoever crosses them, and sends the cat.

## People and appearance

A Briarkin of the Spindle court, three hundred years among mortals. Today she reads as a tiny old woman: a dark wine shawl knitted in bands of rose thread with a gold brooch, a dark
hood with a rolled rose edge round a kind old face with silver hair, calm eyes and a small smile, a spindle with three glowing rose spools, two silver needles, and Mr. Biscuit. In the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md) she is a revamp: long pointed ears through the hood's edge, silver eyes of one colour from lid to lid, skin with the dapple of light through leaves, fingers
a little too long, so that she reads as fae and not as another small human.

## Voice

Warm, slow and comfortable, a grandmother's voice, with something underneath it that is not a grandmother's at all. She calls everyone "dear". She counts stitches aloud when she is
thinking, in a language nobody else speaks.

- **Meeting Pim and Pom:** "Which one are you today? Never mind. Come here."

## Secrets

- The three stitches she dropped at the Fall were three lives she could not hold: the choir's master, the Countess of Gloaming's steward's daughter, and a third she will not name. She
  has looked for them in the cloth for a hundred years. One of them is still alive somewhere, unknitted.
- Pim's and Pom's thread is one thread. If it breaks, it breaks for both. She has knotted it eleven times.
- Mr. Ledger's thread ended sixty years before the Fall. She has been knitting it anyway, out of spare wool, for fifty years.

## What she wants from the Tower

To see the Tower's thread. Everything has one. She has looked for the Tower's in the cloth for three hundred years and never found it, and she would like to know whether it is not
in the cloth, or whether it is the cloth.
