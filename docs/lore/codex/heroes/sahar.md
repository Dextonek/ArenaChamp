# Sahar, the Mirage

*The Dune Throne · [Dune folk](../peoples/dune.md) · from [the Sands](../realms/sands.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

On the Glass Flats north of Tamerit, the plain of green-black glass the sun's heat made on the day of the Fall, the air lifts off the glass all day in shimmering sheets,
and in the shimmer stand things that are not there: lakes, palms, walls, caravans, whole towns. The Flats' people, a few families of glass-gatherers who live on its edge
and cut the lightning glass the Sands sell, grow up among the mirages and learn to tell them from the world; a Flats child who walks toward a lake that is not there does
not live long. Sahar was born on the edge of the Flats in the seventy-third summer after the Fall, to a glass-gatherer's family, and learned to tell the mirages from the
world before he could walk. Then he learned to make them.

He found it out at nine, playing on the glass at noon with his cousins, when he wanted a wall to hide behind and a wall stood there, shimmering, in the heat. His cousins
ran round it. It was not there. They ran into the next one, which was. By twelve he could stand a palm grove on the Flats that a caravan would turn toward; by fifteen, a
wall that a raider would shelter behind while Sahar's arrow came through it. The glass-gatherers took him to Tamerit, to the archers of the mirage: the old office of the
Throne's archers who fight on the Flats, one of the Five Offices, nearly forgotten since the Fall made the Flats too hot to fight on. Sahar was the first boy in a century
the archers had taken who could actually make a mirage, and they made him their captain at twenty.

He is the Throne's archer, slim and sun-dark, in pale linen wrapped against the heat with a lilac scarf across his face, and he shoots at enemies sheltering behind walls and
palms that were never there. In the Ring his mirages look like cover and are not, and his arrows come through them; his ultimate makes the mirages real, all at once, and
whoever is standing in one is standing in a wall. The Arbiter has ruled that this is allowed. The Arbiter did not enjoy ruling it.

The archers of the mirage are twelve now, all trained by him, all Flats children, and they patrol the Glass Flats for the Throne in the
cool of the morning and the evening, when the mirages are thinnest and the raiders of the Storm Canyon try to cross. Sahar leads them at
noon, alone, when nobody else can bear the glass. He says the Flats are honest at noon: everything you see is a lie, and you know it.
The court at Tamerit finds him exhausting and the river towns adore him. He has never once taken a raider alive, and never once killed
one either: he stands mirages round them until they walk in circles and give up, and then he sends for the guard.

[Mum](mum.md), the Court of Dusk's harlequin, builds walls nobody can see; Sahar builds walls nobody can trust; each thinks the other is cheating, and they have fought in the
Ring seven times without ever once looking at each other. [The Great Zambini](the-great-zambini.md) has twice tried to hire him for the stage, and Sahar has twice refused:
the showman wants the mirage for his act, and the archer says real magic does not take a bow. Zambini says that real magic is the only kind that should. They have argued about
it at every Truce dinner since. And [Lyra](lyra.md), the Hourkeepers' star archer, is the archer he most wants to beat: two archers who have never shot at the same target, and
argue about it at every Truce dinner. Sahar calls himself the better archer, and Lyra lets him, which annoys him more than losing would.

## People and appearance

Dune folk of the Glass Flats, born after the Fall: twenty-seven, slim and sun-dark, kohl-lined eyes above a lilac scarf across the face, pale linen wrapped against the heat,
a recurved bow of horn and glass. His look is not built yet; its brief is in the [revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Light, mocking and hard to place: his voice seems to come from a little to one side of where he is standing, a trick of the Flats he cannot turn off. He teases everyone, and
means none of it, and all of it.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Hide behind that. Go on. It's lovely shade."
- **Picked:** "Is that a wall? Are you sure?"
- **Winning:** "It was never there. Neither were you."
- **Winning:** "Thank you for trusting the shade."
- **Losing:** "Real after all. How rude."
- **Losing:** "Shimmer. Fade. Back soon."
- **Meeting Lyra:** "Still letting me win the argument, star-girl?"
- **Meeting Mum:** "Your wall or mine? Neither? Good."

## Secrets

- The Mirage Lord on the Climb's twenty-sixth floor, a prince of the Throne who walked onto the Flats to be alone and became a mirage of himself, was his grandmother's brother.
  His grandmother told him the story to frighten him off the glass. It did not work.
- Some of his mirages do not fade. There is a palm grove on the Flats' eastern edge that he made at twelve, and it is still there, and the glass-gatherers rest in its shade.
- He would like to be on Zambini's stage, very much.

## What he wants from the Tower

To stand at its top and make a mirage of a sun over the Noon Seat, and see who believes it. He thinks the whole Dial might.
