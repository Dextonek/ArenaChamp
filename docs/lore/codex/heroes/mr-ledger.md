# Mr. Ledger, the Tithe-Keeper

*The Truce of the Ring · [the Long Dead](../peoples/longdead.md) · from [the Ring](../realms/tower.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Before he was the Tithe-Keeper, Mr. Ledger was a counting-house clerk in Harrow, the castle town of the old kingdom of Harrowmark, in
the days when its crimson kings grew fat on the tolls of the road to the Tower. He was precise, unremarkable and unmarried, and he kept
the royal tolls for thirty years without a single error, which made him the most hated man in the kingdom and the most trusted. Sixty
years before the Fall, on a winter evening, he sat down to reconcile the year's accounts, and found a discrepancy of one coin.

He died at his desk that night, of his heart, with the pen in his hand and the sum unfinished. Three days later, in the crypt under the
counting house, he got up. The Long Dead do not choose their Reason; it chooses them. His was the sum. He walked back up the stairs, sat
down at his desk, and went on looking for the missing coin.

He never found it. Harrowmark's kings learned not to mind a dead clerk; he was cheaper than a living one and he could not be bribed.
He kept the kingdom's books through three reigns, and when King Orlan raised the crimson banner after the Fall, Ledger kept the books of
the war: every sword, every sack of grain, every life, at what it cost and to whom. When the war ended in the mud and Orlan was buried,
the founders of the Truce found that the only complete record of what every realm owed every other was in a dead man's ledger. They
offered him the Tally. He accepted on one condition: that no debt in his book would ever be forgiven, only paid. He has kept the Tally
since the day the Truce was sworn, in a great red book chained to his wrist so that nobody can borrow it.

He is older than he looks, and he looks very old. The realms fear him more than any champion, because a debt in his book does not fade;
it waits. His workings are debts too: nothing hurts now, everything comes due. He steps into the Ring when a debtor will not pay any other
way, and he has never once lost the paperwork.

He keeps strict hours in the Tally-house, though the hours slip: he opens at the first bell of Aubade, which can be heard at the Ring on
a still morning, and closes when the Arbiter's horn ends the day's last duel. He does not sleep. At night he reconciles. Every obol the
Truce has ever struck passes through his cellar, and he knows the weight of each one to a grain; when a forged obol turns up anywhere on
the Dial, it is Ledger who names the forger, usually within the week.

His largest debtor is the Court of Dusk. In the hungry year after the Fall, when Gloaming had no harvest, the Countess
[Vesper](vesper.md) bought grain on credit from the Tally to feed her people through the Long Dusk. The Court has paid the interest every
year for a hundred years and has never touched the principal. This year, for the first time, Ledger has written to her about it. He
says it is routine. Vesper does not believe him.

He is not without friends, though he would not call them that. [Pip](pip.md) delivers every bill he writes, and has never once been
tipped; Ledger considers this a compliment to the boy's honesty and Pip does not. [Shiori](shiori.md) of the Hourkeepers says his book is
the worst-written history on the Dial, and he says hers has too many pictures and no totals; they meet every year to compare records and
part in perfect mutual contempt. And there is [Grandma Ironknit](grandma-ironknit.md). Fifty years ago Ledger asked to write her into his
book: the only time he has ever offered to record a person rather than a debt. She knitted him a scarf instead. He wears it under the
collar, every day, and it is the only warm thing he owns.

## People and appearance

One of the Long Dead, the only one who works for the Truce. Tall and thin, in long black robes with an oxblood panel and gold coin
chains, a tall oxblood collar, the great book at his side with its red wax seal glowing, a long quill. Under them he is what he is: skin
like old vellum, grey-gold and drawn tight on the bone, the cheeks and temples sunken, bone pale through it at the brow, the cheekbones
and the jaw; deep dark sockets with an ember in each, the cold fire of the Long Dead who keep debts; long thin hands with bone at every
knuckle; thin grey hair slicked flat to the skull, and no brows left. Wick keeps a living old man's face; the Truce's two old officers
read as the living and the dead.

## Voice

Dry, soft and very precise, with the patience of someone who has never once been in a hurry. He speaks of people as accounts and of
violence as settlement. He is unfailingly polite. He never raises his voice; he raises the interest.

## Secrets

- He is still looking for the missing coin from the winter he died. He has started to suspect that it was one of the obols of the
  Truce, struck forty years after his death, which is impossible.
- The Tally's page for the duel of year 90 is blank. He remembers writing it. He has checked the ink: the page was never written on.
- If he ever balances the Tally to the last obol, his Reason is finished and he will lie down. He keeps one small debt open on purpose:
  his own, to Ironknit, for the scarf.

## What he wants from the Tower

To audit it. If the Tower has taken realms, people, a sun and a market full of the Dial's dead, as half the Ring believes, none of it has
been written down. And
to know who won in year 90.
