# Kagen, the Scarlet Sheath

*A loner · [Northerners](../peoples/north.md) · from [Frostfall](../realms/frostfall.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · a draft, not in the game

## Biography

On the cliffs above Frostfall stands the Hollow Shrine, and from its steps, and from no other place in the north, the Tear over the Tower can be seen. The shrine is old, older than the Fall, and it was built for one purpose: to keep a way of the sword that is practised nowhere else on the Dial. Its masters do not train to cut. They train to cut and to sheathe, as one act, and they hold that a cut which is not sheathed is not finished. The blade comes out, the blade goes home, and only then has anything happened.

Kagen was nine years old when the sun fell. He was a Northerner of the cliff villages, the child of herders, given to the shrine at six because he was quiet and did not fidget, and his work was to sweep the steps. He was sweeping them at noon when the light stopped. Everything on the Dial that was lit at that moment kept something of it, and the Hourkeepers call such people Noon-touched. Kagen kept his years: he has aged one year for every three since, and he looks thirty-eight now, though he has lived more than a hundred. He does not speak of it. He says the steps were long and he was sweeping.

The Tear opened a night and a day after the sun fell. His master, a woman or a man, for the shrine's records do not say and Kagen will not, was on the steps when it opened. The master looked up at the torn sky, drew the sword once, and cut at it. Nothing in the sky answered. The master did not sheathe the blade. The master stood on the steps with it held out, facing the Tear, and did not move: through that night, through the winter that followed it, and through the one after. Kagen brought water and it was not drunk. He brought a cloak and it was not put on. In the second spring the master died standing, and the sword was still out, and Kagen, who was eleven, could not take it from the hand. He waited until the hand opened. It took until the thaw.

The blade stayed in the master's grip when they buried the body at the foot of the steps. Kagen took what was left: the sheath, black lacquer, empty, with a cord at the mouth. He did not know what to do with it, so he carried it. Within a year the light on the cliffs had done something to the lacquer that no one has explained. In the last noon light, which the shrine's steps still catch for a few minutes each clear day, the black had turned scarlet. It has not turned back. The sheath is the only red thing at the shrine, and it is the only thing Kagen owns that was not given to him by the shrine itself.

He finished his training alone, in the way the shrine required, and the way left him with one rule that he has never broken. He does not let a cut end until the blade is home. A cut, to him, is not what happens when the steel moves; it is the whole of the thing, the draw and the stroke and the return, and the master died with the middle of it and no end. Whatever Kagen strikes, he strikes carefully, because he has to bring the sword back to the sheath before he can say that he has done anything. He has never left a cut open. The cuts he makes in the Ring leave their marks lying quiet on the air and on the enemy, and they open all at once, when the blade goes home.

He wears black over grey, the coat of the shrine, with a cinnabar sash and one black lacquer plate on the right shoulder where the sword arm swings. The scarlet sheath hangs at his left hip with two paper seals tied to it. He carries a katana in the right hand, a straight line of steel with a plain guard, and he walks with it half out, which in the shrine's way is not a threat but the beginning of a sentence. His hair is dark, still, tied back in the Northerner braid, and the star lines on his jaw are as sharp as the day he was given them. He has a scar on his left cheek that he got from a practice blade in his first year. He is tall, lean and quiet, and he walks as a man does who has only ever had one place to be.

He did not leave the shrine for sixty years. When he came down to the Frostfall road it was because a duellist from the Ring had stopped at the shrine to ask what the Hollow Shrine kept in its hollow, and had said, offhandedly, that the thing from the Tear was said to walk in the Ring. Kagen asked which way the Ring lay. He went the next morning. He is still in the Ring, and he has duelled in it many times, and he has not yet met what he came for. He has met a great many other people.

He is not cruel and he is not sad. He is patient in a way that the shrine made and the Tear made worse. He believes that the cut his master began is still open, that it hangs in the air over the cliffs where nothing has closed it, and that someone has to finish it. He has been told, by people he respects, that this is not how cuts work. He thanks them and does not change his mind.

## People and appearance

A Northerner of the cliffs above Frostfall, Noon-touched at nine: umber skin, fine blue-black star lines along the jaw as every Northerner has them, dark hair tied back in a braid. He looks thirty-eight. Tall and lean, calm in the face, with a scar on the left cheek. Black coat over grey, a cinnabar sash, one black lacquer shoulder plate; at the left hip the scarlet sheath with two paper seals, and in the right hand a katana. Where Isolde's people are lake-born and frost-pale, and Shiori's are of the library, Kagen's are the cliff-folk, the herders who live under the Nail with no roof between them and it.

## Voice

Low, slow and level. He speaks the way he moves: once, completely, and then he is silent. He does not threaten and does not boast, and when he says something is finished he means it has been finished for some time already.

## Secrets

- The sword is not in the sheath he carries; it is in the ground beside his master's grave, where the hand finally opened. The blade he carries is the shrine's second sword. He has never told anyone, because the sheath has always been the thing that mattered.
- He has been to the top of the Hollow Shrine's steps every winter solstice and drawn the blade once at the Tear, and sheathed it, and watched the sky. Nothing has ever answered. He is not sure he wants it to.
- Shiori's seals have been changing under his hands. The first spring, he thought the word was a name. Last spring he thought it was a number. This spring he did not look, and he is afraid of what that means.

## What he wants from the Tower

To stand under the Tear and finish the cut. He does not know what that will do. His master did not know either. He wants only to put the blade home at the end of something, once, and to hear it close.

## Ties

- **[Oblivion](oblivion.md)**, sworn enemy: his master's cut at the Tear was never finished, and the thing that came through the Tear is what he means to finish it on.
- **[Zephyr](zephyr.md)**, rival: the wind blade wants to race his draw. He has never seen it, and Kagen has never seen the race.
- **[Sir Whiskers](sir-whiskers.md)**, rival: they spar every winter, and the lynx calls him slow, always from a safe distance.
