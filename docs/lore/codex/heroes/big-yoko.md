# Big Yoko, the Mountain

*The Truce of the Ring · [Aubade folk](../peoples/aubade.md) · from [Aubade](../realms/aubade.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Yoko was born on the shrine cliffs below Aubade, eighteen years before the Fall, to a family of shrine wrestlers: the men and women who
train in the straw-rope rings cut into the cliff terraces at dawn and wrestle at the festivals for the honour of the bell towers. He was a
giant from the cradle. At ten he was taller than his father; at fourteen he could lift the shrine's bronze bell off its hook. He was also,
everyone agreed, the gentlest child on the cliffs, and he hated to win.

The shrine wrestlers have a teaching: the ring is small, the world outside it is large, and a wrestler chooses which to live in. A bout is
won by pushing the other out of the ring, not by hurting them, and the loser is not beaten but returned to the world. Yoko took it more
seriously than anyone. He wrestled to show that a giant can be pushed out by a quicker hand, and he kept hoping someone would do it.

At eighteen he went to the midsummer market at the Tower's foot to wrestle in its straw-rope circle, which every Aubade wrestler did once.
He was warming up in it when the sun stopped, cracked and fell. The ground jumped; he was thrown off his feet. When he got up the market was
gone, the dust was falling, and a mason's boy with a broom was standing at the circle's edge, not crying. Yoko stood in the circle, because
it was the one thing that was still where it had been, and he did not leave it.

Through the two years of the War of the Banners he stood in it. Knights came to the circle to settle quarrels one against one instead of
with their armies; Yoko was the circle's boundary, and nobody fought outside the rope while he stood there. He threw salt before every bout
to keep the circle clean, as the shrine wrestlers do. He ate what [Madame Cleaver](madame-cleaver.md) brought him from the camps across the
Field. When the realms came to swear the Truce, they swore it round his circle, and the Ring was built on it.

He is the Truce's first champion and its living boundary: the ring stamped on the floor of every duel is drawn after the one he defends.
He came to the Ring to lose, and nobody has managed it since the Ring was opened, which is the great sorrow of his life. Every year at the
Ring's opening he pushes the same great stone against [Bram](bram.md) of the Underforge, and neither has moved it farther than the other.
He considers Bram his closest rival and the only man on the Dial he cannot read.

He throws salt before every bout, still, and the salt ends up all over the Ring's floor, which [Gus](gus.md), the keeper of the sea temple,
has asked him to stop many times. Gus sweeps it up. Yoko throws it again. Each thinks the other is very patient, and they are friends.

He lives, as far as anyone can tell, in the Ring. He has a room under the east stand with a bed that Bram's masons built for him out of
one slab of limestone, and he trains at dawn in the empty circle while Morwen salts its edge, the two of them working in silence. Children
of the Ringside come to watch, and he lets them try to push him out, one at a time and then all at once. They never manage it. He always
thanks them. In the hundredth summer the Climb has drawn champions from every realm past his door, and many of them stop to salute him on
the way to the gate, as if he were part of the Tower. He salutes them back, and asks them, every one, to come down again.

He fights because the Ring is a promise, and a promise needs someone to stand in it.

## People and appearance

Aubade folk of the shrine cliffs: by far the widest hero in the Ring, huge in the shoulder and the belly, with warm skin, a calm round face
and a topknot tied in gold. A straw-rope belt with paper charms, a crimson stole across the chest, crimson lacquered plates on the
shoulders, bare feet. In the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) his figure is unchanged: his skin and dress are already his people's.

## Voice

Deep, slow and kind, with long silences. He speaks in proverbs from the shrine cliffs and means every one. He thanks his opponents before
and after every bout.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "The ring is small. The world outside it is large. Choose."
- **Picked:** "Salt first. Then we begin."
- **Winning:** "Back to the world with you. Gently."
- **Winning:** "Out. Thank you for the bout."
- **Losing:** "Ah. At last. Thank you."
- **Losing:** "The ring is still here. So am I."
- **Meeting Bram:** "The stone again? Of course the stone again."
- **Meeting Gus:** "I'll sweep it myself this time. I promise."

## Secrets

- He did lose, once: in year 3, in an exhibition bout, to a girl of the Coast who stepped round him while he was saluting her. She
  stepped outside the rope to do it, and the Arbiter let it stand. Yoko has been grateful ever since, and never told anyone it counted.
- He believes the circle he stands in is the exact place where the Tower's shadow stood at the moment of the Fall, and that if he ever
  leaves it, the shadow will move.
- He cannot read. Gus reads him his letters.

## What he wants from the Tower

Nothing for himself. He would like whoever climbs it to come back down and step out of the ring when it is time.

## Ties

- **[Madame Cleaver](madame-cleaver.md)**, friend: she has fed the Mountain since he was a boy; nobody else owns a pot big enough.
- **[Bram](bram.md)**, rival: every year at the Ring's opening they push the same stone, and neither has moved it farther than the other.
- **[Gus](gus.md)**, friend: Yoko throws the salt and Gus sweeps it up, and both think the other is very patient.
