# Referee Whistler, the Arbiter

*The Truce of the Ring · [Kilnborn](../peoples/kiln.md) · from [the Ring](../realms/tower.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Whistler was born in the masons' halls of the Deep, fourteen years before the Fall, the youngest son of a Kilnborn stonecutter who
was not yet grey, because nobody was yet. He was small, quick and argumentative, and his father despaired of him: he could not
keep a line straight, but he could tell you exactly where everyone else's line was crooked. When he was twelve his father took a
contract to cut paving for the midsummer market at the Tower's foot, and brought the boy along to sweep.

He was sweeping the straw-rope wrestling circle at the market's edge, the circle where a giant shrine boy called Yoko was warming
up, when the sun stopped over the Tower, cracked, and fell. He remembers the sound, and then the silence, and then the dust. His
father was in the market. He did not come back. Whistler stayed at the circle because Yoko stayed at it, and because there was
nowhere else to go.

Through the two years of the War of the Banners the circle was the one place near the Tower where nobody fought. Champions came to
it instead: a knight of the crimson and a knight of the azure who agreed to settle a village's fate one against one, a pair of
captains, a Ninefold who wanted the practice. Whistler swept the sand between bouts, and he watched. He watched every one of them
cheat. The crimson knight stepped over the rope; the azure knight threw sand; the Ninefold spent a life and claimed he had not.
Whistler wrote it all down on a slate, in the masons' marks his father had taught him, because somebody should.

When the realms came to the circle to swear the Truce in the midsummer of year 2, the founders argued for nine days about who should
judge the duels. Every realm wanted its own judge. On the ninth day a Coast captain asked the boy with the slate what he thought,
mostly as a joke. Whistler read them his list. Every founder in the circle was on it. They laughed. Then they stopped laughing and
made him Arbiter, on the grounds that he was the only person at the Tower's foot whom no realm owned, and because none of them wanted
to be the one who said no. He was fourteen, and Noon-touched, and he has been fourteen-going-on-thirty-four for a hundred years.

He has not laughed much since. He wears the black and ivory tabard so that no realm can claim his colour, and a black helm with a gold
band and a red crest so that every champion in the Ring can find him. His silver horn, the Truce's gift, is the one sound both armies of
the Banners still stop for: in the Climb's War chapter the dead soldiers lower their spears when he blows it. His sigil tablets carry
the Truce's rules, each cut in stone in the masons' marks: when he holds one up, that rule binds whoever stands under it. He does not
fight to win. He fights because a rule nobody enforces is only a rumour, and he has a long list of rumours to put down.

The Ring knows two things about him that he would rather it did not. The first is [The Great Zambini](the-great-zambini.md), whose
every duel Whistler has watched and whom he has never once caught cheating, which convinces him that Zambini always does. The second is
[Pim and Pom](pim-and-pom.md), who hold the Ring's record for cards: thirty-one, all for swapping without asking, which they do not
consider cheating and he does. He has tried to explain the rule to them for ninety-eight years. They look ten. He looks thirty-four.
He is not sure who is winning.

The grey came on him in his twenties, by count, long after he had left the Deep: Kilnborn skin turns in a generation, and his did, even
at the Ring, as if the Deep would not let him go. He keeps the helm on in the Ring and does not discuss it.

Now, in the hundredth summer, he has a new worry. The Tally's page for the duel that opened the Tower's gate in year 90 is blank. He
called that duel. He remembers calling it. He cannot remember who won.

## People and appearance

Kilnborn, born in the masons' halls of the Deep before the Fall and grey since his twenties: short, square and solid, ash-grey skin,
pale amber eyes, a big calm square face. Dark armoured shoulders with gold-edged pauldrons, the black and ivory tabard, the silver horn
on its gold chain, a red and gold sigil tablet with a glowing sigil, a black helm with a gold band and a red crest. In the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md) he gets a touch: the ash-grey skin of his people under the helm.

## Voice

Flat, precise and loud enough to reach the back of the stands without shouting. He speaks in rulings. When he is tired he speaks in
numbers. He never says "I think"; he says "the rule is".

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "That is a dash. I saw it. Everyone saw it."
- **Picked:** "Rules are posted. Read them. I'll wait."
- **Winning:** "Foul. Upheld. Next."
- **Winning:** "Written down. Witnessed. Done."
- **Losing:** "Noted. Under protest."
- **Losing:** "The ruling stands. So will I, shortly."
- **Meeting The Great Zambini:** "Sleeves up. Both of them. Higher."
- **Meeting Pim and Pom:** "One at a time. I am counting."

## Secrets

- He remembers calling the duel of year 90 that opened the gate, and remembers raising the winner's arm. He cannot see the face.
  He has started to wonder if it was his own arm.
- His father's body was never found. He thinks the Tower took him with the market, and he checks every face on the Climb.
- He has, once, let a foul go: in year 3, in an exhibition bout, a Coast girl stepped outside the rope to get round Big Yoko and
  pushed him out. It was a foul, and no fall. He let it stand, because Yoko looked so happy. Nobody else knows.

## What he wants from the Tower

For whoever reaches the Noon Seat to have earned it under the rules, every step. And to find out who won in year 90.

## Ties

- **[The Great Zambini](the-great-zambini.md)**, rival: the Arbiter has never caught Zambini cheating, and has never once believed he
  didn't.
- **[Pim and Pom](pim-and-pom.md)**, rivals: the twins hold the Ring's record for cards, thirty-one, all for swapping without asking.
