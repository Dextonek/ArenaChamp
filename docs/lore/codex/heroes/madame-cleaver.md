# Madame Cleaver, the Feastmistress

*The Truce of the Ring · [Dune folk](../peoples/dune.md) · from [the Field of Banners](../realms/banners.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Madame Cleaver was born on a Brass Road caravan in the Sands, twenty-five years before the Fall, the daughter of the caravan's cook. Her
mother cooked for forty camel-drivers on one fire every night for thirty years, and taught her daughter two things: everyone eats from
the same pot, and nobody argues with the cook. The cleaver was her mother's: a heavy Kilnborn blade bought in the Deep at the far end of
the road, with a hole near its spine so it could hang from the cart.

When the sun fell she was twenty-five and cooking for a Dune Throne caravan that had come north to sell glass at the midsummer market. The
caravan was on the Banner Road when the shadow stopped. In the chaos of the first months it was pressed into the service of King Orlan's
crimson banner, along with its camels and its cook, and she spent the first winter of the War of the Banners feeding the crimson army in
the orchards of Harrow.

The second winter was the Winter of Mud. Neither banner could move; both were starving. The azure camped across the orchard, close enough
to smell the crimson's cooking. One night a starving azure boy crept over to steal from her pot, and she caught him by the ear, and fed
him. The next night there were six. By the end of the month she was feeding both armies from one fire in the ruined orchard of Harrow,
under a flag of white linen she had hung on her cauldron, and the soldiers of both banners ate side by side and did not fight within
sight of it. The officers of both sides tried to stop her. She told them to sit down and eat, and they did.

When the war ended and the Truce was sworn at the Tower's foot, the founders added a clause they had learned in her orchard: whoever eats at
the Truce Table may not strike in anger outside the Ring. Her cauldron's stand became the Table's first leg. She has kept the Table ever
since. Every duel in the Ring ends there, winner and loser side by side, and she fights to get them there: a champion who will not come
to the Table is brought, and she does the bringing. She walked away from the war with her cleaver, her cauldron, and the certainty that
everyone fights better fed. She is Noon-touched, twenty-five at the Fall, and looks forty-five, broad and proud, with a voice that carries
across a battlefield because it has.

Her cleaver went back to the Deep for a new edge in year 30, to the great forge, where [Brannoc](brannoc.md) made it again: same blade,
same hole, an edge that glows like an ember when she is angry. A promise came back with it, which neither of them kept. They still eat at
the same table every midsummer, at opposite ends. And she has fed [Big Yoko](big-yoko.md) since he was a boy in the straw-rope circle: nobody
else on the Dial owns a pot big enough, and nobody else would dare tell the Mountain to finish his greens.

The Truce Table is hers in every way that matters. She decides the seating: rivals side by side, sworn enemies across from each other,
so they must pass the bread. She cooks the truce-pot herself on the night of every great duel, and she has a rule that nobody leaves until
their bowl is empty, which has kept the Countess of Gloaming and the Dawn Paladin at the same table until morning more than once. The
Ringside's cooks are her apprentices, every one, and the stew in every kitchen round the crater is hers by descent. She has fed every
champion of the Ring for a hundred years, and she could tell you what each of them likes, and what each of them is afraid of, which is
usually the same thing.

Her recipes came from nobody. Her temper comes from the fact that champions never say thank you.

## People and appearance

Dune folk of the caravans, born on the Brass Road: broad and strong, with a round proud face, green eyes (her father's, a caravan guard of
the Coast) and a braid at her shoulder. A crimson headwrap in the Sands fashion, mail shoulders, a crimson jerkin with a bandolier of small
knives, and the great dark cleaver with its glowing ember edge. Her skin is the deep brown of her people, with kohl drawn out to the
temples and henna on the backs of her hands (Touched on 2026-11-16 by the [revamp plan](../revamp.md): the headwrap stays).

## Voice

Loud, warm and absolutely certain, a voice made for calling forty camel-drivers to supper. She gives orders as if they were invitations and
invitations as if they were orders. She swears in the desert tongue, so that only the Dune folk blush.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Sit. Eat. Then you can hit each other."
- **Picked:** "Who's hungry? Good. Get hungrier."
- **Winning:** "Plate's clean. Next."
- **Winning:** "Now you sit at my table. Elbows off."
- **Losing:** "Burnt. Happens to the best pots."
- **Losing:** "Back to the kitchen. I'll be out again."
- **Meeting Big Yoko:** "Did you eat? You didn't eat. Sit."
- **Meeting Brannoc:** "Still not sharp enough, smith. Neither are you."

## Secrets

- The starving azure boy she caught stealing from her pot on the first night of the Winter of Mud was the drummer of the azure, the boy
  whose drum still beats on the Climb's forty-eighth floor. She has climbed to the forty-first and turned back. She cannot face the rest.
- The promise she and Brannoc did not keep was to open an inn together at the Waymeet when the war was really over. She still has the deed.
- She has never once eaten at the Truce Table herself.

## What she wants from the Tower

To feed the War chapter's two armies at one fire, once, and see if they stop. The Camp-Mother on its floors learned the trick from her, and
it has not worked yet.
