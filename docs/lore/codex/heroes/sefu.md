# Sefu, the Dune Sovereign

*The Dune Throne · [Dune folk](../peoples/dune.md) · from [the Sands](../realms/sands.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Sefu was born in the Throne Hall of Tamerit twenty-four years before the Fall, the second child of the old king of the Sands, and he was never meant to rule. The
crook was for his sister Asenet, three years older, clever, cold and much admired, who was crowned queen at twenty-five when their father died, two years before
the Fall. Sefu was the king's brother: the commander of the Throne's horse archers, a handsome, proud, ambitious young man with a gift for speeches and none for
patience, who loved his sister and resented her in equal measure, and who had been told every day of his life that the sun was his father.

He believed it. The kings of the Sands always called the sun their father, and on the day of the Fall, when the shadow stopped and the sun cracked and the Glass Flats
turned to glass under its heat, the Sands said it had fallen there, into the dunes, which are still warm. Sefu, twenty-four, rode out onto the Flats that afternoon at
the head of his archers and stood on the smoking glass and felt the warmth come up through his boots, and knew, he says, that the sun had come home to its children.
The Sands said something else as well: that the sun had fallen in the year of Asenet's crowning, under her rule, and that a queen in whose reign the Father fell had
failed him.

Asenet joined the crimson banner of King Orlan in the first months of the War of the Banners, for a share of the sun's pieces; the Sands did not like it. In the spring of
year 1 the river towns rose, the court turned, and the Throne's guard came to Sefu. He took the crown from his sister in the Throne Hall at midnight, with her own guard
holding the doors, and declared himself the sun's heir: if a sun was to hang over the Tower again, he said, it should be his. She was driven out that night. At the gate
of Tamerit, in the dark, she laid a curse on the crown she no longer wore: it would never stay on one head. And the captain of the gate, the young lion Ramet, opened the
gate and let her go, and Sefu has never asked him why.

He has worn the crown for ninety-nine years, Noon-touched, a king who looks forty-four. The curse has passed it from head to head every time he set it down: to a regent
while he rode to the Truce, to his heir during a fever, to a cousin at a festival. Every one of them who wore it too long has fallen: the regent from a horse, the heir to
the fever, the cousin from a balcony. Sefu has not fallen. The Sands say it is because he is the sun's true heir, and he believes it. He wears layered linen, a gold collar
and a striped gold headdress, and carries a sandstone crook, and his crook and his sarcophagus Judge his enemies: a Judged enemy cannot run far.

He holds the first gate of the Climb's Sands chapter, the Storm Canyon in the great storm that blew on the night he took the crown, and in the Tower he sits in judgement at its
end. He sends his champions to the Ring to win the climb, and wants the Noon Seat for himself. [Nebet](nebet.md), the Throne's judge of the dead, weighs the dead for his
Throne, and once, at his order, weighed the living. [Auriel](auriel.md), the Noonsworn's Dawn Paladin, calls his claim heresy: who owns the sun, she says, is the sky.
"My father," Sefu answered, in the Ring, in front of forty thousand, and then, because he cannot help himself, "you never met yours." She has not forgiven it. He regrets
it, and has never said so. And his sister, [Asenet](asenet.md), loose again in the hundredth summer, calls him a thief. Each has sworn to see the other fall.

## People and appearance

Dune folk of the royal house, and Noon-touched: twenty-four at the Fall, he looks forty-four. Dark bronze skin, a short beard, kohl to the temples and gold on the lids;
layered white linen with a striped gold and turquoise headdress, a broad gold collar, gold armlets and greaves, and the sandstone crook in the left hand. In the
[revamp plan](../revamp.md) his figure is unchanged: he reads as the king of the Dune folk.

## Voice

Rich, commanding and theatrical; he speaks in decrees and in the royal "we" when he remembers. He never asks. He pauses for applause, and is used to getting it.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Kneel, or be judged standing."
- **Picked:** "The Father watches. Let us not disappoint him."
- **Winning:** "Judged."
- **Winning:** "The sand takes what the king gives it."
- **Losing:** "Even the sun sets."
- **Losing:** "We will remember your name. Briefly."
- **Meeting Asenet:** "Sister. The crown is heavy. Would you like to try it?"
- **Meeting Auriel:** "Paladin. Your sky is empty. My father is home."

## Secrets

- He has outlasted the curse because Vitriol brews him a draught every night that keeps the crown from taking him; it is shortening his life by a year for every month. He
  knows. He takes it anyway.
- He did not take the crown. He was offered it at midnight, by the guard, and he hesitated, and said yes because he was afraid of what they would do if he said no.
- He has stood on the Glass Flats every midsummer for ninety-nine years and felt the warmth. This year, for the first time, it was colder.

## What he wants from the Tower

The Noon Seat, for himself: to sit where his father sat, and become the new sun. He is not sure what that means. He is sure it means he will never have to set the crown down.

## Ties

- **[Asenet](asenet.md)**, sibling: brother and sister; he took her crown, she cursed it. Each has sworn to see the other fall.
- **[Auriel](auriel.md)**, rival: who owns the sun. The sky, says the Paladin; my father, says the King.
- **[Nebet](nebet.md)**, comrade: she weighs the dead for his Throne, and once weighed the living for him.
