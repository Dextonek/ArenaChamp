# Cantor, the Last Verse

*The Noonsworn · [Aubade folk](../peoples/aubade.md) · from [Aubade](../realms/aubade.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

The sun was held up, the old songs of Aubade say, by the Hymn of Noon, sung every midday from the Tower's top by a choir of a hundred. Cantor
was born thirty years before the Fall in the bell quarter of Aubade, into a family that had sent a singer to the choir in every generation for
three hundred years, and he never wanted to be anything else. He had a light, true baritone and an infallible sense of time: as a boy he could
clap with the bells of all hundred towers at once and never miss. The choir took him at sixteen.

For fourteen years he climbed the Tower every morning with the ninety-nine and sang the Hymn at noon in the Sun Court, under the great dial, with
the Sun Warden standing at his post and the sun overhead like a bell about to ring. The Hymn was a beat before it was a song: the choir's master
kept time with a staff on the floor, and a note off the beat went sour. Cantor never sang one. He sat at the master's left hand. Beside him sat a
young soprano called Mum, from a family of ringers, who sang so purely that the Sun Warden was said to turn his head when she came in, and whom
Cantor loved for nine years without telling her.

On the day of the Fall the choir sang the Hymn at noon, and the sun stopped overhead and cracked and fell anyway. Cantor remembers the moment
exactly, because he has had a hundred years to remember it: the master's staff came down on the beat, the choir came in, and somewhere in the
second line, somewhere to his left, someone was a hair behind. Then the light went white and then went out. When he could see again the Sun Court
was empty except for the Warden at his post and Mum on her knees beside him, and the ninety-eight were gone. Not dead; gone. The two of them came
down the Tower's stair together in the dark. She did not speak on the way down. She has not made a sound since.

He is the last who knows every verse of the Hymn but one: the last verse, which only the master knew, and which was sung only at midsummer. He
teaches it in the Choir Loft of Aubade, where the hundred chairs stand with lilies on ninety-eight of them, to children who will never sing it at
the Tower's top. He sings on the beat, because the Hymn was a beat before it was a song, and he fights on it: in the Ring his workings come one to
a beat, and off the beat they go sour. He has walked the Field of Banners singing the dead armies their marching songs, and the ashen soldiers of
the Climb's War, he has heard, keep step when he sings.

He has never stopped singing to Mum. She came down from the Tower in a porcelain mask, went to Gloaming and the Court of Dusk, and became its
harlequin. He goes to Gloaming every midwinter for the Masque of Houses and sings under her window. She has never answered, and she has never left
the room. The bell-founder [Campana](campana.md) is his closest friend: her bells keep his beat, and his Hymn tells her where to hang them. She is
the only person he has told about the hair behind the beat.

He fights in the Ring for the right to climb, to sing the Hymn once more where it was meant to be sung. He has been as far as the Sea's crown and
stood in front of the Sun Warden, who looked at him for a long time and then said, in the voice Cantor had heard every noon for fourteen years,
"You are late."

## People and appearance

Aubade folk of the bell quarter, Noon-touched: thirty at the Fall, he looks fifty, lean and upright, golden-brown, with dark hair under a plumed hat. A rose and plum coat with a lyre strap, a parchment scarf printed with notes, a golden lyre in the left hand. The dawn line runs gold from his brow down the bridge of his nose, and gold
thread is wound round the locks by his face and at his nape (Touched on 2026-11-16 by the [revamp plan](../revamp.md): he was pale before).

## Voice

A trained baritone, warm and exact; he speaks in measures, and his sentences land on the beat. He hums under his breath when he is thinking, and
counts when he is angry.

## Secrets

- The voice a hair behind the beat, somewhere to his left, he has always believed was Ansel, the tenor who sat at Mum's other side: Auriel's
  father. He has never told Auriel. He is not certain. He thinks about it every noon.
- One of the ninety-eight is in the Climb's Deep, as a ruler: the Blind Cantor, a bass who sat across from him. Cantor does not know.
- He knows Mum knows the last verse. The master taught it to her the week before the Fall. He has never asked her for it.

## What he wants from the Tower

To stand in the Sun Court and sing the Hymn of Noon once more, every verse, on the beat; and to learn the last verse, from someone, before he
does.

## Ties

- **[Mum](mum.md)**, old flame: they sang the Hymn of Noon side by side on the day it failed. She has made no sound since; he has not stopped
  singing to her.
- **[Campana](campana.md)**, friend: her bells keep his beat, and his Hymn tells her where to hang them.
