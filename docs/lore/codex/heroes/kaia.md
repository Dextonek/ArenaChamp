# Kaia, the Tempest Caller

*The Windward · [Coast folk](../peoples/coast.md) · from [the Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Kaia was born on the Storm Spire in the seventy-second summer after the Fall, during a storm, which the storm-callers of the Spires consider the only
proper way to be born. Her mother was a storm-caller and her father a lightning-rod smith from Saltreach, and she grew up in the storm-callers' tower at
the top of the Spire, a thousand feet above the sea, in rooms full of copper rods and weather-glasses, with the sky outside the windows doing whatever it
liked. It always had. Kaia was born into a world whose weather had had no master for seventy-two years, and she never knew another.

The storm-callers of the Spires are an old order, older than the Fall, and before the Fall their work was small: a wind for a becalmed ship, rain for a dry
field, a storm turned away from the harbour. The weather had a master then, the sun, and the seasons kept their appointments, and the storm-callers only
nudged. Since the Fall the storms come when they like, and the storm-callers have become the Coast's defence: they call down the lightning before it can
fall on the town, and turn the storms that come off the Sands out to sea. Kaia was the best of her generation by fifteen and the least obedient by sixteen.
She did not want to turn storms away. She wanted to make them come.

She means to be the sky's master, since nobody else is. She is a storm caller of the Spires, with a tall staff that catches lightning and plants it in the
ground as rods, and her lightning goes where she puts it, in branching trees of hard blue-white light under flat dark cloud. She bends the storms to the
Windward's ships, and sells rain to the Wood when the Wood pays, and has sold storms to people the Council of Captains pretends not to know about. She
fights in the Ring because a storm needs somewhere to land. Her first duel there, at nineteen, emptied the western stands with a stray bolt, and is the
reason the Truce hired a hedge witch to salt the Ring's edge.

[Barry Cloudsley](barry-cloudsley.md), the Windward's augur, forecasts the weather from the Weather Spire; Kaia changes it, mostly to prove him wrong. It began
in year 88, when Barry forecast a clear day for the Spires' Kite Day and Kaia, sixteen and furious about something else, called a thunderstorm on it at noon
in front of the whole coast. Barry said she had broken the forecast. Kaia said she had improved it. They have argued in public for twelve years since, on the
Weather Spire's balcony, in the Council of Captains and in the Ring, and the Coast follows it like a play. They are the two best weather-workers on the Dial
and they have never once agreed on anything. Each is, in private, the other's greatest admirer.

She lives, still, in the storm-callers' tower at the top of the Storm Spire, in a round room with nine windows and a copper rod through
the roof that she sleeps beside on purpose. The order elected her its first caller at twenty-five, over the heads of a dozen elders, because
nobody else could hold the great storm of the ninety-seventh summer off the Lantern Coast; she held it for a day and a night, alone on the
Spire's top with the rod in her hands, and when it broke she walked down nine hundred steps and slept for two days. The Coast forgave her a
great deal for that night, and she has spent most of it since.

And [Saga](saga.md), the dancer of the northern light at Frostfall: lightning and the aurora have never shared a sky politely. When Kaia's storms go north
they foul Saga's curtains, and when Saga's curtains come south the Spires get green flashes at sea that sailors take for omens. They met in the Ring once and
the stands talked about the colours for a year.

## People and appearance

Coast folk of the Spires, born after the Fall: twenty-eight, lean and quick, with silver hair bound up and a hooded face. Today she wears a long rain-blue
gown with a white apron panel and a hood, and holds the hooked staff with a spark in its hook in her left hand; from the game's camera she is often taken for
Isolde. In the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) she is a revamp, third to be made: sea-tanned skin, the tide lines of nine floods round her forearms, sea-glass in her
ears, her silver hair streaked sea-green, and a knee-length oilskin storm coat with a high collar and a short cape over leggings and sea boots, so that she reads
as a storm caller of the Spires and not as a lady of a court.

## Voice

Sharp, bright and fast, with a laugh like a crack of thunder; she talks over people, especially Barry. She swears in the Spires' whistle-talk, which is mostly
the sound of wind.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Stand by the rod. It's safer. For me."
- **Picked:** "Forecast: lightning. Mine."
- **Winning:** "Struck."
- **Winning:** "Tell Barry he was wrong. Again."
- **Losing:** "Clear skies. For now."
- **Losing:** "Every storm blows over. Then it comes back."
- **Meeting Barry Cloudsley:** "What's the forecast, old man? Wrong?"
- **Meeting Saga:** "Pretty lights. Shame about the weather."

## Secrets

- She has tried to call a storm over the Tower, three times, to see what the sky does there. All three times the storm went round. She thinks the Tower is
  holding something up, or back.
- The storms Barry thinks someone in the Sands is calling: some of them she called, for pay, for a house of the Dune Throne. She did not know whom they would
  hit. One hit the Spires.
- She keeps every one of Barry's forecasts, in a box, in order.

## What she wants from the Tower

To stand on top of it in a storm and call the lightning down onto the Noon Seat, and see what lights up. She believes the sky can be mastered, and that the
master's seat is up there.
