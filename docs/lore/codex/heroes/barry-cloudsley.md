# Barry Cloudsley, the Sky Augur

*The Windward · [Coast folk](../peoples/coast.md) · from [the Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Barry Cloudsley was born in Saltreach thirty years before the Fall, the son of a sailmaker, and the Cloudsleys have been reading the sky since there was a
Saltreach: the name is a weather name, given to a great-grandfather who forecast the great gale of a hundred and twenty years before the Fall a week early
and was not believed. Barry was a careful, cheerful, round-faced boy who kept a weather diary from the age of six, and at twenty the Windward's augur on
the Weather Spire, an old woman called Nan Gullwhistle, took him on as her junior, because his diary was better than hers.

Before the Fall the augur's work was a craft: the sky kept appointments, the seasons came in order, and a good augur could tell a fishing fleet tomorrow's
weather from today's clouds and be right nine times in ten. Barry was right nine and a half. He was thirty and calling the noon forecast from the Weather
Spire's balcony on the day of the Fall, and he remembers that he had just said "fair, with a light westerly", when the sun stopped.

Since then the augur's work has been the hardest job on the Dial. The sky no longer keeps its own appointments. Storms come when they like, the winds keep
no seasons, and a forecast holds, Barry has measured, for about eight seconds. He was the Spires' junior augur when the great storm of the twenty-third summer
came, and he forecast it a day early, and the Council of Captains did not believe him, and ships went onto the Spires that night, which the Coast remembers as
the Night of Lanterns. Nan Gullwhistle died the next year and left him the Weather Spire. He has never been disbelieved since. He has, quite often, been wrong.

He stands on the highest Spire in a long storm-slate coat with a cloud mantle on his shoulders, a high gold-rimmed collar, windswept hair, and a weathervane
staff taller than he is with a glowing orb at the top, and he calls the forecast every eight seconds, which is as long as any forecast now holds. He is right
about the weather more often than anyone alive and still mostly wrong, which he blames, loudly, on [Kaia](kaia.md). In the Ring his weather hits both heroes,
and he is the only one who knows what is coming. That is his whole art: not to change the sky but to know it a moment sooner.

The forecast is called from the Weather Spire by bell and by flag, so that every ship in sight of the Spires can read it, and copied by
kite to the harbour towns, where boys chalk it on boards at the quays; by the time the chalk is dry it is wrong, and everyone knows it,
and everyone reads it anyway. Barry keeps every forecast he has ever called, one hundred and forty million of them by his count, in
ledgers in the Spire's lower rooms, and compares them each night with what the sky actually did. The comparison is the most complete
record of the masterless weather on the Dial, and the Hourkeepers send a Reader every year to copy it.

He and Kaia have argued in public for twelve years, since the Kite Day she called a thunderstorm on a day he had forecast clear. He says she breaks the sky;
she says she improves his forecasts. The Coast follows it like a play. He considers her the most gifted weather-worker in a century and has never once said so.

His friends are men who watch the sky. [Bo](bo.md), the hill shepherd of the Moot, trusts his forecasts, which makes one of them: they have written to each
other every month for thirty years, Barry's forecasts for Bo's fleece-readings, and Bo says Barry is right more often than the sheep. And [Professor Puff](professor-puff.md),
the aeronaut, his oldest flying companion: one reads the sky, the other maps it, and they have gone up together in the Professor's basket more times than
either can count, Barry calling the wind and the Professor steering into it.

## People and appearance

Coast folk of Saltreach, Noon-touched: thirty at the Fall, he looks fifty, with a calm round face under windswept brown hair. A storm-slate coat with a cloud
mantle and a high gold-rimmed collar, the weathervane staff with its glowing orb in the left hand. His skin is sea-tanned, and the coat's flared sleeves end below
the elbow, so the tide lines of his people show on his forearms (Touched on 2026-11-16 by the [revamp plan](../revamp.md): twenty-three floods, seven drawn).

## Voice

Warm, measured and cheerful, a professional forecaster's voice that has delivered bad news to sailors for seventy years. He speaks in forecasts ("rain later,
followed by you falling over") and laughs at his own jokes, which are all about weather.

## Secrets

- He believes the storms that come off the Sands are called by someone in Tamerit. He is half right: some of them were Kaia's, sold to a Dune house. He has
  begun to suspect, and would rather not know.
- Since the Climb opened, his forecasts have been right more often. He thinks the Tower is taking the sky's weather into itself, floor by floor, and leaving the
  Dial's sky emptier.
- He has never been up in a storm. Every flight with the Professor, he has forecast clear skies, and made sure.

## What he wants from the Tower

To stand on top of it and read the sky from above the clouds, the one place no augur has ever stood; and to find out where the weather went.

## Ties

- **[Kaia](kaia.md)**, rival: he forecasts the weather and she changes it to prove him wrong; twelve years of public argument.
- **[Bo](bo.md)**, friend: shepherds watch the sky, and Bo is the one man on the Dial who trusts Barry's forecasts.
- **[Professor Puff](professor-puff.md)**, friend: his oldest flying companion: one reads the sky, the other maps it.
