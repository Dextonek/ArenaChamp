# Gus, the Temple Keeper

*The Windward · [Coast folk](../peoples/coast.md) · from [the Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Gus was born in Saltreach in the thirty-fifth summer after the Fall, the son of a rope-walker and a net-mender, and christened Augustin, which only his
mother ever used. He was a stocky, calm, unhurried boy who liked things done properly, and who found, at about the age of nine, that very few things on
the Drowned Coast were done properly. The boats were tied badly, the nets were mended in a hurry, the drowned harbour's canals were full of rubbish, and the
great sea temple, which a girl had danced dry on the day of the Fall and which the whole Coast loved, was filthy.

He began sweeping it at twelve, uninvited. The temple's old keeper, a priest who had survived the Fall on its roof and never quite recovered, let him: he
was quiet, he was thorough, and he did not ask questions. By twenty Gus was doing all the keeping and the old priest none of it. In the sixtieth summer the
priest died, and the Council of Captains, which has no priests and does not want any, asked Gus to keep the temple, and he took up its broom, and has kept
it for forty years, against the sea, the salt and everyone who walks in.

The temple is the great hall of the Drowned Harbour: pale pillars, sea-glass windows, its floor just above the water at high tide, and the tide never far
away. Gus keeps it as clean as a temple has ever been kept. The floor is always wet, because he holds that a clean floor is a wet one, and he has put up
signs. People slide. That, he will tell you, is their own business. He is a stocky, calm old keeper in grey-blue robes and a broad lacquered hat, with a
sweeping staff hung with paper streamers and a bronze urn of glowing temple water at his side, which he throws, when he has to.

He is the nearest thing the Coast folk have to a priest, which he denies. People come to him with their troubles because he is in the temple, and he listens,
and sweeps, and says something sensible, and they go away feeling better and slightly damp. He holds no faith but the Tide, and the Tide, as he explains it,
means keep your floor clean and your boat tied and do not expect the sea to be grateful. He has never once asked the sea for anything.

[Marin](marin.md), the Tide Dancer, is his oldest friend: she danced the water out of his temple on the day of the Fall, before he was born, and he has kept
her floor wet ever since. They have tea on the temple steps every morning, and she dances the noon tide on his clean floor, and he does not mind the footprints,
from her. He comes to the Ring when [Big Yoko](big-yoko.md) has thrown his salt about again. Yoko throws salt before every bout, to keep the circle clean;
it ends up all over the Ring's floor; Gus has asked him to stop, many times, and sweeps it up. Each thinks the other is very patient, and they are friends.
Gus reads Yoko his letters, and has never told anyone the Mountain cannot read.

In the hundredth summer the temple is busier than it has been since the Fall: the Climb's champions come through the Drowned Harbour
on their way to the Ring, and stop at the temple to ask for luck, and Gus, who does not give luck, gives them a towel. He has put up a
new sign: CLIMBERS, MIND THE FLOOR. Nobody has ever asked him what he thinks is at the top of the Tower. He thinks it needs a sweep.

## People and appearance

Coast folk of Saltreach, born after the Fall: sixty-five, stocky and calm, with a heavy-lidded face and a short grey beard under a broad flat lacquered hat;
grey-blue robes with a pale crossed collar and a dark sash; the sweeping staff with its straw head and paper streamers in the left hand, and the bronze urn
of glowing aqua water at his side. In the [revamp plan](../revamp.md) his figure is unchanged: his hat and urn set him apart, and his skin is already the
Coast's.

## Voice

Slow, mild and unsurprised, a voice that has said "mind the floor" ten thousand times. He states rules as facts. He never argues; he repeats himself until
the other person gives up.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Mind the floor. I did warn you."
- **Picked:** "Wipe your feet. Then we'll begin."
- **Winning:** "Swept up."
- **Winning:** "Clean. As it should be."
- **Losing:** "Slipped. Happens to everyone."
- **Losing:** "I'll mop that up later."
- **Meeting Big Yoko:** "Salt again? I've only just finished."
- **Meeting Marin:** "Floor's wet, dancer. Just how you like it."

## Secrets

- He knows about the door from the Gaol of Oaths into the temple's undercroft, and he knows Asenet came through it, because he found her wet footprints on his
  floor that morning and mopped them up before Kessa came. He has not decided why he did it.
- The temple water in his urn glows because it comes from the temple's old well, which goes down below the sea, and nobody knows where. He has never drunk it.
- He does not like the sea. He has kept its temple for forty years and has never once been out in a boat.

## What he wants from the Tower

He has been up to the Climb's first floor and seen the sea temple there as it was on the morning of the Fall: filthy, flooding, and full of frightened people.
He would like to go back with his broom.
