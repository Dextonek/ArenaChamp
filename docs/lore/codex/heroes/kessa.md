# Kessa, the Chainwarden

*The Truce of the Ring · [Kilnborn](../peoples/kiln.md) · from [the Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md)* · [Heroes](README.md) · new, on the cards

## Biography

Kessa was born in the Deep, in the chain-makers' gallery above the great forge, in the thirty-second summer after the Fall. Her mother was
a chain-maker, her grandmother was a chain-maker, and the family's mark, a closed link, was cut on more of the Dial's chains than anyone else's:
ships' anchors, the great lift's cables, the bridges over the Caldera. Kessa was born grey, as the Kilnborn children of that generation
were, and grew up broad and strong and stubborn, with her mother's habit of testing every link by hand.

The family's greatest contract came from the Truce in year 5: the chains of the **Gaol of Oaths**, the prison the Truce was building in the
caverns under the Drowned Harbour for those who break the Truce. Kessa's grandmother forged them. They are made of Caldera iron, forged in
the sun's own fire, and nothing that has sworn on the Truce can break them. When Kessa was eighteen the Gaol's old warden, a Coast captain
who had kept it since it opened, wrote to the chain-makers asking for someone to come and see to the chains, which the sea was rusting.
Kessa went. She never came home.

She found a prison she did not like. The Gaol of Oaths is cut into the rock under the drowned harbour wall, and the spring tides flood its
lower cells; the warden was old, the guards were bored, and the prisoners were the strangest company on the Dial: champions who had broken
the Truce, nobles who had struck at the Table, a Long Dead who had lied, a Ninefold knight who had refused to pay a debt in the Tally. Kessa
saw to the chains. Then she saw to the cells, and the guards, and the floods. By twenty-eight she was running the Gaol in all but name, and
in year 60, when the old warden died, the Truce made her Chainwarden.

She has kept it forty years. She wears its chains wound round her forearms, the old links her grandmother forged, and carries its great
ring of keys at her belt. She believes in the Gaol the way her people believe in a well-made wall: a promise is a chain, and a chain that
breaks was badly made. She is not cruel to her prisoners. She feeds them, talks to them, knows their names and their oaths and exactly how
they broke them. She never lets go of one. In all her years only one prisoner has left without her leave.

That prisoner was [Asenet](asenet.md), the Uncrowned, the cursed queen of the Sands, sent to the Gaol in year 97 for breaking the Truce
Table's peace in Tamerit. On a spring tide in year 99, when the lower cells flooded and the guards were moving the prisoners upward, Asenet
slipped her chain. Kessa still does not know how: the chain was whole, the lock was locked, and the door Asenet left by was not one of the
Gaol's doors. Kessa has not forgiven the sea, the tide or herself, and she has gone up into the Ring to hunt, which a Chainwarden has never
done before. She means to bring Asenet in, one way or the other.

She blames the tide for every flood and every escape, and she blames the tide on [Marin](marin.md), the Tide Dancer, whose sea temple stands
on the drowned harbour above the Gaol and who, Kessa is convinced, turns the tide for her own reasons. Marin says the tide turns itself.
Kessa says that is exactly what someone who turned it would say. They have argued about it for twenty years, on the temple steps, in front
of Gus, who sweeps round them.

She comes up into the Ring when an oath-breaker must be brought in, and she brings them in. She is very hard to let go of.

## People and appearance

Kilnborn of the chain-makers' gallery: broad and short and immensely strong, with ash-grey skin, pale amber eyes and short iron-grey hair;
grey since birth. Dark iron armour, the Gaol's chains wound round both forearms, a great ring of keys at the belt. Her look is not built yet;
its brief, which gives her the Kilnborn's grey skin, is in the [revamp plan](../revamp.md).

## Voice

Blunt, low and patient, with the Deep's short vowels and a jailer's habit of saying names. She explains the rules once, and then enforces
them. She is never angry with a prisoner; she is disappointed.

## Voice lines

- **Losing:** "Slack in the chain. It happens once."
- **Losing:** "I know where you live. I know where everyone lives."
- **Meeting Asenet:** "Asenet. Your cell's still warm."
- **Meeting Marin:** "Hold the tide, dancer. Just this once."

## Secrets

- The door Asenet left by is not on any map of the Gaol, but it is on her grandmother's, in the chain-maker's book at home: the old warden
  had a door cut to the sea temple's undercroft in year 5, and never told the Truce. Kessa found the page a month ago.
- She believes that if she ever loses a second prisoner, her grandmother's chains will break, every one, all over the Dial.
- She has visited Asenet's empty cell every night since the escape and talked to it.

## What she wants from the Tower

Its prisoners. If the Tower has taken realms whole, it has taken their oath-breakers too, and none of them are in her Gaol.

## Ties

- **[Marin](marin.md)**, rival: the Gaol of Oaths lies under Marin's sea, and Kessa blames her tide for every flood and every escape.
- **[Asenet](asenet.md)**, sworn enemy: the only prisoner who ever left Kessa's chain without leave.
