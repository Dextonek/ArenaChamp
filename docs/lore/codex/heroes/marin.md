# Marin, the Tide Dancer

*The Windward · [Coast folk](../peoples/coast.md) · from [the Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md)* · [Heroes](README.md)

## Biography

Marin was born in Sallowmouth, the great port of the Drowned Coast, twelve years before the Fall, the daughter of a harbour pilot and a fishwife, and
at seven she was given to the sea temple as a dancer. The Tide Dancers of the sea temple were an old order: girls and boys who danced the turning of
the tide on the temple floor at every high water and every low, so that the harbour would remember to breathe, the Coast folk said. It was not a working,
exactly. It was a courtesy to the sea. Marin was the best of them by ten, barefoot on the cold stone in a dress of sea-glass beads, with ribbons of blue
silk that she swung like water.

She was twelve and dancing the noon tide in the temple on the day of the Fall. The sun stopped over the Tower; the light changed; the sea began to come
in, not as a wave but as a rising, steady and enormous, over the quays, the fish market, the streets, the first floors of the houses. The priests fled up
the tower. The other dancers fled. Marin did not, because nobody had told her to stop, and because the tide was turning and she was in the middle of the
turn. She danced. And the water that came in at the temple's great doors slowed, and curled, and turned with her ribbons, and went back out.

She danced for eleven hours, the old keeper said afterwards. The sea came in over the whole of Sallowmouth that afternoon and drowned half the city, and
stayed; but the floor of the sea temple was dry at nightfall, and the people who had climbed onto its roof came down into it alive. The Coast folk call it
the **Dry Floor**, and it is why the temple still stands in the Drowned Harbour with its floor just above the water. The ribbons she danced with had turned,
in her hands, into ribbons of water: thick, smooth bands of clear blue water with a white foam crest. They have never turned back.

She has danced the tide ever since. It turns every few seconds in her hands now: high, it pushes; low, it pulls. She is the Windward's tide dancer, and the
Drowned Harbour's, and she keeps the harbour lights with them and the boats off the drowned streets. She holds the first gate of the Climb's Sea, where the
Tower holds Sallowmouth on the morning the water came, and in the Tower, as on that day, she decides when the tide goes out.

[Gus](gus.md) keeps her temple, and has kept it clean for forty years against the sea, the salt and everyone who walks in: she danced the water out of it,
and he has kept her floor wet ever since, because a clean floor, he says, is a wet one. They have tea on the temple steps every morning. [Ostrel](ostrel.md),
the ferryman of the dead, poles his rivers of pale light down to the sea, and her tide carries them out; she asks him nothing about what they carry, and he
is grateful. And [Kessa](kessa.md), the Chainwarden of the Gaol of Oaths under the drowned harbour wall, blames Marin's tide for every flood of her lower
cells and every escape. Marin says the tide turns itself. Kessa says that is exactly what someone who turned it would say. They have argued on the temple
steps for twenty years, while Gus sweeps round them.

She is quiet and warm and a little distant, as people are who have spent their lives listening to something nobody else can hear. She has a coral flower in
her hair, from the reef off the Spires, that a boy gave her before the Fall. He drowned that afternoon.

## People and appearance

Coast folk of Sallowmouth, Noon-touched: twelve at the Fall, she looks thirty-two. Lithe and barefoot (her page always said so), with teal-dyed hair and a
coral flower in it, a wave-blue wrap on the shoulders, a skirt of tiered blue, and sashes of water sweeping round her. The tide lines of her people ring her forearms (she has
lived through twenty-one floods, nine drawn), sea-glass is worked into her hair, and she dances barefoot (Touched on 2026-11-16 by the [revamp plan](../revamp.md): her slippers are gone).

## Voice

Soft and rhythmic, rising and falling as if she were counting a tide; she speaks in short phrases with pauses between. She is patient with everyone except
people who track water into the temple.

## Voice lines

- **Picked:** "Listen. It's turning."

- **Winning:** "The tide decides. It decided."
- **Losing:** "Low water. It comes back."
- **Losing:** "Even the sea gets tired."
- **Meeting Kessa:** "The tide doesn't keep prisoners, Kessa. Neither do I."
- **Meeting Gus:** "Mind the floor, Gus. I'll dance round it."

## Secrets

- She did not choose to keep dancing on the day of the Fall. She could not stop. The tide would not let her. She has been afraid of it ever since, and loves it.
- When she dances at the Climb's gate, the drowned dockers of the first floor stop and watch. One of them is the boy who gave her the coral flower.
- She knows the door Asenet used to leave the Gaol: it opens into the temple's undercroft, under her dry floor. She found it the morning after, wet. She has
  told nobody but Gus.

## What she wants from the Tower

To dance the tide out of the Drowned Harbour, as she danced it out of the temple; and if the Tower holds Sallowmouth as it was, to bring its drowned home.

## Ties

- **[Kessa](kessa.md)**, rival: the Gaol of Oaths lies under Marin's sea, and Kessa blames her tide for every flood and every escape.
- **[Gus](gus.md)**, friend: she danced the water out of his temple, and he has kept her floor wet ever since.
- **[Ostrel](ostrel.md)**, friend: her tide carries his rivers down to the sea, and she asks him nothing.
