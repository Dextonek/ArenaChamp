# 5. The Hundred Years

*Part three, years 3 to 89. The Dial learns to live under a thin sun and settle its quarrels in the Ring, while the Tower fills its
floors and waits.* · [The Long Noon](README.md)

## What the Tower kept, and what it did not show

By the end of the war the Aumbry held the sun's noon, ninety-eight singers, a market, a harbour, a year of the Wood, a canyon in a storm,
the far halls of the Deep and the last night of the war. It took three more in those years, and shut them away deeper than the rest.

It took Frostfall in the **Long Hold**, the winter after the Fall that did not end until the next summer, when the herds starved on the
moors and the shore villages of the lake waited for spring in the dark; the Winter Warden, [Isolde](../heroes/isolde.md), stood on the
lake that whole winter holding the cold still with her spear, and so the hour passed her by, but it took the moors and the villages and a
shepherd's family in every third croft. It took Aubade at dusk on the day of the Fall, the bell quarter round the midsummer lamp, a city
waiting at the foot of the Dawn Road for a hundred singers who would not come home. And it took Gloaming in the **Long Dusk**, the year
without a day, the old and the sick in their beds and the vintners in their cellars and the light going out of the heights.

Those three it did not show anybody, for a hundred years. They are its best pieces. A keeper keeps its best pieces at the back.

## The Ring and the doors

In year 3 the first duel was fought in the straw-rope circle, between a Gloaming house and an Aubade bell quarter, over a debt of grain.
Yoko, the Truce's first champion, stood in the circle as its boundary, and lost no ground. The Truce built stands round him, because he
would not move. Kilnborn masons dug the foundations, and under the circle, in the rock of the crater, they broke into a gallery nobody had
dug, with a hundred doors along it. The doors were already hung. They were already locked. Behind each, when anyone listened, was a wind
from somewhere else: the heat of the Sands, the cold of the north, wet leaves, salt.

They were the Tower's doors. Its roots ran under every realm, and where a root came up under a place it could open a way, the way a root
lifts a paving stone. The Hundred Doors were where its roots met at its foot: its way out into every realm of the Dial, to learn, to reach,
and, when it wanted, to take.

The Truce asked an old lamp-keeper from Frostfall to keep them, because he was the only person who had come to the Ring that year who
did not want to open any. [Wick](../heroes/wick.md) was fifty-three. He walked the gallery once with a lamp, and listened at each door,
and swore before the Arbiter that he would keep them, every one, for as long as he lived. The Aumbry keeps what is sworn in its shadow,
and the gallery lies in its shadow. While Wick keeps a door, the Tower cannot use it.

In year 4 he opened the hundredth door, for a moment, to see. On the other side was the Ring, at night, empty, the stands dark and the
sand unraked: the Ring as it would be a hundred years from then. He shut it, and lit a lamp in front of it, and has kept the lamp lit
since, and told nobody but the Arbiter.

## The Settling

The realms learned to live with the slip. A day ran between twenty and twenty-six of the old hours. The Hourkeepers published the first
Slip-sheet in the autumn of year 0 and every month after. Gloaming's nobility, who had lived through the Long Dusk, found they no longer
needed the light, and some of them found they needed other things, and in year 5 the Countess [Vesper](../heroes/vesper.md) gathered them
as **the Court of Dusk**, which believes the dusk is the Dial's true age. She bought the grain that fed her people through that year on
credit from the Tally, and the dead clerk who keeps it has held the note ever since. In Aubade the families of the hundred swore an oath
before the midsummer lamp to hang the sun again, and became **the Noonsworn**. The Wood folk who swore themselves to the walking Wood grew
horns within a year. The hill people who moved down to the Caldera's heat went grey as ash within a generation and called themselves the
Kilnborn, and called the Fall a gift.

In year 5 the Truce opened **the Gaol of Oaths** under the Drowned Harbour, for those who broke it, with chains forged in the Ember Caldera.
The Gaol lies on one of the Tower's roots, as the deep places of the Dial mostly do, and its first warden, who was clever and frightened of
floods, had a door cut from its lowest cell into the undercroft of the sea temple above, and told nobody.

The Restless got up. The dead of the Dial had always risen now and then, where the crypts lay over a root; after the Fall they rose
everywhere, a little more each year. In the ossuaries under Gloaming, [Mortis](../heroes/mortis.md) gave them work, and measured them, and
found that the curve of their rising matched the Hourkeepers' paling of the light exactly, though he did not know why. The Tower was
drinking the Heart's light as it rose each morning, and a Dial whose light is being kept finds it harder to let anything end.

## Fire and frost

In year 18 a Gloaming girl who looked like a child, though she was twenty-six, went down the Prism Stair into the Deep to break the sun's
light out of its crystals: [Opaline](../heroes/opaline.md). Her sister [Silvane](../heroes/silvane.md) stayed in the Mirror Hall with the
sun's last light shut in a hand mirror. In year 23, on the Night of Lanterns, a junior augur of the Spires called
[Barry Cloudsley](../heroes/barry-cloudsley.md) predicted the worst storm since the Fall a day early, and nobody believed him, and the Coast
folk lit every lamp on the coast to save the ships. In year 40 [Brannoc](../heroes/brannoc.md), master of the great forge in the Caldera,
followed the Winter Warden north to Frostfall Lake, fire after frost; she froze his forge solid to send him home, and it took him a year to
light it. Opaline and Silvane stopped speaking that year, over something neither will name.

## The man who came out of a door

In the spring of year 40 a fox girl in a white robe came to the Hundred Doors and asked Wick to open one.

He refused, politely, as he refused everyone. She came back every night for a month. She told him, in the end, what nobody at the
Ringside knew: that she had fallen out of the Tower on the morning after the Fall, out of a revel of the Fox court that had been going on,
for her, for one night, and for the Dial for three hundred years; that the Fox court did not remember her; that her brother was still
inside, dancing; and that she had spent forty years listening at every crack in the crater for the door behind which the music was. It was
the thirty-first door. She could hear it. Wick put his ear to the door and heard, very far off, a fiddle.

He opened it for the length of one breath. A Briarkin in a violet cape and a tall hat stepped out into the gallery, laughing at something
somebody had said behind him, and the door shut on the music, and Wick locked it, and the man stood blinking in the lamplight and asked
what year it was.

Before Wick could answer, his sister did something that she has never been forgiven for and has never been sorry for. She stole his true
name, out of his mouth, as he drew breath to say it. The Aumbry keeps by name and by hour; a Briarkin with no true name is very hard for
anything to call back. He could never go back to the revel now, and he never forgave her for it, because everyone he loved was still
behind that door.

He decided he was thirty-nine, because he liked the number. He found a stage in the Ringside the same week. He called himself
[The Great Zambini](../heroes/the-great-zambini.md), and swore loyalty, in writing, to every court on the Dial, one after another, in case
any of them could get the others out. None could. He has been thirty-nine for sixty years. He is fading: the Fox court forgot him while he
was kept, and a Briarkin lives only as long as something remembers them, so he performs, every night, to be remembered. His sister calls
herself [Kohaku](../heroes/kohaku.md), and steals names, and has never told the Fox court who she was.

## Fifty years of the Ring

The Ring grew. Its stands rose to hold forty thousand. Every quarrel between realms, factions or houses came to it, and the Arbiter
called every duel, and the dead clerk wrote every one in the Tally, and Cleaver fed winner and loser side by side at the Truce Table after.
A mason's maul and a limestone pillar with a carved face came to the Ring every midsummer, in the hands of a Kilnborn mason called
[Bram](../heroes/bram.md), for the Opening bout against the Mountain; the pillar is called Gerald, and Bram has never told anyone that
Gerald told him what he was before the Deep was dug: one of nine markers set in a ring round the Tower's foot, before anyone remembers, with
four words cut in him that nobody can read.

In year 47 the Moot and the Underforge came closest to war since the Truce, over the Wood's edge at the old Harrow road, and a woodcutter
called [Hank](../heroes/hank.md) settled it in the Ring. In year 50 the Tithe-Keeper, [Mr. Ledger](../heroes/mr-ledger.md), asked to
write Grandma Ironknit into his book, and she knitted him a scarf instead. In year 55 [Professor Puff](../heroes/professor-puff.md)'s gas
floated a hive-tree out of the Wood. In year 60 a Coast boy of twenty-five called [Gus](../heroes/gus.md) took up the broom of the sea
temple, and [Kessa](../heroes/kessa.md), a Kilnborn of the chain-makers, became warden of the Gaol of Oaths. In year 61
[Auriel](../heroes/auriel.md), who was sixty-one and looked eleven, rang the hundredth bell of Aubade at midsummer for the first time: the
bell rung for the choir, which is always one short.

[Anka](../heroes/anka.md) burned and rose, burned and rose. Mortis buried her three times; she never stayed. Each time she was slower to
rise. Cantor walked the Field of Banners singing the dead armies their marching songs. Mum played the Court of Dusk's revels in a porcelain
mask and never made a sound. Every midwinter Cantor came to Gloaming and sang under her window, every verse of the Hymn but the last, which
he did not know. She never answered. She never left the room.

## The first great generation

Between year 70 and year 81 the first great generation since the Fall was born: [Shiori](../heroes/shiori.md), the last of the
Hourkeepers' great calligraphers, whose writing comes true; [Kaia](../heroes/kaia.md), who would call storms; [Ada](../heroes/ada.md), who
would build a hammer that swung itself; [Zephyr](../heroes/zephyr.md), born on the Spires; [Lyra](../heroes/lyra.md), born under a sky that
still kept time; and [Pip](../heroes/pip.md), the fastest courier the Truce ever had. They had never seen the old sun. They grew up under
the thin one and argued with their elders about it with a mixture of awe and boredom. They were the first champions of the Ring who did not
remember the Fall, and the Tower watched them fight, every one, and kept an echo of each, as it had kept an echo of every champion who ever
stood in the Ring, because the Ring lies in its shadow and a keeper keeps what it sees.

In year 75 Auriel was sworn a paladin of the Noonsworn. In year 80 Campana recast the bells of Aubade. In year 86 two Noonsworn
bell-ringers were found asleep on the night road under Gloaming, and never woke, and Auriel demanded a champion of the Court, and fought
Vesper for the first time. Neither won. The bell-ringers were not put to sleep by the Court. They were put to sleep by a shadow of the
Cast that had walked off a wall in Aubade on the day of the Fall and hated the dawn: **the Dawnless**, which the Tower drew to itself as a
lodestone draws iron filings, and which carried sleepers to the Tower's roots, whose hours the Tower took. [Nyx](../heroes/nyx.md) found it
out, and began to hunt it, and did not tell the Countess.

In year 88 Kaia called a storm on a day Barry had forecast clear, and their public argument began. The same year Lyra, aged ten, found a red
star nobody had charted, low over the Tear, and named it for herself.

The Tower had kept the Dial for nearly ninety years since the Fall, and it had kept its gate shut, because it had what it wanted at the top
and a keeper does not open its cupboard to strangers. In the summer of year 90 it found something it had lost, and opened it.
