# The Long Noon

*The story of the Dial, from the making of the Tower to the new sun: the whole arc in a page, and the fourteen chapters.* ·
[Codex](../README.md)

**Where we are.** Chapters 1 to 6, and chapter 7 up to its signs, are the Dial's history, up to now: the spring of the hundredth summer,
where the rest of the Codex stands. From the night the Uncrowned walks into the Truce Table, the Story tells what is still to come.

## The story in a page

Long ago, on a world called Senmere whose sun was going out, a keeper of vaults called Iselm could not bear to lose his dying daughter,
and built a thing that could keep her: a tower of seamless stone that takes a thing at its hour and holds it, as it was, for ever. He cut its
word at its root, and the word was his own name, which meant *keep*. It kept his daughter, and then his people's sun, and then their whole
world, at noon, on its floors. Then, with nothing left to keep and the word still binding it, it crossed the dark between the stars to the next
world with an ageing sun, dragging the evening that world was never allowed to have behind it. Iselm went with it, as its keeper.

That world was the Dial. The Tower, the **Aumbry**, stood at its centre before anyone remembered, and its shadow became the Dial's clock while it
learned every realm. Four hundred years before the Fall its keeper, the Sun Warden, taught Aubade a hymn to sing at its top each noon; the Hymn
drew the sun to the Tower, and only its last verse, sung by the choir's master at midsummer, made it let go. At noon on midsummer of year 0 the
Tower closed its hand. It took the choir in the hour of noon, the master first, before the last verse could be sung, and took the sun's noon;
the sun's body cracked in its grip and fell. One singer, a hair behind the beat, was left: Mum, who knew the last verse, and has not made a sound
since. The Tower took the realms' worst hours one by one, the drowned harbour, the walking Wood, the storm, the dark halls, the war's last night;
the realms went to war over the sun's pieces, and then swore the Truce of the Ring in the Tower's shadow, which the Tower took as its own law.

For ninety years its gate stayed shut. In year 90 Mum won a duel in the Ring, and the Tower, unable to take her, took the hour of her win (the
Tally's blank page) and opened its gate to lure her in; the Dial began to climb. In the hundredth summer the exiled queen who had read the truth
on a tomb wall tells the Truce Table what the Tower is, and that it means to take everything at noon on the Hundredth Midsummer and leave. The
Truce sends a company up the Climb, and its officers hold the Ring. The company frees one kept hour after another, meets the Tower's keeper, and
learns that a made thing obeys the last word written on it. At noon on the Hundredth Midsummer Mum sings the last verse, a fox spirit steals the
Tower's word out of its keeper's mouth, a showman makes it vanish in his hat, a courier runs a new word in a calligrapher's hand a mile down to the
Tower's root, and a golem-maker cuts it there: *remember*. Below, a knight sets the last light of the old sun into the fallen Heart; above, the
Heart's light, the sun's two last sparks and the Firebird meet the kept noon, and a new sun rises over the Tower. Senmere has its evening at last,
the Unmaker goes home into it and ends, the Tear is sewn shut, and the Mountain steps out of the Ring, and the shadow moves.

The Tower still stands, asleep, remembering instead of keeping, and is letting its keepings go one at a time. Three realms and older hours are
still inside it, something came down through the Tear before it closed, and a red star is coming.

## Where the modes are in the story

- **The duels** are fought in the Ring, on the Tower's threshold, under the Truce the Tower keeps; the Tower watches every one and keeps an echo
  of each champion ([chapter 5](05-the-hundred-years.md)).
- **The Climb** is the Tower's collection: five realms taken at their worst hours, their people, their rulers, and its keepings of the sun's
  pieces as its crowns ([chapters 6](06-the-gate.md), [8](08-the-sea-and-the-wood.md), [9](09-the-sands-and-the-deep.md),
  [10](10-the-war.md)). After the ending it is the Tower's memory of those hours, and is still climbed ([chapter 14](14-the-floors-above.md)).
- **The Raid** is fought in Noonfall, the hall above the Heart, against the sun's pieces themselves, which the Tower bound to guard the Heart it
  drank: the Warden's sentinel, Ash and Ember, the Heart of Noon. Each win brightened the next morning ([chapter 6](06-the-gate.md)); the last
  Raid carried the Ember down ([chapter 12](12-the-last-verse.md)).
- **The Trials**, the weekly Tower, are the Tower measuring the Dial's champions with its echoes of them ([chapter 6](06-the-gate.md)).

## Where the heroes come from

Most heroes are Dial-born. Six are not: **Returned** (taken by the Tower long ago and come back out), **Half-kept** (half taken in the hour of
the Fall), **Spilled** (made by the Fall's broken taking) and **Followed** (came after the Tower through the Tear). The heroes whose trades seem
odd for a world at war (a referee, a cook, a courier, a herdsman) are odd because of the Ring, not the Tower: the Truce made its officers out
of whoever was standing at the Tower's foot when the war ended.
