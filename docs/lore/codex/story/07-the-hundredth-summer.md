# 7. The Hundredth Summer

*Part three, year 100: now. The Dial is told what the Tower is, and does not want to believe it, and then does.* ·
[The Long Noon](README.md)

## Signs

The hundredth summer came with everything at once.

The Tear widened by a hand's breadth between the spring tide and the first warm week. The Hourkeepers' Slip-sheet showed days of thirty
hours in the north. [Sefu](../heroes/sefu.md) stood on the Glass Flats at the spring festival, as he had every spring for ninety-nine
years, and for the first time the sand under his feet was colder than the air. [Anka](../heroes/anka.md) burned in a duel at the Sandstone
Canyon and took nearly a minute to rise. The coal in [Brannoc](../heroes/brannoc.md)'s chest burned lower, and so did the Caldera, and he
measured both and told nobody. The Restless got up in Gloaming in numbers [Mortis](../heroes/mortis.md) had never recorded, and his curve
and the Hourkeepers' paling, laid one over the other on his desk, were still the same line. The Walking Wood walked east faster than in any
year since the Fall, toward the Tower, and the villages at its edge could not keep up. [Lyra](../heroes/lyra.md)'s red star, which she had
found at ten, was larger. The climbers who went up the Tower that spring came down fewer.

And the light was thinner every morning, and nobody could say it was not.

*That is where the Dial stands now. From here on, the Story tells what is still to come.*

## The Uncrowned at the Table

On the night after the spring's last duel, at the Truce Table in the hall behind the Ring, with winner and loser eating side by side and
two hundred champions and officers on the benches, a woman in faded royal linen of wine and tarnished gold walked in from the dark and sat
down at the end of the table.

[Kessa](../heroes/kessa.md), the Chainwarden, was up before anyone else saw who it was, and the chains on her forearms were already loose.
[Madame Cleaver](../heroes/madame-cleaver.md) was quicker. She put a bowl of truce-pot in front of the Uncrowned queen and a spoon in her
hand, and said, "Eat," and Asenet ate; and whoever has eaten at the Truce Table may not be struck in anger outside the Ring. Kessa stood
where she was with her chains in her hands and her jaw set, and the whole table waited.

Asenet finished the bowl. Then she stood up and told them what she had read on the wall of the first king's tomb a hundred and one years
before.

*The pin keeps suns. It kept one before ours. It will keep ours. Do not sing to it.*

She told them about the painting under it, a country of terraces under a red sun that did not move. She told them about the man in gold and
white who had come through the Gaol's secret door on the night of the spring tide, and what he had said: *it will be at noon, on the
hundredth midsummer.* She said that the Tower had taken the sun, that it was a thing that kept, that the floors of the Climb were not
copies but the realms' own lost hours with their lost people in them, and that at noon on the Hundredth Midsummer it would take whatever was
left.

Then she sat down again, and the table laughed.

Not all of it. Sefu did not laugh; he was white with rage, because his sister had said in front of the whole Ring that the Father had not
come home to the Sands but been stolen, and that she had known for a hundred years. [Auriel](../heroes/auriel.md) did not laugh, because she
had been raised on the Hymn. The Countess [Vesper](../heroes/vesper.md) did not laugh, because she was watching who did.

[Wick](../heroes/wick.md) stood up. He was a hundred and fifty years old by the count, and looked seventy, and he had never in a hundred
years spoken at the Truce Table. He told them about the hundredth door, and the Ring on the other side of it, at night, empty, a hundred
years from the night he opened it. He had worked it out years ago: that night was four summers away. Whatever emptied the Ring would
have done it by then.

The table stopped laughing.

## The pieces on the table

What came out that night, one champion at a time, was a hundred years of things each of them had kept to themselves.

[Bram](../heroes/bram.md) set Gerald on the table and showed them the four words cut into the limestone under the carved face, which nobody
had ever read, and said there were nine stones like him round the Tower's foot, and he had found three. [Kohaku](../heroes/kohaku.md) said she
had seen that script before: on the lanterns of the revel she had danced at, three hundred years ago, in the Tower, which she had fallen out
of on the morning after the Fall. The Great Zambini, who had never once told the Ringside where he came from, took off his hat, and said
quietly that it was true, and that he had come out of the thirty-first door in year 40, and that everyone he had ever loved was still behind
it, dancing. [Hesk](../heroes/hesk.md) said the old word on the wall of the cave by the black water, where his first golem had walked into
the dark, had been in that script, and that it was gone now, and his own name was cut there in its place.

[Tempo](../heroes/tempo.md) read them a line from the Hourkeepers' Noon Folio, which had never been read outside Frostfall: *it is dying,
and something hastens it.* He told them he had seen himself on the War floors of the Climb, a heartbeat ahead, going up. [Barry
Cloudsley](../heroes/barry-cloudsley.md) said, for once without blaming Kaia, that he believed the Tower was taking the weather into itself,
floor by floor. [Mortis](../heroes/mortis.md) laid out his curve. [Grandma Ironknit](../heroes/grandma-ironknit.md) said she had looked for the
Tower's thread in the cloth of the Dial for three hundred years and never found it, and that she had come to think it was not in the cloth,
but was the cloth. [Mr. Ledger](../heroes/mr-ledger.md) closed his red book and said, in the voice he used for debtors, that if half of this
was true, the Tower had taken a sun, a choir, a market, nine realms' hours and every climber who had not come down, and had never once paid
for any of it.

Then [Mum](../heroes/mum.md) got up from her place among the Court of Dusk, and walked out of the hall, and nobody stopped her, because
nobody ever did.

## The page

She came back with the Tally.

Ledger let her take it; he did not know why. She set it on the Truce Table among the bowls and opened it at the page for the Opening's duel
of year 90, the blank page, the one Shiori's ink would not take on. Then she took a stick of chalk from her sleeve, the chalk she draws her
walls with in the Ring, and wrote on it.

The chalk took.

What she wrote is still on the page: *Cantor of Aubade, for the bell quarter, against Mum of the Court of Dusk. Mum. A wall.* As she wrote it
the hour came back to everyone in the hall who had been in the stands that day, all at once, like a word on the tip of the tongue: the noon,
the forty thousand, the voice filling the stands, the man who walked into a wall that was not there and sat down on the sand and laughed. The
Arbiter saw the face behind the arm he had raised. It was a porcelain mask with a crack behind the left eye.

Cantor sat very still. He had fought her, and lost, and laughed, and had not remembered for ten years.

Mum wrote one more line, under the first, on the Tally's page, where nobody can rub it out. She has not written anything else on it since.

*It opened for me. It wants what I know.*

[Cantor](../heroes/cantor.md) understood first. Mum knew the Hymn's last verse. The master had taught it to her the week before the Fall, in
case anything happened to him, and something had. He had always thought she was afraid of the verse because she had failed the Hymn. He saw
now that she was afraid of it because it mattered, and that the Tower had opened its gate for her, and left it open for ten years, the way you
leave a door open for a cat.

## The council

The Truce Table sat until the slip made it the next day.

Every faction said what it had always said, and every one of them heard, for the first time, how it sounded against what Asenet had read.
The Noonsworn would carry the Ember of Aubade to the Noon Seat and hang the sun again. The Court of Dusk would keep the Seat empty, until
Vesper learned from Kohaku that the Tower held Gloaming's Long Dusk among its keepings, the year without a day, with the sick in their beds and
the vintners in their cellars, and that her steward's daughter, who had gone to the midsummer market, was kept with the market; then the Countess said nothing at all for a long time. The Dune Throne wanted the Seat for its king,
and its king, for the first time in a hundred years, did not say so. The Underforge wanted the Heart left below. The Moot wanted the roots cut.
The Windward wanted whatever worked. The Hourkeepers wanted to understand it first, and [Shiori](../heroes/shiori.md) said, gently, that they
had had three hundred years.

It was the Arbiter who settled it, because that is what he is for. [Referee Whistler](../heroes/referee-whistler.md) stood up on the bench
and read them the Truce.

The first clause: no realm makes war over the sun again. So no realm would go up alone. The fourth: the Tower's gate opens only for a champion
of the Ring. So whoever went would go as champions, under the Truce, with every faction in the company and none of them in charge. The fifth:
every duel is written in the Tally, and every debt it settles is paid. The Tower had sat in the Truce's shadow for a hundred years, he said,
and kept the Truce's fourth clause cut in its own gate, and it was bound by the others as much as by that one. It owed. The Truce would go
up and present the bill.

"A challenge isn't real," said [Pip](../heroes/pip.md), from the far end of the table, "until it's delivered by hand." And Ledger began,
without being asked, to write it.

## What they knew, and what they did not

They did not know yet what the Tower was, or where it came from, or what its word was. They did not know that the man in gold and white at
the top had made it. They knew enough.

They knew that every made thing has a word, because Hesk had made a hundred golems and each obeyed whoever had written the last word on its
brow. They knew Shiori's writing comes true, a little, for a while, and once for ever. They knew that Kohaku can steal a word out of the air,
and had once stolen her own true name off a golem's brow, where someone who should not have known it had written it. They knew Mum held a verse
the Tower wanted. They knew that [Itzel](../heroes/itzel.md) of the Underforge carried a word the Caldera's fire had given
her at fifteen, which she believed was the sun's own name and had never said aloud; and that the fire, she said that night, was waiting for
someone to come down to it, and had told her who. She looked down the table at [Orren](../heroes/orren.md), the Sunbearer, with the Ember of
Aubade in its brass shrine on his back. The Noonsworn had always said the Ember went up.

And they knew they had until noon on the Hundredth Midsummer, which was nine weeks away.

## The company

It went up in the second week of the summer, through the gate, with the Arbiter's horn and forty thousand people in the stands who had come to
see the Ring and stayed to watch the gate.

It was not an army. The Truce allowed none. It was a company of champions, one at least from every faction, and some who belonged to none:
Mum, for whom the gate had opened; Cantor, who would not let her go up without him a second time; Auriel of the Noonsworn, whose father had
been a tenor of the hundred; the Countess Vesper, who wanted to see what was in the Seat before anyone else did, and [Nyx](../heroes/nyx.md) in
her shadow; Sefu, without his crown's guard, and his sister Asenet, whom he would not look at; Shiori, with her brush and the Hourkeepers'
blank books, to write down everything; Tempo, to find himself; The Great Zambini and Kohaku, who knew the Tower from the inside; [Sir
Whiskers](../heroes/sir-whiskers.md), for his brother; Anka, to find the rest of her fire; and [Thessa](../heroes/thessa.md) the Sky-Stitcher,
who meant to sew the Tear shut from close to. At the gate, two Briarkin children who looked ten were found to be inside Shiori's book-pack, and
were not sent home, because by the time anyone noticed they were on the second floor.

The rest of the Dial did its own part. Each chapter's heroes were waiting at their chapter's gate: the Windward at the Sea, the Moot at the Wood,
the Throne's court at the Sands, the Underforge at the Deep, and at the War, a cook. Below the Ring the Raid's pairs went down to Noonfall, with the
Sunbearer among them, carrying the Ember the other way. And the Truce's own officers stayed at the Ring, to hold it: the Arbiter, the
Tithe-Keeper, the Doorwarden, the Saltwarden, the Chainwarden, the Feastmistress and the Mountain, who had not stepped out of his circle in a
hundred years and was not going to start now.
