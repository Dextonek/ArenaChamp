# 8. The Sea and the Wood

*Part four, the Climb, floors 1 to 20. A drowned harbour that is still drowning, and a forest in its first year of walking.* ·
[The Long Noon](README.md)

## The harbour at dawn

The first floor of the Tower is a morning a hundred years old.

The company came through the gate into grey light and the smell of salt, onto a quay above a harbour full of wrecks. The sea was in the
streets of Sallowmouth and still rising. Lanterns burned on the docks. Gulls wheeled over everything. Every seventy-eight seconds the tide
came in, fifteen seconds of it, and the tidal flats flooded and the company ran for the high ground, and then it went out again, and the
harbour bell rang. [Shiori](../heroes/shiori.md) timed it, and wrote down that it was the only thing in the Tower that kept perfect time.

The bell was rung by a woman in the sea temple's tower, the **Bell-Warden**, who had rung it all night to call the boats home, and who
did not know that the night had been a hundred years long. She came down to the quay to meet them, because strangers on the first floor
were rare and she was a polite woman. She asked if they had seen her husband's boat. They had not. A man called Tam, a net-mender, had
lost his way between the net sheds and the chapel, and the **Net-Father**, who mended the harbour's nets on the quay as if nothing had
happened, asked them to lead him home. [Pim and Pom](../heroes/pim-and-pom.md) did, by tagging each other down every alley until one of
them found his door. The **Lamp-Bearer**, out on the shore road, kept the shore lamps lit for the boats, and the lamps kept going out.

[Gus](../heroes/gus.md) had come up with them as far as the first floor, with his broom. He had been here once before and seen the sea
temple as it was on that morning: filthy, flooding, and full of frightened people. He went straight to it. The company left him there,
sweeping the water off the temple floor toward the door, and telling the frightened people, in his calm way, to mind where they stepped,
and leading them, one by one, up out of the water to the temple's high floor, where it was dry. Nobody had done that for them in a hundred
years. When the company looked back from the harbour road the temple's lamps were lit, and its floor was wet, which, as Gus says, is how
you know it is clean.

## The Tide Dancer at the gate

At the end of the harbour, where the road went up toward a gate of grey stone, the Tower's echo of [Marin](../heroes/marin.md) held the
gate shut, as it had for ten years: a barefoot girl in sea-glass with the tide in ribbons in her hands, kept from her first duel in the
Ring sixty years before.

The real Marin had come up with them for this. She had fought her echo many times, and won, and the echo had always stood aside and smiled.
This time she did not fight. She stood in front of it on the quay and danced, and the tide came in, and went out, in her hands and in its
hands at once, the same tide, and the echo, which was only a keeping of her at one hour, could not keep up with her at all the hours since.
It stopped. It looked at her with her own face and asked, in her own voice, the one thing the Tower had kept it wondering for sixty years:

"Are you still dancing?"

"Yes," said Marin. "I never stopped. The sea wouldn't let me."

The echo stepped aside. Down on the quays, the drowned dockers had stopped working to watch her, as they always did when she danced, and
one of them, a boy with a coral flower behind his ear, lifted his hand.

## The drowned and their masters

The rest of the Sea was the same harbour, rearranged and harder each floor, and the worst of that morning in each.

On the first and second floors the **Drowned Admiral** fought them from the wreck of his flagship across the harbour mouth, with anchor and
cannon and the tide. He had ordered every ship to stay at anchor to ride out the strange tide, and gone down with all of them, and he still
believed he had given the right order. [Sir Whiskers](../heroes/sir-whiskers.md) duelled him on the wreck's tilted deck, and lost a cuff
and kept his life, and told him, as the Admiral went down under his own anchor, that the right order is the one you can still give the
morning after. The **Salt King** of the third floor weighed them and found them wanting. **Mother of Wrecks** lit false lights on the
headland of the fourth. On the fifth the **Tide Judge** set the tide as their sentence, and [Auriel](../heroes/auriel.md) broke it with
her shields. The **Pearl Tyrant** of the sixth sent his pale diver after them. On the seventh floor was **the Bell Below**: the harbour's old
cracked bell, thrown into the sea when the new one was cast, which rings underwater, and something answers. The something was the new bell, the
Bell-Warden's, ringing back. The bellwright who had cast them both, [Campana](../heroes/campana.md), had come up to this floor once before and
wept on the quay; this time she swam down, and put her bronze-stained hands on the old bell, and struck it once with her own hammer, on its true
note, and it stopped answering, and hung still in the green water, and she said she would bring it home when the Tower let it go. On the eighth
the **Lantern Leviathan** came up out of the deep sea with its lantern burning, and on the ninth the **Gull Queen**, who had fed the gulls
from her hand for forty years, and whom they would not leave.

Each ruler, when it fell, said the same thing, in its own way. *It was the worst morning. Somebody had to keep it.*

## The Warden as he was

At the tenth floor, the Sea's crown, on a court of white stone above the harbour with a great dial set into the floor, a man in gold and
white was on one knee with both hands flat on the stone.

It was the Sun Warden, or the Tower's keeping of him, as he was in the instant it took the sun: struck from inside, kneeling at his dial.
When they came near he stood up, and received them as if they were late, and fought them, and he was very strong, because the moment it had
kept was the moment the Aumbry had been strongest. The dial struck every few heartbeats, and where it struck, the floor burned gold. It took
the whole company to bring him down, and [Anka](../heroes/anka.md) burned, and rose, slowly.

When he went down on one knee again, as he had been, his dial struck nine times, and stopped, and he spoke, in a tongue none of them knew,
one line. [Mum](../heroes/mum.md) knew it. She stood quite still. It was the first line of the last verse.

[Cantor](../heroes/cantor.md) looked at her and saw her lips move. No sound came out. She shut her eyes.

## The Wood walking

The eleventh floor was the Walking Wood in its first year: trees that did not stand still, paths that moved while you were on them, a
moss chapel at the forest's edge with its door open, and a great dark heart to the forest that was not dark, because something at its centre
glowed.

[Fenna](../heroes/fenna.md) and [Hank](../heroes/hank.md) were waiting at the Wood's gate, where the Tower's echo of Fenna held it. Fenna
did not fight her echo. She planted a flower at its feet, and stood among the flowers with it, and they grew, and the echo, which had only the
strength of one hour, had no flowers of its own; it stood aside. Then the two of them led the company to the forest's heart.

The **Elder Root** stood there, a trunk too wide to see round, roots that arched over the paths like bridges, a core that glowed, and three
great roots that fed it, running out through the soil to the edge of the floor and into the Tower's wall. It was the oak Fenna had planted at
twelve, which had walked away with the Wood on the day of the Fall. The Tower had drawn the whole Wood toward itself through its roots, and
drawn this tree furthest, into the forest's heart, and set it to drink from the Tower's own root, as a cutting is set in water. Fenna had stood
in front of it before and known it, and it had known her. Now she put her hand on its bark and asked it, aloud, to let go.

It could not. It was kept. Hank cut the three roots that fed it, one after another, with the great green-edged axe, while the bark hounds
came out of the dark for him and the company held them off; the third root, where it went into the wall, was the one he had cut ten years
before, and the cut on his hand opened again and bled the same glowing sap. When the third root parted the Elder Root shuddered from crown to
foot, and its core dimmed, and the paths of the forest, for a moment, stood still.

"Not yet," said Fenna, to the tree, and to Hank. "But it will."

## The two abbesses

The keeper of the Wood's ten floors is the **Moss-Abbess**, who keeps the moss chapel at the forest's edge and sounds a horn at dusk to help
the Wood sleep. There were two of them.

One was a woman of forty in a moss-green habit, with a horn at her belt, keeping the chapel in the Wood's first year as if it were the only
year there had ever been. The other was an old woman of the same face, in a faded habit of the same green, who sat beside her on the chapel
step and helped her with the horn. The old one had walked in at the gate in year 90, looking for the chapel she had kept long ago, and found
it, and found herself in it, kept, and sat down beside herself. She had been helping herself keep the paths for ten years. She did not want
to go home. She was not unhappy. She had simply stopped.

[Vela](../heroes/vela.md), the moth priestess, had come up the Wood's floors to find her. She had dreamed the Abbess was alive, and she was.
Vela sat on the step between the two of them, the young one and the old one, and folded her pale green wings round them both, and did not lay
them to sleep. She told the old Abbess what the Wood was doing outside: walking east faster than ever, toward the Tower, because the Tower was
pulling it, and the villages at its edge could not keep up. The old Abbess listened. At the end she stood up, slowly, and said she supposed she
had better come and help, and took her horn, and came. The young Abbess stayed, and sounded hers at dusk, alone.

It was the first time anyone kept by the Tower had chosen to leave it. Shiori wrote it down. It was not the last.

## Thorns and the lost

The Wood's rulers were what the Wood had been worst at in its first year. The **Moss Warden**, the Moot's first warden, guarded it from
everyone, including the folk. The **Thorn Mother** wove thorn hedges round everything. The **Antler King** led the bark hounds in the hunt. The
**Hollow Elder** carried a light inside it that was not a fire. The **Briar Witch** had sold curses at the Wood's edge and been taken by her own
briars. The **Lantern Root** lured the lost with an amber light, the **Grey Oak** would not fall, and the **Mire Heart** swallowed what walked
across it.

Among the thorn sprites, the faded Briarkin who half came back inside the Tower because nobody remembered them well enough to bring them back
whole, was the greatest of them, **Grandmother Thorn**, who remembered being of the Spindle court and hated Grandma Ironknit for being remembered.
She came for Pim and Pom in a hedge of thorns. The twins stopped running for once and told her, together, that they were Ironknit's, and that
Ironknit had dropped three stitches at the Fall and looked for them in the cloth every night for a hundred years, and that she would look for
Grandmother Thorn too, if they told her the name. Grandmother Thorn told them. The thorns let them through.

On the Wood's floors the Moot's young Huntmaster, [Arden](../heroes/arden.md), found at last the end of a trail he had followed for ten years: a
Moot speaker, the one killed by the Gloaming road in year 90, sitting under a hive-tree in the Wood's first year, alive, kept, puzzled. Beside the
speaker's footprints in the soft ground were other prints, long and flat, that left no weight: a shadow's. Arden had always known they were not
Nyx's. [Nyx](../heroes/nyx.md), who had come up in the Countess's shadow, crouched beside him in the dark and looked at them a long time, and said,
"The Dawnless," which was the first word she had ever spoken to him. They hunted it together after that.

## The sparks

At the twentieth floor, the Wood's crown, in a clearing the trees would not go near, burned **Ash and Ember**: the sun's two last sparks, as the
Tower had kept them at the instant they fell, one a hunched brute of basalt and magma on his knuckles, one a flame in a burnt mantle with two
lanterns orbiting her. The Wood feared fire above everything, and the trees round the clearing leaned away.

They fought, because the Tower had set them there and they could not do otherwise. When they were down, they did not go out. They looked at
[Anka](../heroes/anka.md), as they had looked at her once before, and she asked them what she had asked them then: whether an Ember could
choose to go home.

"Not yet," they said, as before. And then, because she had come further this time, the flame in the mantle said more.

"When the hand opens."
