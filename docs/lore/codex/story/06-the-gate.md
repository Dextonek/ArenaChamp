# 6. The Gate

*Part three, years 90 to 99. A duel nobody remembers opens the Tower's gate, and the Dial begins to climb.* · [The Long Noon](README.md)

## The duel of year 90

In the spring of year 90 [Cantor](../heroes/cantor.md) did something he had not done in ninety years of singing under a window. He
challenged the Court of Dusk in the Ring.

The quarrel was an old one, of the kind the Ring was made for: a bell quarter of Aubade against a house of Gloaming. The bell quarter
claimed one of its daughters, a singer of the hundred who had gone west in the first year and never come home; the Court said she was
its harlequin, and free, and staying. Cantor stood for the bell quarter. He had told nobody why, after ninety years, that spring; he
did not know himself. He was a hundred and twenty and looked forty-eight, and he thought, perhaps, that if he could not sing her home he could fight
for her, and that she would have to look at him.

The Court's champion was named on the morning of the duel, at Mum's own asking. It was Mum.

They fought at noon on midsummer, at the Opening of the Ring, in front of forty thousand. [Referee Whistler](../heroes/referee-whistler.md)
called it. [Mr. Ledger](../heroes/mr-ledger.md) wrote it. Cantor sang on the beat, as he always did, and his voice filled the stands, and
Mum made no sound at all: her walls came up where nobody could see them, and her ball came out of nowhere, and the chalk lines she left on
the sand drew the fight as it went, as if the world had to be drawn back in by hand. It was close. In the end Cantor walked into a wall that
was not there, and sat down on the sand, and laughed, which nobody had heard him do since the Fall. The Arbiter raised the winner's arm: a
pale glove, a white sleeve in ivory diamonds.

It was noon on midsummer. The Tower's shadow lay across the Ring, as it always did, over Yoko's circle. Down that shadow, for the first time
in ninety years, the Aumbry looked at the voice that had got away.

It knew her. It had taken the choir in the hour of noon and missed her by a hair, and for ninety years she had lived a hair behind every
hour there was, unknitted, out of its reach. It wanted her more than anything on the Dial, because she was the last who knew the verse
that had made it let go of a sun, every midsummer, for four hundred years. It reached for her and could not hold her. So it took what it
could hold: the hour of the duel. The duel, its winner, its record, everyone's memory of it.

Forty thousand people blinked. The sand was raked. The sun was lower. Nobody could remember who had won, or quite why they were there. In
the Tally-house, the page for the Opening's duel was blank, and Ledger, who had written it, checked the ink and found that the page had
never been written on. The Arbiter remembered raising an arm and could not see the face; it was behind a porcelain mask, but he did not
remember that either. Cantor remembered challenging. Mum remembered nothing.

And on the Tower's grey face the gate, which had been shut since the Fall, stood open.

## Who went in

It had opened for Mum. The Aumbry keeps what is sworn in its shadow, and the Truce had sworn that the gate opens only for a champion of the
Ring; so it opened for the champion it wanted, and it did not close again, because now it wanted her to come, and a cupboard left open is how
you catch a cat. Mum did not know it was for her. She went back to Gloaming. She did not go in.

Others did. For a week the Truce argued about who had the right, and then the Arbiter ruled, standing under the cut letters on the gate:
the clause said a champion of the Ring, and the Ring had a great many. Any champion who had won there might climb.

The first through the gate was [Marin](../heroes/marin.md). She had heard, from the Ring's stands, a sound coming out of it: the harbour
bell of Sallowmouth, which she had last heard on the morning after the Fall, ringing the drowned home. Inside, the first floor of the Tower
was a harbour at dawn, with the sea in its streets and still rising, and a woman in the sea temple's tower ringing the bell. The Bell-Warden
did not know Marin. She did not know how long she had been ringing. Down on the quays the drowned dockers were working, and one of them was
the boy who had given Marin a coral flower.

At the end of the harbour, where the road went up toward a gate of grey stone, a woman stood holding the gate shut: a dancer of the sea temple,
barefoot, in sea-glass, with the tide in ribbons in her hands. It was Marin. It was the Tower's echo of her, the one it had kept from her first
duel in the Ring sixty years before, set up there as a keeper sets its best piece at the end of a room. Marin fought herself on the
quay, and won, and the echo stood aside, and smiled at her with her own face.

The same summer the Noonsworn chose a Bearer for the Ember of Aubade, to carry it up the Tower now that the Tower could be climbed: a broad,
slow knight called [Orren](../heroes/orren.md), who swore never to set it down except to fight. The same summer the Dawnless carried a speaker
of the Greenwood Moot through the thickets by the Gloaming road and in at the open gate, and left a trail that a young hunter called
[Arden](../heroes/arden.md) would follow for ten years and never tell the Moot about. And the Moss-Abbess, an old friend of the moth
priestess [Vela](../heroes/vela.md), walked in at the gate one evening to look for the chapel she had kept in the Wood's first year, and did
not come out.

## The Climb grows

The Tower showed its collection one room at a time, as climbers proved they could reach it. At each chapter's first gate it set the echo of a champion of that realm; at the gates above, the echoes of whichever champions it had kept from the Ring, of any realm, drawn to its gates as it draws everything.

**The Sea**, floors 1 to 10, was Sallowmouth on the morning the water came, the shipyard and the drowned chapel and the lighthouse and the
point of rock the gulls would not leave, ruled by the worst of that morning: the Drowned Admiral in the wreck of his flagship, who had ordered
every ship to stay at anchor and went down with all of them; the Salt King, Mother of Wrecks, the Tide Judge, the Pearl Tyrant; the ringer of the harbour's old cracked bell, who wears it and rings it underwater; the Lantern Leviathan and the Gull Queen. At its tenth floor stood the Sun Warden himself, or the
Tower's keeping of him, as he was at the moment it took the sun, gold and white with his great dial striking, who met each climber as if they
were late, and fought them.

**The Wood**, floors 11 to 20, opened in year 92. Its gate was held by the Tower's echo of [Fenna](../heroes/fenna.md), and at its heart stood the Elder Root, an oak in an old man's shape, drinking from a root of the Tower's own: Fenna went in, and knew it for the oak she had planted at
twelve, and it knew her. [Hank](../heroes/hank.md) cut a piece of the root where it went up into the Tower's wall, and it bled sap that glowed,
and the cut on his hand has not healed. Its rulers were the Moss Warden, the Thorn Mother, the Antler King, the Hollow Elder, the Briar Witch, the
Lantern Root, the Grey Oak and the Mire Heart, and at its crown burned Ash and Ember, the sun's two last sparks, as the Tower kept them at the
moment they fell. When Anka reached them, they looked at her a long time, and said, "Not yet."

**The Sands**, floors 21 to 30, opened in year 94: the Storm Canyon on the night of the great storm, its gate held by the echo of
[Sefu](../heroes/sefu.md) on his first night as king. The Scarab Colossus and her son the Scarab King, the Dune Colossus, the Brass Sphinx, the
Glass Serpent, the Mirage Lord, the Sand Tyrant, the Red Jackal and the Sun-Eater ruled it, and at its crown was **the Heart of Noon**: the
Tower's keeping of the sun's heart in the moment it fell, a sphere of white fire in a hall of black glass. It is the closest any climber has come
to looking at what was taken.

**The Deep**, floors 31 to 40, opened in year 96: the far halls on the night their crystals went out, with the Echo-Hermit listening to the rock
and the gate held by the echo of [Opaline](../heroes/opaline.md). The Prism Mother, the Hollow Queen, the Lamp Warden, the Glass Abbot, Mother of
Echoes, the Seam King, the Blind Cantor, the Gem Tyrant and the Drowned Lantern ruled it. At its crown the Sun Warden waited a second time, and this
time it was not the Tower's keeping of him. It was Iselm himself, come down from the Sun Court, because the word made him guard the Tower's crowns
and he wished to see, with his own eyes, who was coming. He remembered every climber he had met on the tenth floor.

**The War**, floors 41 to 50, opened in year 98: the Field of Banners on the night before the Ninth Field, with both armies on it and the Herald
of both banners going from camp to camp trying to arrange a truce, and the Last Marshal guarding both. Its gate was held by the echo of the Unmaker,
standing at the Field's edge where it had walked a hundred years before, because the war could not end while it stood there. The Iron Marshal, the
Siege Lord, the Ashen General, the Banner Knight, the Crow King, the Black Herald, the War Drummer and the Grey Captain ruled it, and at its crown Ash
and Ember burned again, under whatever was above. [Madame Cleaver](../heroes/madame-cleaver.md) climbed to the forty-first floor, found a woman at a
cauldron feeding both armies from one fire, and came down without a word. [Sir Whiskers](../heroes/sir-whiskers.md) met a lynx knight on his last
life in a helm he would not take off, and knew his brother's voice, and told nobody.

Few climbers came down from above the twentieth floor. The Ringside said they had died. They had not, mostly. They had stood still somewhere,
in a hall of drowned bells or a forest that would not stop walking, and grieved, and been kept.

## The Hall of Noon

In year 93 the Underforge's drill, which [Brannoc](../heroes/brannoc.md) had begun because he wanted the Heart's fire brought up through the rock,
broke into a hall beneath the Ring's west tunnel, between a white court of fallen stone and a pit of embers, with the Hall of Noon under it all.
The Truce closed it within the hour, and then, because it was a quarrel between the Underforge and everyone else, opened it to the Ring.

What waited there was not the Tower's keeping of the sun's pieces but the pieces themselves, and they were not free. The Tower had bound them to
guard the Heart it drank: a sentinel of the Sun Court made in the Warden's likeness, stone and gold round a small trapped sun; Ash and Ember up out of
the Cinder Pit, two shapes of fire that fought whatever came near the Heart; and the Heart itself, which was no longer only a sun's heart but a
furious, wounded thing that had been drunk from every morning for ninety years. The Truce named the place **Noonfall**, and let champions go down in
pairs, with its leave, to fight them.

The Hourkeepers measured what happened when they won. On the morning after a pair beat the Heart of Noon, the light that rose through the Tower was a
little brighter, for a day, because for that day less of it was drunk. It was the first thing anyone had done in ninety years that made the morning
better. The Ringside calls it **the Raid**, and the Truce keeps the count.

## The Trials

In year 95 a second stair opened off the Ring's west tunnel, below the first. It led up, through a tower of rooms that were never the same two weeks
running: halls of fire and halls of dark, crystals to break and floors to hold, and on every tenth room a hero of the Ring standing guard, drawn huge,
with a boss's moves. Champions fought their way up it and came back down, and the next week it had changed. They called it **the Trials**, the weekly
Tower.

The heroes who guarded its rooms were the Tower's echoes of them, the ones it had kept from every duel since the Ring began. The Trials were the Aumbry
measuring the Dial's champions, the way a collector weighs a piece in the hand before deciding where to put it. It was choosing which of them to keep.

## The door under the sea temple

In year 97, at the Homecoming table in Tamerit, [Asenet](../heroes/asenet.md) struck her brother, and was sent to the Gaol of Oaths, where
[Kessa](../heroes/kessa.md) chained her in the lowest cell. Asenet was content there. She had nowhere to go, and nobody had believed her in
ninety-seven years, and a cell is quiet.

On the night of a spring tide in year 99 someone came to her through the old warden's secret door, from the other side: from the undercroft of the
sea temple, and below it, from the root of the Tower that runs under the harbour. He was tall and old and wore gold and white, and he stooped under the
low arch. He could not tell her what he was. He could not tell her what the Tower was, or what it meant to do. The word would not let him. But a door is
only a door, and he could open one.

"It will be at noon," he said, "on the hundredth midsummer. Tell them what you read."

Then he went back down into the dark, and left the door open, and the Uncrowned walked out of the Gaol of Oaths through the temple's undercroft,
leaving wet footprints on the floor, which a temple keeper called Gus found at dawn and mopped up before the Chainwarden came, without quite knowing why.
[Marin](../heroes/marin.md) found the floor still wet. Kessa found the page in her grandmother's chain-book a month later. Wick knew the door was not one of
his, and it frightened him more than anything since the Tear.

Asenet went south, to the Sands, and then west, and then nowhere, for a year, working up the courage to walk into the Ring, where everyone would be, and
say a thing that nobody had ever believed.

That year twenty new champions came to the Ring. One of them was a fox spirit who had been in the Tower. One was a ferryman of the dead whose rivers had
begun to run uphill. One was the Uncrowned queen herself.
