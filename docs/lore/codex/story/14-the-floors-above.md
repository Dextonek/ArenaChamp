# 14. The Floors Above

*Epilogue. A Tower that remembers, the keepings it has not yet opened, what came through the Tear before it closed, and a red star.* ·
[The Long Noon](README.md)

## The Tower asleep

On the first night of the new sun, the moth priestess [Vela](../heroes/vela.md) flew up the outside of the Tower at dusk, as she had flown over
the Walking Wood for a hundred years to lay it down to rest, and shook the dust of her wings over its top. She had dreamed, awake, since the Fall,
that the Tower had not slept in all that time either, and was dreaming. She was right. It slept. It is the only thing on the Dial that Vela has ever
laid to sleep and heard, afterwards, say thank you.

It still stands at the Dial's centre, a shaft of pale grey stone with no seam in it, its top in the cloud, and its shadow walks round the Dial every
day and tells every realm its hour. It is the Dial's gnomon again, and its clock, with a clockmaker at its dial. It does not keep. It remembers.

Every floor it ever filled is still there, and the gate still opens for a champion of the Ring. Climb it, and you walk the Drowned Harbour on the
morning the water came, the Walking Wood in its first year, the Storm Canyon in the storm, the far halls in the dark, the Field on the last night of the
war: the Tower's memory of each hour, exact to the gull and the grain of sand. The people are gone out of them. What is left is the hour itself, and
the worst of it, which the Tower remembers as clearly as the rest: the Drowned Admiral still rides his wreck, the bark hounds still run, the dead still
get up on the Field when the drummer's memory beats. Champions climb it, as they always did, for the right to climb and for what it teaches; the
Hourkeepers call it the best record of the Dial ever made, and send their young up it to learn history. It is still the Climb. It is only that now,
when you fall on a floor, nothing keeps you there.

## The keepings not yet opened

The Tower is letting go the way an old man opens a box of letters, one at a time, reading each, and it has not finished.

Behind the Climb's fifty floors, in its back rooms, are the three realms it never showed. They have not been let go yet, because an hour let go needs
someone to walk its people out of it, as Gus walked the frightened out of the sea temple, and these three are the largest.

- **Frostfall in the Long Hold.** The moors under snow, the herds lying down, the shore villages waiting in the dark for a spring that would not come.
  The Winter Warden [Isolde](../heroes/isolde.md) has said she will go in, with her sister [Saga](../heroes/saga.md), who will dance the aurora over
  the lake at noon, as she danced it on the day of the Fall, and this time, she thinks, the sun will answer. The cold spoke to Isolde once, in the Long
  Hold, and told her that if the Tower's hour were set going it would have to let go of everything it keeps, the library, the lake, and her. It is
  going. She does not know yet what she will be when it has.
- **Aubade at dusk on the day of the Fall.** When the choir came down the Dawn Road, there were families missing from the crowd at its foot: the ones
  the Tower took at dusk, standing still in their grief, waiting for the singers. They are still waiting, in the Tower's back room, at the foot of a
  road with every bell ringing, for a hundred singers who have already come home. The Noonsworn mean to go in and bring them down the road to meet them.
  It will be the hardest homecoming in the history of the city, and the bells are ready.
- **Gloaming in the Long Dusk.** The year without a day, the old dying in their beds and unable to finish. The Court of Dusk has sworn to walk its
  dying out of it, and the ferryman of the dead to take them across, and [Mortis](../heroes/mortis.md), who has never let a dead thing lie down if it
  could work, has asked, to everyone's surprise, to go too.

Deeper still, in rooms the Tower took long before the Fall, are older keepings: the **revel of the Fox court** behind the thirty-first door, which
[The Great Zambini](../heroes/the-great-zambini.md) and [Kohaku](../heroes/kohaku.md) mean to open from the inside, together, without stealing anyone's
name; the **rooms of the Spires** that nobody built, from whose salt [Professor Puff](../heroes/professor-puff.md) distils his rose gas without knowing
what it is; and the single lives the Tower took, now and then, for a thousand years, through its roots and its doors. Nobody knows yet how many there
are. [Shiori](../heroes/shiori.md) has begun the list. It is long.

## What came through the Tear

The Tear did not close at once. For the length of the last verse it hung wide over the Tower, the widest it had ever been, onto the dark between the
stars; and the Aumbry was not the only thing that travels that dark.

Three shapes came down its last edge as Thessa drew it shut, and went into the ground at the Tower's foot, under Noonfall, among the roots. The
Hourkeepers have not named them. The Truce has heard them, at night, under the hall where the Raid was fought, and the Heart of Noon, which burns free
in the Hall of Noon now and is no longer anyone's to guard, flares when they move. And the **Dawnless**, the shadow of the Cast that hated the dawn and
carried sleepers to the Tower for a hundred years, fled down the roots when the hand opened, with [Nyx](../heroes/nyx.md) and
[Arden](../heroes/arden.md) a step behind it. It is somewhere under the Dial, in the dark, where the Tower's roots still run. They are still hunting
it, together. Arden says every trail ends somewhere. Nyx has started, very occasionally, to answer him.

The Truce has reopened Noonfall to the Ring's pairs. The Raid is not over.

## The red star

On the night before the Hundredth Midsummer, on the Sun Court, among all the questions the company asked Iselm, there was one they did not think to ask,
and the twins asked it, the next morning, of the girl at the window, because it was the kind of question a child asks. Whether Senmere had made only one.

The girl at the window had looked at them with her father's careful face and said nothing at all, which, in that family, is not no.

[Lyra](../heroes/lyra.md)'s red star, the one she found at ten low over the Tear and named for herself, is larger every month. It is not a star. It moves
against the others, slowly, toward the Dial, the way the Aumbry once moved through the dark toward an ageing sun. But the Dial's sun is not ageing now.
It is the youngest sun anyone has ever seen, a hundred days old, small and gold, with an Ember in it who once walked out of the sea; and whatever is coming
toward it has come a very long way, and is perhaps not coming to take.

Or perhaps it is. The Dial knows, now, what to do with a thing that keeps. It knows the last verse, which a soprano of Aubade is teaching, aloud, to a
child of the bell quarter, in case anything happens to her. It knows a word, cut in the script of a world that has had its evening, in the root of the
Tower, in a calligrapher's one stroke. It knows a Truce, and a Ring, and a table where winner and loser eat side by side.

## Dawn at the Ring

Every morning, before the Ring opens, the Arbiter sweeps it. He does not have to; there are a dozen people at the Ringside who would. He sweeps the sand
in the straw-rope circle where a boy he did not like for the first hour once threw salt, and his father, who lays the Ringside's paving now, tells him his
lines are crooked. Then the Tower's shadow comes round the Dial from the north, as the sun comes up over the morning cliffs, and lies for a moment across
the circle, the way it lay there for a hundred years, and then moves on.

The Hourkeepers write the hour down. The bells of Aubade ring a hundred. The gate opens for the first champion of the day.

The Long Noon is over. The day has begun.
