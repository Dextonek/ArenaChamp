# History of the Dial

*From the first star chart to the hundredth summer, and what is happening now.* · [Codex](README.md) · [The canon](canon.md)

Years are counted from the Noonfall: year 0 is the year the sun fell. Earlier years are counted down ("600 before"). Dates before
the Fall come from the Hourkeepers' Long Count, the tomb records of the Sands and the mole-folk's seam-songs, and are rounded where
those disagree.

## Before the Fall: the Age of the Shadow

**Before the count.** Nobody knows who raised the Tower, or whether it was raised. Every people's oldest story has it already
standing. The mole-folk's oldest seam-songs speak of "the pin above"; the Briarkin say it was there when they woke; the tomb
paintings of the Sands show the first caravans walking toward it.

**About 1,200 before.** Herders camped on the shore of Frostfall Lake notice that the stars turn round a fixed point, the Nail,
and build a stone ring to watch them. The first star chart is drawn: the Long Count begins. Within a century they have learned to
tell the hour from the Tower's shadow as well, and the observatory and the first Hourkeepers follow.

**About 1,100 before.** A fishing village on the eastern cliffs learns to read the Tower's shadow across the bay. Its people say a
woman on the cliff edge sang back to the rising sun, and it came up faster. The village becomes Aubade.

**About 900 before.** The mole-folk's oldest dated seam-song, the Song of the First Lamp, already describes halls under the southern
hills that are "older than the singer's grandmother's grandmother". The hill people (later the Kilnborn) begin to dig beside them.

**About 600 before.** The first king of the Sands takes the crook at the oasis of Sefet and calls the sun his father. The Dune
Throne begins, and with it the Valley of Lids, where the kings are buried with their hearts weighed. The caravans find the Brass
Road through the southern hills.

**About 500 before.** The Coast folk's register in the Spires begins (they have lived there longer, but did not write it down).
The Spires already have rooms nobody can find the doors to.

**About 450 before.** In Gloaming the dead begin to get up in the deep crypts. The nobility and the Long Dead make the **Ossuary
Pact**: the ossuaries are sealed with the first bone-lord inside, the Long Dead are given halls and honour, and in return they keep
the city's dead quiet. The Vigil is founded.

**About 400 before.** Aubade sends its first choir up the Tower to sing the Hymn of Noon at the top at midday. The Hymn is sung
every noon from then until the Fall. The Sun Warden, already keeping his post at the Tower's top, receives them; no record says when
he came there.

**About 300 before.** The Hourkeepers measure the first paling of the sun on their great lens: a little less light at midsummer
than the year before. They keep measuring, and say nothing. In Gloaming, a scholar of the dead dies at his desk with a question
unanswered, and three days later gets up: Mortis.

**About 250 before.** The first Ninefold knight swears her oath at the Keep of Nine on the edge of the Field, then a green valley in
the kingdom of **Harrowmark**, the realm of the middle that lies between the Wood and the Deep. Ninefold knights serve every court
of the Dial within a century.

**About 200 before.** The Walking Wood, then the Still Wood, is closed to woodcutters for a generation by treaty with the Briarkin
after the Burning of the Third Glade. The treaty ("the folk keep to the paths, the fae keep to the glades") holds until the Fall.

**About 150 before.** Harrowmark's crimson kings grow rich on the road traffic to the Tower; their castle at Harrow stands where the
Field's road meets the Banner Road.

**About 80 before.** Harrowmark at its height under King Aldwin the Crimson, grandfather of Orlan. The Field is farmland and
orchards; the Keep of Nine holds eighty knights.

**60 before.** Mr. Ledger (as the Long Dead count it) dies at his desk in the counting house of Harrow, mid-sum, and does not stay
dead.

**About 40 before.** The Spires' weather post begins its continuous record. The Moot of the Wood meets under the Antler Glades for the
first time as a single council of all the forest's villages.

**12 before.** Silvane is born in the mirror quarter of Gloaming; Opaline four years later.

## The year of the Fall (year 0)

The day of the Fall is told hour by hour in the Noon Folio. As every people on the Dial lived it:

- **At first light**, the bells of Aubade ring the sun up as on every morning. It is midsummer, and the Ring's ancestor (a market
  and a straw-rope wrestling circle at the Tower's foot) is full for the festival.
- **In the morning**, the choir of a hundred climbs the Tower, as every day. At midsummer they carry a lamp to be lit at noon from the
  sun's own light. Two of the hundred are Cantor (thirty) and Mum (twenty-six).
- **Before noon** the choir lights the lamp from the sun, early, as is the custom, and sends it down by a runner. It will reach
  Aubade at dusk: the **Ember of Aubade**.
- **At noon** the choir sings the Hymn. The sun stops over the Tower. The Hourkeepers' lens records that it stops for the space of
  nine heartbeats. Then it cracks, with a sound heard from Frostfall to the Coast, and falls.
- **The heart** comes down at the Tower's foot and goes into the ground, and the Hall of Noon opens round it. Two embers fall into a
  pit beside it: the Cinder Pit. The festival market at the Tower's foot is gone. Big Yoko, eighteen, a shrine wrestler from the
  cliffs, is standing in the straw-rope circle a mile away and is thrown off his feet.
- **The shadow stops.** Across the Dial the Tower's shadow lies where it was at noon, and stays.
- **The Tear opens** where the sun hung, black with a pink edge, and something steps out of it onto the Tower: the Unmaker.
- **The sea rises.** On the Coast the sun's heat lifts the sea over the great port of Sallowmouth in an afternoon. Half the city
  drowns. Marin, a girl of twelve, dances in the sea temple as the water comes in.
- **The light goes down.** In the Deep, mole-folk see a light run through the rock and into the crystals of the halls: the
  Shining. Mabel, twenty, sees it.
- **The fire comes up.** Under the southern hills the cold crater fills with fire from below: the Ember Caldera.
- **The sand warms.** On the Glass Flats glass spreads over the sand where the heat strikes; the Sands say the sun has come home.
- **The Wood walks.** In the west the forest pulls up its roots and starts east, toward the Tower.
- **The light leaves Gloaming** first. Vesper, Countess of the heights, watches it go from her vines.
- **The shadows stand.** On the wall of the Night Temple a shadow thrown by the sun's last light does not move when the light
  changes, then steps off the wall: Nyx.
- **At dusk** the Ember of Aubade reaches the city; the lamp is the last light of the old sun anyone can hold. Two singers come down
  the Tower, Cantor and Mum, and Mum does not speak.
- **The next morning** the sun rises thin and late from the Heart through the Tower. In Aubade Auriel is born at first light. Off
  the Drowned Coast a woman of flame walks out of the surf: Anka.

The rest of that year is the **Long Dusk** in Gloaming (no full day for a year), the **first slip** (the Hourkeepers publish the
first Slip-sheet in the autumn), and hunger everywhere the harvest failed.

## The War of the Banners (years 0 to 2)

**Year 0, autumn.** Pieces of the sun lie all over the Dial: shards of its crust, warm stones, glass, embers. Whoever holds them has
heat, light and power. King Orlan of Harrowmark, whose kingdom lies nearest the Tower, declares the pieces his by right of
neighbourhood and raises the **crimson banner** to collect them. The Dune Throne, then in turmoil, and some Gloaming houses join him
for a share.

**Year 1, spring.** The other realms answer as **the Azure League**: Aubade's paladins, the Coast's levies, the Underforge's axes,
the Hourkeepers' surveyors, the Moot's hunters, and Ninefold knights on both sides. The **Last Marshal**, a knight of Harrowmark who
had refused Orlan, commands the azure.

**Year 1.** Sefu takes the Dune Throne from his sister **Asenet**, whom the Sands blame for the Fall; on the night she is driven out
she lays a curse on the crown: it will never stay on one head. Ramet, a young lion of the Ninefold and captain of the gate, opens it for her and lets her go (the gate
captains of Tamerit have been Ninefold lions for three generations). The Throne withdraws from the crimson banner.

**Year 1, winter.** Two Briarkin children, one life between them, are left on Grandma Ironknit's doorstep: Pim and Pom. On the Field,
the **Winter of Mud**: neither banner can move. Madame Cleaver, twenty-five, a caravan cook's daughter, feeds both armies from one
fire in the ruined orchard of Harrow, and the soldiers of both sides eat together under a flag of white linen she hangs on her
cauldron.

**Year 2, spring.** The **Battle of the Ninth Field**: the last great battle. Both banners are broken. King Orlan dies in the mud and
is buried where he fell, under the Barrow of Harrow. The Last Marshal is never found. The Keep of Nine loses sixty knights in a day,
many of them on their eighth or ninth lives.

**Year 2, midsummer.** **The Truce of the Ring** is sworn at the Tower's foot by every realm, faction and people that fought, round
the straw-rope circle where Big Yoko has stood all through the war. Madame Cleaver's cauldron is the first Truce Table; Mr. Ledger
writes the first page of the Tally; the founders choose a mason's boy who swept the sand, Whistler, fourteen, to keep count of the
fouls. The first obols are struck in the autumn.

## The century since

**Year 3.** The first duel in the Ring, between a Gloaming house and an Aubade bell quarter, over a debt of grain. Big Yoko, the
Truce's first champion, stands as the Ring's boundary. The Ring's stands are built by Kilnborn masons; the Hundred Doors are found
beneath them, already there, and Wick, fifty-three, a lamp-keeper from Frostfall, is asked to keep them.

**Year 5.** The **Gaol of Oaths** is opened under the Drowned Harbour for those who break the Truce, its chains forged in the Ember
Caldera.

**Years 5 to 20.** The **Settling**. The realms learn to live with the slip. Gloaming's Court of Dusk is formally founded, with
Vesper at its head. The Noonsworn are sworn in Aubade. The Hourkeepers open the frozen library to every realm. The Wood folk who
swore the Moot's oath begin to grow horns. The Kilnborn's skin goes grey. The mole-folk reopen the Lamp Road.

**Year 12.** The Underforge and the Dune Throne open the **Brass Road** to regular caravans. It has run every month since.

**Year 18.** Opaline, still a child in the eyes of the world, goes down the Prism Stair; her sister Silvane stays in the Mirror Hall.
They have not spoken since the year 40.

**Year 23.** The **Night of Lanterns** on the Drowned Coast: a storm, the worst since the Fall, drives ships onto the Spires. The
Coast folk light every lamp on the coast; the Lantern Coast is named. Barry Cloudsley, then the Spires' junior augur, predicts the
storm a day early and is not believed.

**Year 30.** The Court of Dusk's long nights have been bought on credit from the Tally since the first harvest. The debt passes ten
thousand obols.

**Year 35.** Gus is born in Saltreach.

**Year 40.** Brannoc follows Isolde north to Frostfall Lake. She freezes his forge solid to send him home. It takes him a year to
light it.

**Year 47.** The Moot and the Underforge come closest to war since the Truce, over the Wood's edge at the old Harrow road; settled
in the Ring by Hank, who looked thirty, against an Underforge champion. Hank wins.

**Year 50.** Mr. Ledger asks to write Grandma Ironknit into his book. She knits him a scarf.

**Year 55.** Professor Puff's gas floats a hive-tree out of the Wood, by accident. The swarm remembers.

**Year 60.** Gus takes up the broom of the sea temple. Kessa becomes warden of the Gaol of Oaths.

**Year 61.** Auriel, who looks eleven, rings the hundredth bell at midsummer for the first time.

**Years 70 to 81.** The **first great generation** since the Fall: Shiori (70), Kaia (72), Ada (74), Zephyr (76), Lyra (78), Pip
(81). They grow up under the thin sun, have never seen the old one, and are the first champions of the Ring who do not remember it.

**Year 75.** Auriel is sworn a paladin.

**Year 80.** Campana recasts the bells of Aubade. Fenna and Hank share their first winter fire.

**Year 86.** Two Noonsworn bell-ringers are found asleep on the night road under Gloaming and never wake. Auriel demands a champion.
Her first duel with Vesper.

**Year 88.** Kaia's and Barry Cloudsley's public argument begins, over a storm she called on a day he forecast clear.

**Year 90.** **The gate opens.** At midsummer, for the first time since the Truce, the Tower's gate opens for a champion of the Ring:
the champion is not recorded, because the Tally's page for that duel is blank, which has never happened before. Inside is the first
floor of the Climb, a harbour drowned on the morning the water came. Orren takes up the Ember of Aubade. Arden begins tracking Nyx.

**Years 90 to 99.** The Climb grows: the Sea's ten floors, then the Wood, the Sands, the Deep and the War, fifty floors in five
chapters. Every faction sends champions. Few come down past the twentieth floor; none has passed the fiftieth.

**Year 99, spring.** Asenet slips Kessa's chain on a spring tide and walks out of the Gaol of Oaths.

## Now: the hundredth summer (year 100)

What is happening this year, written so that each can become an event or a season:
- **The Hundredth Midsummer.** The Ring opens on the hundredth anniversary of the Fall. Aubade means to ring the noon bell a hundred
  times; the Court of Dusk means to stay indoors; the Truce expects trouble.
- **The Climb is open to fifty floors.** The crowns of the five chapters are the sun's own: the Sun Warden (10 and 40), Ash and Ember
  (20 and 50), the Heart of Noon (30). Above the fiftieth floor is the Noon Seat. Nobody has reached it.
- **Twenty new champions** have come to the Ring, among them the Sunbearer with the Ember on his back, the Uncrowned queen, and the
  ferryman of the dead.
- **The Tear widens.** A hand's breadth since spring. Thessa sews; it unpicks. Wick has started locking doors that have never been
  locked.
- **The slip quickens.** The Hourkeepers' Slip-sheet shows days of thirty hours in the north. Tempo is seen in two places at once more
  often.
- **The light pales.** The Noonsworn's bells ring louder. Anka burns more often and rises slower.
- **The crown passes.** The cursed crown of the Sands has passed three times this year. Sefu still wears it. Nebet has been asked to
  weigh something she will not name.
- **The Court's note comes due.** Mr. Ledger has written to Vesper, for the first time in seventy years, about the principal.
- **The Wood walks east.** Faster than ever, toward the Tower, and the villages at its edge cannot keep up.
- **The Gaol is one prisoner short.**

## Possible seasons and events

Hooks the lore supports, for future modes:
- **The Hymn Gathered:** the Hymn of Noon's verses are scattered across the Dial (Cantor has most, Mum knows the one he lacks);
  gathering them opens something.
- **The Hundredth Bell:** an Aubade festival event at midsummer.
- **The Long Evening:** a Gloaming season, fought at night.
- **The Gaol Break:** Kessa hunts Asenet and the escaped.
- **The Brass Road:** escort the caravan between the Deep and the Sands.
- **The Wood's March:** hold the villages as the Wood walks.
- **The Tear Opens:** a Raid against what comes through.
- **The Noon Seat:** the end of the Climb, and a choice.
