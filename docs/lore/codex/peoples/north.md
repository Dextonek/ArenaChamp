# Northerners

*Human. The people of Frostfall, under the pole star, who write everything down.* · [Peoples](README.md) ·
[Frostfall](../realms/frostfall.md) · [The Hourkeepers](../factions/hourkeepers.md)

## Origin

The Northerners came to the top of the Dial following the stars. Their first settlers, the Hourkeepers say, were herders who
noticed that at Frostfall the stars turn round one fixed point, the pole star the Northerners call **the Nail**, and that a
people who lived under the Nail could always know the hour. They built a stone ring on the shore of Frostfall Lake to watch the
stars turn, about 1,200 years before the Fall; the Long Count begins with its first chart. The ring became the observatory, the
observatory a town, and the town a people who measure.

## Homeland

[Frostfall](../realms/frostfall.md), at the twelfth hour of the Dial: the observatory and the frozen library on Frostfall Lake,
the town of Hearthmere at the lake's south end, the reindeer moors, the ice coast, the Frostfall road that runs south past the
Walking Wood to the Ring. Many Northerners live away from home as clerks, surveyors and tutors: a Northerner is welcome in every
court that needs something counted.

## Body

Broad and tall, with big hands and a slow, sure walk. Skin deep brown to warm umber, weathered at the cheeks. Their hair goes
white early, often by thirty, and they wear it long in many thin braids, each tied with a bead for a skill learned. The mark of
the people is the **star lines**: fine blue-black tattoos along the jaw and the backs of the hands that chart the sky at the hour
of the wearer's birth, so that any Northerner can show you when they were born.

The **lake-born** are a lineage of the Northerners from the shore villages of Frostfall Lake, the families that keep the ice:
generations of the cold have left them frost-pale, nearly white-skinned with a blue undertone, and their hair is white from
birth. The Winter Wardens have always come from the lake-born. [Isolde](../heroes/isolde.md) and [Saga](../heroes/saga.md) are
lake-born sisters.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body.
- **Can be:** skin from deep brown to umber, or frost-pale for the lake-born; long white or dark hair in many braids; star lines
  as thin dark lines on the jaw and the backs of the hands; night-blue wool, white fur, silver, ink blue and parchment; star
  charts embroidered; spears, staffs, bows, brushes, clocks.
- **Cannot be:** horns or animal features; glowing eyes (that is the Gloaming folk).
- **Reads as:** dark skin and white braids in blue and silver, or a white figure in white for the lake-born. The lake-born and
  the Gloaming folk are both pale: the Gloaming folk have eyeshine and violet, the lake-born white hair and blue.

## Lifespan and the Fall

The longest-lived humans on the Dial, often past ninety; the cold keeps them, they say. The Fall touched them as time. At
Frostfall the hours slip worse than anywhere, because the Hourkeepers can measure them, and a Northerner born after the Fall is
taught as a child that the clock is a promise and not a fact. The observatory saw the Fall better than anyone, and wrote it down
minute by minute; the record of the Fall, the **Noon Folio**, is the most read book in the frozen library. A few Northerners were
touched more strangely: [Tempo](../heroes/tempo.md) fell out of time at the moment of the Fall and has lived every moment twice.

## Culture

A people of lists, charts and long winters. Every house has a clock and a shelf; every child learns to read before they learn to
skate, and to skate before they learn to swim. Quarrels are settled by finding out who is right, which takes a long time and is
considered entertaining. The winter is the season of stories, told in order, with dates. Their food is fish from under the ice,
reindeer, barley, cloudberries and a strong black tea with salt and butter. Their crafts are lenses, paper, clocks, wool and
ink. They are quiet, kind, stubborn and rarely surprised, and they are the Dial's best teachers.

## Faith

The **Measure**. The Northerners hold that the world keeps an order, and that the order can be learned, and that learning it is
the same as keeping it. Their temples are the observatory and the library. They pray by writing: on the longest night every
Northerner writes down one true thing they learned that year, and the pages are bound into the year's book. Since the Fall the
Measure has had to make room for the slipping hours, and has done so by measuring the slip. Most Northerners believe the sun
must hang over the Tower again, because the shadow only falls when a sun hangs above it; but they want to know why it fell before
anyone hangs it.

## Names

Two-part given names, a sky word and a craft word, shortened in daily use: Lyra, Isolde, Saga, Thessa, Shiori (a scribe-name from
the library's eastern script, given to calligraphers), Wick (a lamp-keeper's name). Family names are the hour of a birth:
"Lyra Ninth-and-a-half" was born at half past the ninth hour. Nobody uses them aloud except clerks.

## How they fight

Slowly and from a distance: with long spears on the ice, with bows from the observatory's terraces, and with the cold. Their
warriors, the Wardens of the Winter, are few and fight to hold ground, not take it. Northern duellists are patient, read their
opponents like a chart, and like to win in the last second.

## Ties to other peoples

Friends of the Aubade folk; the Briarkin are their neighbours on the Frostfall road and the subject of many careful notes; the
Kilnborn sell them lenses' brass; the Gloaming folk are their oldest quarrel, because the Court would keep the hour stopped.
