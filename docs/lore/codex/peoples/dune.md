# Dune folk

*Human. The people of the Sands, children of the oldest court on the Dial.* · [Peoples](README.md) · [The Sands](../realms/sands.md)
· [The Dune Throne](../factions/dune.md)

## Origin

The Dune folk came out of the east when the Sands were grass, say their tomb paintings, and stayed when the grass went, because
the sun stayed. They count their history by kings: the first king took the crook about 600 years before the Fall, and the
Throne has passed from hand to hand, and lately from head to head, ever since. They were caravan people first and city people
second, and every Dune family still keeps one camel saddle in the house, even if nobody in it has ridden for a century.

## Homeland

[The Sands](../realms/sands.md), at the third and fourth hours of the Dial: Tamerit, the city of the Throne, on the river Sefet
between the Glass Flats and the Storm Canyon; the Valley of Lids, where the kings are buried; the oases along the Brass Road;
the caravan towns of the Storm Canyon. Since the Fall a good many Dune folk live on the Field and at the Ring, where the Truce's
cooks, clerks and caravan masters are often from the Sands.

## Body

Lean, long in the neck and the fingers, made to lose little water. Skin dark bronze to deep brown. Hair black and thick, shaved
by the court and the priests, worn in fine braids by everyone else and bound with copper. Eyes dark, and lined with kohl out to
the temples, men and women alike, against the glare; the court adds gold leaf on the lids. Hands are often hennaed in the
pattern of the wearer's house.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body, unchanged.
- **Can be:** skin from dark bronze to deep brown; a shaved head, a striped headcloth or braids with copper rings; kohl to the
  temples and gold on the lids (a face-texture stripe); layered linen in white, ochre and sand with turquoise and gold; collars,
  armlets, a crook, a scale, a veil, a bow, a lion's mane hood.
- **Cannot be:** a gorgon's living snakes for hair (Lithea's crown is bronze coils, made, not grown); an animal head (that is
  the Ninefold).
- **Reads as:** dark skin under white and ochre linen with turquoise and gold. They and the Aubade folk both wear white; the Dune
  folk wear it layered and belted, with collars, the Aubade folk long and plain.

## Lifespan and the Fall

About seventy-five years in the cities, less in the caravans. The Fall came to them as heat. The Sands say the sun fell into
their dunes, not the Tower's foot, and the sand is still warm a hand's depth down even in the coldest night. Glass grew on the
Flats that day where the heat struck, a whole plain of it. The Noon-touched of the Sands are many, and include their king.
The Fall also cost them their queen: the Sands blamed [Asenet](../heroes/asenet.md), who ruled that year, and the crown went to
her brother [Sefu](../heroes/sefu.md).

## Culture

The oldest manners on the Dial: a guest is given water before a name is asked, a quarrel is settled before the evening meal, and
every family keeps its dead in the house for three days. Trade is honour: a broken bargain is a broken family. They are great
record keepers, though their records are of the dead (the tombs are libraries). The food is flatbread, dates, lentils, goat,
and a sour drink of fermented barley that every foreigner is made to try once. Their crafts are glass from the Flats, linen,
turquoise, perfume and embalming, which is an art and a medicine both.

The Throne is everything. Even the Dune folk who hate Sefu bow to the crook, because a Sands without a king, they say, is just
sand. Since the curse on the crown they have become a people who watch their rulers' heads.

## Faith

The kings of the Sands called the sun their father, and the Dune folk call it **the Father** still. Their faith says the sun did
not fall but **came home**: it walked down into the Sands to rest among its children, and it will rise again from them in a
king. The priests weigh the hearts of the dead against a feather so that the Father knows whom to take with him when he rises.
The tombs are holy, and the veiled priestesses who guarded them with their eyes are remembered with fear and love. A smaller
faith of the caravans, the Way of Water, holds that the sun is only a lamp, and that what matters is the next well.

## Names

Short names of one or two syllables in the old desert tongue, with a meaning in the house's trade: Sefu ("sword"), Asenet,
Nebet ("lady"), Sahar ("dawn-glare"), Ramet ("gate"), Lithea (a priestess name, never given to a child). A Dune person also
carries a **house name** after the given one, said only at weddings, funerals and in court: Sefu of the Crook, Nebet of the
Scale. Embalmers take no name at all when they put on the mask, and are called by their trade: [Vitriol](../heroes/vitriol.md)
is a word for the oil he uses.

## How they fight

From horseback and camelback, with the bow and the curved blade, in loose skirmish lines that fade into the heat. The Throne's
guard fights on foot in bronze scales and lion hoods behind long spears. Their priests fight with poison and with the dead, their
archers with the mirage. A Dune army never gives battle at noon.

## Heroes and Climb people

- **In the game:** [Sefu](../heroes/sefu.md), [Vitriol](../heroes/vitriol.md), [Madame Cleaver](../heroes/madame-cleaver.md)

- **New:** [Lithea](../heroes/lithea.md), [Nebet](../heroes/nebet.md), [Sahar](../heroes/sahar.md), [Asenet](../heroes/asenet.md).
- **On the Climb:** the Sands' five, the Star-Reader, the Storm-Counter, the Beacon-Keeper, the Silk-Merchant and the Glass-Seller; the Water-Carrier at her work on every floor; and the caravan raiders and slingers, who are Dune folk caught in the storm ([the Sands](../climb/sands.md)).

## Ties to other peoples

Old trade partners of the Kilnborn on the Brass Road; allies of convenience with the Gloaming folk; rivals of the Aubade folk over
whose sun it is; impatient with the Coast folk, whose sailors laugh at camels. They welcome the Ninefold, who have kept their gate
for three generations.
