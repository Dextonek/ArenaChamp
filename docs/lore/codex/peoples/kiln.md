# Kilnborn

*Human, changed by the fallen sun's heat. The ash-skinned people of the Deep's halls and forges.* · [Peoples](README.md) ·
[The Deep](../realms/deep.md) · [The Underforge](../factions/underforge.md)

## Origin

Before the Fall they were the hill people of the south: miners, masons and smiths who dug into the southern hills beside the
[mole-folk](mole.md), and lived half above ground and half below. They called themselves the Delvers, and the Dial called them
hillmen. The great forge was a quarry then, and the Ember Caldera a cold crater.

On the day of the Fall, the sun's heat came up through the rock under the southern hills. The cold crater filled with fire from
below: the Ember Caldera. The Delvers moved down toward the heat, built the great forge round it and called themselves the
Underforge. Over one generation their skin went grey as ash and their eyes pale as a coal's edge, and the Dial began calling them
by the name they now use themselves: **the Kilnborn**.

## Homeland

[The Deep](../realms/deep.md), at the sixth and seventh hours: the halls under the southern hills, the great forge in the Ember
Caldera, the masons' halls, the Prism Stair, the Lamp Road, the Crystal Cavern, and the far halls of the lightless Deep, where the
fire never reached. Kilnborn smiths work in every city on the Dial, and the Ring's iron is theirs.

## Body

Short and broad, thick in the shoulder and the wrist, with big careful hands. Skin **ash-grey**, from pale dove to dark slate, and
dry to the touch. Eyes pale amber, like the edge of a coal. Hair iron-grey or black, cut short or shaved for the forge; beards are
kept long and braided by those who do not work the fire, short by those who do. Those who work the great forge for years show
**ember seams**: thin lines in the skin at the knuckles, the throat and the collarbones that glow a dull orange when they are
hot, and go dark when they cool. The Kilnborn of the far halls, who live where the Caldera's fire never came, kept their old
colour: pale, from never seeing daylight, but not grey.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body, the broad build.
- **Can be:** ash-grey skin; pale amber eyes; short grey or black hair, braided beards; ember seams as thin emissive lines at the
  knuckles, throat and collarbones (kept dim: under the floor's glow); leather aprons, copper, soot, limestone; hammers, mauls,
  pry-bars, tools, clockwork, obsidian.
- **Cannot be:** stone skin or crystal growths (that is the stone-woken or Opaline's crystal, not the Kilnborn); a short dwarf's
  body (they are short for humans, not a different shape).
- **Reads as:** a broad grey-skinned figure in brown and copper, with a faint orange glow at the hands. The grey skin is what sets
  the Underforge apart from every other workman on the roster.

## Lifespan and the Fall

About sixty-five years: the forge is hard on them, and the grey skin cracks in old age. The Fall made them, and they say so
with pride: "The sun fell to us." Their elders were Noon-touched like everyone; their children were born grey. The Kilnborn are
the only people who speak of the Fall as a gift, and it makes the others uncomfortable.

## Culture

Builders. A Kilnborn is judged by what they made and how long it stands. Houses are cut from the rock and passed down; a family's
honour is its mark, cut on everything it makes. They are blunt, hospitable, quick to argue and slow to sulk. Meals are taken at
long tables in the halls, under crystal light: mushrooms, root bread, goat from the hill farms, cheeses aged in the caves, and
a thick dark beer. Their crafts are iron, copper, brass, glass from the Caldera, clockwork, golems and powder. The Brass Road to
the Sands is their lifeline, and the Ring's iron their pride.

They have two sore points. The Wood folk say the Kilnborn's mines eat the Wood's edge; and the Noonsworn want to carry the fire the
Kilnborn live by up into the sky.

## Faith

The **Anvil**. The world is a thing being made, and the maker's duty is to make it well. They honour the fire as a craftsman
honours a good tool, and the fallen sun as the best tool the Dial ever had: their faith says the sun fell into the Deep to be
**reforged**, and that it will rise as a sun underground, lighting the halls and warming the Dial through the rock. The smiths'
priests are called Keepers of the Bellows. A small and growing teaching among the young, the Lamp-and-Key, says the fire is not
theirs to keep, only theirs to tend until its owner comes back for it.

## Names

Short, hard names, often of metals and tools: Brannoc, Bram, Ada, Hesk, Itzel (an old Caldera name for black glass), Kessa
(a chain-maker's name). Family names are makers' marks: Bram Gerald's-Mason (he insists), Ada of the Turning Gear. A Kilnborn
who makes something great takes its name: Brannoc's line is called the Ashen line after his first forge.

## How they fight

Heavy and close, with hammers, mauls and axes, behind iron shields; and with engines: turrets, powder, golems, hooks. A Kilnborn
army is slow and almost impossible to move. Their duellists are tanks and engineers, and they win by lasting.

## Ties to other peoples

Brothers of the mole-folk in the old seams, though the mole-folk say the Kilnborn came second; partners of the Dune folk on the
Brass Road; rivals of the Aubade folk over the sun's fire; enemies of the Wood folk at the Wood's edge.
