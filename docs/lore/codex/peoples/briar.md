# Briarkin

*Not human. The fae of the Walking Wood, in their courts under the Underbough.* · [Peoples](README.md) ·
[The Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md) · [The Greenwood Moot](../factions/greenwood.md)

## Origin

The Briarkin were in the Wood before the Wood folk, and possibly before the Wood. Their story is that they are the forest's
dreams, which got up one night and did not lie down again. The Hourkeepers have no date for them: the first chart that mentions
the Wood already mentions "the small people in the glades who will not be counted". They live in **courts**, each a household of
a kind, under the **Underbough**: the deep glades and hollow hills of the Walking Wood, where the paths of the Wood folk do not go.

## Homeland

The Underbough, deep in the [Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md). Each court has its glade: the Acorn court under the great oaks, the
Moth court in the night meadows, the Hive court in the hive-trees, the Fox court in the thickets round Gloaming (the only court
outside the Wood), and the Spindle court, which is wherever its spinners sit. Since the Wood began to walk, the courts move with
it, and the Underbough is never in the same place two years running.

## Body

Briarkin look like people, nearly, and the nearly is the point. Their ears are long and **pointed**. Their eyes are **one colour
from lid to lid**, with no white: leaf green, amber, silver, black. Their skin is any colour a forest has, from birch-pale to
bark-brown, and faintly patterned like light through leaves. They are small or tall as they please, within a span: a Briarkin can
look ten or seventy, and keeps the age that suits them. And each court has its **sign**, grown, not worn:
- the **Acorn** court: small and quick, with nut-brown skin and green eyes ([Pim and Pom](../heroes/pim-and-pom.md));
- the **Moth** court: great soft wings that fold like a cloak, and feathered antennae ([Vela](../heroes/vela.md));
- the **Fox** court: a fox's mask of a face by night, and one tail by day, nine in the old stories ([Kohaku](../heroes/kohaku.md));
- the **Hive** court: chitin plates at the shoulders and brow, faceted eyes, two pairs of wings ([Sirix](../heroes/sirix.md));
- the **Spindle** court: silver eyes, long clever fingers, and great age worn lightly ([Grandma Ironknit](../heroes/grandma-ironknit.md)).
A Briarkin of **no court** has left theirs, and hides the sign or wears it plainly, as they choose
([The Great Zambini](../heroes/the-great-zambini.md)).

## In the figure

On the pack's human body, at any height the build allows (small for the Acorn court, as Pim and Pom are now).
- **Can be:** pointed ears; eyes of one colour (a face-texture change, no white); skin in forest colours with a faint dapple; a
  court's sign built as costume on the body (moth wings and insect wings carried as a cloak that moves with the shoulders; a
  carved fox mask, one stiff tail; chitin plates); leaves, acorn caps, silk, wax, amber.
- **Cannot be:** flying (the pack has no flying clips; wings are a cloak); a fox on four legs; tiny figures smaller than the twins.
- **Reads as:** a figure in leaf colours with pointed ears and a court's sign. The sign is what reads at the game's distance.

## Lifespan and the Fall

Nobody knows. Briarkin do not die of age; they **fade**, when nothing remembers them, and they are reborn when a story about them
is told again, which they do not like to discuss. The Fall touched them more than anyone guessed. The Wood began to walk, and
the courts with it; the old treaty with the Wood folk, which said the fae kept to the glades, had nothing to say about glades
that moved. And something worse: in the first winter after the Fall, Briarkin children began to be born **swapped**, two for one,
each a half of a single life. Pim and Pom were the first. The courts were frightened, and left them on a doorstep.

## Culture

Courtly, playful, ruthless and bound by rules nobody else can see. A Briarkin will not lie, will not refuse a fair bargain, will
not cross a line of salt or cold iron, and will not give their full name, because a name given can be taken (the Fox court lives
by taking them). They love games, riddles, music, gifts that are not quite gifts, and above all **audiences**: a Briarkin who is
not watched grows thin. They eat honey, dew, flowers, cream, and whatever is left out for them, and take offence if nothing is.
Their crafts are glamour (the art of seeming), silk, wax, wine from flowers, and promises.

## Faith

They have none, they say, because they are what others have faith in. In truth they keep the **Telling**: everything that is
remembered is alive, and everything forgotten fades, so the holiest act is to tell a story well. They fear the Tower, which
they believe swallows stories whole, and the [Unmade](fallborn.md), which unmakes them.

## Names

Every Briarkin has a true name, never given, and a **use-name**, given freely and often changed: Pim, Pom, Vela, Kohaku (a fox
name meaning amber), Sirix, and Ironknit (the Spindle court's word for a spinner who has chosen to live among mortals). The Great
Zambini is a use-name of the third kind: a stage name worn over a use-name worn over a true one.

## How they fight

By not fighting fair, which they consider the only honest way. Glamour, tricks, swaps, sleep, stolen names, stings, threads and
traps; they win by making you fight the wrong thing. Struck with cold iron, they flinch.

- **On the Climb:** the Wood's thorn sprites and wisps are Briarkin who faded inside the Tower and half came back

## Ties to other peoples

Treaty-friends of the Wood folk; old players of the Ninefold, who enjoy their games; curious about the Northerners, who keep
writing them down (which the Briarkin secretly love, because being written down is being remembered); afraid of the Long Dead,
who remember too well, and of the Unmade.
