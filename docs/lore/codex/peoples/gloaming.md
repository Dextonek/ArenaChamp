# Gloaming folk

*Human, changed by a hundred years of dusk. The people whose eyes shine back.* · [Peoples](README.md) · [Gloaming](../realms/gloaming.md)
· [The Court of Dusk](../factions/dusk.md)

## Origin

Before the Fall the Gloaming folk were an ordinary people of the north-western hills: mirror-makers, vintners, keepers of the
night roads, and a nobility that had always been a little proud of being pale. Gloaming stood at the eleventh hour of the Dial,
where the Tower's shadow came at evening, and its people had a reputation for late nights and good wine. Their nobles kept the
ossuaries under the castle-city with the help of the [Long Dead](longdead.md), and paid them in the only coin the Long Dead take:
remembrance.

On the day of the Fall the light left Gloaming first and came back last. For the first year after the Fall there was no full day
there at all, only a long blue dusk. The people who lived through it changed.

## Homeland

[Gloaming](../realms/gloaming.md), the castle-city round the Night Temple on the north-western heights, and the dusk valleys
around it: the vine terraces, the mirror quarter, the village of Lantern-under-Hill, the night roads that run east to Frostfall
and south to the Walking Wood.

## Body

Tall, narrow and long-handed, with fine bones. Skin very pale with a blue-grey cast, as if lit by a cold lamp; it does not tan
and burns in an afternoon. Lips dark, almost violet. Hair black, silver or a deep violet that is their own and not a dye. The
mark of the people is the eyes: since the long dusk, a Gloaming child's eyes **shine back** light like a cat's, silver or
violet, so that a crowd of Gloaming folk at night is a field of small moons. They see well in the dark and badly at noon, and
squint in the Ring's light.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body.
- **Can be:** pale skin with a cold blue-grey tint; dark lips; black, silver or violet hair; eyes with a soft emissive ring
  (eyeshine), which is the one mark every Gloaming hero should carry; velvet, high collars, mirror silver, crimson and violet;
  a stone skin for the stone-woken (Corbel), with wings carried as a stone cloak.
- **Cannot be:** fangs or bat wings (they are not a people of monsters, and the Court will tell you so); glowing skin.
- **Reads as:** a pale, narrow figure in violet or crimson with two points of light in the face. The eyeshine is what tells a
  Gloaming hero from a frost-pale Northerner at the game's distance.

## Lifespan and the Fall

About ninety years, and they age slowly after forty, which the Court calls breeding and the Hourkeepers call the dusk. Besides
the eyes, the long dusk gave them a horror of noon and a hunger for the night that the old Gloaming did not have. A few of them,
the ones who went furthest into the dark, found other hungers: [Vesper](../heroes/vesper.md) has lived a hundred years on blood
and is not Noon-touched at all; she stopped. The stone-woken are rarer still: Gloaming folk who were turned to stone and woke
again at dusk, as [Corbel](../heroes/corbel.md) did.

## Culture

A people of the evening. The working day begins at sunset; the markets open with the lamps; children are told to come home
before the morning. They make the best mirrors and the best wine on the Dial, and the most elaborate manners: a Gloaming
conversation is half compliment, and the other half is a knife. The nobility are bound by houses (the Countess's house, the
Mirror house, the house of Bells) and by debts, and no one of them is without both. The commons are night-road wardens,
gravediggers, glaziers and grape-pickers, and are proud, quiet and a great deal more honest than their lords.

The Court of Dusk ([faction](../factions/dusk.md)) is the nobility's answer to the Fall, and most Gloaming folk support it: a new
sun would hurt them. They are not cruel because they are pale. They are frightened, and well dressed.

## Faith

The **Vigil**. The Gloaming folk keep watch for the dead, and believe that the night is when the Dial remembers. The Night Temple
holds no god but a great dark mirror that reflects nothing, and before it the names of the dead are said aloud each night. Since
the Fall the Vigil has added a teaching: that the dusk is not an ending but an age, the **Long Evening**, and that it is as holy
as any noon. The Court has made this teaching law.

## Names

Long, soft names with many vowels: Vesper, Silvane, Opaline, Corbel, Moraine, Ysolt. Nobles add the house: Silvane of the Mirror
house, Vesper of the Countess's house. The commons add a trade: Corbel the Templeguard. Children born in the long dusk of year 0
were all given names of evening things, and that generation is still called the Vespers, after the Countess.

## How they fight

At night, in small bands, with rapiers, crossbows and mirrors: a Gloaming duellist uses light as a weapon, catching a lamp in a
glass and throwing it in your eyes. The Court's soldiers are few and good. Its real strength is its allies (the Long Dead's
bone walls, the Cast's knives) and its information.

## Heroes and Climb people

- **In the game:** [Vesper](../heroes/vesper.md), [Silvane](../heroes/silvane.md), and [Opaline](../heroes/opaline.md), Silvane's
  younger sister, who went south to the Deep and to the Noonsworn.
- **New:** [Corbel](../heroes/corbel.md), stone-woken.
- **On the Climb:** none of the people; the War's Crow King, a ruler of floor 46, was a Gloaming lord who rode to the Banners
  ([rulers](../climb/rulers.md)).

## Ties to other peoples

Bound to the Long Dead by old debts and older love; allied with the Dune folk; served by the Cast; at war in all but name with the
Aubade folk; and quarrelling with the Wood folk, in whose soil the Court buries the dead it cannot fit under Gloaming.
