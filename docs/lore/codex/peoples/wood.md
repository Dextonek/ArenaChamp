# Wood folk

*Human, changed by a forest that walks. The horned people of the Walking Wood and the high pastures.* · [Peoples](README.md) ·
[The Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md) · [The Greenwood Moot](../factions/greenwood.md)

## Origin

The Wood folk are the woodcutters, herders, hunters, charcoal burners and hive-keepers of the great western forest and the hill
pastures above it. Before the Fall the forest stood still, and they were a plain human people with a reputation for silence and
good cheese. They shared the forest with the [Briarkin](briar.md) of the Underbough by old treaty: the folk keep to the paths,
the fae keep to the glades, and nobody whistles after dark.

On the day of the Fall the Wood began to walk, as if it had felt the sun go and set off to find it. Within a year something else
began: the Wood folk who had sworn themselves to the Wood, the wardens and hunters of the Moot, began to grow **horns**.

## Homeland

[The Walking Wood](../realms/wood.md), at the ninth and tenth hours, and the high pastures where the Wood meets the hills: the
Antler Glades, where the Moot meets; the hive-trees; the charcoal clearings; the villages at the Wood's edge, which move when the
Wood does; the shepherds' summer towns on the hills.

## Body

Middling height and strong, with long arms. Skin ruddy to bark-brown, freckled. Hair brown, auburn or fox-red, worn long and
braided with wooden beads. Eyes a moss green that is their own; the Wood folk say it came from looking at leaves. The mark of the
people is the **horns**, and they come with an oath. A Wood person who swears the **Moot's oath**, to keep the Wood and be kept by
it, grows horns within a year of swearing: branching antlers in the forest, curling ram horns among the hill shepherds, and on a
few, like [Fenna](../heroes/fenna.md), antlers that are living branches and put out leaves in spring. Horns are shed and regrown
each year in the Wood, and kept for life in the hills. A Wood person who has not sworn, as most of the villagers and traders have
not, grows none.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body.
- **Can be:** ruddy, freckled skin; moss-green eyes; braided hair with beads; **horns** on the sworn (antlers, ram horns or
  leafing branches), which are the people's one strong mark and carry well from the game's camera; leaf cloaks, fur mantles,
  wool, bark, amber; axes, crooks, bows, horns to blow.
- **Cannot be:** a deer's or a ram's head (they are human-faced); hooves; bark skin (that is the Wood itself, not the folk).
- **Reads as:** a strong figure in green and brown with a crown of horns. Unsworn Wood folk read as plain country people in
  green and amber, as the Climb's five do.

## Lifespan and the Fall

About seventy years, a little longer for the sworn: the Wood keeps its own. The Fall made the Wood walk, and the folk have
followed it ever since. Villages are built on skids; fields are planted where the Wood was last year; a farmer may wake to find
an oak grove in the barley. The horns are the second change. The Wood folk take them as a sign that the Wood has chosen to keep
its people, and they are proud of them. Hunters and smiths from elsewhere call them the Horned, not kindly.

## Culture

Quiet, stubborn and cooperative: the Moot decides everything by talking until nobody objects, which can take a season. They are
people of rules about the forest: a felled tree is paid for with a planted one; a hive is not robbed in the first frost; a stag
is not hunted in the year it sheds. They eat bread, cheese, honey, mushrooms, venison, mutton and a hard cider. Their crafts are
timber, charcoal, wool, amber, honey, wax and the long horns they blow to call the flocks and the Moot.

They argue with each other about almost everything, and the arguments are friendly; [Fenna](../heroes/fenna.md) and
[Hank](../heroes/hank.md) have shared a fire every winter for twenty years. They agree about the Tower, which they believe
drinks the Dial.

## Faith

The **Root**. The Wood folk believe the world is one living thing and the forest its oldest part, and that everything taken from
it is owed back. They keep shrines at old trees, leave the first cut of every harvest at the Wood's edge, and bury their dead
standing, with an acorn in each hand. Since the Fall they hold that the Wood walks to find the sun, and that the Tower, which
stands where the sun fell, has roots that drink the Dial. The Moot wants those roots cut.

## Names

Plain names, often of trees, birds and tools: Fenna, Hank (a hewer's name), Bo, Arden, Rowan, Tam. Family names are the place of
birth, which moves: Fenna of the Third Glade, which is now somewhere else. The sworn take an oath-name from the horn they grow:
Bo is called Bo Curlhorn in the hills.

## How they fight

As hunters and woodsmen: bows from the trees, axes in the thickets, herds driven into the enemy, traps, horns to call each other
through the forest. The Hunt of the Moot rides elk. A Wood folk fighter is hard to find, hard to move and harder to chase.

## Heroes and Climb people

- **In the game:** [Fenna](../heroes/fenna.md), [Hank](../heroes/hank.md), [Bo](../heroes/bo.md).
- **New:** [Arden](../heroes/arden.md).
- **On the Climb:** the Wood's five, the Moss-Abbess, the Path-Lighter, the Kiln-Mother, the Amber-Seller and the Charm-Pedlar,
  none of whom swore the Moot's oath; the Moss-Abbess's horn is an instrument, not her own ([the Wood](../climb/wood.md)).

## Ties to other peoples

Old treaty-friends of the Briarkin; friends of the Coast folk; enemies of the Kilnborn at the Wood's edge; enemies of the Gloaming
folk, whose Court buries its overflow dead in their soil; hosts of the Northerners on the Frostfall road.
