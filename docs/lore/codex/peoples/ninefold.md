# The Ninefold

*Not human. The cat-headed knights of the Dial: lynx on the Field, lion at the gate of Tamerit.* · [Peoples](README.md) ·
[The Field of Banners](../realms/banners.md)

## Origin

Nobody, the Ninefold included, knows where the first of them came from. Their own legend is that the first Ninefold knight was a
great lynx who saved a king of Harrowmark from drowning nine times, and was asked what she wanted in return; she asked for hands,
to hold a sword, and a vow, to have something to keep. The king gave her both, and her children walked upright. Whatever the
truth, by about 250 years before the Fall there were Ninefold knights in every court of the Dial, and they swore their oath at the
**Keep of Nine** on the edge of the Field of Banners.

There have never been many. A Ninefold mother has one child, rarely two, and many Ninefold never have any.

## Homeland

The Keep of Nine, the old lynx knights' keep on a ridge above the Field of Banners, now half empty; and, for three generations,
the gate of Tamerit in the Sands, which a lion line of the Ninefold has kept for the Dune Throne. Most Ninefold live wherever their
oath has taken them.

## Body

A tall, lithe human figure with a great cat's head. The **lynx line**: tawny or grey fur, black ear tufts, a ruff at the jaw,
amber eyes. The **lion line**: heavier, gold fur, a mane that the men wear long and the women braid, eyes gold or green. Fur
covers the head and throat and the backs of the hands, and the hands end in short claws they keep trimmed for the sword. They
stand a little taller than a human and move a great deal better.

## Nine lives

The Ninefold are named for it. A Ninefold who dies gets up again, eight times: the wound closes, the heart starts, and they are
a little different after, as if a room in them had been shut. On the ninth death they stay dead. Every Ninefold knows how many
lives they have left, and wears the count somewhere (a ring of studs on the collar, nine knots on a sword cord). They spend their
lives, as the saying goes, like coin: on a vow, a wager or a friend. A Ninefold on their last life is called **one-left**, and is
treated with great courtesy and kept out of fights.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body, with a cat's head built on the human head, as [Sir Whiskers](../heroes/sir-whiskers.md) has now.
- **Can be:** a lynx's or lion's head (tufted ears, ruff, mane), fur at the throat and hands, short claws; armour of every court
  they have served; rapiers, spears, cloaks; a count of nine somewhere on the costume.
- **Cannot be:** a tail that moves (the pack has no tail bones; a short lynx tail can be built as a stiff piece, or left out);
  digitigrade legs; a cat on all fours.
- **Reads as:** a slim armoured figure with a cat's head and tufted ears. The lion line reads bigger, with a mane.

## Lifespan and the Fall

A Ninefold lives about eighty years if all their lives are kept, which none are. The Fall touched them strangely: the knights
alive that day found their counts of lives had all gone back to nine. The Ninefold call it **the Noon Grace**, and a good many of
them spent it in the War of the Banners, on both sides.

## Culture

A people of vows. A Ninefold knight swears to a court, a cause or a person, and keeps it with great style until it is kept or
broken; then they swear again elsewhere. They are proud, vain, courteous, curious, and fond of risk. They do not lie, but they
enjoy misleading. They eat fish, fowl and cream, and drink almost nothing. Their art is the duel, which they treat as a
conversation.

The Ninefold are fewer every generation, and they know it. The Keep of Nine was full of knights in the year of the Fall and has
eleven now. The young ones are trying to change that, without much luck.

## Faith

None that they will name. They keep **the Count**: each life is a coin lent to them, to be spent well, and the ninth is returned to
whoever lent it. Some say the lender was the sun, which would explain the Noon Grace.

## Names

Old knightly names, often long, often shortened by friends: Sir Whiskers' full name is Sir Wystan Aurelio of Nine, which he will
recite if asked and sometimes if not. The lions of the gate take gate names in the Dune tongue: Ramet means "gate".

## How they fight

As duellists, with rapiers and spears, light armour and a great deal of movement; they leap, land on their feet, and spend a
life when a fight is worth it. In war they were the Dial's best scouts and its best rearguard.

## Ties to other peoples

They have served all of them. They are fondest of the Dune folk, who give them a gate, and of the Briarkin, who play their games;
they are wary of the Long Dead, who know something about getting up again that the Ninefold would rather not learn.
