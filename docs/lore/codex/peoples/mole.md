# Mole-folk

*Not human. The Deep's first people, who call themselves the Loam.* · [Peoples](README.md) · [The Deep](../realms/deep.md) ·
[The Underforge](../factions/underforge.md)

## Origin

The mole-folk lived in the Deep before anyone, and say so whenever it is useful. Their **seam-songs**, sung while digging and
passed from mother to daughter, go back about nine hundred years before the Fall by the Hourkeepers' reckoning, and the oldest
of them already describe halls that are older still. They call themselves **the Loam**, and the world above ground "the Thin",
because there is so little of it between you and the sky. Nobody knows where they came from. Their own story is that the first
mole-folk dug up out of the dark below the world, found the Deep comfortable, and stopped.

## Homeland

[The Deep](../realms/deep.md): the deepest seams under the southern hills, below the Kilnborn's halls, where the rock is warm and
the air is still. Their towns are warrens of round rooms joined by tunnels exactly as wide as a mole-folk's shoulders, which is
why other peoples visit rarely. The Lamp Road, the great tunnel that runs from the Deep's mouth down to the crystal halls, is
theirs, and they keep it open.

## Body

The figure of a short, stout person, with a mole's head. Velvet fur, dark brown to black, short and dense, covering the head,
the throat and the backs of the hands. A long, mobile muzzle with a small pink or rose nose, which they use more than their
eyes. Small eyes, black and calm, that see poorly in bright light and well enough in the dark. Ears hidden in the fur. And the
**claws**: hands broad as shovels with long curved nails, which they gild at the tips when they come of age. They are about a
head shorter than a human and broader, and very strong for their size.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body, short and broad, with a mole's head built on the human head (as Sir Whiskers' cat's head is), as
[Mabel](../heroes/mabel.md) is now.
- **Can be:** a mole's head with a long muzzle and small eyes; dark fur at the head, throat and hands; big hands with long gilded
  claws (built on the hand, moving with the pack's fingers); helmets with crystal lamps; studded leather; picks, charges, pry-bars.
- **Cannot be:** a mole's body (no digging on all fours: the pack's clips are human); a tail; fur over the whole body under armour
  that would hide the human frame anyway.
- **Reads as:** a short dark figure with a long-nosed head and a glowing lamp on top, and gold at the hands. Nothing else on the
  roster has that silhouette.

## Lifespan and the Fall

About sixty years, and they age fast at the end. The Fall touched them first of all the peoples and least. They were deepest
when the sun fell, and they were the first to see its light come down: it ran into the crystals of the Deep through the rock,
and the dark halls they had known for nine hundred years were suddenly lit. The mole-folk call that day **the Shining**, and are
divided about it. The old say it ruined the dark. The young, like [Mabel](../heroes/mabel.md), say the light belongs to the
miners who dug down to it. The Noon-touched among them are very few, because most of their elders were deep enough not to be
touched by anything.

## Culture

A people of mothers and seams. Every warren is run by its Old Mother, every seam by the family that opened it, and every quarrel
by the oldest song that mentions it. They are polite, literal, curious and very hard to offend, except by a cave-in, which they
take personally. They eat roots, grubs, fungus, cave fish and a kind of bread made from root flour that keeps for a year. They
trade crystals and ore to the Kilnborn for tools, and the two peoples have mined side by side for nine centuries with exactly one
long argument: who was there first.

## Faith

The **Dark**. The mole-folk believe the world is a body and the Deep is its warm middle, and that the dark is not the absence of
light but a thing in itself, comfortable and safe. They do not worship anything; they listen. A mole-folk prays by putting a
paw to the rock and being quiet. Since the Shining a young teaching has grown, the Lamp, which says the light in the crystals was
sent to them, and should be dug out, carried and shared, and certainly not given to the Noonsworn.

## Names

Soft, round names with the sound of digging in them: Mabel, Tova, Odda, Bessom, Mudge. Family names are the seam: Mabel of the
Deepest, Tova Lamp-Road. The Old Mothers are named only by their warren.

## How they fight

From below. Mole-folk dig under the enemy and come up beneath them; they collapse floors, open pits, and fight close with their
claws and short picks. In the open they are slow and know it, and they rarely fight in the open.

## Ties to other peoples

Neighbours and rivals of the Kilnborn; suspicious of the Aubade folk, who want their light; friendly with the Briarkin, who also
live under things; frightened of the Fallborn's Embers, because fire in a warren is death.
