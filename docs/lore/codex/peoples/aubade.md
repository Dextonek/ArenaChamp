# Aubade folk

*Human. The people of the morning cliffs, who ring the sun up.* · [Peoples](README.md) · [Aubade](../realms/aubade.md) ·
[The Noonsworn](../factions/noonsworn.md)

## Origin

The Aubade folk say they were the first to be woken by the sun, because their cliffs face the morning and the light reached them
before it reached anyone. Their oldest songs begin with a woman on a cliff edge who sang back when the sun rose, and the sun,
pleased, came up a little faster the next day. From her, they say, come the bells, the choir and the Hymn of Noon. The
Hourkeepers' charts put the first town on the cliffs about 1,100 years before the Fall, a fishing village that had learned to
read the Tower's shadow from across the bay.

For four hundred years before the Fall the Aubade folk sent a choir of a hundred up the Tower every day to sing the Hymn at its
top, and they were proud of it in the way a family is proud of a lamp it has never let go out. When the sun fell during the Hymn,
it fell on their pride first.

## Homeland

[Aubade](../realms/aubade.md), the white city on the eastern cliffs, at the first hour of the Dial; the shrine cliffs below it
where the wrestlers train; the terraces of vines and olives inland; the Dawn Stair down to the harbour of Lowlight. Many Aubade
folk live at the Ring too, as the Truce's couriers, clerks and cooks, because the road from Aubade to the Tower is the oldest
paved road on the Dial.

## Body

Tall and long-limbed, with a straight back they are taught as children ("stand like a bell rope"). Skin warm golden-brown to
olive, freckling across the nose in summer. Hair dark gold to black, never cut short except in mourning, worn long and bound
with gold thread: one thread for each oath kept. Eyes brown or hazel, often light at the rim. The sworn of the Noonsworn carry
the **dawn line**, a thin gold tattoo from the middle of the brow down the bridge of the nose, put on at dawn on the day of the
oath.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body, unchanged: the Aubade folk are the plainest human people, and their mark is in colour and dress.
- **Can be:** skin in the golden-brown range; long bound hair with gold threads; the dawn line as a thin gold stripe on the face
  texture; white linen, ivory and gold; a bell, a lantern-shrine, a sun disc; layered white robes for the choir and heavy plate
  for the paladins.
- **Cannot be:** wings of feather or light grown from the body (Auriel's are armour, built as a cloak of plates, and must stay
  so); glowing skin (that is an Ember, not Aubade folk); hair cut short on a hero who is not in mourning.
- **Reads from the game camera as:** a tall figure in white and gold with dark hair and warm skin. Beside the Dune folk they are
  lighter and longer; beside the Northerners, slimmer and gold where those are blue.

## Lifespan and the Fall

They live about eighty years. The Fall touched them twice. Every Aubade child alive that week is Noon-touched, as everywhere,
and many of Aubade's elders are; and the choir did not come home. Of the hundred who sang the Hymn on the day, two came down the
Tower: [Cantor](../heroes/cantor.md) and [Mum](../heroes/mum.md). Every family in Aubade lost a singer, and the city has kept a
year of mourning a hundred times over: the bells are rung every morning, and the hundredth bell is rung short, for the choir.

## Culture

The Aubade folk are early risers, oath-takers and keepers of rhythm. A child's first gift is a small bell; a marriage is a
duet; a death is a verse. They argue in public and forgive in private. Their houses are whitewashed every spring and face east
with a window that is never shuttered. Their food is bread, olives, white cheese, figs and a black honey from the cliff flowers,
and the dawn meal is the important one. They make the best bronze on the Dial outside the Deep, and the best paper outside
Frostfall.

Since the Fall the city has become harder. The Noonsworn ([faction](../factions/noonsworn.md)) run it in all but name, and an
Aubade child who says the sun should not be hung again will be listened to kindly and not invited back. The poorer quarters
under the cliff, where the light comes last, are less certain, and send more children to the Ring than to the barracks.

## Faith

The Aubade folk do not worship the sun so much as keep it company. Their faith is the **Oath of the Dawn**: that every night
ends, that the end can be helped, and that whoever helps it must keep faith with it every morning. The temples are bell towers.
There are no priests, only ringers, singers and the sworn. Since the Fall most Aubade folk believe the sun can be hung again and
that it is their duty to do it. A quieter teaching, the Low Verse, holds that the sun fell because the Hymn was sung for pride
and not for company, and that it will come back when it is asked kindly. The Noonsworn do not forbid the Low Verse. They simply
never ring for it.

## Names

Given names are bell-words and light-words in the old cliff tongue, often softened: Auriel ("first light"), Campana ("bell"),
Orren ("bearer"), Cantor ("singer"), Pip (a courier's nickname: his given name is Pellam-of-the-Stair, which nobody calls him). Family names are
the house's bell: Auriel of the White Bell, Orren of the Low Lantern. A sworn hero drops the family bell and takes the oath's
name.

## How they fight

In shield walls of white and gold, with long swords and longer patience: the Aubade way is to hold, then break out all at once,
"as the dawn does". Their paladins wear plate and wings of plates, and their shields are made to burst outward when broken, an
old smith's trick that became a creed. The shrine wrestlers below the cliffs fight inside a rope circle and count a fall as a
lesson. The choir fought only once, at the Fall, and lost.

## Ties to other peoples

Old friends of the Northerners, who share their wish for a sun over the Tower; uneasy with the Dune folk, whose king calls the sun
his father; cold to the Gloaming folk since the Fall, though before it Aubade's choir and Gloaming's mirror-makers traded songs
for glass every midsummer. They trade bells for copper with the Kilnborn and do not trust the copper.
