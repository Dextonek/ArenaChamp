# Coast folk

*Human. The people of the Drowned Coast and the Spires, who read the weather and sail anyway.* · [Peoples](README.md) ·
[The Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md) · [The Windward](../factions/windward.md)

## Origin

The Coast folk have always lived where the land runs out: fishers and pilots on the south-eastern shore of the Dial, and the
tower-dwellers of the Spires, the broken sky towers that stand in the sea off the coast. Nobody built the Spires, as far as the
Coast folk know, and they have lived in them as long as they have lived anywhere: there are rooms in the Spires that have been
lived in for a thousand years and rooms nobody has found the door to. The Coast folk say the land was given to the others and
the sea and the sky to them, and that this was the better deal.

## Homeland

[The Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md), at the fifth hour: the Drowned Harbour, which was the great port of Sallowmouth before the
sea came in over it; the harbour towns behind it (Saltreach, Tern's Landing); the Lantern Coast, with a lighthouse on every point;
the sea temple; and the Spires, standing in the sea. Coast folk crew every ship on the Outer Sea and most of the Truce's ferries.

## Body

Wiry and quick, with strong forearms. Skin sea-tanned, from light bronze to brown, lined early round the eyes. Hair salt-bleached
from sand to near white, and often dyed sea-green or blue with kelp. Eyes grey, green or brown. The marks of the people are the
**tide lines**, thin blue tattoos round the forearms, one for each great flood the wearer has lived through (a Coast elder may
have twenty), and **sea-glass** worn in the ears and hair. The Spire-born are lighter and longer-limbed than the harbour folk, and
can stand on a ledge in a gale without holding on.

## In the figure

On the pack's human body.
- **Can be:** sea-tanned skin; salt-bleached or sea-green hair; tide-line bands on the forearms (thin blue rings on the skin
  texture); sea-glass in the ears; short coats, oilskins, sashes and scarves; bare feet in the temple; staffs, urns, kites,
  weathervanes, balloons, ribbons of water.
- **Cannot be:** gills, fins or scales (the Coast folk are told they have them by people from the Sands, and are tired of it).
- **Reads as:** a lean figure in blues, sea-greens and white, with weathered skin and pale hair. Beside the Northerners (dark skin,
  white braids) they are lighter in skin and wear short, wind-cut clothes, not long wool.

## Lifespan and the Fall

About seventy years; the sea takes many sooner. The Fall came to them as water. The sun's heat lifted the sea, and on the day of
the Fall the sea came in over Sallowmouth and did not go out: the Drowned Harbour. Half the city drowned that afternoon. Since then
the weather has had no master, the storms come when they like, and the Coast folk have become the Dial's readers of the sky,
because they had to. Every Coast child learns to swim before they walk, and the first tide line is drawn at the first flood.

## Culture

Practical, generous, loud and superstitious. The Coast folk share everything that floats and argue about everything that does
not. A boat is a family; a family is a crew; a crew elects its captain every spring. They whistle to call the wind and never
whistle indoors. They eat fish, shellfish, seaweed bread, oranges from the terraces behind the coast, and a fiery fish soup that
is the true test of a guest. Their crafts are boats, rope, sails, glass floats, kites and weather-glasses. Since the Fall they
have become inventors too, of balloons and lightning rods and things nobody else would try.

## Faith

The **Tide**. The sea comes in and goes out, and so do lives, fortunes and storms; the wise person keeps their floor clean and
their boat tied. The sea temple of the Drowned Harbour is the centre of the faith, and its keeper ([Gus](../heroes/gus.md)) is
the nearest thing the Coast folk have to a priest, which he denies. The faith has no view on the sun. Most Coast folk would back
whoever can mend the sky and give them back seasons, and say so.

## Names

Short, bright names, bird names and wind names: Marin, Kaia, Zephyr, Lian, Morwen, Gus (short for Augustin, which only his mother
used). Surnames are boats or weather: Barry Cloudsley, Tam Netmender, Ilse Fairwind. Professor Puff's name is his own choosing,
from the sound his balloons make; his birth name is in the Spires' register and he will not say it.

## How they fight

At sea, by boarding, with short blades, hooks and nets; on land, as skirmishers who never stand still. Their fighters use the
weather: the storm callers of the Spires bring down lightning, the tide dancers turn the water, and the Spire-born fight on the wind
itself. A Coast fighter wins by being somewhere else when the blow lands.

## Ties to other peoples

Friends of the Wood folk (rain for the Wood, timber for the ships); trade with everyone; quarrel with the Dune folk over the Sands'
storms and the Sands' king; mistrust the Kilnborn's Gaol of Oaths under their harbour; admire the Northerners and think them slow.
