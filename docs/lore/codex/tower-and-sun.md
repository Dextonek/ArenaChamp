# The Tower and the Sun

## The answer

The Tower took the sun. It is a made thing, the **Aumbry**, the keeping-place, built long ago on another world, **Senmere**, by a
people whose sun was failing, to keep what was ending. It takes a thing at its hour and keeps it as it was, for ever, on one of its
floors. It kept its makers' sun and then their world, and when there was nothing of Senmere left to keep, the word set in it at its
making (*keep*, which was its maker's own name) sent it across the dark between the stars to the next world with an ageing sun. It
came to the Dial before anyone on the Dial could remember, and stood at its centre as its gnomon. For thousands of years its shadow
told the hours, because a keeper must know the hour to take it.

Three hundred years before the Fall it began to draw on the sun at noon, a little each day, and the sun paled. At noon on midsummer day
of year 0 it closed its hand. The sun stopped for nine heartbeats, and the Tower took its noon into itself: its light, its hour and
the choir singing in that hour. The sun's body was too great to keep. It cracked in the Tower's grip and fell. Since then the Tower has
drunk the fallen heart's light as it rises through it each morning, and taken the Dial's realms one by one, each at the hour of its
greatest loss. At noon on the Hundredth Midsummer it means to take what is left, and go.

It is not cruel. It was made out of grief, to save, and it cannot tell keeping from taking. Everything ends, and so to the Aumbry
everything is ending, and must be kept.

## What the Dial saw

What every realm agrees on, because thousands saw it, and how each piece fits the answer:

- **The sun stopped for nine heartbeats, then cracked and fell.** The nine heartbeats were the Tower's grip. Bo's sheep still stand
  still for nine heartbeats at noon on midsummer, facing it; [Tempo](heroes/tempo.md)'s clock points nine heartbeats past noon.
- **Of the choir of a hundred, two came down.** The other ninety-eight are kept, still singing, in the hour of noon. Cantor and Mum
  were out of step with it.
- **The Hourkeepers' Noon Folio measured three hundred years of paling.** That was the Tower drawing on the sun. "Something hastened
  it," the Hourkeepers wrote. Something did.
- **The festival market at the Tower's foot was gone.** Not burned: taken, with everyone in it, as **the Market of Noon**.
- **The Tear opened where the sun had hung.** It is the hole the taking tore in the sky, and it opens on the dark the Aumbry travels.
- **The Tower's gate was shut ninety years, and inside are realms as they were at some hour, with their people in them.** They are
  the Tower's collection.
- **The light pales and the Tear widens a little every year.** The Tower drinks, and fills, and makes ready to leave.

## The old candidates, and what became of them

Before the user's choice the codex offered three answers to each question. They are kept here because the Dial believed them, and
the characters still argue them.

- **"The Hymn broke."** Believed by the Noonsworn, by Cantor about Ansel, by Mum about herself. In truth the Hymn was the Tower's
  instrument, taught by its keeper; Mum's missed beat saved her, and her silence keeps the last verse.
- **"Something came through the Tear."** Believed by Wick, Thessa and much of the Ringside. The Unmaker did come through, but it was
  pulled: it is Senmere's ending, which the Tower drags behind it. The Tear is the Tower's wound in the sky, not a door someone else
  broke.
- **"The Tower is a clock."** The Hourkeepers' answer. It was the Dial's clock, as a hunter keeps a calendar of the herd. In the
  Story's ending it becomes one in earnest.
- **"The Tower is the sun's own stalk, growing a new one."** The Underforge's and Anka's hope. The Tower grows nothing. But the Ember
  of Aubade is a seed, and in the ending it grows.

## Why the Tower collects

The makers of Senmere lived under a sun that was going out. They could not save it, and they could not bear to lose it, so they made
a thing that would keep it: a tower to stand at their world's centre, gather in its noon at the last, and hold it there for ever,
with their world around it at its best hour. Iselm, who wrote the word, made it in his own name, so that the Aumbry would keep as he
would have kept. It worked. Senmere is on the Aumbry's highest floor, under its sun, at noon. Iselm did not let himself be kept: he
stayed outside, to watch over it.

What he had not thought of was what a keeper does when it has kept everything. The word did not end. The Aumbry pulled up its roots
and went into the dark, and Iselm went with it, because he could not leave it alone. It came to the Dial, whose sun had ten thousand
years left, and to a keeper ten thousand years is ending. It planted itself and waited, and kept the hours, and when the sun began to
age it began to draw.

Behind it, through every hole it has made in every sky, comes what is left of Senmere: the evening that world was never allowed to
have. The Hourkeepers call it the Unmaker.

## What it means for the game

- **The Climb** is a way into the thing that took the sun. Its chapters are realms the Tower collected; its people were taken in
  those hours; its rulers are the worst of each hour, kept with the rest; its crowns are the Tower's keepings of the sun's pieces.
- **The Raid** is fought in the Hall of Noon beneath the Ring, against the sun's pieces themselves, which the Tower has bound to
  guard the Heart it drinks.
- **The Trials**, the weekly Tower, are the Tower's measure of champions: its echoes of every hero who fought in the Ring.
- **The duels** are fought on the Tower's threshold, under clauses the Tower itself keeps.
- **The Noon Seat** is where the Tower keeps the sun's noon. Whoever reaches it decides what hangs there, because the Tower keeps
  what was sworn in its shadow, and the Truce swore its gate to the Ring's champions.
