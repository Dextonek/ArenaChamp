# The Tower and the Sun

*Two questions the Dial cannot answer: why the sun fell, and what the Tower is.* · [Codex](README.md)

The user left both open on purpose. The codex is written so that any of the answers below fits: every page gives them only as
what someone believes. This page sets out what is known, what each faction believes, and the candidate answers, each with what it
would mean for the game. The choice is the user's.

## What is known

- At noon on midsummer day a hundred years ago the sun stopped over the Tower, cracked and fell. Many thousands saw it.
- At that moment the choir of a hundred was singing the Hymn of Noon at the Tower's top, as it had every noon for four hundred
  years. Two singers came down: Cantor and Mum. Nobody else who was at the top that day has been seen since, except the Sun Warden,
  who did not come down at all.
- The Hourkeepers' **Noon Folio** records that the sun had been paling for about three hundred years before the Fall: a little less
  light each midsummer, measured on the same lens every year. They did not tell anyone; they were not sure.
- The sun's heart lies at the Tower's foot in the Hall of Noon and still burns. Its last embers burn in the Cinder Pit. The thin
  light that rises each morning rises from the Heart through the Tower.
- The Tear opened above the Tower at the moment of the Fall, and the Unmaker came through it the same day.
- The Tower's gate was shut for ninety years and has opened since to champions of the Ring. Inside it, floor above floor, are
  places of the Dial as they were at some hour, with their people still in them.
- The Tear widens a little every year. The light pales a little every year.

## Why the sun fell: three candidate answers

### A. The Hymn broke

The sun was held up by the Hymn of Noon: not by its music but by its **keeping**, the daily promise of a people to the sky, sung
on the beat, at the top of the gnomon, for four hundred years. On the day of the Fall the beat was lost. Someone in the choir sang
off the beat, or stopped, or sang for pride and not for company; the keeping broke; the sun, which had been paling for centuries,
was no longer held, and fell.
- **Fits:** Cantor's beat and Mum's silence (they would be the two who know), the Noonsworn's bells, Campana's belief that enough
  bells could call it back, the Hourkeepers' measurements of a paling sun.
- **Means for the game:** the Hymn can be sung again. A future mode or event could be the gathering of the Hymn's verses; the Noon
  Seat answers to song as well as to strength. Several heroes privately believe they know who missed the beat (Cantor believes it
  was Auriel's father; Mum believes it was herself); in this answer one of them is right.

### B. The Tower took it

The Tower is a thing that takes. It was built (or grew) to hold the sun at noon, and for a thousand years it held it; then it began
to draw it in, the way it now draws in the realms. The sun paled for three hundred years as the Tower drank it. On the day of the
Fall the Tower took it whole: the sun cracked, its heart fell to the Tower's foot, and the Tower has been feeding on that heart
since, which is why the light that rises through it each morning is thinner every year.
- **Fits:** the Greenwood's roots, the floors that hold realms "as they were at the hour it took them", the paling light, the Climb
  as a way into the thing that did it.
- **Means for the game:** the Climb is a fight against the Tower itself. Its rulers are what it has digested. The fiftieth floor
  and the Noon Seat are where it keeps what it took, and winning the Climb could free the sun, or the realms, but perhaps not both.

### C. Something came through

The sky was not torn by the sun's fall; the sun fell because the sky was torn. Something on the far side of the sky, where the
Unmade come from, broke through at noon: the Tear opened, the sun was cracked by what came through, and fell. The Unmaker is not the
cause but the first of what followed, and the Tear is a wound still open. The sun's three centuries of paling were the far side
pressing on the sky.
- **Fits:** Oblivion, the Tear widening, Wick's and Thessa's work, the Unmade in the Climb's war, the Hourkeepers' "something
  hastened it".
- **Means for the game:** the true enemy is outside the Dial. The factions' quarrel over the Noon Seat is a quarrel over a house
  with a hole in the roof; the endgame is the Tear, sewn or sealed, and a new sun cannot hold until it is.

The three can mix: the user could choose "the sun was dying (A's keeping, failing), and the Tower drew it down (B), and through the
gap came the Unmaker (C)". The codex never says which.

## What the Tower is: three candidate answers

### 1. The clock

The Tower is an instrument: the gnomon of a great sundial built, long before the Long Count, by a people nobody remembers, to hold
the sun at noon and keep the Dial's hours. Its floors are its works, rooms of a machine, and the places inside them are records:
the Tower keeps a copy of whatever its shadow touched at each hour, the way a clock keeps the time. The Sun Warden is its keeper.
The Noon Seat is where the sun was set to turn. The clock is broken, not dead, and can be mended.
- **Fits:** the Hourkeepers, the dial in the Sun Court that still strikes, the stopped shadow, the slipping hours.
- **Means for the game:** the Climb is a repair. Each chapter's crown is a part of the clock (the Warden, the embers, the heart).
  The fiftieth floor could be the clock's face.

### 2. The collector (the proposal so far)

The Tower collects worlds. Since the Fall it has been taking the realms in, floor by floor, as they were at the hour it took them:
the Sea on the morning the water came, the Wood walking, the Sands in their storm, the Deep without light, the War still fought.
The people of the floors were there when the Tower took them and still keep their posts. Each floor's ruler is the worst thing its
realm had. Whether the realms outside are thinner for it, nobody can prove.
- **Fits:** the Climb as built (five chapters, each a realm), the Greenwood's belief, the people on the floors.
- **Means for the game:** more chapters are more realms (Frostfall, Aubade, Gloaming have not been taken yet, or have they?); a
  chapter can be a realm's dark mirror. This is the one the Climb's writing has used so far, marked as a proposal.

### 3. The nest

The Tower is the sun's own: not a building but a stalk, a living thing the sun grew from, or grew on, as a fruit on a tree. When the
sun fell the stalk began to grow it again, slowly, from the Heart at its root; the floors are its rings of growth, each one made of
what the sun's light had touched; the thin morning light is the new sun's first glow. Whatever is set in the Noon Seat will become
the new sun's seed.
- **Fits:** the Ember of Aubade as a seed, the Underforge's "reforged", the Embers as sparks that want to go back, Anka's climb.
- **Means for the game:** the Noon Seat is a choice of what the next sun will be: the Ember (the Noonsworn), a king (the Throne),
  nothing (the Court), something else. A strong ending for a season.

## How the codex keeps both questions open

- Every page says "the Noonsworn believe", "the Moot say", "the Hourkeepers suspect". The narrator states only what the Dial saw.
- The Climb's chapters call the realms-in-the-Tower "the proposal" and describe the floors as places, without saying how they came
  to be there.
- The heroes' secrets are beliefs and private knowledge, never the answer.
- The Noon Seat is "where the sun rested at noon; it is empty; whoever reaches it decides what hangs there". That sentence fits all
  nine combinations.
