# The canon at a glance

The facts every codex page agrees with: the peoples, which hero belongs to which, the count of years, the dates that matter
and the new names. If a page and this sheet disagree, this sheet wins, and the page is fixed. The short source is still
[LORE.md](../LORE.md); this sheet adds what the codex needs. Back to the [codex](README.md).

## Two questions left open

Two things are never said as fact by any codex page, because the user has not chosen them: **why the sun fell** and **what
the Tower is**. Every page gives them as what someone believes. The candidate answers are in
[The Tower and the Sun](tower-and-sun.md).

## Counting the years

- The Dial counts from the Noonfall: **year 0** is the year the sun fell, at noon on midsummer day. Years before it are
  counted down ("40 before the Fall"). Now is **the hundredth summer**: 100 after the Fall.
- The old count, before the Fall, was the Hourkeepers' **Long Count**, from their first star chart; the Fall came in its
  1,212th year. Nobody uses it now except the Hourkeepers and Mr. Ledger.
- Since the Tower's shadow stopped, the hours slip: a day runs between twenty and twenty-six of the old hours, and no two
  realms agree what time it is. Midsummer is still kept, by the bells of Aubade, and it is when the Ring opens.
- **The Noon-touched**: everyone alive on the day of the Fall, or born in the week after it, ages about a year for every five.
  A Noon-touched person who was 20 at the Fall looks 40 now.

## The twelve peoples

Twelve peoples live on the Dial. Four are human; three are human peoples the Fall changed; five are not human at all. Each has
its own page.

| [Dune folk](peoples/dune.md) | human | The Sands | Lean; dark bronze to deep brown skin; black hair shaved or in fine braids; kohl to the temples; gold on the lids at court | Sefu, Vitriol, Madame Cleaver; *new:* Lithea, Nebet, Sahar, Asenet |

| [The Fallborn](peoples/fallborn.md) | other | Wherever the Fall made them | Three kinds, each its own: the Embers (skin that glows at its edges, hair like flame), the Cast (a matte-black body with no face), the Unmade (a shape of black with pink rifts) | Anka (an Ember), Nyx (of the Cast), Oblivion (the Unmade) |

Kins inside a people (shown after its name where it matters): the Briarkin's courts (Acorn: Pim and Pom; Hive: Sirix;
Spindle: Grandma Ironknit; Fox: Kohaku; Moth: Vela; none: Zambini), the Fallborn's kinds, the Ninefold's lynx and lion
lines, the Wood folk's antlered and ram-horned, and Corbel, who is Gloaming folk turned to stone and woken.

Siblings share a people: Opaline and Silvane (Gloaming folk), Isolde and Saga (Northerners, lake-born), Sefu and Asenet
(Dune folk). The lore check holds to it.

## The Climb's people, rulers and enemies

Built figures and names already in the game (`src/tower/world/folk.ts`, the floors). Their peoples are given on the
[Climb](climb/README.md) and [bestiary](climb/bestiary.md) pages. The 25 people were built as plain humans before the peoples
were written; where a people's mark is missing on a figure, the revamp plan lists it as a later touch, and the lore says why
(a hood, a vow not taken, a hall far from the fire).

- **The Sea (1-10):** the Bell-Warden, the Net-Father, the Lamp-Bearer, the Tide-Broker, the Coral-Pedlar: Coast folk.
- **The Wood (11-20):** the Moss-Abbess, the Path-Lighter, the Kiln-Mother, the Amber-Seller, the Charm-Pedlar: Wood folk,
  none of them sworn to the Moot (so no horns).
- **The Sands (21-30):** the Star-Reader, the Storm-Counter, the Beacon-Keeper, the Silk-Merchant, the Glass-Seller: Dune folk.
- **The Deep (31-40):** the Echo-Hermit, the Candle-Crowned, the Ferryman, the Crystal-Seller, the Firefly-Seller: Kilnborn of
  the far halls, where the Caldera's fire never reached (so not ash-grey).
- **The War (41-50):** the Herald, the Turncloak, the White Sister, the Camp-Mother, the Quartermaster: the Field's last
  people, Harrowmark's children; the Turncloak is of the Ninefold under his helm, and lost the head of his line.

## Names the codex keeps from elsewhere in the game

Some names in the game meet each other on purpose, and the checks allow them: Cantor (the hero) and the Blind Cantor (a ruler
in the Deep, who sang in the Hymn and went blind at the Fall); Ostrel the Ferryman (a hero) and the Ferryman (a quest giver in
the Deep, who brings the living back: they do not speak); Itzel the Obsidian Herald and the Herald (the War's keeper); the
Drowned Admiral (the ruler of floors 1 and 2), once called the Drowned Captain in the floor's oldest code; the Scarab
Colossus (floor 21) and the Scarab King (floor 22), mother and son.
