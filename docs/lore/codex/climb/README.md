# The Climb

*Fifty floors inside the Tower, in five chapters, each a realm of the Dial as the Tower holds it.* · [Codex](../README.md) ·
[The Tower and the Ring](../realms/tower.md) · [The Tower and the Sun](../tower-and-sun.md)

## The gate opens

For ninety years after the Truce the Tower's gate stayed shut. In the ninetieth summer it opened for a champion of the Ring, and
the Tally's page for that duel has been blank ever since. Inside was a harbour drowned on a morning a century ago. Since then the
gate opens to every champion of the Ring who has earned it, and the Climb has grown floor by floor: fifty floors now, in five
chapters of ten.

## What the Climb is

The Tower is a collector of worlds ([The Tower and the Sun](../tower-and-sun.md)). Each chapter is a realm it took, as it was
at the hour it took it, with the people who were in that hour; the realm outside goes on without them, thinner. Few climbers know
this in the hundredth summer. What every climber agrees on is what is there: each chapter is a realm of the Dial as it was at one hour. **The Sea**
is the Drowned Harbour on the morning the water came, **the Wood** is walking, **the Sands** are in their storm, **the Deep** is
without light, and **the War** is on the Field of Banners, where the two armies still fight because nobody inside has told them it is
over. The people of the floors say they have always been there, and they keep their posts. Each floor's ruler is the worst thing its
realm had.

What the floors *are* is argued in every tavern of the Ringside. The Greenwood Moot says the Tower collects worlds: that it has taken
these realms in, as they were at the hour it took them, and that the realms outside are thinner for it. The Hourkeepers say the floors
are records, the way a clock keeps the time. The Noonsworn say they are the sun's stair. The Moot are nearest. The floors are not records yet, though they may become them
([The Long Noon](../story/README.md)).

| [The Deep](deep.md) | 31 to 40 | The far halls of the Deep on the night their crystals went out | The Echo-Hermit | [Opaline](../heroes/opaline.md) | The Sun Warden |

In each chapter one floor (the first) is made by hand; eight floors are its own parts rearranged and stronger; and the tenth is a
**crown**, where a piece of the fallen sun waits: the Sun Warden, who kept the sun's post; Ash and Ember, its two last sparks; the
Heart of Noon, its heart. The crowns are the sun's own, and the Raid's bosses.

## Who you meet

- **The keeper** of a chapter is the same person on all ten floors, the thread through it: they know the Tower better than anyone
  and do not know how they got there.
- **The quest givers**, one at the camp and one out in the land, have something they need done that they could not do in a hundred
  years.
- **The traders** take turns floor by floor, and will take obols, which nobody in the Tower should know about.
- **The rival**, a hero of the Ring who climbed ahead and wants the right to go higher, waits on some floors for a duel.
- **The gatekeepers.** A hero of the chapter's realm holds the gate up at the end of the chapter's first floor: not the hero
  themselves, but the Tower's echo of them, kept from a duel in the Ring. Four of the five first gates are held by heroes of that
  realm; at the War it is Oblivion, because that endless war is the one thing on the Dial it has truly unmade. The gates of the
  floors above are held by champions the Tower drew to its gates: echoes of whoever has fought in the Ring, of any realm, set
  where the Tower wants a keeper. A climber of the Sands may find a gate held by a Gloaming knight or a Coast dancer; the Tower
  collects, and does not sort.
- **The ruler** of each floor, the worst thing its realm had ([rulers](rulers.md)), and its enemies ([bestiary](bestiary.md)).

## Above the fiftieth floor

**The Noon Seat**, where the sun rested at noon. From below it looks empty: it is where the Tower keeps the noon it took. Whoever
reaches it decides what hangs there. No champion has.

## What the factions say about the Climb

- **The Noonsworn** climb to carry the Ember to the Seat; Auriel and Orren have gone up as far as the Sea's crown.
- **The Court of Dusk** climbs to see the Seat first; Vesper has reached the Wood's crown.
- **The Greenwood Moot** climbs to find the Tower's roots, and believes the floors are realms the Tower has eaten.
- **The Underforge** climbs to reach the Heart before anyone moves it.
- **The Dune Throne** climbs for its king.
- **The Hourkeepers** climb to measure, and say the floors are records, not realms.
- **The Windward** climb for whoever pays, and for the view.
- **The Truce** keeps the gate, and counts who goes up and who comes down.
