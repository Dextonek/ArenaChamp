# The rulers of the Climb

*The worst thing each realm had, floor by floor, and the crowns that are the sun's own.* · [The Climb](README.md) ·
[Bestiary](bestiary.md)

Each floor of the Climb has a ruler who must fall before its gate opens. The handmade first floor of each chapter has its own; the
generated floors have theirs; the tenth floor of each chapter is a crown, a piece of the fallen sun. Their stories, as the floors' people tell them. Each is told as the Climb draws it: the Tower keeps the worst of a realm in the shape of a person, even when the realm remembers a beast or a thing, and the person bears the beast's or the thing's name.

## The Sea

- **The Drowned Admiral (floors 1 and 2).** Admiral of Sallowmouth's fleet, who on the morning of the Fall ordered every ship to stay
  at anchor to ride out the strange tide, and went down with all of them. His flagship's wreck lies across the harbour mouth; he
  fights with its anchor and its cannon, and calls the tide. He still believes he gave the right order.
- **The Salt King (3).** The master of the salt pans north of the harbour, who sold salt by weight and cheated every buyer by a
  fingertip. The sea took his pans and made him its own: crusted white, he weighs climbers now.
- **Mother of Wrecks (4).** A wreckers' matriarch who lit false lights on the headlands to bring ships onto the rocks. The water came
  in the morning after her best night. She lights them still.
- **The Tide Judge (5).** The harbour's magistrate, who judged the drowned debtors on the quay steps at low tide, and set the tide as
  their sentence. Now the tide is his.
- **The Pearl Tyrant (6).** A pearl merchant who owned every diver on the coast and the debts of their families. She drowned holding
  the strongbox, and the Tower keeps her as she went down: crowned in red coral, in a gown of deep blue and sea-green, strung with the
  pearls she never sold. The Pale Diver serves her still.
- **The Bell Below (7).** The ringer of the harbour's old bell, which hung in the sea temple's tower before Campana cast the new one.
  When it cracked and was thrown into the sea he would not let it go, and went down with it; he wears it now as a ribbed brass helm
  with a round window, in a judge's black robe, a long glaive in his hands, and rings it underwater, and something answers.
- **The Lantern Leviathan (8).** A deep-sea diver of the Lantern Coast who went down after the great fish with a lantern on its head,
  and came back up on the morning the water rose, following the lamps into the harbour: a giant in a black robe and a brass diving
  helm with a light burning in its window. The Coast folk gave him the fish's name.
- **The Gull Queen (9).** The keeper of the harbour's gulls, who fed them from her hand for forty years, and whom they would not leave
  when she drowned. They have grown large.
- **The Sun Warden (10, the crown).** The keeper of the sun's post at the Tower's top, who kept it for longer than any record, received
  the choir every noon for four hundred years, and did not come down when the sun fell. He wears gold and white and a great dial, and
  his dial still strikes. Climbers argue whether he is a man, a Long Dead, a Fallborn or something the Tower made. He is none of them: he
  is the last of the people who made the Tower, and on this floor the Tower's keeping of him, as he was at the hour it took the sun.
  He meets climbers as if they were late.

## The Wood

- **The Elder Root (11).** The heart of the Walking Wood as the Tower holds it: the oak [Fenna](../heroes/fenna.md) planted at twelve,
  which the Tower drew here, set to drink from one of its own roots, and gave it a shape that stands before a climber: an old man taller
  than the lower branches, white-bearded, crowned with its roots, its roots spreading from his hem, the glow of its core in the head
  of his staff, and three roots that feed it. The Moot say it is one of the Tower's own roots, come up through the forest to drink.
- **The Moss Warden (12).** The first warden of the Moot, who swore its oath before it had a name and grew horns of moss; in the Tower
  he guards the Wood from everyone, including the folk.
- **The Thorn Mother (13).** A Briarkin of the old courts who wove thorn hedges round the Underbough to keep the folk out, and in the
  Tower weaves them round everything.
- **The Antler King (14).** A great stag-crowned lord of the Wood, the Stag-Crowned's elder, who leads the bark hounds in the hunt.
- **The Hollow Elder (15).** The oldest of the Moot's elders, who walked with the trees when they began to walk and was hollowed by
  the lightning of that first storm: an old man crowned with roots, and inside him, showing at his staff's head, a light that is not a
  fire.
- **The Briar Witch (16).** A Wood folk herbwoman who sold curses at the Wood's edge and was taken by her own briars in the first year.
- **The Lantern Root (17).** A huntsman of the Wood who hung a lantern of amber on its roots to lure the lost into its dark, until the
  roots took him for it: antlered and cloaked in leaves, with a hunting spear, he carries its amber light still, and feeds on what
  comes.
- **The Grey Oak (18).** The keeper of the Moot's oath-tree before the Fall, a dead oak grey as ash. He swore under it, and when it
  died he took its name: its bleached branches stand on his brow like antlers, its bark and leaves lie on his shoulders like a shield,
  and he carries the spear he kept it with. He would not fall, and walks anyway.
- **The Mire Heart (19).** A bog-witch of the Wood's lowest point, who drowned in her own mire on the night the Wood woke and came up
  with the bog's heart beating in her: thorn-crowned, in a gown of black peat with briars and their roses round it, she swallows what
  walks across it.
- **Ash and Ember (20, the crown).** The fallen sun's two last embers, which fell into the Cinder Pit and burn there: two shapes of fire
  who watch each other. The greatest of the [Embers](../peoples/fallborn.md). In the Tower they are met in the Wood, which fears fire
  above everything.

## The Sands

- **The Scarab Colossus (21).** A queen of the old Sands whom the great storm uncovered from under the Storm Canyon, a giantess sealed
  in the sacred beetle's shell: a beetle's helm that shows no face, wing-cases on her back, a hooked sword. She burrows, marks the
  sand and calls the storm. The Dune folk say she rolled the sun across the sky once. She is the mother of the Scarab King.
- **The Scarab King (22).** Her son, smaller, quicker and gilded, in the same shell, worshipped by the raiders as a god.
- **The Dune Colossus (23).** The first king's guard of the Brass Road, made a giant, the tomb paintings say, by the first king
  himself and buried under a dune in his beetle armour to keep the road; he walks with the dunes.
- **The Brass Sphinx (24).** The riddle-keeper of a city the Sands buried long ago, who kept its gate behind a brass mask with a
  woman's calm face, a lion's mane and a crown of sun rays. She asks a riddle nobody has answered, and fights everyone who tries.
- **The Glass Serpent (25).** A raider of the Flats who was out on them the day the sun's heat struck the sand, and walked out of the
  glass it made: born that day, his people say. He wears the raiders' black beast-mask with its tall gold-lined ears, and his spear is
  tipped with the lightning glass he walked out of; he strikes like the serpent they named him for.
- **The Mirage Lord (26).** A prince of the Throne who walked onto the Glass Flats to be alone and became a mirage of himself.
  [Sahar](../heroes/sahar.md) has heard of him from his grandmother.
- **The Sand Tyrant (27).** A caravan master of the old days who sold water by the cup in a drought, and drowned in sand.
- **The Red Jackal (28).** The Raider Chief's master, a bandit lord of the Storm Canyon, who rode for the crimson banner and took his
  share.
- **The Sun-Eater (29).** A priest of the Father who tried to swallow a piece of the fallen sun to become it, and burns from inside.
- **The Heart of Noon (30, the crown).** The fallen sun's heart, or what the Tower holds of it: a sphere of white fire in a hall of black
  glass, whose light is the day. The Hourkeepers will not call it Fallborn, because it was there first.

## The Deep

- **The Prism Mother (31).** The keeper of the far halls' greatest crystal, a crystal the size of a hall, who sat by it so long that
  when the others went out its light went into her: a tall, pale woman in black glass, crowned with violet crystal, a spear of it in
  her hand, who turns its beams in the dark to find them. She is what the Deep's light becomes with nowhere to go.
- **The Hollow Queen (32).** An Old Mother of the mole-folk who dug too deep alone, and came back hollow.
- **The Lamp Warden (33).** The keeper of the far halls' lamps, who swore they would never go out, and went out with them.
- **The Glass Abbot (34).** The head of a small order of glass-cutters in the far halls, who cut his own eyes into crystal to see in
  the dark.
- **Mother of Echoes (35).** A woman of the galleries that answer every step twice, who called down them for her lost crew until her
  voice was all they kept of her. She walks them now, crowned in crystal, and answers a third time.
- **The Seam King (36).** A Kilnborn mine-lord who owned the violet seam and worked his miners in the dark until the seam went out.
- **The Blind Cantor (37).** One of the hundred: a bass of the choir who sang the Hymn on the day of the Fall and was blinded by the
  sun's cracking, and did not come down the Tower, but came out somewhere else. [Cantor](../heroes/cantor.md) does not know he is here.
- **The Gem Tyrant (38).** A crystal-merchant who hoarded the Deep's light and was buried by it.
- **The Drowned Lantern (39).** A lamplighter of the far halls who fell into the black water with his lamp and kept it burning under
  it, and now walks out of it, hooded, a ring of silver caging his face, the lamp hung from his staff.
- **The Sun Warden (40, the crown).** The keeper of the sun's post, met a second time, and this time himself, come down from the
  Sun Court; he remembers the first. The word that binds him makes him fight. He fights to see whether you are enough.

## The War

- **The Last Marshal (41).** The knight of Harrowmark who refused King Orlan and led the Azure League; never found after the Ninth
  Field. In the Tower he guards both banners: the guard marches at his call, the line burns, and at the end he fights alone on a horse
  of smoke.
- **The Iron Marshal (42).** The crimson's marshal, Orlan's cousin, who held the siege line through the Winter of Mud.
- **The Siege Lord (43).** An Underforge engineer who built the crimson's siege engines for pay, and stayed to see them fire.
- **The Ashen General (44).** The general of the ashen soldiers, a commander who died in the first battle and has led the dead since.
- **The Banner Knight (45).** The crimson's standard-bearer, who swore the banner would never touch the ground, and has held it up for
  a hundred years.
- **The Crow King (46).** A Gloaming lord who rode to the crimson banner with his house guard and his crows, for a share of the sun.
- **The Black Herald (47).** The crimson's herald, who carried King Orlan's last orders and has been carrying them since.
- **The War Drummer (48).** A drummer boy of the azure whose drum still beats the advance, and the dead get up when they hear it.
- **The Grey Captain (49).** An azure captain who deserted on the night before the Ninth Field and has regretted it on every floor.
- **Ash and Ember (50, the crown).** The fallen sun's two last embers again, at the top of the Climb, under the Noon Seat.
