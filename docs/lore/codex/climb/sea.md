# The Sea (floors 1 to 10)

*The Climb's first chapter: the Drowned Harbour on the morning the water came.* · [The Climb](README.md) ·
[The Drowned Coast](../realms/coast.md) · [Coast folk](../peoples/coast.md)

## The land

The first floor of the Tower opens on a harbour at dawn: the great port of Sallowmouth, as it was on the morning after the Fall,
with the sea already in its streets and still rising. Wrecks lie against the quays, lanterns burn on the docks, gulls wheel over a
grey sea, and every seventy-eight seconds the tide comes in for fifteen: the tidal flats flood, slow and burn, and only the higher
ground stays dry. The chapter's floors are the same harbour, rearranged and harder each time: the shipyard, the broken galley, the
sea caves, the lighthouse, the drowned chapel, the sailors' chapel, the wreck, the point of rock the gulls will not leave.

## Its people

All of them Coast folk, who say they were there on that morning:
- **The Bell-Warden**, the keeper of all ten floors, rings the harbour bell that [Campana](../heroes/campana.md) cast. She has rung it
  every morning since, to call the drowned home. She does not know how long that has been.
- **The Net-Father**, the camp's quest giver, mends the sea nets, hauls them, and needs things found in them.
- **The Lamp-Bearer**, out in the land, keeps the shore lamps lit so that boats can find the harbour; the lamps keep going out.
- **The Tide-Broker** (odd floors) weighs what the tide gives up and pays for it in coin a century old.
- **The Coral-Pedlar** (even floors) sells the reef, and charms against drowning that she will not wear.

And on every floor the chapter's common folk walk at their work, and do not stop for climbers:
- **The Dockhand**, a Coast man with a crate on a porter's frame, carries the morning's catch up from the quays. The morning has
  not ended, so neither has the carrying.
- **The Fishwife**, a Coast woman with a creel on her back, cries the catch through the drowned streets to buyers who are under
  the water, and gives the best of it to the gulls.

On the first floor a man called Tam the net-mender needs leading home.

## Its rulers and enemies

The **Drowned Admiral** rules the first two floors from the wreck of his flagship, with anchor, cannon and tide; above him the Salt
King, Mother of Wrecks, the Tide Judge, the Pearl Tyrant, the Bell Below, the Lantern Leviathan and the Gull Queen ([rulers](rulers.md)).
Their packs are the drowned dockers and net casters, the brine crabs, and the dockers' bigger kin: the Harbourmaster's Mate, Old
Barnacle, Saltjaw, Rustfin, Mother Kelp, Captain Gull, the Pale Diver, the Brine Widow and the Tidecaller ([bestiary](bestiary.md)).

## The gate and the crown

[Marin](../heroes/marin.md), the Tide Dancer, holds the chapter's first gate: she danced the water out of the sea temple on that very
morning, and in the Tower she is still the one who decides when the tide goes out. At the tenth floor, the crown, **the Sun Warden**
waits: the keeper of the sun's post at the Tower's top, who did not come down. The gates of the floors above are held by other champions' echoes, of any realm, drawn to the Tower's gates from the Ring.

## What climbers say

The Coast folk who climb say the worst of the Sea is not the dead but the bell: you know the woman ringing it, or your grandmother
did. The Noonsworn say the Sea is a test of faith. The Hourkeepers have measured the tide: it keeps perfect time, the only thing in
the Tower that does.
