# The bestiary of the Climb

*Every kind of enemy on the fifty floors, chapter by chapter, and what it was before the Tower held it.* · [The Climb](README.md) ·
[Rulers](rulers.md)

<!-- team -->The enemies of the Climb are drawn in the game's own code, not built as figures; where a kind is people-shaped, its people is named.
<!-- /team -->The dead who walk here are **the Restless**, dead who got up without a will of their own, never the [Long Dead](../peoples/longdead.md).

## The Sea (floors 1 to 10)

- **Drowned docker.** A hunched drowned man in a dock-worker's coat, grey-green, dripping, with a boathook. Coast folk who were on
  the quays when the water came, and drowned, and got up again, and still try to work the docks. Walks the land side and strikes
  hard. They do not attack the Bell-Warden; they stop and listen when her bell rings.
- **Net caster.** A drowned docker with a net on a pole. Throws it: you are rooted a moment. In life they were the Net-Father's crew,
  and he knows every one of them by name.
- **Brine crab.** A crab the size of a cart: a wide armoured shell low to the ground, two great claws, eight legs, slow. The sea
  brought them in with the water, from deeper than any Coast folk had fished. They guard wrecks the way crabs guard holes.
- **The Harbourmaster's Mate** and **Old Barnacle.** The dockers' bigger kin: the Mate, a broad drowned officer with a lantern and
  a voice that still gives orders; Old Barnacle, a docker so long under water he is more shell than man. They lead packs on most floors
  of the chapter.
- **Saltjaw** and **Rustfin.** Great brine crabs, one white with salt, one red with rust from the wrecks it lives in.
- **Mother Kelp.** A drowned woman wrapped in kelp to the eyes, who drags climbers toward the water. In life she sold seaweed bread
  on the fish-market steps.
- **Captain Gull.** The drowned captain of a fishing boat, with gulls on his shoulders that peck.
- **The Pale Diver.** A drowned pearl-diver, white as a fish's belly, quick in the water and slow on land.
- **The Brine Widow.** A drowned harbour widow in black, who walks the quays looking for a boat that never came in.
- **The Tidecaller.** A drowned priestess of the sea temple who calls the tide early. Marin knew her.

## The Wood (floors 11 to 20)

- **Bark hound.** Low, long and quick: a dog of bark and root with a ruff of leaves on its back and amber eyes. The Wood breeds them
  in its dark; outside, the Hunt of the Moot keeps the paths clear of them.
- **Thorn sprite.** A glowing pink core in a ball of thorns, two leaf wings, hovering. Briarkin who faded inside the Tower and half
  came back: nobody inside remembers them well enough to bring them back whole, and they are angry about it.
- **Moss giant.** A hunched mass of stone and moss on two thick legs, boulder fists, mushrooms on its back. Slow and heavy. Standing
  stones the Wood woke when it began to walk, the oldest of which were there before the Briarkin.
- **Feeding root.** A mound with thorny roots rising round a bulb that pulses when it throws thorns. It cannot move. Three of them feed the Elder Root on the eleventh floor, the oak in an old man's shape; cut them and its door opens.
- **Wisp.** A pale blue light that bobs, with two smaller lights trailing it: lost things the Wood has bound. Not enemies: freed and
  led home, they follow you.
- **The Stag-Crowned.** A tall gold-and-bark figure with a great crown of antlers that glows; it charges along a red line and stands
  winded after. The Wood folk say it is what the Moot's oath would make of a person if they swore it with no limit.
- **The Hollow Oak.** A walking tree with a dark hollow for a face and amber eyes in it, two branch arms and a crown.
- **Mossjaw** and **Old Rootfoot.** A moss giant with a jaw of stone, and the oldest of the walking trees, who limps.
- **Grandmother Thorn.** The greatest of the thorn sprites, who remembers being a Briarkin of the Spindle court, and hates Grandma
  Ironknit for being remembered.
- **The Pale Stag**, **the Bark Widow** and **the Hollow Hunter.** A white stag that has never been hunted in the year it shed, a
  mother hound who guards her den, and a hunter of the Wood folk who went into the trees in the first year and became part of them.

## The Sands (floors 21 to 30)

- **Raider.** A wrapped figure in rust with a turquoise sash, a round shield and a curved blade. Dune folk of the caravan towns, caught, the Sands say, in the great storm,
and who rob what passes: in the Tower, they are still waiting for the caravan.
- **Slinger.** A raider with a sling instead of a blade, from the rim of the canyon.
- **Dust wraith.** A tall swirl of dark sand with pale rings, no legs, two pale slits for eyes. The storm given a shape; the Dune folk
  say they are the souls of caravaners lost in it.
- **The caravan.** A covered cart on two wheels with a lantern on its pole, which rolls when it moves. Not an enemy: escort it home.
- **The Dune Mother.** A hooded matriarch of sand in a robe of rust and turquoise bands, gold eyes under the hood, a ring of stones
  that turns. A caravan mother who swore the storm would not take her children, and became part of it to keep them.
- **The Raider Chief.** The raiders' leader, who once rode for the crimson banner.
- **The Storm Count.** The storm's herald, a figure of wind and glass who walks before each storm on every floor of the chapter.
- **The Glass Widow.** A woman of lightning glass from the Flats, sharp-edged and singing; seen in the Sands and, strangely, in the
  Deep.

## The Deep (floors 31 to 40)

- **Shard mite.** Small, quick, crystal-backed things that come in swarms: what the crystals breed when their light goes out.
- **Crystal golem.** Slow, armoured, made of crystal and rock, throws a bolt back. Not Hesk's: the Deep made these itself, or the
  Tower did.
- **Lurker.** Waits in the dark and leaps. Nobody has seen one clearly in the light, because they will not come into it. The far-hall
  people say they were miners lost in the dark too long.
- **The Glass Widow.** Here too, in a flooded vault: the same singing glass, or her sister.

## The War (floors 41 to 50)

- **Crimson footman, bowman and knight.** The soldiers of King Orlan's crimson banner: Harrowmark's levies, Dune Throne riders and
  Gloaming house guards, as they were in the last spring of the war. They fight the azure on sight, and anyone else after.
- **Azure footman, bowman and knight.** The soldiers of the Azure League: Aubade's paladins, Coast levies, Underforge axes, Northern
  surveyors, Moot hunters, and Ninefold knights on both sides. They fight the crimson on sight.
- **Ashen soldier.** The dead of both banners, grey with the ash of the burned farms, still fighting: the Restless of the Field. Both
  banners treat them as their own.
- **The Ash Captain.** The captain of the ashen soldiers, who leads them on most floors of the chapter: a crimson officer who died on
  the Ninth Field still giving orders.

## Who is not an enemy

The keepers, quest givers and traders of each chapter, the wisps and the caravan, and the rivals and gatekeepers (heroes of the Ring,
fought in duels by choice).
