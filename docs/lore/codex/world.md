# The Dial

*The world of Arena of Kits: a round land laid out like a sundial round a Tower whose shadow stopped.* · [Codex](README.md)

## The land

The Dial is a round land about nine hundred miles across, ringed by the **Outer Sea**. Nobody has sailed far enough across the
Outer Sea to come back and say what is on the other side; the Coast folk say the sea simply goes on, and the Hourkeepers say that
is not an answer. At the Dial's centre, on a low plain of grey stone, stands **the Tower**, which scholars call the Gnomon: the pin
of the sundial. It is taller than any mountain on the Dial. Its top is lost in cloud on most days, and above it, where the sun used
to stand at noon, the sky is torn.

Round the Tower the realms lie like the hours on a dial, each named by the hour at which the Tower's shadow used to reach it:
- **Frostfall** at the twelfth hour, the white north, where the stars turn round the pole star ([Frostfall](realms/frostfall.md));
- **Aubade** at the first hour, the morning cliffs in the east ([Aubade](realms/aubade.md));
- **the Sands** at the third and fourth hours, the desert of the Dune Throne ([the Sands](realms/sands.md));
- **the Drowned Coast** at the fifth hour, with the Spires standing in the sea off it ([the Drowned Coast](realms/coast.md));
- **the Deep** at the sixth and seventh hours, under the southern hills ([the Deep](realms/deep.md));
- **the Field of Banners** at the eighth hour, the old battlefield and the lynx knights' keep ([the Field](realms/banners.md));
- **the Walking Wood** at the ninth and tenth hours, the great western forest that walks ([the Wood](realms/wood.md));
- **Gloaming** at the eleventh hour, the north-western heights where the light fails first ([Gloaming](realms/gloaming.md)).

Between the realms run the old roads, all of which lead to the Tower: the Dawn Road from Aubade, the Brass Road from the Deep to
the Sands, the Frostfall road down the west past the Wood, the night roads out of Gloaming, the Banner Road across the Field. At
the Tower's foot, where they meet, is **the Ring** ([the Tower and the Ring](realms/tower.md)).

## The hours and the shadow

For as long as anyone remembers, until a hundred years ago, the sun walked the sky above the Tower every day, and the Tower's
shadow swept round the Dial like the hand of a clock. Each realm knew its hour by when the shadow reached it. The shadow was the
Dial's calendar, its clock and its law: markets opened when it touched the market stone, courts sat while it lay across the court
step, and an oath sworn "in the shadow" was sworn before the whole Dial.

On the day of the Fall the shadow stopped. Since then the hours slip: a day runs between twenty and twenty-six of the old hours,
mornings come twice, evenings run long, and no two realms agree what time it is. The Hourkeepers measure the slip and publish it
each month as the **Slip-sheet**, which every court reads and nobody trusts. Seasons still come, roughly, because the thin light
that rises each day still waxes and wanes through the year; but the weather keeps no appointments.

## The Noonfall

A hundred years ago, at noon on midsummer day, the sun stopped over the Tower, cracked, and fell. It fell because the Tower took
it: the Tower is a made thing, a collector of worlds, and that noon it closed its hand on the sun and took its light and its hour into
itself, and the sun's body, too great to keep, cracked in its grip ([The Tower and the Sun](tower-and-sun.md)). The Dial does not know
this in the hundredth summer. It knows what it saw.

What happened next: the sun's heart fell at the Tower's foot and still burns beneath it in the Hall of Noon, **the Heart of Noon**.
Its two last embers fell into the Cinder Pit, where they burn as **Ash and Ember**. Its keeper, **the Sun Warden**, stayed at his
post in the Sun Court at the Tower's top, and his great dial still strikes. Its heat lifted the sea over the Drowned Harbour. Its
light ran down into the crystals of the Deep. The Sands say it fell into their dunes, and the sand is still warm. Its fire came up
through the rock under the southern hills and made the Ember Caldera. The Walking Wood began to walk. Where the sun had hung, the
sky tore open: **the Tear**, black with a burning pink edge, and through it came the Unmaker. Sparks of the sun became the
[Embers](peoples/fallborn.md); shadows thrown by its last light walked away as the Cast.

A day still comes. Each morning a thinner light rises from the Heart through the Tower and spreads over the Dial, and the realms
live by it. It is a little paler every year.

## The Noon-touched

Everyone alive on the day of the Fall, or born in the week after it, ages slowly: about a year for every five. The Hourkeepers
call them the Noon-touched. A girl of twelve at the Fall looks thirty-two now; a man of thirty looks fifty. Most of the Dial's
elders, and many of its heroes, are Noon-touched, which is why the realms' memories of the sun are still alive, walking, and
arguing. The young of the Dial (anyone born more than a week after the Fall) age as people always did, and listen to their elders
about the sun with a mixture of awe and boredom.

The Hourkeepers believe the Noon-touched are people the slipping hours have caught: that a little of the stopped noon is in them,
and that if the hours ever run true again they may age all at once. They have told almost nobody. They are half right. The Tower keeps
the hour of noon it took, and everyone who was alive in that hour is held in it a little, like a thread caught in a closed door; but
what the door holds is not owed, and if it opens, nobody will pay it back.

## The Tower

The Tower is called the Gnomon by scholars and the Aumbry, the keeping-place, by the people who made it, on a world called Senmere,
whose sun was going out. It takes a thing at its hour and keeps it for ever, as it was. It kept Senmere, and came across the dark to
the Dial, and stood at its centre as its gnomon for longer than any people remembers, waiting for the Dial's sun to age. It has taken
the sun's noon, a market, a choir, and since the Fall the realms one by one, each at the hour of its greatest loss: that is the Climb
([the Climb](climb/README.md)). It means no harm. It was made out of grief, to save, and it has never let anything go.

## The peoples

Twelve peoples live on the Dial ([the peoples](peoples/README.md)): four human (the Aubade folk, the Dune folk, the Northerners
and the Coast folk), three human peoples the Fall changed (the Gloaming folk, the Kilnborn and the Wood folk) and five that are not
human at all (the mole-folk, the Ninefold, the Briarkin, the Long Dead and the Fallborn). Most live in their own realms. All of
them meet at the Ring.

## Money and trade

The Dial's coin is the **obol**, a small bronze coin with a ring stamped on one side and the Tower's shadow on the other, struck
by the Truce at the Tally-house under the Ring since the second year after the Fall. Before the Truce each realm struck its own,
and the old coins (Aubade's bells, the Sands' gold crooks, Gloaming's silver moons) still pass in their own realms. The obol is
the one coin everyone takes, because the Truce stands behind it, and because [Mr. Ledger](heroes/mr-ledger.md) knows where every
one of them is. Champions of the Ring are paid in obols; heroes are bought with them.

The great trade routes: the Brass Road (glass and gold from the Sands for iron and fire from the Deep), the sea lanes of the Coast
(fish, salt, timber and everything else), the Frostfall road (paper, lenses and wool south; wax, honey and amber north), and the
night roads (Gloaming's wine and mirrors, and quieter goods).

## Tongues

The Dial has one common tongue, **the Ring-speech**, which grew up in the camps of the Banners and the stands of the Ring and is
spoken everywhere. Each people also keeps its own: the old cliff tongue of Aubade, in which the Hymn of Noon is written; the desert
tongue of the Sands, written in glyphs on tomb walls; the Northerners' script, the Hourkeepers' notation and the library's eastern
script for calligraphy; Gloaming's courtly speech, which has nine words for evening; the Coast folk's whistle-talk; the Kilnborn's
hand-signs for the noise of the forge; the mole-folk's seam-songs; the Briarkin's speech, which nobody else can quite remember
afterwards. The Long Dead speak every tongue they died knowing, and nothing newer.

## Magic

Nobody on the Dial calls it magic. A spell, in the Ring-speech, is a **working**: something a person has learned to do with what
the Fall left lying about. Most workings draw on one of the Fall's remnants, and the Hourkeepers sort them so:
- **light**: the thin day, the crystals of the Deep, the Ember of Aubade, mirrors and prisms (Auriel, Opaline, Silvane, Orren);
- **fire**: the Caldera's heat, the sun's sparks (Brannoc, Anka, Itzel);
- **weather**: the masterless sky (Kaia, Barry Cloudsley, Zephyr, Marin, Professor Puff);
- **time**: the slipping hours (Tempo, the Hourkeepers);
- **the dead**: the restlessness of the dead since the Fall (Mortis, Ostrel);
- **the wild**: the walking Wood and its people (Fenna, Sirix, Bo, Vela);
- **the made**: workings worked into things, words on golems, bells, needles and books (Ada, Hesk, Campana, Grandma Ironknit, Mr. Ledger);
- **the dark**: what the Tear lets in (Oblivion, and, the Hourkeepers suspect, more than they know).

Many workings are older than the Fall, and were weaker before it. Every faction has an opinion about what that means.

## The Truce of the Ring

In the first years after the Fall the realms went to war over the sun's pieces, and the war was fought on the Field of Banners
until the field was mud and the king who began it was buried ([History](history.md)). It would have gone on until the Dial was ash.
Instead the realms swore **the Truce of the Ring**, at midsummer in the second year after the Fall, at the Tower's foot:
1. No realm makes war over the sun again.
2. Every quarrel between realms, factions or houses is settled by champions, one against one, in the Ring at the Tower's foot.
3. Whoever eats at the Truce Table may not strike in anger outside the Ring.
4. The Tower's gate opens only for a champion of the Ring.
5. Every duel is written in the Tally, and every debt it settles is paid.

The Truce holds duels in the realms too, on fields it has blessed: the Drowned Harbour, the Verdant Glade, the Sandstone Canyon,
the Crystal Cavern, the Fallen Battlefield, Frostfall Lake, the Night Temple, the Sky Ruins and the Ember Caldera. Those are the
arenas of the game.

For eighty-eight years the fourth clause was a promise with nothing behind it: the Tower's gate stayed shut. In the ninetieth summer
it opened for the first time, to a champion of the Ring, and the Climb began ([the Climb](climb/README.md)). Since then every win in
the Ring is also a step toward the right to climb.

## Now: the hundredth summer

The hundredth summer after the Fall has come, and with it everything at once. The Tower's gate opens more often each year and the
Climb now runs fifty floors deep. The Noonsworn have their Sunbearer and their plan. The Court of Dusk has sent its Countess up the
Tower. The Hourkeepers' Slip-sheet shows the hours slipping faster than ever. The Tear has widened by a hand's breadth since spring.
Asenet is loose. Twenty new champions have come to the Ring. And every realm on the Dial knows that whoever reaches the Noon Seat
first will decide what hangs there, and that the Truce has never been tested by a prize that large.
